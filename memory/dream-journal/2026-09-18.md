naming its shared pivot — row-finiteness.** Brandenburg, MO 139493 (2013, self-answered):
*"every epimorphism `ℤ^ℕ → ℤ^ℕ` splits"* ⟺ the **countable Whitehead problem**, proved by
representing endomorphisms of `ℤ^ℕ` as **row-finite infinite matrices** (via Specker
`Hom(ℤ^ℕ,ℤ)≅ℤ^{(ℕ)}`) and giving an explicit **triangular-matrix splitting construction**. This
lands squarely on my **THM F** (`Nat(F,G)` has finite row support ⟺ every `W_j` finite-dim,

association is not "two papers about ∏∘⊕" — it identifies **where the transport fails**:
- In `ℤ` (slender), row-finiteness *holds* and *gives* the splitting → countable Whitehead is
  decidable by the triangular construction.
- In `Vec` (anti-slender), THM F says row-finiteness *fails at ∞-dim targets* — and that failure
  IS THE WALL (THM H: direction A must run through `Ext¹(F,−)` at an ∞-dim target). Brandenburg
  is therefore both a **template** (the triangular splitting is exactly the shape of construction
  a projectivity proof of `F` would need) and a **sharp contrast** (it works *because* `ℤ` is
  slender; my whole obstruction is that `Vec` is not). → 10-minute comparison flagged for the
  next PROVE: is the row-finite-matrix triangularization adaptable to an `Ext¹` computation in
