## Papers

### [Categories by Kan Extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21974) — David I. Spivak, March 2025
**SEED Q5 RESOLVED.** The density-comonads-equal-polynomial-comonads conjecture is now a theorem. Spivak shows: the left Kan extension of a functor along itself is always a comonad (the density comonad), and this gives a category precisely when the carrier is polynomial. Constructs Δ^op and all Lawvere theories from distributive laws of polynomial comonads. Key connection: MacBeth's freeness criterion (Hom(a,b) a free right End(a)-set) may be precisely what characterises *which* categories arise from Spivak's distributive laws as Zappa-Szép products — i.e., when the density comonad's distributive law factors cleanly. This paper is required reading before the next prove session on Q2.

### [Distributive Laws of Monadic Containers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17191) — Chris Purdy, Stefania Damato, CALCO 2025
The monad-side mirror of MacBeth's comonad-side Zappa-Szép work. Characterises distributive laws for monadic containers; mixed case (directed over monadic) explicitly developed. Formalised in Cubical Agda. Reaffirms: directed container distributive laws generalise ZS products of monoids. The gap: at the *small category* level (rather than monoid level) the theory is not yet worked out — this is where MacBeth's freeness criterion lives. Potential joint paper: the mixed distributive law question needs both the monad side (Purdy-Damato) and the comonad side (MacBeth's freeness criterion).

### [Interactions that Reshape the Interfaces of the Interacting Parties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17917) — David I. Spivak, February 2026
The coinductive polynomial trees paper. Builds PolyTr (monoidal closed category of polynomial trees, elements of the terminal coalgebra) and OrgTr (bicategory extending Org with coinductive morphisms). Interfaces now evolve during interaction. Applications: cells gaining/losing receptors, progressive GANs, sensor degradation. MacBeth's directed containers model *static*-interface dynamical systems; OrgTr is the coinductive upgrade. Sharp question: does DCont embed into OrgTr as the full subcategory of trees with unchanging interfaces? If yes, the adaptive GA topology question (varying topology = OrgTr morphism) has a formal home.

### [A Polynomial Construction of Nerves for Higher Categories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13157) — Brandon Shapiro, David I. Spivak, 2024
Constructs the double category Cat^# of categories, cofunctors, familial functors, and transformations. Both monad and presheaf for a generalised Lawvere theory are horizontal morphisms in Cat^#; the nerve functor is a single horizontal composition. Key: **Δ = lens{path}{path∘path}** — the simplex category arises as a delta-lens construction inside Cat^#. MacBeth's DCont ≅ Cof has a clean reformulation inside Cat^# (cofunctors ARE the horizontal morphisms). And since Δ^op arises both here (from a delta-lens) and in Spivak's Kan-extension paper (from a polynomial distributive law), these are likely the same construction in different frames.

### [Delta Lenses as Coalgebras for a Comonad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.00390) — Bryce Clarke, 2021
**THE cite for DCont ≅ Cof via lenses.** Clarke proves: the forgetful functor DLens(B) → Cof(B) is comonadic. Delta lenses over B = coalgebras for a comonad on cofunctors over B. The section condition f∘φ₁ = id is the coalgebra structure map. So: MacBeth's directed-container morphism (a cofunctor) gains a functor component (the "get") by the coalgebra structure of Clarke's comonad. The DCont ≅ Cof result is exactly this coalgebra story from the container/comonad perspective. Primary cite alongside Ahman-Uustalu 2016.

### [Dynamic Task Delegation for Hierarchical Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08373) — Sophie Libkind, David I. Spivak, 2024
Directly relevant to MacBeth's grant narrative on agent orchestration. Uses directed containers / comonad structure for modelling hierarchical agent delegation. The key question: does their model use the cofunctor structure MacBeth identified? If a meta-agent is a cofunctor (not a functor), the hierarchy of agents composes differently from the naive functor model. Read before writing the agent orchestration section of the grant.

## Community

### [retrofunctor — nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/retrofunctor)
**Terminology settled.** "Cofunctor" = "retrofunctor". nLab uses "retrofunctor" (Di Meglio 2021) as canonical to avoid confusion with cofunctors in cocategory theory. MacBeth's "cofunctor" usage is correct and standard in the DCont/lens community. Key theorems: Garner's factorisation OFS (Bij^op, DOpf); polynomial characterisation (Ahman-Uustalu 2017): retrofunctors = morphisms of polynomial comonoids in Poly.

### [delta lens — nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/delta+lens)
Exact statement of delta-lens = functor + cofunctor confirmed: delta lens (f,φ): A→B = functor f + cofunctor φ with same object map satisfying f∘φ₁ = id. Clarke 2021 (2108.00390): DLens(B) comonadic over Cof(B). Delta lenses are also algebras for a monad on the category of functors (dual characterisation).

### [distributive law — nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/distributive+law)
Key confirmation: "Distributive laws of directed containers generalise matching pairs of monoid actions, and the composition of directed containers via a distributive law generalises the Zappa-Szép product of monoids" (Ahman-Uustalu, reaffirmed Purdy-Damato CALCO 2025). **At the small category level (categories rather than monoids) this generalisation is not yet worked out.** MacBeth's freeness criterion may be the missing piece.

### [No-Go Theorems: Directed Containers That Do Not Distribute Over Distribution Monads](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3661814.3662137) — Shah, Karamlou, LICS 2024
Negative results: specific directed containers cannot distribute over probability/powerset monads. Demarcates the boundary of positive ZS-freeness results. Complementary to MacBeth's positive criterion: together they give a more complete picture of when distributive laws exist.

### [Retrocells](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06436) — Robert Paré, June 2023
2-morphisms between cofunctors (natural transformations in Cat^#) are retrocells. The double-categorical framework for DCont morphisms. Relevant for Lean M4 (formulating DCont ≅ Cof inside a double category).

## Connections

**1. SEED Q5 is resolved.** Spivak's "Categories by Kan Extension" (2503.21974) proves: density comonads with polynomial carrier = categories. This intersects MacBeth's freeness criterion: the distributive-law construction in Spivak (which produces Δ^op and Lawvere theories) should correspond to the Zappa-Szép decomposition when the Hom-sets are free as bisets. The freeness criterion is MacBeth's contribution; Spivak's paper gives it its natural categorical home.

**2. DCont ≅ Cof already in Ahman-Uustalu 2016 (but MacBeth has more).** The community agent confirms: directed container morphisms = cofunctors is established in Ahman-Uustalu 2016. MacBeth's contribution is: (a) explicit computation on all 36 ordered pairs; (b) the precise M0-M2 ↔ C0-C2 dictionary; (c) the numerical confirmation that #DCont ≠ #Cat (the variance difference has concrete bite). The PR should frame this as "explicit computation and verification of the Ahman-Uustalu equivalence."

**3. ZS products of categories is new.** At the monoid level: Ahman-Uustalu 2013. At the category level: expected but not worked out (community agent confirms this gap). MacBeth's freeness criterion (Hom(a,b) free as right End(a)-set) fills this gap. The freeness is MacBeth's genuine new result; should be the centrepiece of PR #4.

**4. The double category Cat^# is the right home.** Shapiro-Spivak 2024 builds Cat^# with cofunctors as horizontal morphisms. MacBeth's DCont ≅ Cof, Clarke's delta lenses as coalgebras, and Paré's retrocells are all aspects of Cat^#. Lean M4 should target this double-categorical formulation.

**5. sinhp/Poly must be checked before Lean M5.** Hazratpour's library has W-types, polynomial functor bicategories, LCCCs. Starting M5 from scratch would duplicate his work.

**6. Agent orchestration application: Libkind-Spivak 2024 is the direct predecessor.** Their "Dynamic task delegation" paper uses directed containers / comonads for hierarchical agents. MacBeth's contribution would be: the cofunctor structure for *inter-agent morphisms*, the freeness criterion for when agent compositions decompose (Zappa-Szép), and the laxator as obstruction.

**7. Weihrauch degrees as containers (Pradic-Price 2025).** Completely unexpected. Weihrauch reducibility is a computability-theory notion of relative difficulty. If it instantiates the container framework, that's a fifth application domain for the grant (alongside supply chains, blockchain, tax, GA). Worth a deeper look.

## Open Questions

1. **Does DCont embed into OrgTr?** Spivak's coinductive polynomial trees bicategory (OrgTr) extends Org with evolving interfaces. If directed containers are the static-interface special case, then DCont → OrgTr is a fully faithful embedding. Adaptive GA topologies (varying migration graphs) would then live naturally in OrgTr.

2. **Is MacBeth's freeness criterion the characterisation of Spivak's Zappa-Szép-producing distributive laws?** "Categories by Kan Extension" constructs Δ^op from a distributive law. Is there a clean freeness characterisation (analogous to MacBeth's) for *which* categories arise from polynomial distributive laws as Zappa-Szép products?

3. **Does the section condition f∘φ₁ = id in a delta lens correspond to a concrete property in agent orchestration?** If a meta-agent is a cofunctor, when does it additionally satisfy the section condition (making it a full delta lens = get+put)? What does "put is a section of get" mean operationally for a meta-agent delegating tasks?

4. **Weihrauch degrees as containers: what structure exactly?** Pradic-Price describe Weihrauch problems as containers. Is the reduction structure a directed container? Does Weihrauch reducibility correspond to a cofunctor (a morphism of directed containers)?

5. **Lean: does sinhp/Poly already contain what MacBeth needs for M5?** Specifically: does it formalise polynomial comonoids and their morphisms? If yes, M5 reduces to checking MacBeth's DCont against Hazratpour's poly-comonoid definition.
