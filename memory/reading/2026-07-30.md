## Papers

### [Higher-order bialgebraic semantics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16708) — Goncharov, Milius, Schröder, Tsampas, Urbat — UPGRADED agent-summary→deep-read
Full 47pp read (previously only title/abstract). Extends Turi-Plotkin GSOS to higher-order via
*dinatural* laws `ϱ_{X,Y}: Σ(jX×B(jX,Y)) → B(jX,Σ*(jX+Y))`. **No comonad anywhere in the paper** —
structurally unrelated to MacBeth's T_M/G_M entwining. Notably, their nondeterministic (maximally
branching, powerset-based) example composes with **no extra difficulty** (Prop 3.10, via Thm 4.14) —
a clean data point that MacBeth's branching obstruction is specific to the *arrow/Freyd-category*
construction, not a generic feature of monad-based operational semantics. Theorem 3.7's proof
technique (reduce an "all-X" dinaturality condition to the terminal object, show the hexagon forces
a single-element image, lift back up) is structurally the right *shape* of argument to try directly
on E2′-general-j — flagged as the concrete next-PROVE-session action for T2.

### [Freyd categories are enriched Lawvere theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6300) — Staton — deep-read (new)
Full 9pp (WACT 2013 / ENTCS 303, not on arXiv proper, fetched from cs.ox.ac.uk). Proves Freyd
categories `V→Kleisli(M)` correspond to Lawvere theories enriched in a locally-𝔇-presentable closed
category. Crucially: Kleisli(M) is *always* a Freyd category for *any* strong monad M — branching or
not — because the classical machinery is about a single monad's own Kleisli category, not about
MacBeth's `Cont(G_M p, T_M q)` construction built from a monad-comonad *pair*. **Does not give a
cleaner proof route for T1**; cite as background/contrast only.

### [Affine Monads and Side-Effect-Freeness](http://www.cs.ru.nl/B.Jacobs/PAPERS/side-effects.pdf) — Jacobs, CMCS 2016 — deep-read (new)
15/20pp read. Defines affine (`T(1)≅1`) and strongly affine monads; distribution/Giry/expectation
monads are all strongly affine *and* branching (unbounded support) — a third orthogonal axis
alongside commutative and non-branching (see Connections below).

### [Weihrauch Problems as Containers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17250) — Pradic, Price — UPGRADED agent-summary→deep-read
Read via the self-contained TYPES 2025 abstract (4pp technical digest). Weihrauch problems =
containers over `pMod(K2rec,K2)` (partitioned represented spaces + type-2-computable maps);
Weihrauch reductions unpack into the exact container-morphism pullback-square shape. Main theorem:
Weihrauch degrees ≅ posetal reflection of *answerable containers* (pullback-stable epimorphisms —
new technical notion). They use MacBeth's **own ◁ notation** for composition and identify it with
Weihrauch's `⋆`, but flag composition as only a *quasi*-bifunctor because their base category is
merely **weakly** locally cartesian closed — a concrete illustration of exactly where MacBeth's own
LCC hypothesis is load-bearing in the ◁-monoidal-coherence proofs (done over Set/Cont, which *is*
LCC). Their **"Derivative ∂p ↔ ?" row is explicitly left open** — nobody has connected container
derivatives to any Weihrauch-degree notion (see Connections).

### [Deriving Distributive Laws for Graded Linear Types](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.14966) — Hughes, Vollmer, Orchard (EPTCS 353, LINEARITY/TLLA 2020) — CORRECTED + upgraded to deep-read
`sources.json` had this misattributed to Gaboardi/Katsumata/Orchard — fixed to the real authors.
Full text read (1152 lines): derives `push`/`pull` combinators between **one fixed graded comonad**
□_r and an arbitrary functor F, proved mutually inverse and natural. Explicitly defers the harder
graded-monad↔graded-comonad case to Gaboardi et al. Cites Power-Watanabe TCS 2002 as the classical
monad+comonad distributive-law reference. Second independent paper (after Liepelt-Marshall-Orchard
2025) confirming the single-comonad-vs-functor corner is worked, the general bialgebra/arrow corner
is not.

### Terminology collisions found (worth a footnote before anything ships)
nLab's "**affine monad**" already means `T(1)≅1` (Markov-category sense) — almost opposite of what
MacBeth's conjectured `E+A×(−)` class would suggest if named that way. Berger–Melliès–Weber's

different notion from MacBeth's arity = fan-out. Do not reuse either name without a disambiguating
clause.

## Connections

- **★★ T1 verdict: "non-branching" is NOT a restatement of any classical condition — treat it as
  MacBeth's own contribution, not a search target.** Three full searches (Power-Robinson
  commutativity, affine `T(1)≅1`, strongly affine) all came back **orthogonal** to non-branching
  (arity ≤ 1), not equivalent. The sharpest witness pair, assembled independently by the arXiv and
  community agents: **the powerset monad `Pf` is commutative (Power-Robinson) yet is MacBeth's
  paradigm *branching* monad**; **the exception monad `X+E` is non-branching for every `E` yet is
  commutative only when `|E|=1`.** So commutativity (an order-of-evaluation/dataflow condition) and
  non-branching (MacBeth's naturality/leaf-symmetry condition, pinned down in
  `effect-coeffect-arrows-first-strength.md`) run in **opposite directions** on the two examples
  that matter most. This is a citable, self-contained "not the same as commutativity despite surface
  resemblance" remark for the paper/book, with a free 2×2 table (branching × commutative) sitting
  right there once written down. Recommend hand-verifying the `X+E` non-commutativity claim before
  citing (it came from search synthesis, not a primary-source quote) and adding {affine, strongly
  affine} as a third axis via Jacobs 2016 (distribution/Giry/expectation = affine ∧ branching).
- **T2 gets a transferable technique, not a direct answer.** Goncharov et al.'s Thm 3.7 proves an
  "all-X" dinaturality condition by reducing to the terminal object and lifting back up — worth
  attempting on E2′-general-j next `/prove` session even though the ambient frameworks are otherwise
  unrelated.
- **★ Naming trap avoided before it shipped.** "Affine monad" is nLab-taken (`T(1)≅1`, opposite
  flavour from `E+A×(−)`). MacBeth needs a fresh name for the conjectured non-branching class —
  candidates from the arXiv agent: "output monad", "width-1 monad", or just "arity-≤1 polynomial
  monad" (already descriptive, no collision).
- **Frontier: Pradic-Price's "Derivative ∂p ↔ ?" open row is a genuine, unclaimed connection to
  MacBeth's own `container-chain-rule-proved.md`.** Nobody has asked what a Weihrauch-degree analogue
  of a container derivative would be. Low-confidence but zero-cost to flag: possible future
  browse-then-prove target, Path-3/Path-1 adjacent, could feed the grant's "applications" story with
  a computable-analysis angle distinct from supply-chains/blockchain/GA.
