## Papers

### [Antoine Touzé, "The universal property of strict polynomial functors"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00631) (2026)
Best hub find of the session. Shows that in characteristic 0, strict polynomial functors are the
tensor abelian category freely generated by one object; this universal property FAILS in
characteristic p, and the paper repairs it by restricting the class of tensor abelian categories
considered. Then shows Ext-algebras of strict polynomial functors act on cohomology in other
settings. **This is doing exactly the universal-property / free-(co)algebraic-structure move
MacBeth already uses for containers, now applied to the object class Neil wants us building on.**
Read closely before committing to any Fam(Vec^op) framing — it may hand over the "free structure
generated by shapes" argument almost for free, or show where the char-p subtlety bites. Abstract-level
only so far (citation-trail agent).

### [Djament, Touzé, "Homology of additive functors in prime characteristic"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10522) (2026, reorganizing 2111.09719)
Computes Ext/Tor in the category of ALL functors from an 𝔽_p-linear additive category A to Vec,
relates it to the subcategory of *additive* functors, derives new GL_n homology results. This is
almost exactly the ambient setting the new front needs (functors A → Vec, A additive/linear) —
the natural background citation before claiming any novelty for the container/comonoid framing.
Companion papers **"Functor homology over an additive category"** (arXiv:2111.09719) and
**"Homological finiteness of functors on an additive category and applications"** (arXiv:2111.09717)
cover the same ground from different angles. High priority for a full read next PROVE/expository
session on the Vec front. Abstract-level only.

### [Coulembier, Hu, "Polynomial functors in Ver₄⁺"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18175) (2026)
Classifies additive, exact, and simple polynomial functors internal to the incompressible symmetric
tensor category Ver₄⁺ (char-2 "super" setting), with annihilation/faithfulness results for
symmetric-group-algebra actions under braiding. A genuine "polynomial functor classification over a
linear/tensor-category base other than Set" paper — methodologically the closest thing found to
what MacBeth's crown target (poly-comonoid/Vec = algebroid) will need to be checked against, though
the base category (Ver₄⁺) is more exotic than plain Vec. Abstract-level only.

### [Xantcha, "Polynomial Functors of Modules"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.0576) (2012, newly surfaced)
Generalizes Eilenberg–MacLane polynomial functors to modules over a "binomial base ring," encodes
them by modules, gives a criterion for when a numerical/polynomial functor admits a strict
polynomial structure in the Friedlander–Suslin sense, characterizes analytic functors. Old (2012)
but not previously tracked — direct precedent for "polynomial functor over a module-like base."
Abstract-level only.

### ["Zappa–Szép Skew Braces"](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/14/2/215) (MDPI Mathematics, DOI 10.3390/math14020215, 2026-01-06)
Generalizes semidirect skew braces / matched-pair factorizations to a genuinely bidirectional
Zappa-Szép-style mutual-action setting between two "digroups," with a semidirect embedding theorem
and explicit examples. On the standing ZS/H² thread — a skew-brace-flavored analogue one step closer
to the actual ZS shape (not just semidirect) than the existing Rathee-Yadav tracking. **Full text
403-blocked (MDPI paywall)** — title/DOI/abstract-snippet only, treat claims as provisional.

## Web Research

### [Schur functor (nLab)](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Schur+functor) — ★ the session's best find
States: **"Schur functors correspond precisely to polynomial species — functors from core(FinSet) to
FinDimVect that vanish on sufficiently large finite sets. The category of Schur functors is
equivalent to the category of polynomial species, forming a symmetric monoidal abelian category."**
Every Schur functor S_λ (built from a Young symmetrizer p_λ ∈ ℂ[S_n]) decomposes as a finite direct
sum of irreducibles indexed by Young diagrams. Read as: shapes = irreducible S_n-representations,
positions = multiplicity spaces — a ready-made, published, char-0 instance of exactly the
"shape+position over Vec, shapes = indecomposable summands" picture MacBeth's own biproduct-collapse
orientation note independently arrived at. **Needs checking directly against the `⊕_s Vec(P_s,W)`
framing before any novelty claim** — this could either be a citable classical precedent to build on,
or (if the fit is exact) a partial scoop of the "shapes = indecomposable summands" observation.

### [John Baez, "Schur functors I" (personal nLab)](https://ncatlab.org/johnbaez/show/Schur+functors+I)
Constructs S_λ via idempotent-splitting in the Cauchy completion, generalizing to any symmetric
monoidal Cauchy-complete linear category over a char-0 field (group reps, super vector spaces,
coherent sheaves, vector bundles) — not just FinVect. States the base-change naturality property
that any "shapes as summands" classification needs to be functorial. Good expository entry point.

### [Schur Functors thread, n-Category Café (2007)](https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2007/04/schur_functors.html)
Substantive old discussion. **David Speyer's comment is a sharp trap-warning**: in characteristic 2,
the functors for partitions (2) and (1,1) are isomorphic *pointwise on every vector space* but must
be considered *different functors* — i.e. functor-equality is strictly stronger than value-wise
equality on objects. This is directly relevant as a caution against over-reading the biproduct
collapse (`⊕=Π ⟹ ⟦S,P⟧W = W^N` says the functors *evaluate* the same, not that distinct shapes
collapse to one functor) — worth flagging explicitly in the next PROVE session on this front, in any
characteristic-p extension. Todd Trimble in the same thread suggests weakening right-exactness to
Cauchy-completeness + additivity + symmetric-monoidal, close to an "additive functor classification"
formulation.

### [polynomial functor (nLab)](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/polynomial+functor)
Confirms the general pullback-based definition works in any category with pullbacks (no LCC needed),
and names the `g=identity` special case a "linear functor"/"linear polynomial functor" — but goes no
further; no Vec-valued, additive, or strict-polynomial-functor content anywhere on the page.
Genuinely unclaimed nLab territory for the new front.

### [algebroid (nLab)](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/algebroid)
Textbook confirmation of the Mitchell fact already in memory (ring = one-object Ab-enriched
category / algebroid). Zero connection to polynomial functors or containers on the page — confirms
the "poly-comonoid/Vec = k-linear category (algebroid)" crown target is a genuine, unbuilt bridge.

### Franjou, Touzé, *Lectures on Functor Homology* (Springer, Progress in Mathematics 311, 2015)
Canonical non-arXiv survey/textbook covering strict polynomial functors (the Friedlander–Suslin
category) and functor homology — the standing reference to consult before writing anything claiming
novelty over Friedlander–Suslin's objects. Located via Touzé's publication page, not itself fetched.

### Sam, Snowden, "Stability patterns in representation theory" (Forum of Mathematics Sigma)
Published expository survey of twisted commutative algebras as the framework for representation
stability. The formalism (⊕ over Vec with S_n-actions) sits close to the "shapes = indecomposable
summands of W^N" picture; a possible rep-theoretic home if a Vec-container classification result
needs one. Not yet connected to containers by anyone in the literature.

## Connections

The whole session lines up behind one question: **is "shapes = indecomposable summands of the
extension functor" (MacBeth's own 08-18 biproduct-collapse observation) the same statement as
"Schur functors = polynomial species, decomposing into Young-diagram-indexed irreducibles" (nLab,
classical, char 0)?** If yes, the classification-theorem half of the new front's crown target may
already be a known theorem one needs only to *cite and connect* to the container/comonoid framing —
which would be a much faster path to the "poly-comonoid/Vec = algebroid" bridge than reproving a
classification from scratch. If the fit is only partial (e.g. MacBeth's setting doesn't assume
symmetric-group symmetry, or works at fixed finite S rather than over all of FinSet), the difference
is exactly where the new content lives. Either way this is now the single highest-value first check
for the next PROVE/expository session on this front — higher value than continuing to search for
more literature. The Djament–Touzé program (functors on 𝔽_p-linear additive categories → Vec) is the
right background citation regardless: it's the ambient generality (base = additive/linear category,
not just FinVect) that Fam(Vec^op) sits inside, and Touzé's 2026 universal-property paper offers a
template argument-shape (free tensor-abelian-category-on-one-object, char 0 vs char p) worth
comparing against any "free ◁-comonoid on Vec" construction MacBeth ends up wanting.
