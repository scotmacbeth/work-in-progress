### [The Only Distributive Law Over the Powerset Monad Is the One You Know](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13144) — Goncharov, Hofmann, Nora, Schröder, Wild
Genuinely new (abstract only). Classical non-container result: an accessible Set functor admits a
distributive law over `Pf` iff it preserves weak pullbacks, and the law is then essentially
unique (Barr extension); `Pf` has exactly 3 distributive laws over itself. Useful classical
citation for *why* `Pf` is the natural test case for the entwining reverse-orientation failure —
not a scoop (monad-monad, not monad-comonad).

## Community

### [Distributive Laws](https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2017/02/distributive_laws.html) — n-Category Café, Liang Ze Wong (2017)
Comments explicitly discuss mixed distributive laws between a monad and a comonad (bimonads, Hopf
monads), citing Bruguières–Virelizier and Mesablishvili–Wisbauer — a citation trail for the
"mixed distributive law"/"entwining" terminology if not already in the entwining write-up's
bibliography. One framing line worth keeping: "(End(Set),∘,Id) is not braided — which is why we
need distributive laws" — a clean gloss on why the entwining exists in only one orientation (no
symmetry argument could force the reverse).

### [Formal Theory of Monads (Following Street)](https://golem.ph.utexas.edu/category/2014/01/formal_theory_of_monads_follow.html) — n-Category Café, Eduard Balzin (2014)
Origin of the "wreath" terminology: `EM(EM(K))` = wreaths in Street's 2-categorical monad theory
— the classical ancestor Capucci-Myers's "Contextads as Wreaths" categorifies. Also covers the
monad-in-`K^co` / comonad-in-`K` duality, adjacent to the transfer machinery (`T_M`/`G_M`).
