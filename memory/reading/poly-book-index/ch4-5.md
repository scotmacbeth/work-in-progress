## AT A GLANCE — the 10 most citation-dangerous results in Ch. 4–5

1. **Eq. (4.75), p. 125** — `[q,r] := Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◦ (q[j]·y)`. The **⊗/Dirichlet internal hom**, in closed form. A container theorist rediscovers this as "the internal hom for the Dirichlet tensor of containers: shapes = Π over shapes of q of r-substituted-at-q[j]".
2. **Exercise 4.78 / Eq. (4.79), p. 126** — `[q,r] ≅ Σ_{f : q→r} y^{Σ_{j∈q(1)} r[f₁ j]}`. Rediscovered as "the internal-hom container has *container morphisms* as its shapes and the total space of pulled-back r-positions as its positions". THIS is the exercise that scooped MacBeth.
3. **Eq. (5.28) + Theorem 5.31, pp. 152–153** — `r^q := Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◦ (y + q[j])`, and `Poly(p, r^q) ≅ Poly(p×q, r)`: **Poly is cartesian closed**. Rediscovered as "Cont is cartesian closed, exponential `Π_s F∘(−+P s)`" (= Altenkirch–Levy–Staton).
4. **Eq. (4.82), p. 126** — `[p, y] ≅ Γ(p) · y^{p(1)}`. Rediscovered as "the dual/section container `p^⊥`: shapes are the sections, positions are the shapes of p". Companion: **Exercise 4.84** — `[[p,y],y] ≅ p` **iff** p is linear (`Ay`) or representable (`y^A`) — the reflexivity/self-duality classification.
5. **Proposition 4.54 / Eq. (4.55), p. 113** — `Γ(p ⊗ q) ≅ Set(q(1), Γ(p)) × Set(p(1), Γ(q))`. Rediscovered as "an interaction pattern between two containers splits into two one-sided, position-parametrised sections" — dangerously close to a Zappa–Szép-flavoured splitting statement.
6. **Proposition 5.49 + Corollary 5.50, p. 158** — `p⊗q` is the **pushout** of `p ⊗ q(1)y ← p(1)y ⊗ q(1)y → p(1)y ⊗ q` (and the n-ary **wide pushout**). "To give `φ : p⊗q → r` it suffices to give `φ_p` and `φ_q` agreeing on positions." Rediscovered as "a lens out of a tensor is exactly a *pairwise* family of one-sided information flows" — MacBeth's pairwise/ZS instinct lands right here.
7. **Corollary 5.15 / Eq. (5.16), p. 148 (principal monomial)** — `Poly(p, I y^A) ≅ Set(p(1), I) × Set(A, Γ(p))`; adjunction `Poly ⇄ Set × Set^op`, `p ↦ (p(1), Γ(p))`. Rediscovered as "the shape/section adjunction; every container maps universally into its principal monomial `p(1)·y^{Γ(p)}`".
8. **Proposition 5.52 + Proposition 5.59, pp. 158–161** — **vertical/cartesian factorization system**, and: a lens is cartesian ⟺ every on-directions map is a bijection ⟺ it is a **cartesian natural transformation** (all naturality squares pullbacks). Rediscovered as "cartesian container morphisms = shape map + position iso; every morphism factors as vertical then cartesian".
9. **Theorem 5.68 + Eq. (5.69), pp. 163–164** — **cartesian lenses in Poly are exponentiable** (`f* : Poly/q → Poly/p` has a right adjoint `f_*`, with an explicit wide-pushout formula). Rediscovered as "dependent products of containers along cartesian maps exist" (a Π-type / distributive-law construction).
10. **Theorem 5.73 + Eq. (5.75), p. 165** — for `f : A → B`, `f_! ⊣ f* ⊣ f_*` on `A.Poly ⇄ B.Poly`, with `f_! p ≅ Σ_{b∈B} y^{Π_{a↦b} p[a]}` and `f_* p ≅ Σ_{b∈B} y^{Σ_{a↦b} p[a]}`; Poly is a **monoidal ∗-bifibration** over Set (Shulman). Rediscovered as "reindexing containers along a shape-map has both adjoints, and the left one uses Π while the right one uses Σ".

## THE UNIFORM-CLOSURE QUESTION (answer up front)

- **The uniform formula is TRUE and both instances are in the book.**
  - `[q,r]_⊗ = Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◦ (q[j] · y)` — Eq. **(4.75)**, p. 125 — i.e. `Π_j r ◁ (y × q[j])`.
  - `r^q = Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◦ (y + q[j])` — Eq. **(5.28)**, p. 152 — i.e. `Π_j r ◁ (y + q[j])`.
  - Side by side these are visibly `[q,r]_⋆ = Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◁ (y ⋆ q[j])` with `⋆ = ×` and `⋆ = +`. Even the two *proofs* (Prop. 4.85, p. 127; Thm. 5.31, p. 153) are the same four steps — coproduct UP, Yoneda, Yoneda, (co)product UP.
- **The book states them as SEPARATE results, in separate chapters, and never unifies them.** §5.3 opens (p. 152): *"We have already seen in Section 4.5 the closure operation [−,−] for one monoidal structure on Poly, namely (y, ⊗). But this is not the only closed monoidal structure on Poly: in fact, we will show that Poly is cartesian closed as well."* That is the closest the book comes: it juxtaposes them and stops. No common formula, no cross-reference from (5.28) back to (4.75), no `⋆`.
- **The book DOES have the general Day-convolution family** — but only as a *monoidal* statement, with **no closure claim**: **Proposition 3.79, p. 70** (Ch. 3, outside this range): *"For any monoidal structure (I, ★) on Set, there is a corresponding monoidal structure (y^I, ⊙) on Poly, where ⊙ is the Day convolution. Moreover, ⊙ distributes over coproducts. In the case of (0,+) and (1,×), this procedure returns the (1,×) and (y,⊗) monoidal structures respectively."* with **Eq. (3.81)**: `p ⊙ q ≅ Σ_{(i,j)∈p(1)×q(1)} y^{p[i] ★ q[j]}`. The book explicitly says of Day convolution: *"we will not use them elsewhere in the book."*
- **Condition for existence:** the book imposes **none** — it never asks when `⊙` is closed. It gives no hypothesis like "`R ⋆ (−)` is polynomial / preserves wide pullbacks". (MacBeth's proposed condition is exactly what's needed to make `r ◁ (y ⋆ q[j])` land back in Poly, and it is *not* in the book. Note both live instances satisfy it trivially: `A + (−)` and `A × (−)` are polynomial.) Chapter 9 "New horizons" (p. 349) lists 14 open questions — **none** of them is "is ⊙ closed for general ★".
- **Verdict:** the uniform closure theorem `[q,r]_⋆ = Π_{j∈q(1)} r ◁ (y ⋆ q[j])`, together with a condition on `⋆` for it to exist, is **not in this book**. What IS in the book, and must be cited, is (i) Prop. 3.79 + Eq. (3.81) as the Day-convolution family of monoidal structures, and (ii) Eq. (4.75)/Prop. 4.85 and Eq. (5.28)/Thm. 5.31 as the two instances. Any write-up must be framed as *unifying two known results under a hypothesis the book never states* — not as discovering either instance.
- **Also relevant, outside range:** the `◁` (composition) product is **not** closed but has a **left coclosure** — Proposition 6.57, pp. 204–207 ("closed monoidal structure, 125, 152" and "coclosure, 204–207" are the only index entries).

### 5.73 Theorem — [p. 165]  ★★
**Statement:** For any `f : A → B`, `f* : B.Poly → A.Poly` has **both adjoints** `f_! ⊣ f* ⊣ f_*` (Eq. 5.74), with (Eq. 5.75) `f_! p ≅ Σ_{b∈B} y^{Π_{a ↦ b} p[a]}` and `f_* p ≅ Σ_{b∈B} y^{Σ_{a ↦ b} p[a]}` — **the LEFT adjoint uses Π and the RIGHT adjoint uses Σ** (because `Poly ≃ ∫ (A ↦ (Set/A)^op)`). ### 3.79 Proposition — Day convolution   [p. 70]  ★★★
**Statement:** "For any monoidal structure `(I, ★)` on Set, there is a corresponding monoidal structure `(y^I, ⊙)` on Poly, where `⊙` is the Day convolution. Moreover, `⊙` distributes over coproducts. In the case of `(0,+)` and `(1,×)`, this procedure returns the `(1,×)` and `(y,⊗)` monoidal structures respectively." **Eq. (3.81): `p ⊙ q ≅ Σ_{(i,j) ∈ p(1)×q(1)} y^{p[i] ★ q[j]}`.** The book adds: "we will not use them elsewhere in the book." **No closure claim is made for general `⊙`.**
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Proposition 3.79 / Eq. (3.81), pp. 70–71
**Keywords:** Day convolution, general monoidal structure on Poly, ⊙, (I,★), coend, uniform family of tensors
