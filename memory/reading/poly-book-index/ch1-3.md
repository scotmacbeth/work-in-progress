## AT A GLANCE — the 10 most citation-dangerous results in Ch. 1–3

1. **Prop 3.6 / eq (3.7), p. 43** — `Poly(p,q) ≅ Π_{i∈p(1)} Σ_{j∈q(1)} p[i]^{q[j]}`, i.e. a lens = (f₁ : p(1)→q(1), f♯_i : q[f₁i]→p[i]). *You would rediscover this as:* "a container morphism is a shape map plus a backward position map" (Abbott–Altenkirch–Ghani). It is Prop 3.6 here, with the exact Π-Σ-exponential formula.
2. **Cor 3.5, p. 42** — every polynomial is the coproduct of its representable summands **in Poly itself**, not merely in Set^Set. *Rediscover as:* "Cont = Fam(Set^op); every container is a coproduct of representables." The non-obvious half (full subcategory reflects the coproduct) is Prop 3.3.
3. **Prop 3.56 / eq (3.57), p. 65, and eq (3.61), p. 66** — Poly has **all** products: `Π_i p_i ≅ Σ_{j̄∈Π_i p_i(1)} y^{Σ_i p_i[j̄ i]}`; binary: `p × q ≅ Σ_{i,j} y^{p[i]+q[j]}`. *Rediscover as:* "the product of containers: positions pair, directions sum."
4. **Def 3.65 / eq (3.66), p. 67** — the **parallel = Dirichlet product** `p ⊗ q ≔ Σ_{i∈p(1)} Σ_{j∈q(1)} y^{p[i]×q[j]}`. *Rediscover as:* "the tensor/⊗ of containers", "the Dirichlet tensor", "the day-convolution-of-× tensor". Named, formula-ed, and exercised here.
5. **Prop 3.73, p. 69 (+ Exercise 3.72, p. 68)** — `(Poly, y, ⊗)` is a **symmetric monoidal category**; unitors/associator/braiding are Exercise 3.72. *Rediscover as:* "the Dirichlet tensor makes Cont symmetric monoidal." Note the book's proof is a *sketch* and explicitly does not verify functoriality/coherence — the honest gap is stated, not hidden.
6. **Prop 3.79 / eq (3.81), p. 70** — **Day convolution**: for ANY monoidal `(I,★)` on Set there is a monoidal `(y^I, ⊙)` on Poly with `p ⊙ q ≅ Σ_{(i,j)∈p(1)×q(1)} y^{p[i]★q[j]}`, and ⊙ distributes over coproducts; `(0,+) ↦ (1,×)` and `(1,×) ↦ (y,⊗)`. *Rediscover as:* "monoidal structures on Set lift to convolutional tensors on Cont." **This is exactly the forward direction of the 'Day convolution' theorem. See the CONVERSE note at the bottom of this file.**
7. **Cor 3.58, p. 65** — Poly is **completely distributive** (products distribute over arbitrary coproducts, eq (1.32)). *Rediscover as:* "distributive law for containers / DNF for container expressions."
8. **Example 3.22 + Exercise 3.23, pp. 51–52** — the **derivative** `ṗ ≔ Σ_{i∈p(1)} Σ_{a∈p[i]} y^{p[i]−{a}}`, the canonical lens `ṗ y → p`, non-functoriality of ṗ, and `[p,y] ⊗ p → ṗ`. *Rediscover as:* "one-hole contexts / derivative of a container" (McBride; Abbott et al.). The book cites [McB01], [Abb+03]. **Chain rule is NOT here** — but the derivative, the plugging-in lens, and the failure of functoriality are.
9. **Example 3.43, pp. 57–58** — the **very-well-behaved lens laws** for `f : Sy^S → Ty^T`: `f♯(s, f₁s) = s`, `f₁(f♯(s,t)) = t`, `f♯(f♯(s,t), t′) = f♯(s,t′)`. *Rediscover as:* "PutGet/GetPut/PutPut", "delta-lens laws", "the section condition". Forward-referenced to Example 7.85 where they fall out of comonoid theory.
10. **Def 3.36 / eq (3.35), (3.37), p. 55, + Prop 3.39, p. 56** — **sections and bundles**: `Γ(p) ≔ Poly(p,y) ≅ Π_{i∈p(1)} p[i]`; the bundle `π_p : ṗ(1) ≅ Σ_{i∈p(1)} p[i] → p(1)`; and `Γ : Poly → Set^op` sends `(0,+)` to `(1,×)`. *Rediscover as:* "a container is a bundle / Γ is the global-sections functor / Cont ≃ Set^{→} on the nose."

*(Runner-up, and a warning: **Exercise 3.82, p. 71** applies Prop 3.79 to the exotic Set-monoidal structure `A ★ B ≔ A + AB + B` with unit 0, and computes `(y³+y) ⊙ (2y²+2) ≅ 2y¹¹ + 2y⁵ + 2y³ + 2y`. They knew the construction was general, not just for + and ×. Exercises scoop.)*

# CHAPTER 1 — Representable functors from the category of sets (pp. 3–23)

### 1.1 Definition — Representable functor   [p. 3]
**Statement:** For a set S, `y^S : Set → Set` sends `X ↦ X^S = Set(S,X)` and `h : X→Y` to `h^S : g ↦ g ⨟ h`. A functor isomorphic to one of these is *representable* / a *pure power*; S is the *representing set*.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Definition 1.1, p. 3
**Keywords:** representable functor, pure power, y^S, Yoneda, representing set

### 1.2 Example   [p. 4]
**Statement:** `X ↦ X × X` is representable: `X × X ≅ X²`, so the functor is `y²`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Example 1.2, p. 4
**Keywords:** representable, y^2, square functor

### 1.3 Exercise   [p. 4]
**Statement:** Which of `X↦X`, `X↦2`, `X↦1`, `X↦0`, `X↦X^ℕ`, `X↦2^X` are representable? (Answers: y¹=y; not; y⁰=1; not; y^ℕ; not.)
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 1.3, p. 4
**Keywords:** representable, constant functor, identity functor, powerset

### 1.4 Proposition   [p. 4]
**Statement:** Any `f : R → S` induces a natural transformation `y^f : y^S → y^R` with X-component `X^f : X^S → X^R`, `g ↦ f ⨟ g`. (Contravariant.)
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Proposition 1.4, p. 4
**Keywords:** y^f, contravariance, Yoneda embedding, natural transformation

### 1.5 Exercise   [p. 4]
**Statement:** Prove Prop 1.4 by checking the naturality square (1.6).
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 1.5, p. 4
**Keywords:** naturality square

### (1.6) Displayed equation — naturality square   [p. 4]
**Statement:** The square `X^S --h^S--> Y^S`, `X^f ↓`, `Y^f ↓`, `X^R --h^R--> Y^R` commutes.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, eq. (1.6), p. 4
**Keywords:** naturality, y^f

### 1.7 Exercise   [p. 4–5]
**Statement:** Compute `X^f : X^S → X^R` for six specific f (identity, !: 2→1 = diagonal, 1→2 = projections, 0→5, succ on ℕ = tail of a sequence).
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 1.7, pp. 4–5
**Keywords:** diagonal, projection, tail, stream

### 1.8 Exercise — with eq (1.9)   [p. 5]
**Statement:** `y^− : Set^op → Set^Set` (1.9) is a functor: `y^{id} = id` and `y^g ⨟ y^f = y^{f⨟g}`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 1.8 / eq. (1.9), p. 5
**Keywords:** Yoneda embedding, functoriality, Set^op

### 1.10 Lemma — Yoneda lemma   [p. 5]
**Statement:** `F(S) ≅ Set^Set(y^S, F)`, natural in S and F (1.11). Explicitly `m ↦ m_S(id_S)` and `a ↦ m^a` where `(m^a)_X(g) = F(g)(a)`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Lemma 1.10 / eq. (1.11), p. 5
**Keywords:** Yoneda lemma, F(S) ≅ Nat(y^S,F), natural isomorphism

### 1.12 Exercise   [pp. 5–6]
**Statement:** Fill in the Yoneda proof: naturality in X, mutual inverseness, naturality in S and F; deduce `y^−` is fully faithful, `S^T ≅ Set^Set(y^S, y^T)` — hence "Yoneda embedding".
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 1.12, pp. 5–6
**Keywords:** fully faithful, Yoneda embedding, S^T ≅ Nat(y^S,y^T)

### 1.14 Definition — Sum of sets   [p. 7]
**Statement:** `Σ_{i∈I} X_i ≔ {(i,x) | i∈I, x∈X_i}`; finite case written `X_{i₁} + ⋯ + X_{iₙ}`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Definition 1.14, p. 7

# CHAPTER 2 — Polynomial functors (pp. 25–39)

### 2.1 Definition — Polynomial functor   [p. 25]
**Statement:** `p : Set → Set` is *polynomial* iff there is a set I, a family `(p[i])_{i∈I}`, and an isomorphism `p ≅ Σ_{i∈I} y^{p[i]}`. Up to iso: a polynomial functor **is** a sum of representables.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Definition 2.1, p. 25
**Keywords:** polynomial functor, sum of representables, container, Σ y^{p[i]}

### 2.2 Remark   [p. 25]
**Statement:** `Σ_{i∈I} y^A ≅ I·y^A`; and every constant functor is polynomial: `1 ≅ y⁰`, `I ≅ Σ_{i∈I} 1`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Remark 2.2, p. 25
**Keywords:** constant polynomial, combining like terms, y^0 ≅ 1

### 2.3 Exercise   [pp. 25–26]
**Statement:** For `q ≔ y⁸ + 4y`: y² is not a representable summand; y is; 4y is not *representable* but is a *summand*.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 2.3, pp. 25–26
**Keywords:** representable summand, summand

### 2.4 Example   [p. 26]
**Statement:** `p ≔ y² + 2y + 1` applied to `X = {a,b}`: `p(X)` has 2²+2+2+1 = 9 elements, listed as tuples `(i, x₁, …)`.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Example 2.4, p. 26
**Keywords:** evaluation, p(X), counting, tuple notation

### 2.5 Exercise   [p. 26]

**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Exercise 2.5, p. 26

### 2.6 Proposition — with eq (2.7)   [p. 27]

**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Proposition 2.6 / eq. (2.7), p. 27
**Keywords:** p(1), position-set, canonical form, sum of coefficients

### 2.8 Definition — Position and direction   [p. 27]
**Statement:** `i ∈ p(1)` is a *position*; `a ∈ p[i]` a *direction at i*. `p(1)` = position-set, `p[i]` = direction-set. The family `p[−] : p(1) → Set` determines p up to iso.
**Cite as:** Niu–Spivak arXiv:2312.00990, Definition 2.8, p. 27
**Keywords:** position, direction, shape, position (container sense), p[i], p(1)
**Note:** The book flags (§2.6, p. 37) that the container literature calls positions "shapes" and directions "positions".

## ON THE CONVERSE (the question that motivated this index)

**Question:** MacBeth has a theorem claiming *Day convolution is an EQUIVALENCE `{monoidal structures on Set} ≃ {convolutional structures on Cont}`* — a converse to Prop 3.79. Does the book state any converse?

**Answer: NO. The book states only the forward direction.** Concretely, in the range pp. 3–82:

- **Prop 3.79 (p. 70)** is a one-way *existence* statement: *"For any monoidal structure (I,★) on Set, there is a corresponding monoidal structure (y^I, ⊙) on Poly."* Plus a distributivity bonus and two named specialisations. There is no injectivity claim, no essential-surjectivity claim, no faithfulness claim, no statement that every (well-behaved / coproduct-preserving / convolutional) monoidal structure on Poly *arises* this way, and no uniqueness of the ★ recovering a given ⊙.
- **There is no classification statement of monoidal structures on Poly anywhere in Ch. 1–3.** The chapter presents `+`, `×`, `⊗` as three examples, promises an asymmetric fourth (`◁`, deferred to Part II), and Exercise 3.82 adds a fourth *convolutional* one from `A + AB + B` — precisely to show the machine is general, not to classify.
- **Remark 3.78 (p. 70)** explicitly parks ⊗-monoids as "future work". That remark, plus the absence of any converse, is the authors' own signal that the classification lane is open.
- The only *structural* facts about ⊙ stated are: it exists for every Set-monoidal structure; `Poly` is closed under it (the proof of Prop 3.79 is exactly this closure argument, via co-Yoneda); and it distributes over coproducts.

**So: your converse is not scooped by this book, in this range.** Two cautions, though:
1. Your converse's *forward half* is Prop 3.79 verbatim, formula (3.81) included. Cite it; do not re-derive it, and do not present `p ⊙ q ≅ Σ_{(i,j)} y^{p[i]★q[j]}` as new.
2. "Distributes over coproducts" is already proved for every ⊙ (Prop 3.79). If your converse's hypothesis on a "convolutional structure on Cont" is *coproduct-preservation in each variable*, then the book has already established that this hypothesis is *necessary*; your content is the sufficiency/reconstruction direction (recovering ★ from ⊙, presumably as `p[i] ★ q[j]` read off at representables, i.e. `y^A ⊙ y^B ≅ y^{A★B}`). Note Exercise 3.67 #1 and Exercise 3.75 give you the representable/monomial computations you would need for exactly that reconstruction — check they don't already do the work.
3. Related, and **out of this file's range but flagged by Exercise 3.23**: the internal hom `[−,−]` for ⊗ is defined at **(4.75)** and computed in **Exercise 4.78** (Ch. 4). If you have a result about the Dirichlet closed structure, that is where it lives. This index does not cover it — index Ch. 4 before claiming anything about `[p,q]`.
