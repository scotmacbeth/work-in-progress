### [Monads and Distributive Laws in Substructural Contexts (Extended Version)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13533) — Soichiro Fujii, Yun Chen Tsai, Yoàv Montacute, Ichiro Hasuo, submitted May 13 2026
Builds a general theory of distributive laws ST→TS between monads on Set using Tronin's verbal categories to encode structural rules (contraction/weakening/exchange etc.), introducing "W-operadic" and "W-commutative" monad classes and a canonical construction producing a distributive law whenever S is W-operadic and T is W-commutative. This directly targets the same object as MacBeth's Zappa-Szép/H² program (distributive laws of Set-monads) but from a totally different angle — presentation/operad-theoretic rather than fibrational/pullback. Worth reading properly (only abstract fetched so far) to check whether their "canonical construction" recipe overlaps with, generalizes, or is orthogonal to the ZS-product/H² obstruction story. Flag as a **near-neighbour requiring a closer read**, not yet cleared.

### [The Only Distributive Law Over the Powerset Monad Is the One You Know](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13144) — Sergey Goncharov, Dirk Hofmann, Pedro Nora, Lutz Schröder, Paul Wild, submitted Feb 13 2026
Proves an accessible Set functor admits a distributive law over the powerset monad iff it preserves weak pullbacks, and in that case the "power law" (Barr extension) is the unique one; also shows the powerset functor itself has exactly three distributive laws over itself, so uniqueness fails for non-accessible functors. Only abstract read. Not about Zappa-Szép/semidirect products or polynomial/container functors specifically, but the weak-pullback-preservation ⟺ distributive-law-exists result is adjacent to MacBeth's cartesian-monad/pullback-preservation hunting ground (item 1) — worth a mention as a sibling result even though the object (powerset monad specifically) differs. Orthogonal-to-neighbour, low priority.
