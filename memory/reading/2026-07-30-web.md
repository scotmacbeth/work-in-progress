### 2501.17250 — "Weihrauch Problems as Containers" (Pradic & Price, 2025) — UPGRADED to deep-read

Read via the TYPES 2025 conference abstract (https://msp.cis.strath.ac.uk/types2025/abstracts/TYPES2025_paper3.pdf,
pdftotext'd in full — this is a self-contained 4-page technical digest of the preprint, not just an
abstract-only snippet) plus the arXiv abstract page. Also in Springer LNCS
(https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-95908-0_28).

**Core idea.** A container in a category C is formalised as an object of C/I (a bundle P: X → U);
morphisms are the usual (φ,ψ) pullback-square container morphisms. Weihrauch problems are exactly
such bundles: U = domain of the problem, and the fibre over u is the set of valid answers. A Weihrauch
reduction P ≤_W Q — "compute P given one oracle call to Q" — literally unpacks into the same
(forward-map, backward-map) shape as a container morphism (they draw the identical composite-square
diagram used in both settings). **Main theorem (their framing, Table 1 row 1):** Weihrauch degrees are
isomorphic to the posetal reflection of the full subcategory of **answerable containers** over
pMod(K2rec, K2), the category of partitioned represented spaces and type-2-computable maps.
"Answerable" is defined generally, for any category C with pullbacks: P: X→U is answerable iff it is a
**pullback-stable epimorphism** — i.e. a (possibly noncomputable) section of P exists, and this
persists under pullback. This is a genuinely new technical notion for MacBeth's vocabulary — a
containers-with-a-properness-condition subcategory, not directed containers, not plain containers with
no constraint.

**Category used:** containers over the category of **projective represented spaces** (equivalently
pMod(K2rec,K2), partitioned represented spaces + type-2-computable maps) — NOT Set-based containers,
NOT directed containers per se. This is "containers over C" for a specific computability-theoretic base
category C, i.e. an instance of the general C/I container-fibration picture MacBeth already has
(matches `contravariance-is-fibrewise-op.md` framing almost exactly, as previously logged for
2601.15420).

**Second correspondence:** Bauer's *extended* Weihrauch degrees ≅ posetal reflection of (unrestricted,
not just answerable) containers over **partitioned assemblies** pAsm(K2rec,K2).

**Third, "most recent ongoing work":** strong Weihrauch reducibility ≅ **dependent adaptors** over
pMod(K2rec,K2) — a notion they explicitly say they adopt from an unpublished Jules Hedges seminar talk
("We solved dependent optics!", MSP Strathclyde, 2025 — no other citable source exists yet). This is
worth flagging: "dependent adaptors" sound close to the optics/lens family that MacBeth's own
`dcont-morphisms-are-cofunctors.md` result (DCont ≅ Cof) lives in. Whether strong-Weihrauch's
dependent adaptors are literally cofunctors/directed-container morphisms in disguise is an open
question worth a dedicated look — potential genuine new connection, not yet checked.

**The operator dictionary (their Table 1, transcribed in full — this is the real payoff of the
paper for MacBeth):**

| Container concept | Weihrauch concept | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Answerable containers over pMod(K2rec,K2) | Weihrauch problems | proved |
| Containers over pAsm(K2rec,K2) | Extended Weihrauch problems | proved |
| Dependent adaptors over pMod(K2rec,K2) | Strong Weihrauch problems | proved |
| Product p×q | Meet p⊓q | proved |
| Coproduct p+q | Join p⊔q | proved |
| Tensor product p⊗q | Parallel product p×q (Weihrauch's, different ×) | proved |
| **Composition product p◁q** | **Composition product p⋆q** | proved but only a *quasi-bifunctor* |
| Free monad on p | Iterated composition p⋄ | open / conjectural |
| Closed structures ⊸, ⇒ | ? | open |
| **Derivative ∂p** | **?** | **open** |
| ? | First-order part ₁p | open |
| ? | Deterministic part det(p) | open |

Two rows are directly interesting for MacBeth:

- They use **MacBeth's own notation ◁** for the container composition product, and identify it with
  Weihrauch's composition operator ⋆ ("ask Q, then depending on the answer ask P"). The caveat is
  important and connects to a real subtlety in MacBeth's own ◁-monoidal work: composition of
  containers over pMod(K2rec,K2) is only a *quasi*-bifunctor, "because Weihrauch problems correspond
  to containers over a category which is only **weakly (locally) cartesian closed**." MacBeth's own
  ◁ pentagon/associativity/monoidal-coherence proofs (`monoidal-coherence-four-structures.md`,
  `lean-monoidal-coherence-done.md`) are done over Set/Cont, which IS locally cartesian closed — this
  is a live example of what breaks when you move the base category to something weaker, i.e. a
  concrete illustration of exactly where the LCC hypothesis is load-bearing in the ◁ theory.
- The **"Derivative ∂p ↔ ?" row is explicitly left open** — they have not identified what
  Weihrauch-degree notion (if any) corresponds to the container derivative. This lands squarely on
  MacBeth's own turf (`container-chain-rule-proved.md`: ∂(G◁F) ≅ (∂G◁F)×∂F). Nobody in this cluster
  has pursued it. Genuinely open, not scooped, and a plausible "applications" angle for the grant
  (derivative of a Weihrauch problem = ?) — flagged as a candidate for a future browse-then-prove
  session, not attempted here.

**Related work section (useful citation map):** they distinguish themselves from Ahman-Bauer
2409.17664 (who link extended Weihrauch reducibility to containers too, but via the coarser "instance
reducibility" over families of truth values, not bundles of partitioned assemblies directly) and from
Trotta-Valenti-de Paiva (Dialectica-interpretation route, posetal only) and Uftring (syntactic
Dialectica route). They note: "the category of containers over C can be recovered by completing the
terminal fibration over C by arbitrary products and then sums and taking the fiber over 1" — a clean
one-line construction of Cont(C) worth having in MacBeth's toolkit phrasing.

### 2601.15420 — "Problems with Fixpoints of Polynomials of Polynomials" (Pradic & Price, LICS 2026)

Already deep-read 07-15/07-24 (chain-rule check, negative). This session added the official Dagstuhl
LIPIcs abstract (https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.LICS.2026.77), confirming
and sharpening the existing memory note rather than changing it: studies fibred endofunctors over the
fibrewise-opposite-of-codomain-fibration ("themselves representable by families of polynomial
endofunctors") and three fixpoint varieties (initial algebra, terminal coalgebra, and a third "ζ"

and parallel products," interpreted in type-2-computable maps to capture concrete Weihrauch degrees

operator**. This "answerable part" operator is the same "answerable" notion from 2501.17250 (pullback-
stable epimorphism / has-a-section), now used as a fixpoint-extraction device rather than a
classification device — the two papers share the technical backbone. No new derivative/chain-rule
content surfaced; existing "ZERO chain-rule overlap" verdict stands.

## 2. Hughes, Vollmer, Orchard — "Deriving Distributive Laws for Graded Linear Types"

**IMPORTANT CORRECTION to `sources.json`:** this paper is **already logged** at entry `"2112.14966"`,
but the `authors` field currently reads "Gaboardi, Katsumata, Orchard, et al. (follow-up to Combining
Effects and Coeffects via Grading, ICFP 2016)" — **this is wrong**. The actual authors, confirmed from
the paper's own title page (pdftotext'd in full, arxiv.org/pdf/2112.14966), are **Jack Hughes,
Michael Vollmer, Dominic Orchard**, School of Computing, University of Kent. Published EPTCS 353
(2021), pp. 109–131, at LINEARITY & TLLA 2020 (eds. Dal Lago, de Paiva) — matching the lead flagged in
the prior browse session exactly. Gaboardi/Katsumata are cited BY this paper (as related work, "Graded
Distributive Laws" section) but are not its authors. **Recommend fixing the authors field in
sources.json.**

Extraction level upgraded from `agent-summary` (title/abstract snippet) to **deep-read** (full text
pdftotext'd, 1152 lines, both the technical core and the related-work section read directly).

**Core technical content.** Granule/Linear-Haskell-style graded necessity modality □_r A (r drawn from
a semiring, generalising Bounded Linear Logic's !_r A). The paper gives an **automatic derivation
procedure** (generic-programming-style, structural recursion on the type F) for two combinators:
- `push : □_r (Fα) → F(□_r α)` — distribute the graded modality inward over a data type F
- `pull : F(□_r α) → □_r (Fα)` — distribute it outward

implemented via Template Haskell metaprogramming in both Granule and Linear Haskell. They prove:
push/pull are mutually inverse (Props 3.1–3.2, for first-order F without embedded function types),
□_r is verified as an endofunctor, and push/pull are proved **natural transformations** (Props 3.3–3.4)
— i.e. genuine distributive-law-shaped structure, checked properly, not just ad hoc combinators.

**Crucial scope limitation, directly relevant to MacBeth's effect-coeffect arrow question:** this is a
distributive law between **one fixed graded comonad** (the semiring-graded necessity modality □_r) and
an **arbitrary covariant functor F** built from a data type — NOT a distributive law between an
arbitrary graded monad and an arbitrary graded comonad, and NOT an arrow/profunctor/bialgebra
construction. Grepped full text for "arrow categor*", "Freyd", "profunctor", "bialgebra*", "Plotkin",
"Turi": **zero hits**, confirming and reinforcing the prior negative finding
(`effect-coeffect-arrows-*` files) — this remains outside the arrow-unification territory. Two
passages worth quoting for the citation trail:

- Related-work distinguishes their "prosaic" single-comonad-vs-functor laws from **Gaboardi et al.'s**
  genuinely general graded-monad/graded-comonad distributive laws (θ: ε(r,f)·♦_f A → ♦_κ(r,f)·r A,
  interacting the grades on both sides) — explicitly flagged by HVO themselves as future work they
  have NOT done ("we cannot derive push: □_r □_s A → □_s □_r A [between two modalities]... Exploring
  this is future work"). So even within this paper's own stated scope, the harder graded-monad↔graded-
  comonad case is open, deferred to Gaboardi et al.'s framework — which MacBeth has already surveyed.
- They also cite **Power & Watanabe, "Combining a monad and a comonad", TCS 2002** [25] as the classical
  reference for what a distributive law between a monad and a comonad must preserve — worth having in
  MacBeth's citation list for the general (non-graded) monad/comonad distributive-law literature if not
  already there.

**Verdict:** confirms rather than overturns the standing 07-29 finding that a genuine arrow-category /
Freyd-category unification of effects and coeffects is unassembled in the literature; this is the
second independently-surfaced 2020/2021-era paper (after the Liepelt-Marshall-Orchard-Rajani-Vollmer
2025 IFC paper) landing in the same single-comonad-vs-functor corner rather than the general
bialgebra/arrow corner. No action needed on `three-modes-of-composition-dream.md` — this doesn't move
the needle on the branching/non-branching arrow-existence question, it's a narrower practical
combinator-derivation result.
