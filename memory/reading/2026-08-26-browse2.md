## Papers

### [Day, "Biclosed bicategories: localisation of convolution"](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.3485) — Brian J. Day, arXiv:0705.3485 (2007 self-archive of the 1970/1981 original)
**The actual text MacBeth has been chasing, finally obtained.** This is Day's own 1981
summary-without-proofs of his 1970 UNSW PhD thesis "Construction of Biclosed Categories,"
self-archived to arXiv in 2007. Read in full (native PDF, WebFetch could not parse the raw stream)
**independently by two agents** (arXiv + web), cross-confirming the same reading. Precise hypotheses,
finally pinned down:
- **Ground category condition** (governs everything): "All categorical algebra shall be relative to
  a fixed **complete and cocomplete symmetric monoidal closed** ground category V." This is strictly
  stronger than "C has products" — it demands closedness of the BASE as a precondition for
  constructing closedness of the convolution.
- **Extension Theorem (§4, the actual "closedness for free" result)**: given a probicategory A and a
  family of **dense** functors N_xy : A_xy^op → C_xy, the target C gets an extended biclosed
  bicategory structure PROVIDED specific indexed limits/colimits exist in C, AND two induced
  comparison natural transformations are isomorphisms. **This is not a free lunch** — density plus
  existence of specific (co)limits plus two comparison isos is the full hypothesis set, not "any
  cocomplete base works."
- **Verdict for T2**: this sharpens (and partially deflates) the hope that ⊗-closedness over
  Fam(C^op) falls out automatically from citing Day 1971. Vec_k itself IS complete, cocomplete, and
  symmetric monoidal closed (satisfies the ground-category condition) — so the open question is
  whether **Fam(Vec_fd^op)** (not Vec_fd itself) satisfies the Extension Theorem's density +
  indexed-(co)limit + comparison-iso conditions. This is now a concrete, checkable next `/prove`
  target rather than an appeal to an unread classical result.
- sources.json: new entry `0705.3485` (deep-read); `day-1971-biclosed-categories` note updated with
  S2 metadata (Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 1971, DOI 10.1017/S0004972700046979, 91 citations) and
  cross-linked to this entry.

### [De Pascalis, Uustalu, Veltrì, "Monoid Structures on Indexed Containers"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25879) — arXiv:2509.25879 (LSFA 2025)
Already tracked at deep-read from June/July/August sessions, but read again in full (fresh HTML) in
direct response to Neil's new pointer. An I-indexed container `(S : I → Set, P : Π{i} S i → I →
Set)` describes an endofunctor on `Set^I`. **Thm 3.5**: the full subcategory of `Set^I`-monads whose
underlying endofunctors are container extents is equivalent to the category of **Indexed Container
Monoid Structures** — a fully combinatorial description (unit shape, binary op on shapes, plus
index-tracking data `↑/↖/↗` and a coherence condition with **no analogue in the non-indexed theory**).
Worked examples: indexed state monad (mode-changing state), indexed writer monad (index changes via
monoid action), free-monad construction producing well-scoped λ-terms indexed by binder depth.
Explicitly more general than Gambino-Kock polynomial monads (not restricted to Cartesian natural
transformations). **This is exactly Neil's requested generality**: "states that let prompts vary" is
naturally `I`-indexed with `I` = the varying context/prompt-type index, and Thm 3.5 gives a
combinatorial recipe (not just an abstract equivalence) for the resulting monad structure — directly
extends MacBeth's own non-indexed monoid-in-`(Cont,⊗,y)`=monad-on-Set result
([[bare-dirichlet-comonoid-proved]]) to the dependent/indexed setting. Zero reverse citations yet
(too recent, LSFA proceedings only).
