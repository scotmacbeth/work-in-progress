### [On the second cohomology of semidirect products](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.0291) — Hartl, Leroy, arXiv:0707.0291 (2007)
**The headline find of the session** — independently surfaced by both the arXiv agent and the web
agent. For `G = N⋊T` and a G-module `M`, constructs the *simultaneous restriction map*
`res = (res^G_N, res^G_T)^t : H²(G,M) → H²(N,M) × H²(T,M)`, embedded in a five-term exact sequence
relating `H¹`/`H²` of the whole group to `H¹`/`H²` of the two factors. Explicitly extends Tahara
1972 (their ref `[Ta]`), which proved the special case: for trivial `G`-action, `H²(T,M)` is a
canonical *direct factor* of `H²(G,M)`. This is exactly the shape of object the two-`[ω]`-sites
question needs — a genuine, worked restriction map between a composite-group cohomology class and
its factors' classes, with an explicit exact-sequence account of what the restriction sees and
loses. Not yet read past the abstract/statement level; a careful read of the five-term sequence is
the natural next step before finalizing the "restriction kills the handoff generator" verdict in
[[two-omega-sites-not-isotropy-restriction]]. New to `sources.json`, `agent-summary`.

## Community

### [MO 229393 — 2-cohomology group of semi-direct products](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/229393/2-cohomology-group-of-semi-direct-products)
2016, 2 answers. Asks whether `H²(G,A) ≅ H²(N,A)^T × H²(T,A)` for `G=N⋊T` and trivial-action
module `A` — explicitly cites Tahara but the asker couldn't derive the general statement from it.
Both answers point to the **Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence** (`E_2^{p,q}=H^p(T,H^q(N,V))
⇒ H^{p+q}(G,V)`) and its associated five-term exact sequence as the right general machinery — the
same tool Hartl–Leroy use above. No general (non-coprime) closed formula given; open beyond the
spectral-sequence pointer. Second, independent corroboration (after Hartl-Leroy) that
Hochschild–Serre is the standard route to a restriction-map answer for the two-`[ω]`-sites
question.

### [MO 477767 — Does a crossed extension with trivial Postnikov invariant admit a section?](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/477767/does-a-crossed-extension-with-trivial-postnikov-invariant-admit-a-section)
2024, 1 accepted answer. For n-fold crossed extensions (`π₀ ≅ H^{n+1}(G,K)`), trivial class does
**NOT** imply a section for `n>1` — special to ordinary (`n=1`) extensions. A useful cautionary
parallel: naive "trivial class ⟹ splits" reasoning breaks once you leave the simplest single-
extension case, echoing MacBeth's own 08-14 finding that naively identifying the two `[ω]` sites
is false (the restriction map is well-typed but kills the handoff generator).

### [MO 496378 — A conjecture for factorization of group-cohomology classes](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/496378/a-conjecture-for-factorization-of-group-cohomology-classes)
2025, unanswered, physics-motivated (SPT/SET states). Conjectures a *restriction-map-image*
condition controlling when a class `ω∈H^n(G,U(1))` factors through an extension. Structurally the
same shape as the two-`[ω]`-sites question (an image-of-restriction condition gating an
identification) — no proof, but useful as a structural mirror.

### [cstheory 51020 — Why can't opaque optics form a category?](https://cstheory.stackexchange.com/questions/51020/why-cant-opaque-optics-form-a-category)
Haskell-engineering thread, low mathematical content, but first entry logged in the
optics/lens-SE territory: `optics` package lenses don't fit `Control.Category`'s 2-argument shape
without currying `(s,t)`/`(a,b)` into pairs. Concrete illustration of "type-changing optics as a
category on pairs," relevant background texture for the Cybercat/dependent-lens reading.
