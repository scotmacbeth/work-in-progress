## Papers

### [Henning Urbat, "Higher-Order Behavioural Conformances via Fibrations"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18509) — POPL 2026 / PACMPL 10(POPL):1586–1614

Deep-read in full (PDF via `mcp__research__download_pdf`, ~2500 lines). Builds a uniform
categorical account of Howe's method for proving behavioural conformances (bisimilarity,
behavioural pseudometrics) are congruences in higher-order languages, including quantitative/
probabilistic ones. §5 sets up the fibrational machinery: CLat⊓-fibrations (fibers = complete
lattices, **always faithful/thin** by Lemma 5.2(1)), refined to (involutive) Qtl-fibrations for
quantale-valued (fuzzy/Wasserstein) conformances. Def 5.11 gives standard Hermida–Jacobs-style
functor lifting along a fibration; Prop 5.16 shows free algebras/cofree coalgebras of a lifted
functor lift automatically along any CLat⊓-fibration.

**Connection to seed**: this was flagged last session as the load-bearing new citation for
MacBeth's just-proved proof-relevance boundary theorem (□-predicate-lifting on Sub(Set), reverse-
total, vs proof-relevant `T_M` container-monad lifting on Cont, forward-total). Grepped the full
text for "Sub(Set)", "codomain fibration", "proof-relevant", "Cont(", "container monad",
"Mendler" — **zero hits**. Every fibration used is thin/faithful by construction (Lemma 5.2(1)) —
this paper lives entirely on the propositional/□ side of the boundary, never touching the
proof-relevant `T_M` side. **Third independent witness** (after Orestis's Agda and Goncharov–
Milius–Schröder–Tsampas–Urbat) that classical fibrational predicate/relation-lifting technology
is *structurally* confined to thin fibrations — not incidentally, but because CLat⊓-fibrations
are faithful by Lemma 5.2(1). Clean citable neighbour, zero scoop risk.

**Idea sparked**: Prop 5.16 ("lifting along any CLat⊓-fibration commutes with free/cofree
construction") is the same *shape* of claim as MacBeth's own `free-cofree-up-mirror`, just for a
different (thin) fibration class. Worth a citation-list check next session, not urgent.

## Community

### [MO 302631 — "What is the polynomial functor for the Bag monad?"](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/302631) (Ben Sprott, 2018, new to index)

Peter LeFanu Lumsdaine's accepted answer: the bag/multiset monad `B` is **not polynomial** —
explicit pullback-non-preservation proof (two distinct bags `{(a,y),(b,z)}` and `{(a,z),(b,y)}`
in `B(X×Y)` map to the same element of `B(X)×_{B(1)}B(Y)`; commutativity is the obstruction).
Cites Makkai–Zawadowski and Simpson for the same phenomenon elsewhere in higher category theory.
Second answer notes multiset *is* polynomial in a categorified sense over groupoids (Fiore et
al., "Data Types with Symmetries and Polynomial Functors over Groupoids").

**Connection**: independent, citable, community-sourced corroboration of MacBeth's own
"Bag=outside-∏-Mendler OPPOSITE Reader" result — same external-witness role Kevin Carlson's MO
answer and Orestis's Agda played for other parts of the boundary work. Also connects to the
groupoid/symmetric-container angle via the Fiore et al. citation.

## Connections

The single strongest thread this session: **three independent literatures now corroborate that
classical predicate/relation-lifting technology (Hermida–Jacobs style) is structurally confined
to thin/faithful fibrations** — Orestis's Agda (all-`Type`-valued but routes every drop-monad
example through `∃`/`◇`), Goncharov et al.'s bialgebraic framework, and now Urbat's CLat⊓-
fibrations (provably faithful by Lemma 5.2(1)). This isn't incidental convergence — thinness is
what makes a fibration "propositional" in exactly MacBeth's sense, so of course the classical
literature (built for logical relations / behavioural conformances, which want thin) never
crosses into the proof-relevant `T_M`/Cont side. The boundary theorem's "the two fibrations pull
in opposite totality directions" is not just a local observation about Reader/State — it is a
structural fact about *which fibrations the field builds tools for* (Sub(Set)-flavoured, thin)
versus the one MacBeth is working in (Cont→Set, non-thin). Worth a remark in the book's Ch7
motivation: predicate-lifting technology "already knows" it lives on the thin side; MacBeth's
contribution is naming and proving the boundary at the non-thin `T_M` side, which the field has
apparently never had reason to formalize.

Second, smaller connection: MO 302631's Bag-non-polynomial proof and MacBeth's own affine-
classification machinery together sit right next to Sprott's own unanswered MO 461865 ("when is a
container a monad?") — a cheap, high-goodwill community contribution.
