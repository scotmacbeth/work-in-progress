## T1: is "non-branching" = Power-Robinson commutativity, or something else?

### Power-Robinson commutativity (1997) — classical reference, NOT read directly
Premonoidal categories were introduced by Power-Robinson to model side-effecting
languages. Classical fact (via Staton 2014 citation [32] + nLab): the Kleisli
category of any strong monad M is always **premonoidal**; it is **monoidal iff M
is commutative** in the strength-symmetry sense.

**This is a different axis from MacBeth's non-branching (arity ≤ 1) condition.**
Evidence collected (via websearch + nLab + Jacobs 2016):
- Powerset monad P is **commutative** (nLab) yet **maximally branching**
  (arity unbounded).
- Writer monad M×(−) is **non-branching** (arity = 1, single output slot) for
  *every* monoid M, but is Power-Robinson-**commutative only if M is a
  commutative monoid**.
- Exception monad E+(−) is **non-branching** (arity ≤ 1) for every set E, but
  is commutative only when |E| ≤ 1.

So commutativity and non-branching are **orthogonal conditions**: neither
implies the other. Power-Robinson commutativity is the wrong classical target
for T1 — it answers "when is Kleisli(M) monoidal," not "when does M avoid
branching."

**Verdict:** classify as a clean negative result — cite Power-Robinson 1997 as
context/contrast, not as the equivalence MacBeth is looking for.

### Jacobs, "Affine Monads and Side-Effect-Freeness" (CMCS 2016) — deep-read, 15/20pp
`/home/agent/papers/jacobs-affine-monads.pdf` (via http://www.cs.ru.nl/B.Jacobs/PAPERS/side-effects.pdf).

Defines **affine** (T(1) ≅ 1) and the stronger **strongly affine** (a pullback
condition, Def. 1 / diagram (10)). Examples: distribution monad 𝒟, Giry monad
𝒢, expectation monad ℰ are all strongly affine — and all three are **branching**
(unbounded support). The writer monad M×(−) is affine only when M is the
trivial (one-element) monoid, yet is non-branching for every M regardless.

**This adds a third orthogonal axis.** None of {commutative, affine,
non-branching} imply each other:
- 𝒟, 𝒢, ℰ: affine ∧ branching.
- writer M×(−), M nontrivial noncommutative: non-branching ∧ ¬affine ∧
  ¬commutative.
- powerset P: commutative ∧ branching ∧ ¬affine (P(1) has 2 elements).
- exception E+(−), |E|>1: non-branching ∧ ¬commutative; affine only if E=∅.

Also useful: Prop. 2 constructs the "affine part" T_a of any monad T as the
universal affine submonad (pullback of T(!) along η: 1→T(1)); this pullback
technique might be a reusable pattern if MacBeth ever wants to extract a
"non-branching part" of a general monad by an analogous universal
construction (untested idea, not in the paper).

**Verdict for T1:** MacBeth's target condition ("arity ≤ 1", i.e. M ≅ E+A×(−)
for sets E, A with A a monoid) is **not literally identical to any of the
three standard named conditions** (commutative, affine, strongly affine) found
in this literature sweep. It looks like a genuinely more specific classical
notion that doesn't yet have a citation home in what I found — candidate
search terms for next session: "output monad", "linear monad" (in the monad
sense, not linear-logic), Manes' "monad theory" arity classification, or
possibly it's just definitionally what MacBeth already calls it (E+A×(−)
shape = the general form of an arity-≤1 polynomial monad on Set) with no
further classical name needed — i.e. T1's conjecture may already BE the
correct and complete statement, just not yet phrased as an "iff a known
class" theorem citing prior art. Recommend treating "non-branching ⟺
E+A×(−) shape" as MacBeth's own original observation rather than continuing
to search for a scoop.

### Staton, "Freyd categories are enriched Lawvere theories" (WACT 2013 / ENTCS 303) — deep-read, full 9pp
`/home/agent/papers/staton-freyd-lawvere-2014.pdf` (cs.ox.ac.uk, not on arXiv).

Main theorems (3.3, 3.5): Freyd categories (V → C identity-on-objects,
action-preserving, e.g. C = Kleisli(M) for a strong monad M on V) correspond
exactly to Lawvere theories enriched in a locally 𝔇-presentable closed
category, for 𝔇 = empty doctrine (plain Freyd cats, Thm 3.3) or 𝔇 = finite
products (distributive Freyd cats = Power's enriched Lawvere theories, Thm
3.5). Proof goes via an action/power correspondence (Prop 2.7, a
Kelly-style enrichment lemma).

**Critically, this machinery is about a single strong monad's Kleisli
category** — Kleisli(M) is *always* a (premonoidal) Freyd category for *any*
strong monad M, branching or not; that's the entire classical point (no
arity/branching restriction anywhere in the paper). It says nothing about
comonads, and nothing about building a category from a monad-comonad *pair*.

MacBeth's arrows p⇝q := Cont(G_M p, T_M q) are hom-sets in the **different**
ambient category Cont, built from **both** T_M and G_M (derived from M via
the transfer construction), not simply Kleisli(M)'s own hom-sets. Staton's
V → Kleisli(M) Freyd category is structurally a different object.

**Verdict:** does **not** give a cleaner proof route for "arrow category
exists iff M non-branching." The classical Freyd/Lawvere-theory
correspondence has no branching obstruction because it answers a different
question (does Kleisli(M) admit *a* premonoidal/Freyd structure — yes,
always) rather than MacBeth's question (does the specific Cont(G_M−,T_M−)
construction form a genuine category — no, unless M is non-branching). Cite
as background/contrast, not as a shortcut. Recommend NOT pursuing this
route further for T1's proof.

## T2: E2'-general-j — does 2405.16708 help?

### Goncharov, Milius, Schröder, Tsampas, Urbat, "Higher-order bialgebraic semantics" (arXiv:2405.16708) — UPGRADED to deep-read, full 47pp
Previously logged at agent-summary (title/abstract only, 2026-07-29). Now
fully read via `/home/agent/papers/2405.16708.pdf`.

Extends Turi-Plotkin abstract GSOS to higher-order languages. Core notion:
a *V-pointed higher-order GSOS law* is a family
ϱ_{X,Y}: Σ(jX × B(jX,Y)) → B(jX, Σ*(jX+Y)), **dinatural in X, natural in Y**,
where B(X,Y) = Y + Y^X (deterministic case) is a mixed-variance behaviour
bifunctor. Main compositionality result (Thm 4.14): in a regular base
category, under mild preservation conditions on Σ and B, coalgebraic
bisimilarity is always a congruence — proved via a kernel-pair/reflexive-
coequalizer argument, entirely independent of comonads.

**Is there an arrow/Freyd category from a monad-comonad pair?** No. This
paper is about a distributive-law-style GSOS law of a syntax *functor* Σ over
a mixed-variance behaviour *bifunctor* B; no comonad appears anywhere in the
47 pages. Structurally unrelated to MacBeth's T_M/G_M entwining construction.

**Is there a branching/non-branching obstruction?** No — and this is worth
noting explicitly: Section 3.3 extends their combinatory-logic example to
nondeterministic xCL^⊕ using B^⊕(X,Y) = 𝒫_ω(Y+Y^X) (powerset, i.e. maximally
branching), and compositionality (Prop 3.10) holds with **no extra
difficulty**, following automatically from Thm 4.14 (𝒫_ω preserves both
surjections and injections, Example 4.20). So in *their* bialgebraic
framework, branching effects do not obstruct compositionality — a useful
data point that the branching obstruction MacBeth found is specific to the
arrow/Freyd-category construction, not a generic feature of monad-based
operational semantics.

**Is there a "general-j" naturality condition, and how do they handle it?**
Yes, structurally analogous in shape (not content) to E2'-general-j: their
dinaturality-in-X condition must hold for *every* set X (the hexagon (3.8)).
The proof of Theorem 3.7 (pp. 15-16) establishes this for all X,Y at once via
a clean technique worth trying on E2': factor through the unique map into the
**terminal object 1**; show the dinaturality/naturality hexagon forces the
image of ϱ_{1,1} to be a single element of Σ*(1+1) or (Σ*(1+1))^1 (because a
map *into* a coproduct/disjoint-union out of the terminal-times-terminal
domain must land in one summand); then use that this single element is
independent of X,Y to pull the general statement back up along
naturality-in-Y and dinaturality-in-X. This is a genuine "prove-for-all-X by
reducing to the terminal/representable case and lifting" pattern — **worth
attempting on E2'-general-j** even though the ambient frameworks (dinatural
GSOS laws vs. effect-coeffect entwining) are otherwise orthogonal.

**Verdict:** neighbour, not a scoop, confirmed on full read. Does not directly
close E2'-general-j, but contributes one transferable proof technique
(terminal-object reduction for an "all-X" (di)naturality condition) that
MacBeth should try next `/prove` session on E2'.
