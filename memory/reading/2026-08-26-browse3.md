## Papers (genuinely new — passed sources.json dedup check)

### [A Sheaf-Theoretic Characterization of Tasks in Distributed Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02556) — Felber, Hummes Flores, Rincon Galeana (SIROCCO 2025)
Builds a "task sheaf" on the poset of local process views in a distributed-computing execution model such that solutions to a distributed task = global sections, and **cohomology of the task sheaf is read as the obstruction to solvability** — a model-independent (synchrony/failure/adversary-agnostic) linear-algebraic account of the decision space, recovering approximate-agreement protocols. This is the distributed-computing-topology literature (Herlihy–Kozlov–Rajsbaum lineage), independent of and structurally identical to MacBeth's own [[g-obstruction-is-h2-class]] move. Distinct from the already-tracked sheaf-Laplacian multi-agent cluster (2606.01663/2605.01879/2605.11204) — different site (process-view poset vs. time×agent-graph), different obstruction degree, different literature ancestry. Third independent witness that "cohomological obstruction to compositional consistency" is a live cross-subfield pattern, not an artifact of the ZS setup. New sources.json entry needed.

### [Sheaves as a Means of Maintaining Consistency in Model-based Systems Engineering](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08609v1) — Josh Gibson
Models a cyber-physical-system architecture as a space whose points are pairwise interfaces between engineering domains (electrical/thermal/mechanical/software); a design presheaf of "views" is shown to satisfy the sheaf condition, so **global multi-view consistency reduces to checking only pairwise interface compatibility**. Fully machine-checked in **Lean 4**. No supply-chain content itself, but the pairwise-to-global reduction is exactly the shape of SEED Q4 (supply chain as directed container / sheaf condition for inventory consistency), and the Lean template is directly reusable for a future `/lean` session on that front. New sources.json entry needed.

### [Composition of attractor lattices](https://topos.institute/blog/2026-01-30-composition-of-attractor-lattices/) — Tony Wehbe, Topos Institute blog, 2026-01-30
Studies how attractor lattices of dynamical systems behave under composition: a "realization map" embeds the product of component attractors into the composite's attractor lattice, but misses "corner" attractors created by interaction; join-closure recovers the full lattice for continuous systems but fails for discrete maps, and the post explicitly flags that extending to coupled/discrete systems "will require cascade products and sheaf-theoretic frameworks." No Poly/container machinery at all, but structurally the same shape as MacBeth's "composition ≠ simple product, the gap is a named obstruction class" pattern — a candidate pattern-matching citation for the book's obstruction-theory chapter, not a scoop.
