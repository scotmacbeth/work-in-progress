## Papers

### Monoid Structures on Indexed Containers (arXiv:2509.25879)
**Authors:** Michele De Pascalis, Tarmo Uustalu, Niccolò Veltri (Tallinn/Tartu, 2025)
**Key results:** For I-indexed containers, the composition product ◁ makes them into a monoidal category; monoids in this category = monads on Set^I. Includes: state monad, writer monad, product-of-two-monads construction, free monad example. All in Cubical Agda.
**Connection:** This is the indexed version of MacBeth's WRITE target. The non-indexed case (I=1) is the book chapter. The free monad construction gives C* = tree-shaped container as the initial monoid — exactly MacBeth's result. **The comonoid/cofree comonad side is absent** — not addressed in the indexed setting either. This confirms that the cofree comonad = comonoid direction is MacBeth's original contribution to the book chapter.
**Cite as:** primary prior-art citation for the monoid = monad direction; book chapter should note "the indexed generalization is proved in [De Pascalis-Uustalu-Veltri 2025]."

### Comodule Representations of Second-Order Functionals (arXiv:2409.17664)
**Authors:** Danel Ahman, Andrej Bauer (JLAMP 2025)
**Key results:** Right comodules for monads on Cont carry representations of second-order functionals. Monads on Cont = monoids in (Cont,◁,y) appear explicitly. Key construction: a monad from a monad on shapes + weak Mendler-style monad algebra on position types.
**Connection:** Provides a computational semantics application for the book chapter on monoids in (Cont,◁). The "monad-from-shapes" construction is an explicit description of how the ◁ product gives monoid structures — concrete material for the examples section.

### Distributive Laws of Monadic Containers (arXiv:2503.17191)
**Authors:** Chris Purdy, Stefania Damato (CALCO 2025)
**Key results:** Classifies distributive laws for monadic containers (those whose extension functors carry monad structure). Completes the container zoo alongside Ahman-Uustalu's directed (comonadic) case: all four combinations of monad/comonad distributive laws are now characterised for containers.
**Connection:** Direct peer to the ZS product / DCont≅Cof work. The mixed distributive laws (directed container over monadic container) are the container-level analogue of the monad-comonad interaction in ZS products. After Ch4 (free/cofree), this is the natural Ch5 direction.

### What Kind of Linearly Distributive Category Do Polynomial Functors Form? (arXiv:2407.01849)
**Authors:** David Spivak, Priyaa Varshinee Srinivasan (2024)
**Key results:** (Poly, y, ⊗, ◁) is a **normal duoidal category** and an isomix linearly distributive category. "Normal" = both tensor products share the unit y. Every linear polynomial has a representable right dual. Introduces linear monoids, comonoids, and bialgebras in Poly.
**Connection:** **Key for PROVE target.** The four monoidal structures on Cont are (restricted to containers) part of the duoidal structure (⊗, ◁) on Poly. Normal duoidal categories have established coherence theorems (Garner-López Franco). This is the right structural frame for proving pentagon+triangle coherence — the coherence conditions are instances of the normal duoidal coherence. **Action:** read duoidal coherence theorem before PROVE session.

### Formalising Inductive and Coinductive Containers (arXiv:2409.02603)
**Authors:** Stefania Damato, Thorsten Altenkirch, Axel Ljungström (ITP 2025)
**Key results:** Removes UIP from container fixed-point preservation proofs. The result is that W-types (initial algebras) and M-types (terminal coalgebras) can be defined for containers without assuming UIP. Formalised in Cubical Agda.
**Connection:** Direct relevance to MacBeth's free monad chapter: the free monad C* = W-type of C, and the cofree comonad C^∞ = M-type. This paper is the Cubical Agda companion to MacBeth's Lean formalization. The UIP-free version may be relevant for Lean 4 (which also has HoTT options).

---

### Problems with Fixpoints of Polynomials of Polynomials (arXiv:2601.15420)
**Authors:** Cécilia Pradic, Ian Price (LICS 2026)
**Key results:** Studies fixpoints of fibred container endofunctors. Weihrauch degrees from closed choice on {0,1} to determinacy of infinite parity games are captured by ζ-fixpoints of container endofunctors. The free polynomial monad construction is the technical entry point.
**Connection:** **Most surprising find.** Weihrauch complexity — a branch of computable analysis / reverse mathematics — turns out to use the free container monad as its semantic backbone. This is a completely new application domain for the grant narrative: "our framework applies not just to supply chains and blockchains but to computability-theoretic degree structures." This deserves a note in the grant's impact section.

### Unified Initial Semantics (arXiv:2502.10811)
**Authors:** Thomas Lamiaux, Benedikt Ahrens (2025)
**Key results:** Unifies Σ-monoids, modules over monads, and Matthes-Uustalu heterogeneous substitution systems under a monoidal framework. Matthes-Uustalu is Uustalu's earlier work on initial semantics.
**Connection:** Adjacent to MacBeth's free monad chapter since it unifies the Uustalu tradition of initial/terminal semantic objects with the monoidal framework. The Matthes-Uustalu arm is the proof-theoretic ancestor of the container free monad construction.

## Hub Papers Found (new to MacBeth's notes)

### Uustalu, "Container Combinatorics: Monads and Lax Monoidal Functors" (TTCS 2017)
**Status:** THE hub for monoidal structures on containers. Cited by every 2024-2026 paper in this space.
**Key results:** Characterises monads on containers in terms of lax monoidal functors for the composition monoidal structure. Four monoidal structures and their relationship are developed here.
**Action:** Read before writing Ch4. This is the foundational citation MacBeth is currently missing.

### Gambino-Kock, "Polynomial Functors and Polynomial Monads" (Math. Proc. Cambridge 2013, arXiv:0906.4931)
**Status:** THE reference for free monad on a polynomial functor being polynomial.
**Key results:** Free monad on polynomial P = polynomial with positions = P-trees, directions = leaves. The monoid structure is grafting. Polynomial monads = polynomial functors with cartesian unit and multiplication.
**Action:** Cite centrally in Ch4. The Gambino-Kock formula C* = Σ_{n≥0} C^{◁n} (as P-trees) is the construction.

## Connections

1. **The WRITE target has excellent bibliographic support now.** Ch4's main theorem (monoid = monad in (Cont,◁,y)) is confirmed by three independent sources: Uustalu TTCS 2017 (hub), De Pascalis-Uustalu-Veltri 2509.25879 (indexed version), Gambino-Kock 0906.4931 (polynomial monad construction). The cofree comonad = comonoid direction is genuinely MacBeth's original — no source covers it.

2. **The PROVE target is reframed.** The four monoidal structures on Cont are not just four independent structures — they sit inside a normal duoidal structure (Spivak-Srinivasan 2407.01849, Shapiro-Spivak 2305.00167): ⊗ and ◁ interact duoidally, sharing the unit y. This is the correct structural frame for the coherence proof. The pentagon/triangle for ◁ are instances of duoidal coherence.

3. **Weihrauch complexity as application domain.** Containers = semantic domain for Weihrauch degrees (Pradic-Price LICS 2026). This is the most surprising new connection and has direct grant impact: "compositional correctness has computability-theoretic consequences."

4. **The "polynomial slice" narrative.** Fairbanks "Set-sets" blog + forthcoming Fairbanks-Carlson-Spivak paper frames polynomial comonads (= directed containers) as the polynomial slice of a richer comonad story. General comonads on Set = generalised directed containers that can be non-polynomial. This is a beautiful grant narrative: "we work in the polynomial slice because that is where categorical composition is most tractable, but our framework points toward a broader comonad geometry."

5. **Cofunctors have a free/cofree bifibration (Clarke-Scherer-Zeilberger 2511.07314).** The universal property of cofunctors that MacBeth needed for Ch4 may live in Clarke's zigzag double category Z(C). This deserves follow-up in the write session.
