## 5. Unplanned but notable find — methodological echo, NOT a scoop
"Monads and Distributive Laws in Substructural Contexts (Extended Version)" — Fujii, Tsai, Montacute,
Hasuo, LICS 2026, arXiv:2605.13533. Read: a fairly deep pass via WebFetch on
arxiv.org/html/2605.13533 (full abstract, key definitions, Theorem 5.2 statement, and worked examples
extracted) — more than abstract-only, upgrading its prior "abstract"-level tracking (from
07-29-browse3) toward deep-read. Builds a general theory of monad-monad distributive laws δ:ST→TS on
Set via Tronin's verbal categories W: introduces W-operadic monads and W-commutative monads; gives a
canonical construction of δ when S is W-operadic and T is W-commutative. Examples: exception, reader,
writer, multiset, affine multiset, valuation, probability-distribution monads on Set.

**Why flagged:** Theorem 5.2's compatibility condition has an exceptional clause — "unless S is so
lean that it is generated by operations of arity ≤1" — gating when the construction needs the
exchange rule. Structurally reminiscent of MacBeth's own non-branching/arity-≤1 criterion for
effect-coeffect arrow categories. **Not a scoop:** confirmed the paper does NOT discuss branching,
leaf-symmetry, Freyd/Hughes arrow categories, or coeffects — monad-monad (not monad-comonad)
distributive-law-existence via a completely different engine (verbal categories, not arrow-category
axioms). Worth keeping as a citable parallel-discovery neighbour if the branching-arity criterion is
ever written up for a broader PL audience.
