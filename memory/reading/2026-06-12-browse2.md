## Papers Read

### [What kind of linearly distributive category do polynomial functors form?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01849) — Spivak, Srinivasan (July 2024)

(Poly, ⊗, ◁) is a **bi-closed isomix LDC** (linearly distributive category). "Bi-closed" is a new notion: ⊗ (Dirichlet) has a right internal hom [p,q] with Poly(r,[p,q]) ≅ Poly(r⊗p,q); ◁ (substitution) has a **co-closure** with Poly([q,p],r) ≅ Poly(p,r◁q). The isomix condition holds because both monoidal units equal y. Not *-autonomous.

**Crown jewel for the book:** The ◁ co-closure is exactly where DCont morphisms live. DCont(p,q) corresponds to Poly(p, y◁q), and by the co-closure, to maps out of [q,p]. So DCont morphisms = elements of the co-closed structure. Three distinct closed structures on Poly (×-cartesian closed, ⊗-Dirichlet closed, ◁-co-closed) coexist without conflict. **Ch3 closes cleanly with this.**

### [The compact double category Int(Poly*) models control flow and data transformations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05462) — Kondyrev, Spivak (September 2025)

**Critical disambiguation:** Poly* here = pointed **sets** (Set*), NOT polynomial functors. Set* is traced; Int(Set*) = free compact category; ℤInt(Set*) = compact double category for trajectory-tracking control flow. Architecturally separate from DCont. The naming collision ("Poly*") was confusing the literature.

Book use: contrast example when discussing double categories, illustrating that "compact double category" (Patterson's sense, from traced ×-monoidal structure) ≠ "double category from ◁-bicomodules" (Shapiro-Spivak Cat#). Do not conflate.

### [Weihrauch problems as containers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17250) — Pradic, Price (January 2025)

Weihrauch problems over projective represented spaces are containers: shapes = inputs, positions = valid outputs. Container morphisms = Weihrauch reductions. Parallel product = ⊗; composition product = ◁. Extended Weihrauch degrees = posetal reflection over partition assemblies. No DCont structure (no comonad). 

**Fifth application domain** for containers. Demonstrates generality: the framework works over non-Set bases. Good for Ch1 motivation alongside supply chains, blockchain, agent orchestration, and GA composition.

### Spivak, "Categories by Kan extension" (arXiv:2503.21974) — revisited

New depth: "selection categories" (dual to Lawvere theories from monads) may yield new directed-container constructions. Spivak builds Δ^op from a distributive law of a monad over a polynomial comonad — check whether this is an instance of the pairwise ZS criterion.

## Papers Identified (Unread — Priority Queue)

### [Derivatives for Containers in Univalent Foundations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17484) — Joram, Veltri (Dec 2024)

**Highest priority find of this session.** Tackles exactly ∂(G◁F) in Cubical Agda. Key theorem: a fully invertible chain rule fails globally for untruncated containers (non-set types), but holds in the set-truncated (classical) setting. MacBeth's target lives in the classical directed container setting — **no higher-type obstruction applies**. Their proof technique may transfer to Lean 4. **Must read before starting chain rule PROVE session.**

The chain rule ∂(G◁F) ≅ (∂G◁F)×∂F is NOT in AAGM 2003/2005 — those papers have sum/product rules and a chain rule for *function composition* ∘, not substitution ◁. This formula is **genuinely original** (in the classical setting), with Joram-Veltri as the first engagement after 20 years.

### [Homology and twisted C*-algebras for self-similar actions and Zappa-Szép products](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09600) — Mundey, Sims (2023)

Homological spectral sequence E¹_{p,q} = H_p(C; H_q(D; ℤ)) for ZS products. **Cohomological dual E₁^{p,q} = H^p(C; H^q(D; A)) appears genuinely absent** from the paper (confirmed via abstract + metadata; PDF unrendered). If this is confirmed by reading §§, this is a short open theorem waiting to be written.
