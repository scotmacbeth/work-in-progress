\begin{abstract}
An AI agent presents an \emph{interface}: a set of queries it may issue and, for each,
a set of admissible replies. This is exactly a polynomial functor, or container --- a
reading due to Spivak and, in the orchestration setting, to Ghani, with the
interface/implementation mechanism (free-monad implementations composed along wiring
diagrams, dependent-polynomial specifications) developed in dependent type theory by
Aberl\'e. We take that reading seriously enough to ask what it says about \emph{composing}
orchestrations that share resources, and we find that the natural model is not a functor
but a distributive law: welding two orchestrations into one interleaved system is a
Zappa--Sz\'ep product $\C\bowtie\D$ of their handoff categories --- the distributive-law
construction for directed containers being Ahman--Uustalu's. This reframing has teeth,
because a Zappa--Sz\'ep product need not exist, and its obstruction is known: a degree-two
cohomology class $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)$ on the handoff category with coefficients
in the reply functor. Our main result works the smallest non-trivial case --- a supervisor
dispatching to workers, parametrized by a one-bit turn token --- and proves that the
obstruction class equals the token-mutation bit on the nose: the joint agent exists if and
only if the worker fixes the token, and the sole obstructed regime is \emph{unprotected
re-entrancy}, a worker mutating the supervisor's shared state during a pending call, which
presents exactly as the nonzero generator of $H^2\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The degree is the
point: the multi-agent-systems literature places $H^0$ (consensus) and $H^1$
(identifiability) sheaf obstructions on the \emph{communication} graph, asking whether
agents agree; the container model places $H^2$ on the \emph{handoff} category, asking the
prior question of whether they compose at all.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection*{The problem}
Modern AI systems are increasingly \emph{orchestrations}: a supervisor agent dispatches
subtasks to worker agents, a planner calls tools that call other tools, a proof assistant
runs tactics that invoke sub-tactics. Each participant is a black box with a typed
boundary --- it accepts a query and returns one of a set of admissible replies --- and the
engineering discipline is entirely about how these boundaries are wired together. The
question this note addresses is structural rather than behavioural: given two orchestrations
that share resources, \emph{when can they be run as a single consistent interleaved system},
and when is there a genuine obstruction to doing so?

\subsection*{Agents are containers}
The starting point, due to Spivak in the polynomial-functor programme
\cite{NiuSpivak,SpivakRef} and articulated for agent orchestration by Ghani at Kodamai,
is that an agent's interface is a \emph{container} (equivalently a polynomial functor). An
agent that may issue queries drawn from a set $S$, and for each query $s$ may receive a
reply from a set $P(s)$, is the container $p=(S,P)$, or as a functor
$p\cong\sum_{s\in S}y^{P(s)}$. An oracle call --- issue a query, receive a reply --- is a
morphism $p\to y$ to the unit container; delegating from one agent to another is a container
morphism, forward on queries and backward on replies. On this reading the monoidal
structures of $\Poly$ become an orchestration vocabulary: sequential delegation, parallel
composition, choice. This dictionary is not ours and we do not claim it (Section~\ref{sec:dict}
grades each row); we take it as the setting.

\subsection*{Prior art: the interface/implementation mechanism}
The mechanism that turns this dictionary into a working compositional framework is due to
Aberl\'e \cite{aberle}, whose recent development we take as the immediate prior art. There,
polynomial functors $p=(A,B)$ are program \emph{interfaces}; an \emph{implementation} is a
morphism $p\Rightarrow\mathrm{Free}\,q$ into the free monad on the assumed interface $q$
(assume--guarantee reasoning: a bare lens is the special case of exactly one call, the free
monad lifts it to zero/one/many), and implementations compose along Spivak's operad of
wiring diagrams by Kleisli composition (\cite{aberle}, Thm.~3.1). \emph{Specifications} are
dependent polynomials $(C,D)$ over $p$ --- literally Hoare triples $\{C\}\,f\,\{D\}$ --- and
they compose along the same wiring diagrams (Thm.~5.3). The whole package is a strictly
monoidal forgetful functor $\pi:\mathbf{Spec}\to\mathbf{Int}$ together with a lax monoidal
presheaf of systems and a monoidal natural transformation (Def.~7.1). Everything in the
present note's dictionary that concerns \emph{interfaces, free-monad implementations,
wiring-diagram composition, and dependent-polynomial specifications is Aberl\'e's and is
cited as such}; our theorem-proving-as-container and orchestration-as-workflow readings are
a \emph{domain reinterpretation} of that mechanism, not a new mechanism. (Aberl\'e himself
flags tactics and proof automation as future work.) What Aberl\'e does \emph{not} treat, and
what this note supplies, is the composition of two systems that \emph{interact} over a shared
resource: his only two-system construct is the unobstructed parallel sum. That gap is exactly
where the contribution lives.

\subsection*{The gap}
What the existing dictionary does \emph{not} supply is a theory of when a composition is
\emph{possible}. Prior treatments model an orchestration as a functor over a category of
interfaces --- composition \emph{along} a single fixed topology. But concurrency, a
supervisor sharing workers between two plans, and re-entrant tool-calls are not composition
along one topology; they are the \emph{interleaving of two}. A functor cannot see the
failure mode, because it presumes the composite already exists. We want a model in which the
composite can fail to exist, together with an invariant that detects the failure.

\subsection*{The result}
The model is a distributive law. A handoff topology --- who may hand off to whom, staying
put, concatenating handoffs --- is a small category, equivalently a directed container in the
sense of Ahman--Uustalu \cite{AhmanUustalu}. Interleaving two orchestrations $\C$ and $\D$
into a single joint agent is then a \emph{Zappa--Sz\'ep product} (matched-pair product,
strict factorization system) $\K=\C\bowtie\D$. That a distributive law of directed containers
assembles such a joint container --- generalizing the Zappa--Sz\'ep product of monoids, its
matched pair recording the two mutual actions --- is a theorem of Ahman--Uustalu
\cite{AhmanUustaluDL}; we take the construction from them and claim none of it. Such a product
need not exist, however; the author's pairwise criterion \cite{pairwise} characterizes
\emph{when} the complementary factor can be found, and by the author's obstruction theorem
\cite{gobstr} that existence is controlled by a degree-two cohomology class:
\[
  \text{the joint agent }\C\bowtie\D\text{ exists}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  [\omega]=0\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir),
\]
cohomology of the handoff category's skeleton with coefficients in the reply (direction)
functor $\dir$. The cohomology itself is classical
\cite{rw,baueswirsching,pirashvili}; what is new is the identification of the orchestration
composite with $\C\bowtie\D$ and hence of its obstruction with this class.

\subsection*{The method}
We ground the claim on the smallest faithful instance rather than asserting the dictionary
into a theorem. A supervisor dispatching to workers, with a one-bit turn token recording
whose reply it awaits, is a small category $\K_\varepsilon$ parametrized by a single bit
$\varepsilon\in\mathbb Z/2$: the worker's re-entrant outcome sends the dispatch to
$q\cdot\tau^{\varepsilon}$, so $\varepsilon$ is exactly whether that outcome \emph{mutates}
the token ($\varepsilon=1$) or \emph{fixes} it ($\varepsilon=0$). Interleaving $\K_\varepsilon$
with a second orchestration sharing the workers is a Zappa--Sz\'ep product. We prove
\emph{analytically} --- verifying the freeness condition and the vertex-group hypothesis
directly, computing the orbit category, the cochain complex, and the defect cocycle by hand
--- that the obstruction class equals the token-mutation bit on the nose,
\[
  [\omega(\K_\varepsilon)]=\varepsilon\cdot(\text{generator})\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)\cong\mathbb Z/2,
\]
so the joint agent exists if and only if $\varepsilon=0$. No enumeration of factorization
systems and no machine-verified isomorphism is load-bearing; a parametrized script
cross-checks every step. Two further regimes --- independent supervisors over read-only
workers (a trivial product) and two supervisors interleaving coherently into the non-abelian
join $S_3$ --- round out the picture at the grade of a \emph{computed} illustration; the
proved content is the dichotomy.

\subsection*{The consequences}
Two payoffs. First, a named, structural failure mode: \emph{unprotected re-entrancy} --- the
callee writing to the caller's shared state during a pending call --- is not a heuristic
smell but an exact nonzero cohomology class on the handoff category, the categorical avatar
of a flat bundle with nontrivial holonomy. Second, a clean position relative to the
multi-agent-systems literature. That literature places cellular-sheaf obstructions of degree
$H^0$ (consensus, Nash equilibria) and $H^1$ (identifiability) on the \emph{communication}
graph \cite{sheafMAS,sheafID,sheafPlan}, asking whether agents currently agree. The container
model places an obstruction one degree up, $H^2$, on the \emph{handoff} category, asking the
prior question of whether the agents compose into a joint system at all. The two are
complementary, and the degree axis is exact.

reader should treat this section as a glossary, not a sequence of theorems: most rows are

is flagged $\bigstar$ and proved in Section~\ref{sec:worked}.

$S$ and, for each shape $s$, a \emph{position} set $P(s)$, and represents the polynomial
functor $\sum_{s\in S}y^{P(s)}$.

\medskip
Three remarks on the graded rows keep the reader honest.

The \emph{interface} and \emph{dynamics} rows are Spivak's interaction reading of $\Poly$
\cite{NiuSpivak,SpivakRef}. The \emph{implementation} content of the sequential and
proof-search rows below --- interfaces implemented by free-monad(-on-$\Poly$) Kleisli arrows
and composed along wiring diagrams, with dependent polynomials as specifications --- is
Aberl\'e's mechanism \cite{aberle} (see the prior-art discussion above); we reinterpret it in
two domains but claim none of the mechanism itself.

The \emph{sequential} row is worth pausing on because it is the one place where the underlying
free-monad structure is proved, machine-checked, and directly models a familiar orchestration
pattern. The composition product $p\seq q$ is ``run $p$, and at each of its positions run a
copy of $q$''; iterating it freely produces the free monad on a container, whose elements are
exactly \emph{nested-call workflow trees} --- a shape at the root, and for each of its
positions a subtree. This free monad is Aberl\'e's implementation structure; what we add is a
machine check of its algebra: the three monoid laws (unit on both sides, associativity of
grafting) are formalised in Lean with no remaining axioms beyond quotient soundness
\cite{leanfree}. Under the proof-search reading, this is tactic composition; under the
orchestration reading, it is a call tree.

The \emph{proof-search} instance is the same model with the words changed: a theorem is a
shape, the lemmas it needs are its positions, and a proof is an element of the free monad --- a
tree of theorems reduced to sub-theorems --- with tactic composition realised as grafting.
That the free-monad laws hold on the nose (loc.\ cit.) is what makes tactic composition
associative. This is a domain reinterpretation of Aberl\'e's verified-implementation
framework, which itself names tactics and proof automation as future work.

\section{Why composition is a Zappa--Sz\'ep product}\label{sec:zs}

We now state the three results the composition claim rests on. All are used as black
boxes. The first is Ahman--Uustalu; so, crucially, is the construction the second theorem
rests on --- that a distributive law of directed containers is exactly a matched pair and
assembles the joint Zappa--Sz\'ep container, generalizing the monoid case, is their theorem
\cite{AhmanUustaluDL}. The author's contribution in the second theorem is only the
\emph{existence} criterion $\mathrm{(L)}+\mathrm{(G)}$ for the complementary factor; the
cohomology in the third is classical.

\begin{theorem}[Directed container $\cong$ small category; Ahman--Uustalu \cite{AhmanUustalu}]
\label{thm:T1}
A handoff topology --- objects are roles, morphisms are admissible handoff paths, with
identities (stay put) and composition (concatenate handoffs) --- is a small category, hence a
directed container $(S\seq P,o,\downarrow,\oplus)$, and conversely. The two structures carry the
same data. \textup{(Formalised in Lean in the author's development.)}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion \cite{pairwise}]\label{thm:T2}
Let $\K$ be a small category and $\D\subseteq\K$ a wide subcategory. There is a wide
subcategory $\C\subseteq\K$ making $(\C,\D)$ a strict factorization system --- equivalently a
Zappa--Sz\'ep product $\K=\C\bowtie\D$, equivalently a distributive law
$\lambda:\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ satisfying the matched-pair axioms --- if and only if
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textnormal{(L)}] every hom-presheaf $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is free as a right $\D$-set, and
\item[\textnormal{(G)}] free bases can be chosen closing into a wide subcategory.
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[The closure obstruction is an $H^2$ class \cite{gobstr}]\label{thm:T3}
Under the standard hypothesis (abelian vertex groups, trivial induced action),
$\textnormal{(G)}$ holds if and only if a single cohomology class vanishes,
\[
  \textnormal{(G)}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ [\omega]=0\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir),
\]
cohomology of the skeleton of the handoff category with coefficients in the direction (reply)
functor $\dir$. The relevant cohomology is classical: existence is Rosebrugh--Wood \cite{rw},
the abelian classification is Baues--Wirsching \cite{baueswirsching}, and the non-abelian case
is Pirashvili's Schreier theory \cite{pirashvili}. We cite it; we do not reprove it.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Functor versus distributive law}
Here is the conceptual load of Theorem~\ref{thm:T2}, and the reason it is the right model. A
functor between handoff categories composes \emph{along} a single topology: it presumes one
fixed order of events and transports structure through it. But two orchestrations that share
resources do not present one topology; each has its own, and running them together requires
\emph{interleaving} the two. A distributive law $\lambda:\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ is exactly the
data of a coherent interleaving --- a rule for pushing a $\D$-move past a $\C$-move --- and a
Zappa--Sz\'ep product $\C\bowtie\D$ is the joint category it generates; that a distributive
law of directed containers assembles this joint container is Ahman--Uustalu's
\cite{AhmanUustaluDL}. This is what
concurrency, a supervisor sharing workers between two plans, and re-entrant tool-calls
actually require, and it is strictly more than a functor can express: a functor always exists,
whereas a distributive law can fail to. Theorem~\ref{thm:T3} says the failure is measured in a
single cohomological degree.

\begin{remark}[Scope]
Theorems~\ref{thm:T1}--\ref{thm:T3} are established. The novelty claimed here is the
\emph{identification}: that the joint system of two shared-resource orchestrations is
$\C\bowtie\D$, so that its non-existence is exactly the nonvanishing of $[\omega]$. We ground
that identification, rather than assert it, in Section~\ref{sec:worked}.
\end{remark}

\section{The worked instantiation}\label{sec:worked}

We exhibit the smallest orchestration in which the obstruction is non-trivial and \emph{prove},
analytically, that its obstruction class equals a single design bit. The point is to move the
starred dictionary row from a proposed reading to a theorem: the supervisor--worker family below
\emph{is} a category to which Theorems~\ref{thm:T1}--\ref{thm:T3} apply, and its composition
obstruction \emph{is} a concrete $H^2$ generator, uniformly across the bit. Full computational
detail is in the author's analytic proof note \cite{analytic}; we give the spine here.

\subsection*{The parametrized category}
Fix handoff roles $\Sup$ (supervisor), $\Wk$ (a worker running), $\Rt$ (a return point). The
supervisor carries a one-bit \emph{turn token}; toggling it is an involution $\tau$ with
$\tau^2=\id$, so $\End(\Sup)=\{\id_\Sup,\tau\}\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The generating handoffs are
\[
  p,\,p\tau:\Sup\to\Wk\quad(\text{dispatch in turn-state $0$ resp.\ $1$}),
  \qquad
  s,\,s_2:\Wk\to\Rt\quad(\text{two worker outcomes}),
\]
with composites $q,q\tau:\Sup\to\Rt$ and right $\mathbb Z/2$-action $p\cdot\tau=p\tau$,
$q\cdot\tau=q\tau$. The prescribed right factor is the supervisor-internal (token) part,
\[
  \D=\{\id_\Sup,\ \tau,\ \id_\Wk,\ \id_\Rt\},
\]
a wide subcategory with vertex groups $G_\Sup\cong\mathbb Z/2$ and $G_\Wk=G_\Rt=0$; the left
factor $\C$ --- the genuine dispatch/handoff part --- is what we solve for. Everything turns on
one composite: how a worker outcome, run after a dispatch, sits relative to the token.

\begin{definition}[The family $\K_\varepsilon$]\label{def:regimes}
For $\varepsilon\in\mathbb Z/2$ let $\K_\varepsilon$ be the resulting category with the single
composite fixed by
\[
  s\circ p=q,\qquad s_2\circ p=q\cdot\tau^{\varepsilon},
\]
where $s$ is ``the worker returns normally'' and $s_2$ is ``the worker re-enters'' (calls back
into the supervisor before returning). Thus $\varepsilon=[\,s_2\circ p=q\tau\,]$ records whether
the re-entrant outcome \emph{mutates} the token ($\varepsilon=1$, \emph{unprotected re-entry}) or
\emph{fixes} it ($\varepsilon=0$, \emph{state-protected re-entry / a lock}). Write
$\K_{\mathrm{ok}}:=\K_0$ and $\K_{\mathrm{bug}}:=\K_1$.
\end{definition}

Each $\K_\varepsilon$ is a genuine category with $\D$ wide (associativity reduces to right
$\tau$-equivariance of the two length-two composites; see \cite{analytic}). The reading is
exact: re-entrancy is the callee mutating shared caller state during a pending call, and here
that is precisely the $s_2$-branch dragging the turn token when $\varepsilon=1$.

\subsection*{The obstruction class equals the token bit}

\begin{theorem}[The obstruction is the token-mutation bit]\label{thm:h2}
For each $\varepsilon\in\mathbb Z/2$, the family $\K_\varepsilon$ with distinguished wide
subcategory $\D$ satisfies condition \textnormal{(L)} and the hypothesis of
Theorem~\ref{thm:T3}, the skeleton $\Sk_\C$ is the branch $\Sup\to\Wk\rightrightarrows\Rt$
(independent of $\varepsilon$) with $H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)\cong\mathbb Z/2$, and
\[
  [\omega(\K_\varepsilon)]=\varepsilon\cdot(\text{generator})\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir).
\]
Consequently the joint agent $\C\bowtie\D$ exists --- equivalently a distributive law
$\lambda:\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ --- \emph{if and only if $\varepsilon=0$, i.e.\ iff the worker's
outcome fixes the supervisor's token.} For $\varepsilon=1$ (unprotected re-entry) the
obstruction is the nonzero generator and no serialization exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(L) and the hypothesis.} The right $\D$-action is precomposition; it is the regular
$\mathbb Z/2$-action on $\Sup$-sourced arrows and trivial elsewhere. Decomposing into orbits,
$\Hom(-,\Wk)=\{p,p\tau\}\sqcup\{\id_\Wk\}$ and
$\Hom(-,\Rt)=\{q,q\tau\}\sqcup\{s\}\sqcup\{s_2\}\sqcup\{\id_\Rt\}$; since $p\neq p\tau$ and
$q\neq q\tau$, every $\Sup$-apex orbit is a free $\mathbb Z/2$-torsor and every other orbit is a
singleton, so each hom-presheaf is free --- and this uses only $p\neq p\tau$, $q\neq q\tau$, so
it is independent of $\varepsilon$ (the value $q\tau^\varepsilon$ stays inside the orbit
$\{q,q\tau\}$ either way). The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:T3} holds because the only
nontrivial vertex group $G_\Sup=\mathbb Z/2$ sits at a \emph{source} (nothing maps into $\Sup$
but its own endomorphisms), so the induced left action is trivial.

\emph{The class.} The orbit category $\Sk_\C$ has non-identity arrows
$[p]:\Sup\to\Wk$, $[q]:\Sup\to\Rt$, $[s],[s_2]:\Wk\to\Rt$ with
$[s]\ast[p]=[s_2]\ast[p]=[q]$ (both because $q,q\tau$ share an orbit) --- the same branch for
both $\varepsilon$. Every restriction map of the coefficient presheaf $\dir$ vanishes (each
generator targets $\Wk$ or $\Rt$, where $G=0$). The normalized complex has
$C^1\cong C^2\cong(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ (coordinates $h([p]),h([q])$ resp.\
$\omega([s],[p]),\omega([s_2],[p])$) and $C^3=0$, so $Z^2=C^2$; the coboundary is
$(\delta^1 h)([s],[p])=(\delta^1 h)([s_2],[p])=h([p])-h([q])$, whence
$B^2=\langle(1,1)\rangle$ (the diagonal) and $H^2\cong\mathbb Z/2$. The natural transversal
$T=\{p,q,s,s_2\}$ has defect $\omega_T([s],[p])=\id_\Sup$ (since $s\circ p=q\circ\id_\Sup$) and
$\omega_T([s_2],[p])=\tau^\varepsilon$ (since $s_2\circ p=q\circ\tau^\varepsilon$), i.e.\
$\omega_T=(0,\varepsilon)$; its class is $0$ for $\varepsilon=0$ and the generator
$(0,1)\notin\langle(1,1)\rangle$ for $\varepsilon=1$. The dichotomy then chains
Theorems~\ref{thm:T2}--\ref{thm:T3}: $\C\bowtie\D$ exists $\iff$ (G) $\iff[\omega]=0\iff
\varepsilon=0$. See \cite{analytic} for the complete verification.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[No enumeration, no machine isomorphism]
The argument is analytic: freeness, the hypothesis, the orbit category, the complex, and the
defect are all computed by hand, and the class is read off uniformly in $\varepsilon$. A
parametrized script cross-checks every step (and, independently of the proof, confirms
$\#\mathrm{SFS}=2,0$ for $\varepsilon=0,1$); the isomorphism of $\K_1$ with the ten-morphism
rigid twist of \cite{gobstr} is now a corollary rather than a load-bearing machine check.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Reading the class]
The two coordinates of $\omega$ are the two worker outcomes; the coboundary diagonal is the
freedom to relabel which token-state is ``canonical.'' The class is nonzero precisely because
the two outcomes disagree on that relabelling by one token-flip --- the re-entrant branch's
side effect. This is the categorical form of ``a flat bundle is trivial iff its holonomy
vanishes'': each partial handoff completes freely (by (L)), but the completions fail to glue
into a global serialized agent by a single $\mathbb Z/2$ monodromy around the dispatch/return
loop. It is the obstruction the Kodamai seed reads in Ethereum re-entrancy, now located as an
explicit $H^2$ generator on a handoff category.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The composing regimes and the table}
Theorem~\ref{thm:h2} is the proved core: the two re-entry rows of the table below are its
$\varepsilon=0,1$ cases (the state-protected regime $\K_0$ carries a $\mathbb Z/2$-torsor of
factorization systems, $[\omega]=0$; the unprotected regime $\K_1$ has none). Two \emph{further}
regimes round out the qualitative picture at the grade of a \emph{computed} illustration, not
part of the proved dichotomy: \emph{independent supervisors} over read-only workers compose by
the trivial (swap) law, $\K=\C\times\D$; and two supervisors over a shared worker pool can
interleave nontrivially yet coherently --- taking $\C=\mathbb Z/3$, $\D=\mathbb Z/2$ with the
toggle reversing the rotation gives the dihedral matched pair $\K=\C\bowtie\D=S_3$, a genuinely
non-abelian joint agent that is neither a product nor obstructed, showing the criterion is
non-vacuous. All four rows are machine-checked.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
shared-resource topology & verdict & invariant \\
\midrule
independent supervisors (read-only workers) & composes & $\K=\C\times\D$ (trivial law) \\
two supervisors, coherent nontrivial interleave & composes & $\K=S_3$, non-abelian join \\
re-entry, state-protected (lock) & composes & $\#\mathrm{SFS}=2$, $[\omega]=0$ \\
re-entry, \textbf{unprotected} (worker mutates state) & \textbf{obstructed} &
$\#\mathrm{SFS}=0$, $[\omega]=\text{gen }\mathbb Z/2$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent The single bit that flips the verdict is whether a worker's outcome mutates the
supervisor's shared state. If it does, the distributive law becomes multivalued, (G) fails, and
the obstruction to a joint agent is the nonzero $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)$.

\section{The degree axis}\label{sec:degree}

The container obstruction is best understood by contrast with how the multi-agent-systems
literature uses cohomology, because the two are complementary and the difference is exactly one
degree.

That literature places a cellular sheaf on the agents' \emph{communication} graph and measures
low-degree obstructions. Global sections --- degree $H^0$ --- are consensus states and, in a
game-theoretic sheaf, Nash equilibria \cite{sheafMAS}. A (nonlinear) sheaf Laplacian in degree
$H^1$ measures identifiability of a multi-agent system \cite{sheafID}, with related
sheaf-theoretic formulations for resilient planning \cite{sheafPlan}. These are \emph{state}
questions on the communication graph: \emph{do the agents currently agree, and can we recover
their parameters?}

The container model asks a prior, \emph{structural} question and finds it one degree up. The
relevant graph is not the communication graph but the \emph{handoff} category $\Sk_\C$ --- who
may delegate to whom --- and the coefficients are not a generic data sheaf but the agents'
own reply functor $\dir$, read off from their container structure. The obstruction lives in
$H^2$, and it answers: \emph{can these agents be fused into one consistent joint agent at
all?} The contrast is summarised in the following table.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & sheaf-Laplacian MAS & container / directed-container \\
\midrule
carrier & communication graph & handoff category $\Sk_\C$ \\
coefficients & data / strategy sheaf & reply functor $\dir$ (from the container) \\
degree & $H^0$ (consensus), $H^1$ (identifiability) & $H^2$ (composability) \\
question & do agents agree / can we identify them? & do agents compose into a joint system? \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two programmes are not in competition; they answer different questions at different
degrees. What is distinctive here is the source of the cohomology: everyone else applies
generic sheaf/cochain machinery to a multi-agent graph, whereas the class $[\omega]$ is
extracted from the agents' \emph{polynomial-functor} structure --- the reply functor is the
coefficient system, and the handoff category is the base. To our knowledge the composability
obstruction has not previously been routed through directed-container structure.

\subsection*{The categorical-orchestration landscape}
The sheaf-Laplacian programme is not the only neighbour. Three further categorical accounts of
agentic composition appeared in 2026, and it is worth locating each, because none reaches for the
structure this note turns on --- a distributive law, a Zappa--Sz\'ep or semidirect product, or an
obstruction to composing at all. Aberl\'e's verified-implementation framework \cite{aberle} composes
systems along wiring diagrams by Kleisli composition, its only two-system construct the unobstructed
parallel sum. Banu's polynomial-functor treatment of agent architectures \cite{banu} composes skills
by an \emph{operad} of wiring diagrams --- parallel and serial composition, with a trace --- and its
author checks explicitly that no distributive law, semidirect product, or Zappa--Sz\'ep structure
appears: it is \emph{parallel skill-assembly within one harness}. Waites's typed ``plumbing''
language \cite{waites} models agent coordination in a symmetric monoidal \emph{copy--discard}
category, with session types compiling functorially onto the plumbing and a \emph{traced}-monoidal
inner/outer feedback loop separating an agent's conversation history from its supervisory control;
its characteristic failure mode is context drift under memory compaction, handled --- tellingly ---
by a pragmatic serialisation gate rather than diagnosed as an obstruction class.

The contrast is sharpest against Waites, and it is instructive that both accounts reach for the word
\emph{serialisation}. Waites installs a serialisation gate as an engineering remedy; the present note
characterises exactly \emph{when serialisation is impossible} --- the unprotected-re-entry regime
$\K_1$, where $[\omega]$ is the nonzero generator and no interleaving into a single serialized agent
exists. Copy--discard symmetric monoidal structure with a mutex and Zappa--Sz\'ep/$\Poly$ structure
with an $H^2$ class are two answers to adjacent questions: how to \emph{wire} agents that share
streams, versus whether two agents that share state can be \emph{fused} into one at all. What unites
these neighbours is the negative: operad, parallel-sum, and copy--discard composition are all
unobstructed by construction, so none can express the composability question that is this note's
whole subject.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
account & composition mechanism & two-interacting-agent obstruction \\
\midrule
Aberl\'e \cite{aberle} & free-monad implementations, wiring diagrams & none (parallel sum only) \\
Banu \cite{banu} & operad of wiring diagrams (parallel/serial, trace) & none (unobstructed operad) \\
Waites \cite{waites} & copy--discard SMC, traced feedback & none (mutex / serialisation gate) \\
this note & distributive law / Zappa--Sz\'ep $\C\bowtie\D$ & $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

Read as containers, agents supply not only an interface vocabulary but a composition theory.
The move that makes this productive is small and precise: composing two shared-resource
orchestrations is not a functor along one topology but a Zappa--Sz\'ep product $\C\bowtie\D$
interleaving two, and a Zappa--Sz\'ep product can fail to exist. When it does, the failure is a
single degree-two cohomology class on the handoff category, and the smallest instance of that
failure is a recognisable engineering hazard --- unprotected re-entrancy, a worker writing to
the supervisor's shared state mid-call --- appearing as the nonzero generator of
$H^2\cong\mathbb Z/2$.

\subsection*{Status, honestly}
The scaffolding is proved: Theorem~\ref{thm:T1} is Ahman--Uustalu (and machine-checked in the
author's Lean development); the Zappa--Sz\'ep-product-from-a-distributive-law construction
underlying Theorem~\ref{thm:T2} is likewise Ahman--Uustalu's \cite{AhmanUustaluDL}, the
author's part being the $\mathrm{(L)}+\mathrm{(G)}$ existence criterion; Theorem~\ref{thm:T3}
is the author's; and its cohomology is classical and cited, not reproved. The interface,
free-monad-implementation, wiring-diagram-composition, and dependent-specification mechanism is
Aberl\'e's, cited and not reinvented. The contribution is the obstruction layer on top: the
worked dichotomy of Section~\ref{sec:worked} (Theorem~\ref{thm:h2}, $[\omega(\K_\varepsilon)]=
\varepsilon\cdot\text{gen}$) is \emph{proved} analytically, with a parametrized script as
cross-check. Two things remain at a lower grade. The two extra table regimes (independent
product, $S_3$ interleave) are \emph{computed} illustrations, not part of the proved dichotomy.
And the claim that a real orchestration framework literally instantiates $\K_\varepsilon$ is a
\emph{computed} reading, not a theorem about deployed systems: the models are minimal faithful
abstractions of the supervisor--worker pattern, not literal encodings of any named framework.

\subsection*{The validation ask}
The natural next step mirrors how compositional structure has been validated empirically
elsewhere at Kodamai (the genetic-algorithm work, where migration-topology cases confirmed that
composition determines dynamics): take one concrete orchestration framework, exhibit its
handoff graph as a directed container $(S\seq P,o,\downarrow,\oplus)$, and exhibit one of its
concurrency patterns as an explicit distributive law $\lambda$ --- turning the starred
dictionary row from computed into demonstrated on a system people actually run.

\subsection*{Open directions}
Two stand out. First, a unifying reason the two cohomological pictures of
Section~\ref{sec:degree} should meet: comonads generalise both directed containers and
cellular sheaves \cite{comonadsspaces}, which suggests the $H^2$ composability obstruction and
the $H^0/H^1$ sheaf obstructions are shadows of a single comonadic invariant --- worth making
precise. Second, the $\infty$-analogue: replacing the handoff category by a higher category of
handoffs and asking whether the obstruction tower extends, which is where a re-entrant tool-call
that is itself an orchestration would live.
