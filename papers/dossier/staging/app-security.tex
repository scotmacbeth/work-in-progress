\chapter{Security audits as failed distributive laws}
\label{ch:security}
Professional smart-contract audits (Trail-of-Bits style) keep surfacing the same
shape of bug: two components, each provably correct in isolation, produce a
global failure when composed. This chapter reads that recurring finding through
the obstruction picture of the blockchain chapter (Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain}):
a \emph{state-divergence} audit finding is a failed distributive law, and the
concrete divergence the auditor exhibits is an $[\omega]\neq0$ witness in
$\Hcoh$. It is a modest extension --- a reframing with predictive bite, not a new
theorem --- and is graded \emph{computed} throughout: the only machine-checked
claim it leans on is the pre-existing re-entrancy obstruction, referenced not
reproved.

%========================================================================
\section{From the re-entrancy weld to the audit desk}
\label{sec:security-frame}

Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} models a contract as a coalgebra for
$p(X)=(O\times X)^{I}$ and identifies re-entrancy with the failure of the weld
between two contracts to admit a coherent distributive law
$\delta\colon S\,T\Rightarrow T\,S$; the obstruction is a class
$[\omega]\in\Hcoh$, and the composite is safe exactly when
$[\omega]=\varepsilon$ (the criterion of Chapter~\ref{ch:crit}, machine-checked
in the re-entrancy corpus). The observation of this chapter is that the same
frame names a \emph{second}, larger family of audit findings that are not
temporal re-entrancy at all: \emph{state divergence}, where two representations
of one logical value drift apart under composition. Trail of Bits described one
such bug in exactly the compositional idiom --- ``two independently-correct
components created a security failure through incorrect interaction'' --- and
that idiom is precisely the laxator-as-obstruction picture.

When two effectful components $S$ and $T$ are composed, the composite $S\circ T$
is a well-behaved structure again only if a distributive law
$\delta\colon S\,T\Rightarrow T\,S$ exists and is coherent. Local correctness of
$S$ and of $T$ says nothing about whether $\delta$ exists. A missing or
non-coherent $\delta$ is not cosmetic: it is the obstruction, and downstream it
is the bug. The audit finding --- a concrete input that drives the two
representations apart --- is a witness that $\delta$ does not exist; in the
$\Hcoh$-graded reading of Chapter~\ref{ch:crit} it is an $[\omega]\neq0$ witness.

\section{State divergence as a missing distributive law}
\label{sec:security-toy}

The clean toy case is the Provenance Blockchain finding (Trail of Bits,
2026-08-25). Two modules each hold a notion of token supply: a \emph{Marker}
module carries a cached \texttt{supply} field, and a \emph{Bank} module carries
the live, authoritative supply. Each is correct on its own terms. An
authorization check compares the two supplies to gate a privileged action; when
both read as $0$ --- a state the composed system permits --- the check
degenerates to \texttt{0 == 0} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{true} and grants
unauthorized mint/withdraw. Two correct components, one trivial bypass under
composition.

Model the shared logical value ``token supply'' as an object $L$, and give each
component a writer/state monad over its own copy:
\[
  S = (-)\times L_{\mathrm{cache}}\quad(\text{Marker's stale view}),
  \qquad
  T = (-)\times L_{\mathrm{live}}\quad(\text{Bank's authoritative view}).
\]
Composing the two modules forms $S\circ T$ and reads a single supply out of it.
The intended coherence --- ``the two views agree on the one value they both
denote'' --- is exactly a distributive law
$\delta\colon S\,T\Rightarrow T\,S$ whose unit square commutes: the value read
through the cache equals the value read through the authoritative store. The
invariant $L_{\mathrm{cache}}=L_{\mathrm{live}}$ is the statement that $\delta$
is well-defined. The bug is that no coherent $\delta$ exists: cache and store are
mutated on different code paths, so the square fails to commute after certain
interleavings, and the two representations diverge. The authorization predicate
$\varphi(L_{\mathrm{cache}},L_{\mathrm{live}})$ was written assuming coherence;
under divergence it is evaluated off its intended domain, where
$L_{\mathrm{cache}}=L_{\mathrm{live}}=0$ makes it fire spuriously. The divergence
\emph{is} the failed distributive law; the \texttt{0 == 0} bypass is what the
missing $\delta$ looks like at the call site.

\begin{remark}[The two readings coincide]\label{rem:security-two-readings}
What a category theorist should see is a non-existent distributive law between
two monads over a shared object $L$; what a security engineer should see is their
own bug --- ``two sources of truth for one value, kept in sync by convention
rather than by construction.'' These are the same statement. The audit's
concrete counterexample input is the $[\omega]\neq0$ witness of
Chapter~\ref{ch:crit}: it certifies that the obstruction class is nonzero by
exhibiting an interleaving on which the coherence square fails.
\end{remark}

The remedy is likewise the categorical move of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain}: make
$\delta$ hold \emph{by construction} rather than by assumption. Reading a single
state variable mutated only at controlled entry points, instead of two separately
maintained representations, forces the two views to coincide on the nose --- it
literally makes the distributive law hold, rather than hoping an invariant is
preserved by convention.

\section{A taxonomy indexed by which law fails}
\label{sec:security-taxonomy}

The value of the frame is that it indexes otherwise-unrelated audit patterns by
\emph{which} categorical law breaks, turning ad hoc pattern-matching into a
checklist. The source note collects three shapes; we record them as the frame's
predictive content, not as proved results.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item \textbf{State divergence} $=$ failed distributive law between two monads:
        two representations of one logical value (two modules; two entry points
        into one component; two channels onto one pot; or a verification channel
        and a settlement channel across a bridge) admit no coherent $\delta$.
        This is the best-populated class, spanning public witnesses from a
        re-entrancy accounting slip to a nine-figure cross-chain bridge exploit
        --- the \emph{same} missing $\delta$, differing only in where the two
        non-commuting channels sit.
  \item \textbf{Temporal / cross-callback misalignment} $=$ re-entrancy with an
        $\Hcoh$ obstruction: the order-of-effects law of a single lax structure
        composed across a callback boundary, safe iff $[\omega]=\varepsilon$.
        This is the case already pinned down formally in
        Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain}; the state-divergence class is its sibling
        under a different broken law.
  \item \textbf{Self-composition via iteration} $=$ interchange/associativity
        failure of an iterate $T\circ T\circ\cdots$: a single locally-correct
        operation composed with itself in a loop, where a per-step check does not
        survive the fold. This is the security-domain shadow of the
        self-plethysm phenomenon of the change-of-base chapter --- composition
        survives self-composition only when flatness was present to begin with.
\end{enumerate}

The frame supplies the auditor a hunt list indexed by structure: two sources of
truth for one value (look for the distributive law); a callback boundary (look
for the $\Hcoh$ class); a loop over an externally-observable operation (look for
the non-flat iterate). For the temporal case the criterion is already sharp and
machine-checked ($[\omega]=\varepsilon$); the other two shapes are, at present,
structural descriptions awaiting the same treatment.

\section{Status and honest scope}
\label{sec:security-status}

This chapter is a \emph{stub} extending the blockchain obstruction picture, not a
standalone proved result. No new theorem is established here. The one formally
verified claim invoked is the pre-existing re-entrancy obstruction of
Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} ($[\omega]=\varepsilon$ in $\Hcoh$, machine-checked);
the categorical models of state divergence and of the iteration case are
structural descriptions, and the exploit names, dates, and figures are taken from
public audit sources as recorded in the author's research notes. The grade is
\emph{computed}: a genuine but modest reframing whose bite is unification and
prediction --- one categorical defect, $\delta$ absent, whose blast radius across
the public witnesses spans roughly two orders of magnitude of loss --- rather
than a certificate the chapter itself discharges. The natural next step, and the
reason the stub earns its place in the dossier, is to lift the state-divergence
and iteration shapes to the same checkable $\Hcoh$-graded criterion that
Chapter~\ref{ch:crit} already gives for the temporal case.
