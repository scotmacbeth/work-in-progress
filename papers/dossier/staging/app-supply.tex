\chapter{Supply chains}
\label{ch:supply}

This chapter runs the dossier's spine in the supply-chain domain and, in doing
so, sharpens it. A supply chain, abstracted to a small category, is a directed
container (Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}); welding a supplier flow to a consumer flow
that share a warehouse is a Zappa--Sz\'ep composition $\C\bowtie\D$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:zs}); and an inventory inconsistency between two routes to the
same delivered good is a nonzero obstruction class
$[\omega]\in\Hcoh(\mathrm{Sk}_\C;\Z/n)\iso\Z/n$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:crit}). The
headline is a dictionary --- the same one Chapters~\ref{ch:ga}
and~\ref{ch:blockchain} draw for evolutionary systems and for contracts --- but
here it comes with an extra digit. Because inventory bookkeeping is a
\emph{counter} rather than a bit, the obstruction lands not in $\Z/2$ but in
$\Z/n$, and the class does not merely flag a mismatch: it \emph{measures} it, in
units. The re-entrancy parity bit of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} is the $n=2$
face of the invariant computed here.

\paragraph{Grade discipline.} Nothing in this chapter is new mathematics, and
the chapter is scrupulous about saying so. The general theorems it stands on are
cited or cross-referenced at their established grades and are never re-graded
here: that a small category is a directed container is \emph{lean-verified}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}); the pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion is \emph{proved}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:zs-criterion}); the identification of the global-closure
condition $(\mathrm{G})$ with the cohomological class $[\omega]$ is \emph{proved}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:crit-g-is-omega}); the $n=2$ anchor is \emph{proved}
(Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain}). What is new is the \emph{domain instantiation} ---
an explicit object-level directed container and an explicit warehouse family in
which this machinery computes the inventory-inconsistency invariant --- and it is
graded \emph{computed} throughout, machine-checked in
\texttt{scratch/supply\_chain\_zs.py} over eight $(n,\varepsilon)$ instances. The
one claim held \emph{open} is object-level fidelity: whether a \emph{real} supply
chain literally presents a category. We build a faithful minimal abstraction and
apply the theorems to \emph{it}; we never assert that a supply chain \emph{is}
the category below (Section~\ref{sec:supply-status}).

\section{The base chain as a directed container}
\label{sec:supply-base}

The first dividend of the equivalence chain is definitional: once a chain is
abstracted to a small category, it is automatically a directed container, so the
entire structure theory of Part~I --- the monoidal products, the (co)monoid
classifications, the composition operators --- applies to it verbatim. We make
this concrete on the smallest honest example.

\begin{definition}[The procurement chain $\C_0$]
\label{def:supply-base}
Let $\C_0$ be the poset category on stages
$\mathsf{Src}<\mathsf{Mfg}<\mathsf{Dst}$ (supplier, manufacturing, delivery):
objects are the three stages, and there is a unique morphism $i\to j$ whenever
$i\le j$, read as the \emph{net operation} carrying material from stage $i$ to
stage $j$. Composition is do-then-do; thinness encodes the modelling assumption
that all order-respecting routings from $i$ to $j$ deliver the same material.
\end{definition}

By the equivalence chain (Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}; Ahman--Uustalu
\cite{AU16}), $\C_0$ is a directed container $(S,P,o,{\downarrow},\oplus)$ under
the standard dictionary
\[
\begin{aligned}
S &= \{\mathsf{Src},\mathsf{Mfg},\mathsf{Dst}\} && \text{(shapes $=$ stages)},\\
P(s) &= \{\,(s\to j):j\ge s\,\} && \text{(positions $=$ outgoing operations)},\\
o_s &= \id_s && \text{(root $=$ stay put)},\\
s\!\downarrow\! p &= \cod(p) && \text{(sub-shape $=$ destination stage)},\\
p\oplus q &= q\circ p && \text{(shift $=$ do $p$ then $q$)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Explicitly $P(\mathsf{Src})=\{\id,\ \mathsf{Src}\!\to\!\mathsf{Mfg},\
\mathsf{Src}\!\to\!\mathsf{Dst}\}$,
$P(\mathsf{Mfg})=\{\id,\ \mathsf{Mfg}\!\to\!\mathsf{Dst}\}$,
$P(\mathsf{Dst})=\{\id\}$, and, for instance,
$(\mathsf{Src}\!\to\!\mathsf{Mfg})\oplus(\mathsf{Mfg}\!\to\!\mathsf{Dst})
=(\mathsf{Src}\!\to\!\mathsf{Dst})$: procure-then-ship \emph{is} the direct
delivery operation.

\begin{proposition}[Directed-container laws; \emph{computed}]
\label{prop:supply-dlaws}
$(S,P,o,{\downarrow},\oplus)$ satisfies the five directed-container laws:
\textnormal{D1} $s\!\downarrow\! o_s=s$; \textnormal{D2} $o_s\oplus p=p$;
\textnormal{D3} $p\oplus o_{s\downarrow p}=p$;
\textnormal{D4} $s\!\downarrow\!(p\oplus q)=(s\!\downarrow\! p)\!\downarrow\! q$;
\textnormal{D5} $(p\oplus q)\oplus r=p\oplus(q\oplus r)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\C_0$ is a thin poset category, so D1--D3 are the unit laws (the identities are
the operations $(s\to s)$), D4 says the destination of a composite is the
destination of its second leg, and D5 is associativity of composition --- all
automatic in a poset. Verified exhaustively over the composable triples in
\texttt{supply\_chain\_zs.py}, part~(A).
\end{proof}

This is the object-level content that the category-level orchestration analysis
of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} left implicit: a supply chain, once abstracted to
a category, is literally a $\tri$-comonoid / directed container. The value of the
observation is not the (routine) proof but the transfer --- every result of
Part~I is now a statement about $\C_0$.

\section{Two flows sharing a warehouse}
\label{sec:supply-warehouse}

The composition story lives one level up, where two flows meet. The shared node
is a warehouse $\mathsf{Wh}$ that keeps an internal \emph{lot-cursor}: a cyclic
pointer $\tau$ of order $n$ into $n$ interchangeable bins (a FIFO position
$\bmod\,n$, or a bin index). Physically $\tau$ moves nothing --- it is pure
bookkeeping --- but a unit leaving the warehouse carries whatever cursor value
was current, and advancing the cursor is an automorphism with $\tau^n=\id$, so
$\End(\mathsf{Wh})\iso\Z/n$.

\begin{definition}[The warehouse family $\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$]
\label{def:supply-Wne}
For $n\ge1$ and $\varepsilon\in\Z/n$, let $\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$ be the
small category with objects $\mathsf{Wh}$ (warehouse), $\mathsf{Pr}$
(processing), $\mathsf{De}$ (delivery), and
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\End(\mathsf{Wh})=\{\tau^k:k\in\Z/n\}\iso\Z/n,\quad
  \End(\mathsf{Pr})=\{\id\},\quad \End(\mathsf{De})=\{\id\},\\
&\Hom(\mathsf{Wh},\mathsf{Pr})=\{p\,\tau^k:k\in\Z/n\},\quad
 \Hom(\mathsf{Wh},\mathsf{De})=\{q\,\tau^k:k\in\Z/n\},\quad
 \Hom(\mathsf{Pr},\mathsf{De})=\{s,\ s_2\},
\end{aligned}
\]
with no arrows into $\mathsf{Wh}$ except its endomorphisms and no arrows out of
$\mathsf{De}$ except $\id$ (so the underlying graph is acyclic:
$\mathsf{Wh}\to\mathsf{Pr}\to\mathsf{De}$ with a direct $\mathsf{Wh}\to\mathsf{De}$).
Here $p\,\tau^k:=p\circ\tau^k$, all distinct; the two processing operations
compose through the middle by
\[
   s\circ p=q,\qquad s_2\circ p=q\cdot\tau^{\varepsilon},
\]
extended by right $\tau$-equivariance ($s\circ p\tau^k=q\tau^k$,
$s_2\circ p\tau^k=q\,\tau^{k+\varepsilon}$).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Reading the data.} $p$ dispatches a lot to processing; $q$ is the
direct, expected delivery recorded by the warehouse's books; $s$ is the nominal
production-and-ship line, and $s_2$ is an alternative route --- rework, a second
line, an expedited lane --- that delivers the \emph{same good}. The single datum
$\varepsilon$ records how many lot-positions $s_2$ advances the provenance cursor
relative to the nominal route: $\varepsilon=0$ means the alternative preserves
lot-provenance, $\varepsilon\ne0$ means it shifts it by $\varepsilon$ units. The
wide right factor of bookkeeping moves is
\[
   \D=\{\tau^k:k\in\Z/n\}\cup\{\id_{\mathsf{Pr}},\id_{\mathsf{De}}\}
   \subseteq\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon},
\]
the warehouse-internal relabelling subcategory --- the \emph{second} chain that
shares the warehouse with the physical-flow chain.

\begin{lemma}[$\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$ is a category; \emph{computed}]
\label{lem:supply-cat}
For every $n,\varepsilon$, $\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$ is a well-defined small
category.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only two families of composites need checking: the $\Z/n$-action of $\tau$ on
$\mathsf{Wh}$-sourced arrows (a group action, $\tau^n=\id$), and the two
length-two composites $s\circ p,\,s_2\circ p:\mathsf{Wh}\to\mathsf{De}$. All
longer composites either factor through the sink $\mathsf{De}$ or begin with a
$\mathsf{Wh}$-endomorphism, so associativity reduces to right-equivariance,
$(f\circ p)\circ\tau^k=f\circ(p\,\tau^k)$, which holds by the definition of
$f\circ p\tau^k$. The full associativity table is checked for eight
$(n,\varepsilon)$ pairs in \texttt{supply\_chain\_zs.py}, part~(B).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$(\mathrm{L})$ and the abelian-vertex hypothesis, uniformly in
$\varepsilon$; \emph{computed}]
\label{lem:supply-LH}
Every hom-presheaf $\Hom_{\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}}(-,b)$ is a free right
$\D$-set, and $(\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon},\D)$ meets the hypothesis under which
$(\mathrm{G})$ is cohomological (abelian vertex groups, trivial left action).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$(\mathrm{L})$.} The right $\D$-action is precomposition; it is trivial on
$\mathsf{Pr}$- and $\mathsf{De}$-sourced arrows and is the regular $\Z/n$-action
on $\mathsf{Wh}$-sourced arrows. Decomposing into orbits,
$\Hom(-,\mathsf{Wh})=\{\tau^k\}$ (one regular $\Z/n$-orbit),
$\Hom(-,\mathsf{Pr})=\{p\,\tau^k\}\sqcup\{\id_{\mathsf{Pr}}\}$, and
$\Hom(-,\mathsf{De})=\{q\,\tau^k\}\sqcup\{s\}\sqcup\{s_2\}\sqcup\{\id_{\mathsf{De}}\}$.
Each $\mathsf{Wh}$-apex orbit is a free $\Z/n$-torsor (the $\tau^k$ are
distinct); each other orbit is a singleton with trivial group. So every
hom-presheaf is a coproduct of representables, i.e.\ free. This uses only
distinctness of the $p\tau^k$ and $q\tau^k$ and is therefore independent of
$\varepsilon$: moving $s_2\circ p$ inside the orbit $\{q\tau^k\}$ does not change
the orbit decomposition. \emph{Abelian vertices.} $\D$ is a disjoint union of
abelian vertex groups $G_{\mathsf{Wh}}\iso\Z/n$,
$G_{\mathsf{Pr}}=G_{\mathsf{De}}=0$; the only nontrivial vertex group sits at
$\mathsf{Wh}$, a \emph{source} (no generator targets it), so the left action by a
target automorphism is trivial. Freeness of every orbit is machine-checked in
part~(B).
\end{proof}

With $(\mathrm{L})$ and the abelian-vertex hypothesis in hand, the criterion of
Chapter~\ref{ch:crit} applies and the obstruction is the class of an explicit
$2$-cocycle on the orbit category. We compute both.

\begin{proposition}[Orbit category, complex, and $\Hcoh\iso\Z/n$; \emph{computed}]
\label{prop:supply-h2}
The orbit category $\mathrm{Sk}_\C$ has objects $\mathsf{Wh},\mathsf{Pr},
\mathsf{De}$ and non-identity generators $[p]:\mathsf{Wh}\to\mathsf{Pr}$,
$[q]:\mathsf{Wh}\to\mathsf{De}$, $[s],[s_2]:\mathsf{Pr}\to\mathsf{De}$, with
$[s]\ast[p]=[s_2]\ast[p]=[q]$ \emph{independently of $\varepsilon$} (because $q$
and $q\tau^\varepsilon$ lie in one orbit). The coefficient presheaf has
$\D(\mathsf{Wh})=\Z/n$, $\D(\mathsf{Pr})=\D(\mathsf{De})=0$, with all restriction
maps zero (no generator targets $\mathsf{Wh}$). Hence $C^1\iso(\Z/n)^2$
(coordinates $h[p],h[q]$), $C^2\iso(\Z/n)^2$ (coordinates $\omega([s],[p]),
\omega([s_2],[p])$), $C^3=0$, and
\[
   (\delta^1 h)([s],[p])=(\delta^1 h)([s_2],[p])=h[p]-h[q],
\]
so $B^2=\{(t,t):t\in\Z/n\}$ is the diagonal and
\[
   \Hcoh(\mathrm{Sk}_\C;\D)=(\Z/n)^2/\langle(1,1)\rangle\iso\Z/n,
   \qquad [(a,b)]\mapsto b-a.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the bookkeeping of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} with $\Z/n$ in place of
$\Z/2$: the generators, the vanishing restriction maps, and the two composable
pairs $([s],[p])$, $([s_2],[p])$ are as there, and the Baues--Wirsching
coboundary formula (Rosebrugh--Wood \cite{rw}) reduces to $h[p]-h[q]$ because the
coefficient map along $[p]$ is the zero map $0\to\Z/n$. The identification
$B^2=$ diagonal is machine-checked for all tested $n$ in part~(B).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[The class is the unit-mismatch; \emph{computed}]
\label{thm:supply-class}
With the natural transversal $T=\{p,q,s,s_2\}$, the defect $2$-cocycle is
$\omega_T=(0,\varepsilon)\in(\Z/n)^2$, so
\[
   [\omega(\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon})]=\varepsilon
   \in \Hcoh(\mathrm{Sk}_\C;\D)\iso\Z/n .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both composable pairs have target orbit $\operatorname{orb}(q)$ with chosen
generator $q$. From $s\circ p=q=q\circ\id_{\mathsf{Wh}}$ we read
$\omega_T([s],[p])=0$; from $s_2\circ p=q\cdot\tau^\varepsilon=q\circ\tau^\varepsilon$
we read $\omega_T([s_2],[p])=\tau^\varepsilon=\varepsilon$. So
$\omega_T=(0,\varepsilon)$, and its class under $[(a,b)]\mapsto b-a$ is
$\varepsilon$. Both $\omega_T=(0,\varepsilon)$ and $[\omega]=\varepsilon$ are
machine-verified in part~(B).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Inventory-consistency dichotomy; \emph{computed}]
\label{cor:supply-dichotomy}
For the warehouse abstraction $\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$ with bookkeeping right
factor $\D$,
\[
   \mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}=\C\bowtie\D \ \text{ exists }
   \iff \varepsilon=0
   \iff \text{both routes to $\mathsf{De}$ agree on lot-provenance.}
\]
When $\varepsilon\ne0$ the obstruction $[\omega]=\varepsilon$ is a nonzero
element of $\Hcoh\iso\Z/n$: a genuine inventory inconsistency in which the two
routes to the same delivered good disagree by $\varepsilon$ lot-positions, and no
serialisation of the physical flow with the warehouse bookkeeping exists.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$(\mathrm{L})$ holds (Lemma~\ref{lem:supply-LH}), so by the pairwise criterion
(Theorem~\ref{thm:zs-criterion}) the product exists iff $(\mathrm{G})$; the
abelian-vertex hypothesis holds, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:crit-g-is-omega}
$(\mathrm{G})$ holds iff $[\omega]=0$; and $[\omega]=\varepsilon$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:supply-class}. Chaining the equivalences gives the statement. As
an independent, non-cohomological cross-check, part~(B) brute-forces the strict
factorisation systems and finds $\#\mathrm{SFS}=n$ when $\varepsilon=0$ (a
$\Z/n$-torsor of ways to choose the canonical lot-labelling) and
$\#\mathrm{SFS}=0$ when $\varepsilon\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Why $\Z/n$, and not just a parity bit}
\label{sec:supply-zn}

The re-entrancy analysis of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} needed only $\Z/2$: a
turn token is one bit, and safety is a parity. Inventory is different in kind. It
is a \emph{counter}, and the obstruction lands in the counter's group. The class
$[\omega]=\varepsilon\in\Z/n$ is therefore not the yes/no verdict ``the routes
are inconsistent'' but the quantity ``the routes disagree by $\varepsilon$
lot-positions.'' The obstruction is graduated, and the grading is the unit count
of the discrepancy. The proved parity theorem of Chapter~\ref{ch:blockchain} is
exactly the $n=2$ face.

\begin{remark}[Gauge freedom is the choice of slot zero]
\label{rem:supply-gauge}
The coboundary lattice $B^2=\langle(1,1)\rangle$ has a clean operational reading.
A coboundary shifts \emph{both} routes' recorded provenance by the same amount;
that is precisely the freedom to declare which physical lot-slot counts as ``slot
zero.'' Relabelling the origin of the cursor cannot create or destroy a real
inconsistency, so only the \emph{difference} $\varepsilon$ between the two routes
survives to the quotient. This is the categorical avatar of a flat
$\Z/n$-bundle: it is trivial iff its holonomy --- here the provenance drift
around the two-route loop --- vanishes, which recovers
Remark~\ref{rem:crit-holonomy}'s reading of $[\omega]$ as a holonomy.
\end{remark}

\section{Olog merges: the same machine at $n=2$}
\label{sec:supply-olog}

The abstraction is not special to logistics. The same family serves
ontology and knowledge-graph merges, where it recovers the merge-conflict
phenomenon as the $n=2$ instance of the same obstruction. An olog in Spivak's
sense --- say $\mathsf{Book}\xrightarrow{\text{has}}\mathsf{Author}
\xrightarrow{\text{has}}\mathsf{Name}$ --- is the free category on its schema
graph, hence (Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}) a directed container: shapes are types,
positions out of a type are the aspects available from it, $\oplus$ is aspect
composition, and the path-equations are the schema's facts (the
directed-container laws are checked in part~(C)).

Merging that olog with a second one sharing the type $\mathsf{Author}$ --- for
instance $\mathsf{Author}\xrightarrow{\text{has}}\mathsf{Affiliation}$ --- is the
composition of two directed containers over a shared object, a Zappa--Sz\'ep
problem. Take the shared type $\mathsf{Author}$ to carry a $\Z/2$ token recording
the naming convention (``Last, First'' versus ``First Last''), with
$\mathsf{Pr}$ the name-record and $\mathsf{De}$ the rendered display. Then
Corollary~\ref{cor:supply-dichotomy} at $n=2$ reads: the merge succeeds --- the
product $\C\bowtie\D$ exists --- iff the two schemas agree on the convention
($\varepsilon=0$); a disagreement is the nonzero class $[\omega]=1$, a
\emph{merge conflict}. It is the same $\Hcoh$ bit as supply-chain inconsistency
and agent re-entrancy, computed by the same machine, and it is checked in
part~(C).

\section{Scope and honesty}
\label{sec:supply-status}

\paragraph{The computed table.} Across the eight instances verified in
\texttt{scratch/supply\_chain\_zs.py}, the invariant behaves as follows.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
regime & $[\omega]\in\Z/n$ & outcome & meaning \\
\midrule
$\varepsilon=0$ (any $n$) & $0$ & composes, $\#\mathrm{SFS}=n$ & routes agree on provenance \\
$\varepsilon\ne0$ (any $n$) & $\varepsilon$ & obstructed, $\#\mathrm{SFS}=0$ & inventory off by $\varepsilon$ units \\
$n=2,\ \varepsilon=1$ & $1$ & obstructed & $=$ orchestration re-entrancy bit (Ch.~\ref{ch:blockchain}) \\
olog, $n=2$ & $0$ / $1$ & merges / conflicts & schema conventions agree / clash \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each row checks the category axioms, condition $(\mathrm{L})$, the
$\varepsilon$-independence of $\mathrm{Sk}_\C$, the diagonal $B^2$, the cocycle
$\omega_T=(0,\varepsilon)$, the class $[\omega]=\varepsilon$, and the
$\#\mathrm{SFS}$ cross-check.

\paragraph{Proved versus computed.} The general theorems this chapter invokes ---
$\DCont\simeq\Cat$ (lean-verified), the pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion
(proved), and $(\mathrm{G})\Leftrightarrow[\omega]=0$ (proved) --- are cited at
their established grades and are not re-derived here. The domain instantiation ---
that this abstraction of supply chains and olog merges computes the
inventory-inconsistency and merge-conflict class --- is \emph{computed}: an
explicit by-hand and Python calculation, not a new theorem.

\paragraph{The one open claim.} Whether a \emph{real} supply chain genuinely
presents a category --- operations composing associatively on the nose, the
inventory-consistency path-equations holding exactly --- is a modelling question,
SEED Open Question~4, and it stays \emph{open}. Everything above is a chosen
minimal abstraction: the theorems apply to \emph{it}, and the value is that they
compute the right qualitative --- and, in the $\Z/n$ refinement, quantitative ---
invariant. We do not write ``a supply chain \emph{is} $\mathcal W_{n,\varepsilon}$.''
Earning object-level fidelity is a separate theorem, not discharged here.

\paragraph{Grant line.} Supply-chain composability, ontology-merge consistency,
and agent-orchestration re-entrancy are the \emph{same} degree-two obstruction on
the handoff category; in the supply-chain instance the class is refined from a
bit to a $\Z/n$-valued unit count of the discrepancy. This is the honest shape of
the Impact story: the applications are not new mathematics but new
\emph{instances of one obstruction theorem} --- and, occasionally, as here, a
sharpening of what that one theorem can measure.
