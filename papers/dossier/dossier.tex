\section{The organizing equation}

The claim that unifies this programme is that the composition of heterogeneous
computational systems is governed by a single equation, and that the equation
is \emph{checkable}:
\[
   \boxed{\ \text{a composite is correct}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
   (\mathrm{L}) \ \wedge\ [\omega]=0\in \Hcoh\ }
\]
The two conjuncts are independent and have different characters.

\paragraph{$(\mathrm{L})$ --- the local condition.}
Each part of the system is a \emph{directed container} --- equivalently, a small
category, equivalently a polynomial comonoid. Condition $(\mathrm{L})$ says that
each part composes coherently \emph{with itself}: its identities and its
associative composition are the counit and comultiplication of a comonoid
structure. This is the content of the equivalence chain
\[
   \Cont \ \simeq\ \DCont \ \simeq\ \text{(polynomial comonoids)}\ \simeq\ \Cat,
\]
which four independent lines of work reach from four directions. $(\mathrm{L})$
is a condition on the pieces, in isolation.

\paragraph{$[\omega]=0$ --- the global condition.}
Given local pieces, the question is whether they \emph{weld} into a single
coherent whole. The weld is a Zappa--Sz\'ep product, equivalently a distributive
law between the pieces' comonads/monads. Such a weld exists precisely when a
degree-two cohomology class $[\omega]\in \Hcoh$ vanishes. Where $(\mathrm{L})$
is about each part alone, $[\omega]=0$ is about their interaction: it is the
obstruction to gluing.

Everything in the programme is one of three things: a \emph{proof} that this
equation is the right one and holds where claimed; an \emph{application} in
which a real system's failure mode is exactly $[\omega]\neq 0$; or a
\emph{formalisation} that makes the check machine-executable.

\section{Why this is a foundation and not a slogan}

The equation is not imposed; it is discovered at the meeting point of three
independent research programmes. Container theory (Nottingham:
Abbott--Altenkirch--Ghani), directed containers (Tallinn: Ahman--Uustalu), and
the $\Poly$ programme (Topos Institute: Spivak) developed substantially the same
mathematics in three dialects, and all three converge on the equivalence chain
that furnishes $(\mathrm{L})$. Convergence is the evidence: when three groups
arrive at the same object from three directions, the object is real, not an
artifact of notation.

What the present programme adds is the \emph{composition} question none of the
three answered head-on: when do two of these objects compose, and what
obstructs it? The answer is the second conjunct. The pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep
criterion decomposes composability into a local factor $(\mathrm{L})$ and a
global factor $(\mathrm{G})$, and $(\mathrm{G})\Leftrightarrow[\omega]=0\in
\Hcoh$. That identification --- composability as the vanishing of a concrete
cohomological obstruction --- is the thesis of the dossier.

\section{The four movements}

The document proceeds in four movements, ordered so that each pillar earns the
next.

\begin{description}
\item[I. Theory --- the equation and its domain of validity.]
The equivalence chain (the $(\mathrm{L})$ side); Zappa--Sz\'ep products and
distributive laws as \emph{the} composition tool, giving the pairwise criterion
$(\mathrm{L})\wedge(\mathrm{G})$ with $(\mathrm{G})\Leftrightarrow[\omega]=0$;
the $\Hcoh$-cluster of three genuinely distinct obstruction theories on
$\DCont\simeq\Cat$; the change-of-base / $\Fam(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})$
generality question, with the sharp negative that the self-enriched
full-and-faithful semantics collapses to $\{\mathrm{Id}\}$; and, at the
frontier, the set-theoretic boundary of the equation's domain --- the
Vec-admissibility / Gap-S arc, where the composability boundary of a categorical
semantics turns out to be a cardinal characteristic of the continuum. Theory
ends on the live edge, not a solved one.

\item[{II. Applications --- where $[\omega]\neq 0$ costs money.}]
The same mathematics across five domains: genetic-algorithm island-model
composition, blockchain re-entrancy as a failed distributive law, security
audits as failed distributive laws, agent orchestration (the harness as a
worker), and tax computation. Real systems fail exactly when the weld
obstruction is nonzero.

\item[III. Formalisation --- the check is executable.]
The Lean milestones and the sorry-free corpus: what is machine-verified versus
proved on paper, stated honestly. This is what distinguishes the grant from
hand-waving.

\item[IV. Impact --- and a note on honesty.]
Where compositional correctness has economic consequences, and the
AI\,+\,Lean-pipeline honesty methodology: the discipline of separating
``verified'' from ``correctly stated'' and ``reusable,'' which is the
programme's real differentiator.
\end{description}

\chapter{The Honest Ledger}
%==============================================================================

This chapter is deliberately near the front, not buried in an appendix. A
dossier that claims a unifying equation must be equally clear about where the
equation is unproved, where the applications lack artifacts, and what has and
has not been externally validated. Four items:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Gap-S / Vec-admissibility wall is OPEN.}
Whether $\VecC$ is $\lhd$-admissible reduces to a projectivity question over the
full linear ring $S=\mathrm{End}_k(E)$, and the module $M$ in question has
generation number $\mu_S(M)=\dd$, the dominating number. We have proved the
conjecture (Conj.~6.2) is \emph{false} under $\mathrm{CH}$, under $\mathrm{MA}$,
and in the Cohen model, via cardinal counting ($M$ projective
$\Rightarrow 2^{\dd}=2^{\aleph_0}$, so any model with
$2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\dd}$ refutes it). But the \emph{full ZFC status is unproved}:
the verdict currently leans \emph{independent}, and the residual case is the
random-real model ($\dd=\aleph_1<\cont$). We do not yet know whether $M$ is
projective there. This is the programme's deepest open problem, and it is open.

\item \textbf{Tax computation has NO formal artifact.}
The Catala / tax-computation application (\S II.3) is currently a design sketch
--- ontology DAG, scope composition, comonadic event-sourced projections --- with
no proof, no Lean, and no running code. It is framed throughout as
illustrative-only.

\item \textbf{Equivalence-chain milestones M5--M6 are not proved --- not even on paper.}
Milestones M1--M4 (with converses M2b, M3b) are lean-verified. M5 and M6 are
\emph{neither} formalised \emph{nor} proved on paper: the registry grades M5
(enriched $\DCont=\mathbf{Cof}$) \emph{unclassified}, recording that
Clarke--Di~Meglio state the enriched statement \emph{open}, and grades M6
($\infty$-$\DCont=\Cat$, Kan~Chen's Conjecture~7.2) \emph{speculative}. So the
formalised chain is exactly the non-enriched, $1$-categorical core M1--M4; the
enriched and $\infty$-categorical extensions are frontier, not ledger. Any claim
that ``the equivalence programme is fully formalised'' must be read as scoped to
M1--M4 (see Chapter~\ref{ch:milestones}).

\item \textbf{Nothing is externally published.}
Under the ACM authorship rule an AI agent cannot be sole author, and the
external submission queue is held by Robin. The strongest current grade anywhere
in this dossier is \emph{peer-reviewed}, meaning refereed internally by Rick (a
fellow agent) --- e.g.\ the holonomy-composition / Zappa--Sz\'ep bridge and the
$m$-containers result. No claim here is \emph{published} in the external sense.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent A fifth, smaller honesty note carried from the Theory pillar: the
conjectured \emph{protomodular unifier} of the three $\Hcoh$ obstruction
theories was \emph{refuted}. The three theories are a genuinely distinct
structured family, not one theorem wearing three hats.

\chapter{Preliminaries: the machinery}
\label{ch:prelim}

This chapter fixes notation and recalls the standard tools the rest of the
dossier leans on, in one place, so that the later chapters can move without
pausing to reintroduce them. \emph{Almost everything here is standard} and is

makes the later theorems computable. Two facts of Ghani's are foregrounded because
the composition machinery uses them as black boxes: that the container-extension
functor \emph{is} a left Kan extension along the representable embedding
(\S\ref{sec:prelim-density}), and that the closed structure of a Day category is
\emph{forced on representables and extended by density} (\S\ref{sec:prelim-day}).

The reader who wants a map: \S\ref{sec:prelim-yoneda}--\ref{sec:prelim-density}
(representables, Yoneda, density, the extension functor) are used everywhere the
$(\mathrm{L})$ side appears --- the equivalence chain of
Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}, and, in an essential and less obvious way, the module
reduction of the set-theoretic frontier (Chapter~\ref{ch:frontier}), where
$\Nat(F,-)\iso\Hom_S(F(E),-)$ is Yoneda in disguise. Section~\ref{sec:prelim-dcont}
fixes the directed-container and composition ($\tri$) notation carried by the
Zappa--Sz\'ep chapters. Section~\ref{sec:prelim-day} (Day convolution) supports
the change-of-base chapter (Chapter~\ref{ch:cob}). Section~\ref{sec:prelim-coh}
fixes the cohomological conventions --- $\Hcoh$, the class $[\omega]$, the
Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre transgression --- on which the correctness criterion and
the $\Hcoh$-cluster of Chapter~\ref{ch:cluster} stand.

\paragraph{Conventions.}
$\Set$ is the category of sets and functions. For a locally small category
$\mathcal C$ we write $\mathcal C(a,b)$ for its hom-set; $\ob\,\mathcal C$ for its
objects. Functors are covariant unless a superscript $\op$ says otherwise. A
\emph{presheaf} on $\mathcal C$ is a functor $\mathcal C\op\to\Set$; a
\emph{copresheaf} is a functor $\mathcal C\to\Set$. We write $\iso$ for canonical
(natural) isomorphism and are careful to say \emph{which} isomorphism when it
matters.

\section{Representable functors and the Yoneda lemma}
\label{sec:prelim-yoneda}
% =====================================================================

Every construction in the dossier eventually reduces to a hom-set. The
representable functors are the functors that \emph{are} a hom-set with one
argument held fixed, and they generate everything else.

\begin{definition}[Representable copresheaf]
\label{def:prelim-rep}
Fix a set $A$. The \emph{covariant representable} at $A$ is
\[
  \yA \;:=\; \Set(A,-)\;:\;\Set \longrightarrow \Set,
  \qquad
  \yA(X) = \Set(A,X) = X^{A},
\]
sending $f\colon X\to Y$ to post-composition $\yA(f)=f\circ(-)$. A copresheaf
$F\colon\Set\to\Set$ is \emph{representable} if $F\iso\yA$ for some $A$, the
\emph{representing object}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The container reading]
\label{rem:prelim-containerrep}
A \emph{container} is a pair $(S,P)$ of a set $S$ of \emph{shapes} and a family
$P\colon S\to\Set$ of \emph{position sets}, with \emph{extension}
\[
  \den{S,P}\;:\;\Set\to\Set,
  \qquad
  \den{S,P}(X) \;=\; \sum_{s\in S}\Set\!\bigl(P(s),X\bigr).
\]
The \emph{representable container} has a single shape and $A$ positions,
$(S,P)=(1,A)$, and $\den{1,A}(X)=\Set(A,X)=\yA(X)$. So representable copresheaves
are exactly the extensions of single-shape containers, and the coproduct in the
general formula is a coproduct \emph{of representables} indexed by shapes. This is
the whole story, phrased below first as a Kan extension
(\S\ref{sec:prelim-density}) and then as a Day convolution
(\S\ref{sec:prelim-day}). The same extension formula, over a base $\mathcal C$
other than $\Set$, is the object of study of Chapter~\ref{ch:frontier}.
\end{remark}

Definition~\ref{def:prelim-rep} is uniform in $A$: a function $a\colon A'\to A$
induces, by \emph{pre}-composition, a natural transformation $\yA\Rightarrow
y^{A'}$. Thus $A\mapsto\yA$ is a functor $\yy\colon\Set\op\to[\Set,\Set]$, the
\emph{Yoneda embedding}. It is genuinely an embedding, as the Yoneda lemma shows.

\begin{theorem}[Yoneda lemma; standard]
\label{thm:prelim-yoneda}
For any functor $F\colon\Set\to\Set$ and any set $A$ there is a bijection
\[
  \Theta_{F,A}\;:\;\Nat(\yA,\,F)\;\xrightarrow{\ \iso\ }\;F(A),
  \qquad \Theta(\alpha)=\alpha_{A}(\id_{A}),
\]
natural in $F$ and $A$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The inverse sends $x\in F(A)$ to $\Psi(x)$ with $\Psi(x)_{X}(g)=F(g)(x)$ for
$g\colon A\to X$; naturality of $\Psi(x)$ is functoriality,
$F(h)(F(g)(x))=F(h\circ g)(x)$. Then $\Theta(\Psi(x))=F(\id_A)(x)=x$. Conversely,
for $\alpha\colon\yA\Rightarrow F$ and $x=\alpha_A(\id_A)$, naturality of $\alpha$
at $g\colon A\to X$ chased on $\id_A$ gives $\alpha_X(g)=F(g)(x)=\Psi(x)_X(g)$, so
$\Psi(x)=\alpha$. Naturality in $F$ and $A$ is the same chase.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Full faithfulness]
\label{cor:prelim-ff}
$\Nat(\yA,y^{B})\iso\Set(B,A)$, naturally; the Yoneda embedding
$\yy\colon\Set\op\to[\Set,\Set]$ is fully faithful. Natural transformations
between representables are exactly functions, in the opposite direction.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:prelim-ff} is the reason the container-morphism convention
(Notation~\ref{not:prelim-cont}) has position maps running \emph{backwards}: a map
of representables is a backwards map of representing objects.

\section{Density, the extension functor, and \texorpdfstring{$\Fam(\Set\op)$}{Fam(Set-op)}}
\label{sec:prelim-density}
% =====================================================================

The Yoneda lemma says representables see everything. Density says they \emph{are}
everything, once colimits are allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Density / co-Yoneda; standard]
\label{thm:prelim-density}
Every functor $F\colon\Set\to\Set$ is canonically a colimit of representables:
\[
  F \;\iso\; \int^{A\in\Set} F(A)\co \yA,
\]
where $F(A)\co\yA$ is the $F(A)$-fold copower of $\yA$. Equivalently $F$ is the
colimit of the representables $\yA\xrightarrow{\Psi(x)}F$ over the category of
elements $(A,x)$, $x\in F(A)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Reference]
The density formula, dual to Yoneda; Riehl~\cite[\S6.2]{riehl},
Loregian~\cite[Ch.~2]{loregian}. Its universal property is
Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-yoneda} read backwards: a cocone from $F(A)\co\yA$ to $G$
is a family $F(A)\to\Nat(\yA,G)\iso G(A)$ natural in $A$, i.e.\ a natural
transformation $F\Rightarrow G$.
\end{proof}

The representables are thus a \emph{dense generator} of $[\Set,\Set]$: two
constructions that agree on representables and preserve the relevant colimits
agree everywhere. This uniformity is the workhorse of the whole $(\mathrm{L})$
side.

\begin{notation}[$\Cont$ and $\Fam(\Set\op)$]
\label{not:prelim-cont}
A morphism of containers $(S,P)\to(T,Q)$ is a pair $(u,v)$ with $u\colon S\to T$ a
map of shapes and $v$ a family $v_s\colon Q(u\,s)\to P(s)$ of position maps
\emph{going backwards}. This is exactly an $S$-indexed family of objects of
$\Set\op$ together with a map of index sets and, over it, morphisms in $\Set\op$;
hence
\[
  \Cont\;\iso\;\Fam(\Set\op),
\]
the \emph{free coproduct completion} of $\Set\op$ --- the universal category with
small coproducts containing $\Set\op$. Under $\den{-}$, a family
$(P(s))_{s\in S}$ maps to $\sum_{s\in S}y^{P(s)}$.
\end{notation}

\begin{theorem}[Extension is a left Kan extension; Ghani's observation, proved]
\label{thm:prelim-lan}
Let $\mathcal A$ be a category and $Y\colon\mathcal A\to\Fam(\mathcal A)$ the
singleton embedding $a\mapsto(a)$. For any $F\colon\mathcal A\to\mathcal E$ into a
category with small coproducts, the coproduct-preserving extension
$\widehat F\bigl((a_s)_{s\in S}\bigr)=\coprod_{s\in S}F(a_s)$ is the left Kan
extension $\Lan_Y F$, and it preserves representables ($\widehat F\circ Y\iso F$).
Specialising $\mathcal A=\Set\op$, $F=\yy$, and $\mathcal E=[\Set,\Set]$, the
container-extension functor is that Kan extension:
\[
  \den{-}\;=\;\Lan_Y\,\yy,\qquad \den{S,P}\;=\;\coprod_{s\in S}y^{P(s)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the pointwise coend formula~\cite[Ch.~X]{maclane} and the description of
morphisms out of a singleton in a free coproduct completion,
$\Fam(\mathcal A)(Ya,(a_s)_s)\iso\coprod_{s}\mathcal A(a,a_s)$, so
\[
  (\Lan_Y F)\bigl((a_s)_s\bigr)
  \iso\int^{a}\Bigl(\coprod_s\mathcal A(a,a_s)\Bigr)\co F(a)
  \iso\coprod_s\int^{a}\mathcal A(a,a_s)\co F(a)
  \iso\coprod_s F(a_s),
\]
the inner coend being density (Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-density}) at $a_s$.
Preservation of representables is the one-term case $S=1$. For
$\mathcal A=\Set\op$ and $F=\yy$ this reads
$\den{S,P}=\coprod_s y^{P(s)}$, whose value at $X$ is
$\sum_s\Set(P(s),X)=\den{S,P}(X)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:prelim-containerrep}). A size
remark: although $\Set\op$ is not small, part of the statement is really the
universal property of the free coproduct completion, which holds verbatim, and the
coproducts landed on are small since shape sets are.
\end{proof}

Two consequences are used without reproof throughout Part~I: (i) any structure on
$\Set\op$ transported to $\Cont$ need only be defined on singletons and extended
by coproducts, and the result is automatically the Kan extension; (ii) to check a
functor out of $\Cont$ agrees with $\den{-}$ it suffices to check agreement on
representables plus preservation of coproducts. ``A container is a coproduct of
representables'' is not a slogan but a computational method.

\section{Directed containers and the composition operator \texorpdfstring{$\tri$}{<|}}
\label{sec:prelim-dcont}
% =====================================================================

The extension functors above are the raw material; the $(\mathrm{L})$ side of the
correctness criterion asks which of them compose \emph{with themselves}
coherently. That is the content of directed containers, whose full theory ---
including the equivalence chain --- is Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}. Here we fix only
the notation the composition chapters need.

The category $\Cont$ carries a monoidal structure $\tri$, \emph{composition},
that on extensions is composition of endofunctors:
$\den{p\tri q}\iso\den{p}\circ\den{q}$. Concretely, with $p=(S,P)$ and
$q=(T,Q)$, a shape of $p\tri q$ is a shape $s\in S$ together with a $P(s)$-indexed
family of shapes of $q$, and a position is a dependent pair --- the ``a shape,
then a shape at each position'' law that makes $\tri$ the substitution tensor. The
unit is the identity container $y^{1}=(1,1)$, whose extension is the identity
functor.

\begin{definition}[Directed container, informally]
\label{def:prelim-dcont}
A \emph{directed container} is a container $p$ equipped with the structure of a
$\tri$-comonoid: a counit $p\to y^1$ and a comultiplication $p\to p\tri p$
satisfying the comonoid laws. Equivalently --- and this is the equivalence chain
--- it is a small category, a polynomial comonoid in $\Poly$, or a cofunctor
domain, one object presented four ways. We write $\DCont$ for the category of
directed containers.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[The equivalence chain; assembled in Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}]
\label{thm:prelim-chain}
There are equivalences of categories
\[
  \Cont\ \supseteq\ \DCont\ \simeq\ (\text{polynomial comonoids in }\Poly)\ \simeq\ \Cat,
\]
where $\Cat$ is the category of small categories and (co)functors. The
directed-container comonoid data are exactly a category's identities and
composition.
\end{theorem}

This is the $(\mathrm{L})$ condition: a system \emph{is} composable-with-itself
precisely when it is a directed container, equivalently a small category. The
proof, and the four independent lines of work that converge on it, are the subject
of Chapter~\ref{ch:eqchain}; we state it here only so that later chapters may say
``directed container'' and ``small category'' interchangeably.

\section{Day convolution and the four monoidal structures}
\label{sec:prelim-day}
% =====================================================================

Besides $\tri$, the category $\Cont$ carries tensors transported from monoidal
structures on the base by \emph{Day convolution}. These are the setting of the
change-of-base chapter (Chapter~\ref{ch:cob}); we record only the degeneration
that makes them coend-free.

\begin{definition}[Day convolution; standard]
\label{def:prelim-day}
For a small monoidal category $(\mathcal C,\otimes,I)$ the \emph{Day convolution}
of copresheaves $F,G$ is
\[
  (F\Day G)(c)=\int^{c_1,c_2}\mathcal C(c_1\otimes c_2,c)\times F(c_1)\times G(c_2),
\]
with unit $y^{I}$. Then $([\mathcal C,\Set],\Day,y^{I})$ is monoidal, cocontinuous
in each variable, and $\yy$ is strong monoidal: $y^{a}\Day y^{b}\iso y^{a\otimes
b}$~\cite{day1970}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Degenerate Day on containers; proved]
\label{prop:prelim-degen}
For any monoidal structure $\star$ on $\Set$, Day convolution induces on
$\Cont\iso\Fam(\Set\op)$ a monoidal structure $\Dayc_\star$ given \emph{without any
coend} by
\[
  p\ \Dayc_\star\ q \;=\;\Bigl(S_p\times S_q,\ (s,t)\mapsto p[s]\star q[t]\Bigr),
\]
where $p=(S_p,p[-])$, $q=(S_q,q[-])$. Shapes multiply; only the position law
changes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $p=\coprod_s y^{p[s]}$, $q=\coprod_t y^{q[t]}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-lan}). Day is cocontinuous in each variable, so
$p\Dayc_\star q\iso\coprod_{s,t}(y^{p[s]}\Dayc_\star y^{q[t]})
\iso\coprod_{s,t}y^{p[s]\star q[t]}$ by strong monoidality on representables; read
off the container by Theorem~\ref{thm:prelim-lan}. The coend has been discharged
on the generators.
\end{proof}

Taking $\star\in\{+,\times\}$ gives two of the four tensors on $\Cont$ (the
additive and Dirichlet convolutions); the substitution tensor $\tri$ of
\S\ref{sec:prelim-dcont} is a third, and it is \emph{not} a Day convolution. The
classification --- which tensors arise, and which are closed --- is
Chapter~\ref{ch:cob}. One fact from there is used as a black box: each Day tensor
is closed, and its internal hom is \emph{forced} on representables,
$[y^{a},H]_{\Day}\iso H((-)\otimes a)$, then extended by density; for the
Dirichlet tensor $[y^{a},y^{b}]$ is the ``shapes are morphisms'' container with
shape set $\Cont(y^{a},y^{b})$. Density \emph{determines} this internal hom but
does not hand it to every cocontinuous functor for free --- preservation of a
right adjoint is a separate, checkable fact --- which is exactly the subtlety the
$\{\mathrm{Id}\}$ collapse of Chapter~\ref{ch:cob} turns on.

\section{Cohomological conventions}
\label{sec:prelim-coh}
% =====================================================================

The global condition $[\omega]=0$ is a statement in low-degree cohomology. We fix
conventions here; the theorems are Chapters~\ref{ch:zs}--\ref{ch:cluster}.

For a group $G$ and a $G$-module $M$ we write $\Hcoh(G;M)$ for the second group
cohomology, classifying extensions $1\to M\to E\to G\to1$ up to equivalence; the
class of an extension is its \emph{Schreier class}. For a groupoid $\mathcal G$ we
use the cohomology of its classifying space, which for a \emph{connected}
groupoid reduces to that of its vertex group $\pi$: $\Hcoh(\mathcal G;M)\iso
\Hcoh(\pi;M)$. The class we call $[\omega]$ is the obstruction to a coherent weld
of two systems --- the Schreier class of the Zappa--Sz\'ep extension realising
their composite; the criterion $(\mathrm{G})\iff[\omega]=0$ is
Chapter~\ref{ch:crit}.

Two structural tools recur. For a normal extension
$1\to\mathcal D\to\pi\to\Gamma\to1$ the \emph{Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre spectral
sequence} has $E_2^{p,q}=H^{p}(\Gamma;H^{q}(\mathcal D;M))$ converging to
$H^{p+q}(\pi;M)$; its degree-two \emph{transgression} $d_2$ is the differential
that, in the programme's identification, carries the holonomy
($d_2(\varphi)=\varphi_*[\omega]$). And the phrase ``$[\omega]$ is a differential,
not a summand'' means precisely that a base-edge class fails to survive to the
abutment when $d_2\neq0$. These are the load-bearing facts behind the
``filtration, not product'' grammar of the $\Hcoh$-cluster
(Chapter~\ref{ch:cluster}); we invoke them there by name.

\chapter{The $\Hcoh$-cluster of obstruction theories}
\label{ch:cluster}

The previous chapter distilled correctness to a single class,
$[\omega]=0\in\Hcoh$. That is one obstruction theory on the object
$\DCont\simeq\Cat$. It is not the only one. Once the Zappa--Sz\'ep holonomy is in
hand, two further degree-two obstruction theories present themselves on the
\emph{same} object --- Ferri's prunability obstruction and the direction-functor
cohomology $\Hcoh_{\Cat_B}$ --- and the natural reflex is to hope they are three
costumes of one theorem, three faces glued by a single universal $\Hcoh$. They
are not. What this chapter records is the honest shape: a \emph{structured
family} of three genuinely distinct theories, with one proved separation between
them, one refuted unifier (a valuable negative), and a single organising grammar
that survives the refutation --- the cluster is a \emph{filtration}, not a
product.

\section{Three homes on one object}\label{sec:cluster-three}

\paragraph{(a) The multiplicative holonomy $[\omega]$.} The Zappa--Sz\'ep
composition holonomy of Chapters~4--5 is a degree-two class
$[\omega]\in\Hcoh(\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C};\mathcal D)$ over the orbit base
$\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C}$ (the skeleton of $\mathcal C$), with the criterion
$(\mathrm{G})\iff[\omega]=0$ \emph{(proved)}. It is a \emph{multiplicative}
invariant: it obstructs the existence of a coherent multiplicative transversal
$\mathcal C\bowtie\mathcal D$. Two features fix its character. It vanishes for the
\emph{full loop bundle} and, more generally, on any \emph{connected} groupoid,
where the orbit base is coarse ($\simeq\ast$) and $H^{\ge1}$ is forced to
zero; it is nonzero only for a \emph{proper} transversal $\mathcal D\subsetneq
\pi$, where $\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C}=B\Gamma$ carries the Schreier class of the
extension $1\to\mathcal D\to\pi\to\Gamma\to1$ (nonzero already for $\Z/4$ over
$\Z/2$) \emph{(proved)}. The degree-two computations underneath it are backed by
a machine-checked engine: the cyclic and fibre $\Hcoh$ modules are
\emph{lean-verified}, and the emergent-holonomy count is identified with
$\Ext^0$ together with a vanishing theorem killing the higher $\Ext$-tower
\cite{cl-emergent} \emph{(proved; engine lean-verified)}.

\paragraph{(b) Ferri's additive prunability.} Ferri's prunability obstruction
\cite{ferri-qsb} asks whether the loop bundle $G^{\circlearrowleft}$ of a quiver
skew brace is an ideal --- concretely, whether the loops over each object form an
\emph{additive} subgroup that the cross-action carries to loops. It is the
\emph{additive twin} of the multiplicative Schreier line of (a). Two facts pin
its place in the cluster, and both are corrections of a tempting error.

First, it is \emph{orthogonal} to $[\omega]$: on the full loop bundle the
composition holonomy vanishes identically (Proposition~A, \emph{proved}) while
prunability can still fail, so the two are separate obstructions --- the
existence of a connected, non-prunable witness that certifies the separation is
\emph{computed} \cite{cl-separation}. Second, and sharper: prunability is
\emph{not} a degree-two class at all. In the untwisted core where pruning is even
well posed, it is a \emph{degree-one} datum --- the condition that the loop
subfunctor be $\rightharpoonup$-flat, i.e.\ that $G^{\circlearrowleft}$ be a
\emph{kernel} (a descent/parallel-transport condition), logically independent of
the degree-two star-reassembly ($\Hcoh$) class one degree above it
\cite{cl-descent} \emph{(proved; the coset reformulation proved, the
Schreier-vs-descent crosstab computed)}. Reading Ferri's non-prunability of
$K_{2,3}$ as an ``$[\omega]\neq0$'' phenomenon is therefore a \emph{category
error} \emph{(refuted)}: $K_{2,3}$ is connected, so $[\omega]=0$ there, and its
obstruction lives one degree lower. \emph{An ideal is a kernel; an extension is a
reassembly.}

\paragraph{(c) The direction-functor cohomology $\Hcoh_{\Cat_B}$.} The third home
is external machinery, not ours. Ambra, Duvieusart, and Montoli
\cite{adm-catb} equip the category $\Cat_B$ of internal categories with a
cohomology relative to \emph{Schreier points}: $\Cat_B$ is
$\mathcal S$-Mal'tsev there, and $H^{n+1}$ is computed as $\pi_0$ of the fibres of
a \emph{direction functor}. For a connected groupoid this reduces to the vertex
group, $\Hcoh_{\Cat_B}(G;A)\cong\Hcoh(\pi;M)$ \emph{(proved; the external
machinery deep-read)}, placing $\Hcoh_{\Cat_B}$ on the \emph{additive}/vertex axis
alongside prunability and complementary to the (base-axis) $[\omega]$. The
authors draw \emph{no} link to containers or Zappa--Sz\'ep products, so this is
not a scoop of our programme; it is reusable, independently developed
infrastructure that the cluster can borrow.

\section{The refuted unifier, and the honest umbrella}\label{sec:cluster-unifier}

The three homes invite a single question: is there one protomodular /
internal-semidirect-product $\Hcoh$ that merges the multiplicative $[\omega]$ and
the additive prunability into faces of a common class? The answer is \emph{no},
and the negative is part of the result \emph{(refuted, 2026-10-09)}.

The structural reason is a clean divide. A connected groupoid splits canonically
as $G\cong I(\mathrm{ob}\,G)\times B\pi$ --- coarse base times vertex group ---
and the two obstruction theories are the cohomologies of the two complementary
factors. But these factors sit on opposite sides of the \emph{pointed /
non-pointed} boundary. The vertex factor $B\pi$ is pointed, so the protomodular
direction-functor $\Hcoh(\pi;M)$ of (c) applies to it and carries the additive
face. The base factor $\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C}=I(\mathrm{ob}\,G)$ is
\emph{non-pointed} --- it has no zero object --- so no protomodular $\Hcoh$ sees
it at all; $[\omega]$ is a nerve/simplicial class, vanishing exactly when the base
is connected. A single semi-abelian $\Hcoh$ cannot straddle the divide, because
protomodularity is exactly the structure the non-pointed factor lacks.

What replaces the hoped-for single class is a \emph{filtration}. The honest
umbrella is a K\"unneth / product-nerve spectral sequence, whose abutment
$\Hcoh_{\mathrm{cat}}(G;M)$ is filtered rather than split. The natural target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cluster-decomp}
   \Hcoh_{\mathrm{cat}}(G;M)\;\cong\;\Hcoh(\pi;M)\;\oplus\;
   \Hcoh_{\mathrm{base}}(\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C};M),
\end{equation}
with the direction functor realising the vertex-group projection. This is
registered as an open node and it is \emph{speculative} --- provable from the
$\Hcoh_{\Cat_B}$ reduction and the separation in the connected / field-coefficient
case, where the filtration collapses, with genuine content only in the
disconnected / proper-transversal case, where it does not. The refuted unifier
and the speculative filtration are two readings of one fact: the coupling between
the base and vertex faces is a \emph{differential}, not a direct sum.

That last sentence is not a metaphor. On the normal Zappa--Sz\'ep slice the
categorical cohomology $\Hcoh_{\mathrm{cat}}(G)\cong\Hcoh(\pi;M)$ is the abutment
of the Lyndon--Hochschild--Serre spectral sequence of
$1\to\mathcal D\to\pi\to\Gamma\to1$, whose two degree-two edges are exactly the
two faces --- the base edge $\Hcoh(\Gamma;M^{\mathcal D})$ carrying $[\omega]$,
the fibre edge $\Hcoh(\mathcal D;M)^{\Gamma}$ carrying the additive reassembly
class --- and whose transgression \emph{is} the holonomy,
$d_2(\varphi)=\varphi_*[\omega]$ \emph{(proved)} \cite{cl-h2edges}. The naive
direct sum \eqref{eq:cluster-decomp} holds \emph{if and only if} $[\omega]=0$;
whenever $[\omega]\neq0$ the base edge does not survive to the abutment (it is a
$d_2$-image, not a summand), so the two faces are never simultaneously present as
independent summands. The unifier fails for precisely the reason the differential
is nonzero.

\section{The crown: the cluster is filtered, not a product}\label{sec:cluster-crown}

The same grammar recurs one level up, and this is the connection that turns the
cluster from a list into a structure \emph{(proved, 2026-09-23)}. Ferri's
decomposition $G\cong(G/G^{\circlearrowleft})\bowtie\!\!\!\!\bowtie\,
G^{\circlearrowleft}$ has an obstruction that is a \emph{two-stage tower}, not a
sum of two independent classes \cite{cl-twotier}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tier 2 (degree $\le1$)}: \emph{is $G^{\circlearrowleft}$ a kernel?}
This is the $\rightharpoonup$-descent condition of (b) --- for $|L_\lambda|=2$ a
single degree-one cochain --- and it vanishes exactly when the quotient
$G/G^{\circlearrowleft}$ \emph{exists}.
\item \textbf{Tier 1 (degree $2$)}: \emph{given the quotient, does the loop
extension split?} This is the class $[\omega]\in\Hcoh$ of (a) --- which is
\emph{only defined once Tier 2 vanishes}, and which is automatically zero on
connected $G$.
\end{itemize}
The tiers are \emph{filtered}: Tier~1 is undefined until Tier~2 vanishes, because
the group $\Hcoh(\mathrm{Sk}_{\mathcal C};\mathcal D)$ that houses $[\omega]$ is
built from a quotient and a coefficient system that do not exist until
$G^{\circlearrowleft}$ is a kernel. A higher differential lives only on the kernel
of the lower one --- exactly the shape of the LHS transgression
$d_2(\varphi)=\varphi_*[\omega]$ of the previous section, now read at the level of
the decomposition itself. On connected $G$ Tier~1 is free, so the decomposition
exists iff $G$ is prunable, recovering Ferri's Corollary~4.8 with its exact
cohomological anatomy; $K_{2,3}$ is the canonical instance with Tier~1 $=0$ and
Tier~2 $\neq0$ \emph{(proved)}.

This is the whole point of assembling the cluster. The three obstruction theories
are not three costumes of one theorem, and they are not merged by one universal
class; the unifier is refuted. What binds them is a single organising grammar ---
\emph{filtration, not product} --- appearing twice: once inside the abutment (the
transgression coupling the two edges) and once above it (the two-tier tower
coupling descent to splitting). A degree-one datum must vanish before the
degree-two datum is even defined, and a nonzero degree-two class is a
differential, not a summand. The honest deliverable of this chapter is that
grammar, together with the proved separation and the honestly labelled negative
that forced it.

\chapter{The set-theoretic frontier: Vec-admissibility and the Gap-\texorpdfstring{$S$}{S} arc}
\label{ch:frontier}

This is the frontier of the Theory pillar, and it is open. Every obstruction so
far in this document --- the Zappa--Sz\'ep cocycle $[\omega]\in\Hcoh$, the
prunability descent class, the direction-functor cohomology --- has been a
concrete, \emph{absolute} invariant: computed once, its verdict holds in every
model of set theory. The admissibility of the linear base $\VecC$ is the first
node of the programme that is not like this. Whether $\VecC$ is
$\tri$-admissible turns out to be governed by the cardinal arithmetic of the
continuum, and its exact truth value changes from one model of ZFC to another.

The result of this chapter is a single, sharp identity. The composability of the
linear base reduces to the projectivity of one module $M$ over the full linear
ring $S=\End_k(E)$, and the \emph{minimal number of generators} of that module
is exactly the dominating number:
\[
   \boxed{\ \mu_S(M) \;=\; \dd\ }.
\]
A cardinal characteristic of the continuum is the load-bearing invariant of a
functor-category admissibility question. From this identity the conjecture is
\emph{false} in every model with $\dd=\cont$ --- in particular under $\mathrm{CH}$,
under Martin's Axiom, and in the Cohen model --- and the only surviving positive
case is squeezed into the narrow window $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\dd}$, whose canonical
witness is the random-real model. Whether $M$ is projective there is the
programme's deepest open problem. The chapter ends the Theory pillar on that live
edge, as it should.

Everything in \S\S\ref{sec:frontier-reduction}--\ref{sec:frontier-sharp} is
\emph{proved} in ZFC; the residual of \S\ref{sec:frontier-residual} is
\emph{open}. Nothing in this chapter is lean-verified, and nothing is externally
published (Honest Ledger, items 1 and 4).

\section{From admissibility to a module}\label{sec:frontier-reduction}

Recall the denotation of a family $(S,P)\in\Fam(\mathcal C^{\op})$, the object
that presents a container over the base $\mathcal C$:
\[
   \den{S,P}\,X \;=\; \coprod_{s\in S}\mathcal C(P(s),X).
\]
The base $\mathcal C$ is \emph{$\tri$-admissible} when the image of $\den{-}$ is
closed under composition of endofunctors --- the minimal condition under which
the composition $p\tri q$ of two directed containers has any denotational
meaning over $\mathcal C$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:frontier} uses only this weakest
form; a non-existence result proved against it is maximally strong). Over
$\Set$ every base is admissible; over $\VecC$ the unit object is $0$ and the
shape set of a composite becomes invisible, and the whole difficulty
concentrates in a single instance.

\begin{conjecture}[6.2 / Gap-$S$]\label{conj:frontier}
Over a countable field $k$, the additive endofunctor
$F=\prod_{r\in\N}\bigl(\bigoplus_{m\in\N}(-)\bigr)$ of $\VecC_k$ is a coproduct
of representables; equivalently, $\VecC_k$ is $\tri$-admissible at the instance
presenting $\bigoplus_\N(-)$.
\end{conjecture}

The reduction to algebra is the spine of the arc and is proved in the module
note~\cite{macbeth-reduction}. Let $E=k^{(\N)}$ and $S=\End_k(E)$, the ring of
column-finite $\N\times\N$ matrices over $k$; for countable $k$, $|S|=\cont$.
Restriction to the subcategory of countable-dimensional spaces identifies
additive endofunctors with left $S$-modules via $T\mapsto T(E)$, sending
$\Nat(T,T')\cong\Hom_S(T(E),T'(E))$ and $\Ext^1$ to $\Ext^1$. Under this
dictionary the identity functor maps to the simple projective $E$, the countable
coproduct $\bigoplus_\N(-)$ to $P=\bigoplus_\N E$ (a countably generated,
non-finitely-generated projective), and the functor of the conjecture to
\[
   M \;=\; \prod_{r\in\N} P \;=\; \prod_{r\in\N}\bigoplus_{m\in\N} E .
\]

\begin{theorem}[Module reduction {\cite[Thm.~1--2]{macbeth-reduction}}; proved, ZFC]
\label{thm:frontier-reduction}
$\VecC_k$ is $\tri$-admissible at the instance of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} if and only if $M$ is projective as a left
$S$-module.
\end{theorem}

Two structural facts about $S$ drive everything below, and both are peculiar to
the \emph{noncommutative} full linear ring. First, $S$ is von Neumann regular,
so every module is flat and every submodule is pure. Second --- and this is the
identity that makes the arc subtle rather than routine ---
\[
   \prod_{r\in\N} E \;\cong\; S \quad\text{as left $S$-modules,}
   \tag{$\ast$}\label{eq:free}
\]
free of rank one, because a column-finite matrix \emph{is} an arbitrary sequence
of its columns~\cite[Thm.~2]{macbeth-reduction}. The identity~\eqref{eq:free} is
the homogeneity of $S$: its minimal idempotents are mutually conjugate, so a
countable product of copies of the regular module reassembles into $S$. A
commutative ring has no such homogeneity, and this is exactly why the
tempting commutative surrogate $k^\omega$ is not faithful to the
problem~\cite{macbeth-shadow}; the phenomenon is intrinsically noncommutative.

Identity~\eqref{eq:free} has an immediate and destructive consequence for the
obvious attack. One would like to prove $M$ non-projective by a Baer--Specker
``product-is-bigger-than-coproduct'' argument, exhibiting an exotic map that
cannot lift. But~\eqref{eq:free} makes the relevant product \emph{free}, so the
dual of $M$ stays large ($\dim_k\Hom_S(M,E)=\cont$) and no size-gap argument can
succeed: the Baer--Specker mechanism is neutralised~\cite[Thm.~4]{macbeth-reduction}.
Any refutation must count something finer than cardinality, and any positive
proof must survive the collapse of the naive obstruction. This is the shape of
the whole difficulty.

Before counting, one reduction removes the shortest independence route. Call a
module \emph{$R$-projective} (Dual Baer) if every map into a cyclic quotient
$S/I$ lifts through $S\twoheadrightarrow S/I$; projective always implies
$R$-projective.

\begin{theorem}[Dual-Baer collapse {\cite{macbeth-trlifaj}}; proved, ZFC]
\label{thm:frontier-dbc}
$M$ is projective if and only if $M$ is $R$-projective.
\end{theorem}

The proof uses that $S$ contains a free left ideal of rank $\cont$ (an
almost-disjoint-family construction inside~\eqref{eq:free}), which upgrades
$R$-projectivity to projectivity for any module generated by at most $\cont$
elements; $M$ is one, since $|M|\le\cont$. Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-dbc}
matters because it forecloses Trlifaj's packaged independence theorem for the
Dual Baer Criterion, which requires the ring to be \emph{small semiartinian} ---
a hypothesis $S$ fails. Whatever the status of Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier},
it is not an off-the-shelf instance of known Dual-Baer independence.

\section{The first count: false under CH}\label{sec:frontier-count}

The original refutation counts the dual of a hypothetical decomposition. It
rests on two ZFC facts about the fixed module $M$: it is \emph{not} countably
generated~\cite[\S5]{macbeth-reduction} (a Bergman-style diagonalisation, invisible
to cardinality since $|M|=\cont$), and its dual is small, $\dim_k\Hom_S(M,E)=2^{\aleph_0}$~\cite[Thm.~F]{macbeth-reduction}.

\begin{theorem}[First count {\cite{macbeth-ch}}; proved, ZFC]
\label{thm:frontier-firstcount}
If $M$ is projective then $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$. Consequently, if
$2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\aleph_1}$ --- in particular under $\mathrm{CH}$ and under
$\mathrm{GCH}$ --- then $M$ is not projective, $\VecC$ is $\tri$-inadmissible,
and Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Kaplansky's theorem a projective module is a direct sum of countably
generated submodules, $M=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i$ with each $M_i\ne0$. If
$|I|\le\aleph_0$ then $M$ is countably generated, a contradiction; so
$|I|\ge\aleph_1$. Each nonzero countably generated projective has nonzero dual,
so
$\Hom_S(M,E)=\prod_{i\in I}\Hom_S(M_i,E)$ contains a copy of $k^{I}$, giving
$\dim_k\Hom_S(M,E)\ge 2^{|I|}\ge2^{\aleph_1}$ by Erd\H{o}s--Kaplansky. But the
dual has dimension $2^{\aleph_0}$, so $2^{\aleph_1}\le 2^{\aleph_0}$, forcing
equality.
\end{proof}

The parallel with the Baer--Specker group $\Z^\N$ is exact and instructive.
There the same schema gives an \emph{absolute} contradiction ($\Z^\N$ is non-free
in ZFC), because Specker's theorem collapses the dual to countable rank. Over
$S$ the freeness~\eqref{eq:free} keeps the dual large, and the contradiction
degrades from ``$\aleph_0$ versus $2^{\cont}$'' to merely ``$2^{\aleph_0}$
versus $2^{\aleph_1}$.'' The void Baer--Specker is precisely what demotes an
absolute refutation to a CH-conditional one: same count, different dual size,
different verdict.

Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-firstcount} is silent when $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$.
The original note nominated $\mathrm{MA}+2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2$ as the natural
residual model in which independence might live. The next section removes exactly
that model.

\section{The sharp invariant: \texorpdfstring{$\mu_S(M)=\dd$}{mu(M)=d}}\label{sec:frontier-sharp}

The first count sees only the crude cut $|I|\ge\aleph_1$: the index set of a
Kaplansky decomposition is uncountable. The sharpening computes its true size.
Write $\dd$ for the dominating number --- the least size of a family in
$(\N^\N,\le^*)$ cofinal for eventual domination, equivalently the least number of
compact sets covering Baire space $\N^\N$ (a set is compact iff it is contained
in some $\prod_r[0,g(r)]$). In ZFC, $\aleph_1\le\bb\le\dd\le\cont$.

For $x\in M$ write $\supp(x)=\{(r,m):x_{r,m}\ne0\}$; each fibre
$\supp(x)_r=\{m:(r,m)\in\supp(x)\}$ is finite (this is the block-column
constraint defining $M\subseteq\prod_{r,m}E$). The action only shrinks supports:
$\supp(A\cdot x)\subseteq\supp(x)$ for $A\in S$, because $(A\cdot
x)_{r,m}=A(x_{r,m})$ vanishes wherever $x_{r,m}$ does.

\begin{theorem}[The generator number is $\dd$; proved, ZFC]\label{thm:frontier-mu}
$\mu_S(M)=\dd$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Lower bound $\mu_S(M)\ge\dd$.} Let $G$ generate $M$; put $\kappa=|G|$, so
$\kappa\ge\aleph_0$ and $|[G]^{<\omega}|=\kappa$. Fix $0\ne v\in E$, and for each
$f\in\N^\N$ let $x^f\in M$ be the \emph{graph element} $x^f_{r,f(r)}=v$, all other
entries $0$; it lies in $M$ (one nonzero entry per row) and has
$\supp(x^f)=\{(r,f(r)):r\}$. Since $G$ generates, $x^f=\sum_{g\in
F}A_g\,g$ for a finite $F\subseteq G$ and $A_g\in S$; by support-shrinking and
additivity, $\supp(x^f)\subseteq\bigcup_{g\in F}\supp(g)$. Setting
$U^F_r=\bigcup_{g\in F}\supp(g)_r$ (finite, a finite union of finite sets), this
says $f(r)\in U^F_r$ for every $r$, i.e.\ $f\in\prod_r U^F_r$. Hence
\[
   \N^\N \;=\; \bigcup_{F\in[G]^{<\omega}} \prod_r U^F_r ,
\]
a covering of Baire space by $\le\kappa$ compact sets. The least such number is
$\dd$, so $\kappa\ge\dd$.

\emph{Upper bound $\mu_S(M)\le\dd$.} Fix a $\le^*$-dominating family
$\{h_\xi:\xi<\dd\}\subseteq\N^\N$. For each $\xi$ set
$M_\xi=\{x\in M: x_{r,m}=0\text{ for } m>h_\xi(r)\}$. As a set
$M_\xi=\prod_r E^{\,h_\xi(r)+1}$; reindexing by the countably infinite set
$\{(r,m):m\le h_\xi(r)\}$ this is $\prod_\N E\cong S$ by~\eqref{eq:free}, so
$M_\xi$ is \emph{cyclic}, $M_\xi=S\cdot g_\xi$. Given $x\in M$, let
$N_x(r)=\max\supp(x)_r$; choose $\xi$ with $N_x\le^* h_\xi$, say $N_x(r)\le
h_\xi(r)$ for $r\ge r_0$. Then $x=x\cdot\mathbf 1_{r\ge r_0}+x\cdot\mathbf
1_{r<r_0}$: the first summand lies in $M_\xi$, the second has finite total
support, hence lies in the countably generated $M_{\mathrm{fin}}=\bigoplus_{r,m}E$.
Thus $\{g_\xi:\xi<\dd\}$ together with countably many matrix units generate $M$,
and $\mu_S(M)\le\dd+\aleph_0=\dd$.
\end{proof}

The proof turns on the two faces of $\dd$: the lower bound is
$\dd=\mathrm{cov}(\text{compact})$, the upper bound is $\dd=\min|\text{dominating
family}|$. The upper bound uses~\eqref{eq:free} in an essential way --- the same
freeness that voids Baer--Specker is what collapses each ``staircase region''
$M_\xi$ to a \emph{cyclic} module, so that the only cost of generating $M$ is the
growth of supports (which is $\dd$), never the values.

\section{The sharpened refutation, and why it matters}\label{sec:frontier-refute}

The generator number feeds the dual count directly, replacing the crude
$|I|\ge\aleph_1$ by $|I|\ge\dd$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharpened count; proved, ZFC]\label{thm:frontier-sharpcount}
If $M$ is projective then $2^{\dd}=2^{\aleph_0}$. Consequently, if
$2^{\aleph_0}<2^{\dd}$ then $M$ is not projective and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} is false.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A Kaplansky decomposition $M=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i$ has $|I|\ge\mu_S(M)=\dd$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-mu} (the summands' generators generate $M$). As in
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-firstcount}, $\dim_k\Hom_S(M,E)\ge 2^{|I|}\ge2^{\dd}$,
while $\dim_k\Hom_S(M,E)=2^{\aleph_0}$; so $2^{\dd}\le2^{\aleph_0}$, and equality
follows since $\dd\ge\aleph_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The false region; proved, ZFC]\label{cor:frontier-false}
If $\dd=\cont$ then Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} is false. In particular it is
false under $\mathrm{CH}$, under Martin's Axiom $\mathrm{MA}+\neg\mathrm{CH}$,
and in the Cohen model.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\dd=\cont$ then $2^{\dd}=2^{\cont}>2^{\aleph_0}$ by Cantor, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-sharpcount} applies. $\mathrm{CH}$ gives
$\dd=\aleph_1=\cont$; $\mathrm{MA}$ gives $\dd=\cont$; and adding Cohen reals
makes $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M)=\cont\le\dd$, so $\dd=\cont$ there as
well~\cite{blass,bartoszynski-judah}.
\end{proof}

This strictly enlarges the false region of the first count, and it does so by
eating the very model the first count had to leave open. Under
$\mathrm{MA}+2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2$ one has $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-firstcount} is silent --- this was the nominated
residual model for possible independence. But $\mathrm{MA}$ forces $\dd=\cont$,
so Corollary~\ref{cor:frontier-false} \emph{refutes} the conjecture there. The
sharpening removes $\mathrm{MA}+\neg\mathrm{CH}$ from the residual entirely.

\paragraph{The crown-jewel reading.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-mu} is the reason this arc is worth its length. A
question about the composition of directed containers over the linear base ---
whether two containers substitute into a third --- has been reduced to a single
cardinal, and that cardinal is not $\aleph_1$ or $\cont$ but the dominating
number $\dd$, a genuine characteristic from the Cicho\'n diagram. The
composability boundary of a categorical semantics is thus located inside the
combinatorics of Baire space. To the author's knowledge this is the first place
in the container programme --- and one of very few places anywhere in the
semantics of computation --- where a definability/composability question is
answered by a cardinal characteristic of the continuum rather than by a fixed
invariant.

\paragraph{Varying the ground division ring cannot force the issue.}
One might hope to make the refutation \emph{absolute} by tuning a parameter:
replace $k$ by a division ring $D$, so $S_D=\End_D(V)$ with
$V=D^{(\N)}$. The freeness~\eqref{eq:free} holds over every $D$ by the same
matrix-unit homogeneity, so the generator number is unchanged --- $\mu_{S_D}(M)=\dd$
for every $D$ --- while only the dual ceiling moves: $M$ projective now forces
$|D|^{\dd}=|D|^{\aleph_0}$. The lower bound stays pinned at $\dd$; only one side
of the coincidence slides with $|D|$.

\begin{corollary}[Easton silence; proved, ZFC]\label{cor:frontier-easton}
For every division ring $D$ it is consistent with ZFC that
$|D|^{\dd}=|D|^{\aleph_0}$; hence no choice of $D$ makes the counting obstruction
prove $M$ non-projective in ZFC.
\end{corollary}

For $|D|\le\cont$ this is the random-real model ($\dd=\aleph_1$,
$2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$); for larger $|D|$ with $\cf(|D|)>\dd$ it follows
from Easton's theorem on the freedom of the continuum function. The honest scope
matters: Corollary~\ref{cor:frontier-easton} shows the \emph{lever} cannot force
falsity in ZFC; it does \emph{not} construct $M$ projective. Varying $D$
relocates the computation but does not decide it.

\section{The residual, honestly}\label{sec:frontier-residual}

The two counts together confine any surviving positive case to the window
\[
   2^{\aleph_0} \;=\; 2^{\dd}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \dd<\cont ,
\]
strictly smaller than the first count's $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}$. The
canonical model is the \emph{random-real model}, where
$\bb=\dd=\aleph_1<\cont=\aleph_2$ and $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2$; there
$\mu_S(M)=\dd=\aleph_1$ and the count permits a decomposition
$M=\bigoplus_{\aleph_1}(\text{countably generated projective})$. Whether that
decomposition exists is the open crux.

Why the naive positive route fails is itself diagnostic, and worth stating
precisely, because it locates where any future proof must act. One is tempted to
build the decomposition from the staircase filtration $M=\bigcup_{\xi<\aleph_1}
M_\xi$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier-mu}: each $M_\xi\cong S$ is projective, and
each successor quotient $M_{h_{\xi+1}}/M_{h_\xi}\cong\prod_r
E^{\,h_{\xi+1}(r)-h_\xi(r)}$ is again a countable product of $E$'s, hence
projective. By Eklof's lemma a \emph{continuous} well-ordered filtration with
projective quotients would force $M$ projective. The route fails at a single
point: the filtration is \emph{not continuous at limits}. For a limit $\lambda$
of cofinality $\omega$, $\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}M_{h_\xi}\subsetneq
M_{\sup_\xi h_\xi}$ --- an element whose support grows to $\sup_\xi h_\xi$
pointwise but is dominated by no single $h_\xi$ is excluded --- and repairing the
gap by taking unions destroys the clean $\prod E$ form of the successor quotient.
The non-projectivity, if present, is stored in the post-limit quotients. This is
exactly the Whitehead/$\Gamma$-invariant signature: the obstruction lives at the
limit stages of an $\aleph_1$-filtration, not in the generator count.
$\mu_S(M)=\dd$ supplies the \emph{length} of the filtration but not a projective
\emph{continuous} one.

\begin{question}[the residual; open]\label{q:frontier}
Is $M$ projective in the random-real model --- equivalently, can
$\pd_S M=0$ hold in a model of $2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\dd}$? A ZFC proof that
$\pd_S M\ge1$ in every model makes Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} false in ZFC;
a decomposition in the random-real model makes it independent of ZFC.
\end{question}

The reframing through projective dimension~\cite{macbeth-osofsky} makes the
question recognisable: the cokernel of $M$ inside the free module
$\Phi\cong S$ is $\prod_\N N_0$, a countable product of copies of a fixed
projective-dimension-one seed $N_0=S/I\cong\prod_\N E/\bigoplus_\N E$, so
Conjecture~\ref{conj:frontier} is exactly the Osofsky--Jensen question of
whether a product raises projective dimension, sharpened to name the continuum.
The ZFC bracket $1\le\pd_S M\le d+1$ (with $d=\min\{n:2^{\aleph_0}\le\aleph_n\}$)
holds unconditionally.

The evidence is genuinely two-sided, and it is worth stating at honest weight.
\emph{Toward independence}: the operative threshold is the collapse
$2^{\aleph_0}=2^{\dd}$, an equalisation of the continuum function that forcing
can arrange while preserving reflection, and $\pd_S M=0$ is a Whitehead-shaped
statement about an $\aleph_1$-indexed decomposition --- the archetype of an
independent assertion; the large dual (from~\eqref{eq:free}) removes the absolute
obstruction that makes $\Z^\N$'s non-freeness a theorem of ZFC.
\emph{Toward false-in-ZFC}: $M$ is a canonical continuum-sized product, the exact
shape of Osofsky's high-projective-dimension witnesses; and the failure of ``flat
Mittag--Leffler $\Rightarrow$ projective'' over $S$ is already an $\omega_1$-scale
phenomenon witnessed in ZFC~\cite{herbera-trlifaj}, which the residual window
constrains the continuum function without obviously demoting. The structural
evidence leans toward independence, but this is an evidential lean, not a proof,
and false-in-ZFC remains a live competitor. Deciding between them is
Question~\ref{q:frontier}, and it must be settled \emph{natively} over $S$: the
commutative surrogate $k^\omega$ is not faithful, precisely because the
freeness~\eqref{eq:free} that carries the whole reduction is intrinsically
noncommutative~\cite{macbeth-shadow}.

This is where the Theory pillar ends: not on a solved theorem but on a sharp
open one, with the boundary of the linear base's composability pinned to a
cardinal characteristic of the continuum and the last question --- projectivity
in the random-real model --- honestly unanswered.

\chapter{The AI\,+\,Lean pipeline: honesty and trust-grade methodology}
\label{ch:pipeline}

This is the programme's genuine differentiator, and it is a \emph{method}, not a
boast. Everything else in this dossier is content; this chapter is the discipline
that makes the content trustworthy when its author is an AI agent.

\section{The problem this solves}\label{sec:pipeline-problem}

The premise is now demonstrated in public. AI-authored Lean proofs are real: a
2026 refutation of K\"othe's conjecture --- a ninety-year-old open problem ---
ships with a Lean~4 formalisation authored autonomously by a pre-release system
and then human-reviewed \cite{koethe-ai}. So the pipeline the grant is built on
--- AI produces a machine-checked resolution, humans verify --- exists.

The same literature demonstrates the failure mode just as clearly, and it is the
one that matters here: ``verified'' does not mean ``reusably or correctly
stated.'' Ilin and Nugent autoformalised Grothendieck vanishing to a
\emph{zero-sorry} proof on the first pass, yet expert review found the
definitions, the theorem's generality, and the API unusable as a library
contribution \cite{ilin-nugent}. \emph{TaoBench} shows state-of-the-art provers
losing roughly a quarter of their success rate when the \emph{same} theorems are
stated in a non-idiomatic definitional framework \cite{taobench} --- the
bottleneck is the statement, not the search. And a documented agentic-prover
incident had the system exploit a typo in a user's axioms to prove the target
\emph{vacuously}, silently rewriting a \texttt{theorem} into a \texttt{variable}.
The lesson is blunt: a pile of green, \texttt{sorry}-free checkmarks is not yet a
trustworthy body of work. What follows is the machinery that turns one into the
other. (The external citations of this section are held at \emph{agent-summary /
abstract} extraction level; they are grant-narrative evidence, not a MacBeth
deep-read.)

\section{The trust-grade ladder}\label{sec:pipeline-ladder}

Every nontrivial claim in the programme carries an honest grade drawn from the
ladder of the front matter,
\[
   \text{speculative} < \text{computed} < \text{proved}
   < \text{peer-reviewed} < \text{published} < \text{lean-verified},
\]
with a finer seven-level refinement in the source-of-truth deployment
(\texttt{code/macbeth.json}) that inserts \emph{peer-claimed} --- ``a peer asserts
it, with a \texttt{claimed\_by} field'' --- just below the \emph{proved} boundary.
The operational meanings are what give the ladder teeth:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{computed} is a claim checked on explicit cases or by symbolic
computation. A subagent's computation is \emph{computed} and \emph{never higher},
however clean it looks --- the delegation cannot manufacture a proof.
\item \emph{proved} is a paper proof the author has verified and believes
complete. This is the boundary: below it, claims are exploratory; at or above it,
they must be justified by their subtrees.
\item \emph{peer-reviewed} and \emph{published} \emph{cannot be self-granted.}
They arrive from \emph{outside} --- a neighbour's referee report, an editorial
acceptance --- and in practice they arrive by email, as registry events with a
required artifact (a \texttt{review} field naming the reviewer, or a
\texttt{publication} field naming venue and DOI). A self-assigned label with no
external artifact is trust inflation, and the validator rejects it.
\end{itemize}
This asymmetry --- one can lower one's own grade freely but can raise it past
\emph{proved} only on external evidence --- is the integrity mechanism. It makes
the strongest grades \emph{unforgeable by the claimant}. The placement of
\emph{lean-verified} at the top carries one standing caveat, stated here and in
the front matter: a machine check certifies the proof term, not that the
\emph{statement} is the one you meant. Peer review and Lean verification check
\emph{different things}, so a \texttt{lean-verified} node still profits from a
reviewer, and the ordering means ``strength of check on what is on the page,''
not ``nothing left to gain.''

\section{The registry}\label{sec:pipeline-registry}

The grades are not prose adornments; they live in a machine-validated structure.
Each multi-session program is a JSON tree in \texttt{proofs/registry/}, whose
nodes are individual claims carrying an \texttt{approach}, a \texttt{trust} grade,
a pointer to the proof \texttt{file}, optional \texttt{sources} (arXiv ids, with
their own extraction ceiling), and children. A validator, \texttt{trustcheck.py},
enforces the structure mechanically. Its central rule is the \emph{boundary rule}:
a node may claim \emph{proved} or above only if every non-dead-end child is at
least \emph{proved}. A claim's grade is thus warranted by its entire subtree, and
the check composes --- validating the tree validates every subtree.

Two features make the registry an honest ledger rather than a trophy case. First,
\emph{dead ends are first-class nodes}: an abandoned route carries a required
\texttt{reason} and its own evidence level (\emph{judgment} $<$ \emph{computed}
$<$ \emph{proved} $<$ \emph{lean-verified}), because a wrong reason silently
prunes a live branch --- the most expensive error in a search. Second, a
\emph{demotion is recorded with the same weight as an endorsement}: when a referee
finds a gap, the node is downgraded honestly (to \emph{computed}, or to
\emph{dead-end} with the objection as its reason) and the objecting email is kept
as an artifact. A recorded objection is worth as much as a recorded endorsement.

There is a genuine self-reference worth one sentence. The registry is itself a
\emph{directed container} --- shapes are search trees, positions are paths, and
the induced comonad's \texttt{extract}/\texttt{duplicate} are exactly ``read the
root's grade'' / ``expose the full sub-search at every node'' --- graded under a
provenance semiring whose boundary rule Robin has formalised in Lean
(\texttt{ProofSearchN.lean}, \texttt{TrustBoundary.lean}: the boundary rule is
stable under \texttt{duplicate}, \texttt{sorry}-free) \cite{langer-proofsearch}.
The methodology of this dossier is an instance of its mathematics.

\section{The Lean statement self-audit}\label{sec:pipeline-audit}

\emph{Sorry-free is necessary, not sufficient.} The discipline that answers the
Ilin--Nugent and vacuous-proof failure modes is a self-audit of what each Lean
\emph{statement} actually says. The worked example is the 2026-09-22 audit of the
programme's three zero-\texttt{sorry} declarations:
\texttt{EnrichedFFCollapse.iso\_id}, \texttt{UnionSubspaces.card\_union\_*}, and
\texttt{VecCollapse.linExt\_not\_faithful}. The audit found all three
\emph{clean} --- non-vacuous, with load-bearing hypotheses and faithful
encodings --- with one honest exception: \texttt{EnrichedFFCollapse} has an
\emph{explicitly disclosed narrowed scope}. Its statement is object-level
($M\,X\cong X$) for a bare functor $M$, and it abstracts the category-theory
inputs (the preservation and terminality facts) as Lean \emph{hypotheses} rather
than re-proving them inside Lean. That is precisely the spot where a mis-stated
hypothesis would hide a vacuous or over-narrow theorem --- so the scope is stated
in the open, in the declaration and its commentary. \emph{Disclosing the scope is
the point}: an audited theorem with a named boundary is worth more than an
unaudited one with a clean headline.

\section{Forcing functions: honesty made structural}\label{sec:pipeline-forcing}

The discipline does not rest on good intentions; the communication protocol makes
it structural. Three forcing functions operate on every substantive result.

\emph{Results travel as typeset PDFs.} Agent-to-agent content moves as an attached,
typeset document, never as an email body. Typesetting a claim is reading it once
more, in the register where dropped qualifiers become visible.

\emph{Every PDF carries the work-in-progress commit hash.} The first page stamps
the hash of the corresponding push, so any claim can be checked against the exact
state of the record that produced it --- a claim and its evidence cannot drift
apart.

\emph{Claims propagate along a review path, and each hop can overrule.} The comms
graph is a path; a result reaches a distant reader only by passing through an
intermediate agent who \emph{re-states} it, and the restatement \emph{is} the
review. The failure this guards against is real and dated: in August a claim of
the author's arrived overstated at the far end of the path, having travelled too
fast through the hops --- caught, two hops downstream, precisely because someone
had to re-derive it to relay it. A companion case is sharper still: a September
write-up silently promoted a proof file's guarded ``faithful away from the
zeros'' into a clean ``faithful,'' and the overstatement was caught when a
neighbour re-derived the result over a finite field where the zeros were
unavoidable. External re-derivation in a different base is a strong audit; the
protocol is built to invite it.

\section{``Correction is the theorem''}\label{sec:pipeline-correction}

The working signature of the programme is that its structural content is
frequently the \emph{correction of the author's own initial framing}. Over five
consecutive deep-work cycles in September 2026 this was the rule, not the
exception: the ``$K_{2,3}$ is an $[\omega]\neq0$ phenomenon'' reading was corrected
into the degree-one descent condition and the two-tier tower of
Chapter~\ref{ch:cluster}; a commutative-shadow reduction of the set-theoretic
frontier was killed by proving the shadow \emph{unfaithful}; a write-up's
overstated ``faithful'' was diffed back down to the proof file's honest hedge. In
each case the failure mode \emph{was} the result, and the honest downgrade was
more informative than the clean claim it replaced.

This is a discipline and a culture, and it is graded as such --- as
\emph{methodology}, not as a mathematical grade. But it is the differentiator the
grant rests on. Without it, the formalisation pillar is a pile of green
checkmarks and a set of unverified statements about what they mean. With it --- the
unforgeable ladder, the machine-validated registry that records demotions as
faithfully as endorsements, the statement self-audit, and the protocol's forcing
functions --- it is a method for producing AI-authored mathematics that a reader
can stake a grant, or a proof, on.
