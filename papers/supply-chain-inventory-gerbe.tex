\begin{abstract}
A supply chain, abstracted to a small category $\C$, carries inventory as local stock-data
glued along logistics operations. When charts are compared by \emph{equality} of counts, we
showed earlier that consistency is a degree-one class $[\theta_R]\in H^1(N(\C);R)$. Here we
refine the comparison to \emph{isomorphism of stock-states}: lots are fungible, so charts match
only up to a relabelling group $K=\Aut(F)$. We prove that this refinement turns the gluing data
into a $K$-gerbe over the nerve $N(\C)$ and raises the obstruction by exactly one categorical
level. A strict global inventory exists if and only if a single degree-two class
$[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ vanishes (Theorem~A); $[\gerbe]$ is genuinely degree-two ---
it vanishes on every acyclic (DAG or tree) chain yet is already nonzero on a single reversible
two-lot loop $B(\Z/2)$ (Theorem~B); and it is logically independent of the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld
class $[\omega]$, which lives in the \emph{same} degree but over a \emph{different} coefficient
system (Theorem~C). Finally, $[\gerbe]$ can be nonzero only when $Z(\Aut F)\neq1$, so generic
$\geq3$-fold fungibility carries no tier-two obstruction while gauge-structured stock does
(Proposition~D). The result completes inventory consistency into a two-tier theory --- $H^1$
counts, $H^2$ relabellings --- and exhibits a fourth distinct $H^2$ home on $\mathrm{DCont}
\simeq\Cat$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Composition is where correctness is won or lost, and inventory is where the price is paid. When
two delivery routes reconverge on the same stock-keeping unit, when stock is moved and merged
and counted again downstream, the question is always whether the local records glue to one
consistent global record. When they do not, the failure has an economic name: phantom
inventory, a reconciliation that never closes, a write-off. A compositional theory of supply
chains earns its keep by telling you \emph{which} such failures can occur and \emph{where} they
live.

The categorical model is standard. A supply chain --- nodes as objects, admissible logistics
operations as morphisms --- is a small category $\C$; equivalently, by Ahman and
Uustalu~\cite{au-dccat}, a \emph{directed container}. Its nerve $N(\C)$ is the simplicial set
whose $n$-simplices are composable strings of operations, and the cohomology of $N(\C)$ is the
natural home for obstructions to gluing local data along $\C$.

In a companion note~\cite{h1note} we asked when inventory, recorded as counts transported along
operations, glues consistently. Writing the inventory as a resource presheaf $R$ on $\C$, the
answer was clean: consistency is the vanishing of a \emph{degree-one} class
$[\theta_R]\in H^1(N(\C);R)$, the route-reconvergence discrepancy of stock counts, and this
class is logically independent of the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld obstruction $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;
\Dfr)$ that governs whether $\C$ factors as a product of two chains at all~\cite{gobstr,orch}.
That note flagged one refinement as the natural next question, and the present note answers it.

\paragraph{The gap.}
Equality of counts is the wrong comparison when lots are \emph{fungible}. Two pallets of the
same grade are interchangeable; a local stock-state is a labelled configuration, but the labels
carry no information, so two charts should be regarded as matching when they are
\emph{isomorphic}, not when they are literally equal. Each inventory fibre $F$ then carries a
\emph{relabelling group} $K:=\Aut(F)$, and comparison of local charts is canonical only up to
$K$. Gluing up-to-isomorphism is not a gluing-of-sets problem; it is a gluing-of-\emph{stacks}
problem --- a gerbe. The companion note anticipated that the obstruction would then rise to
$H^2$, but neither the coefficient system nor its relationship to the weld was determined. Does
the obstruction equal $[\omega]$ (which also lives in $H^2$)? What exactly must vanish for a
strict global inventory to exist? For which fibres is the phenomenon even nonvacuous?

\paragraph{The results.}
We make the refinement precise and settle all three questions. Fix a fibre $F$ with relabelling
group $K=\Aut(F)$, centre $\Zc:=Z(K)$, and inner group $\Inn K:=K/\Zc$, giving the central
extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
1 \longrightarrow \Zc \longrightarrow K \xrightarrow{\ \pi\ } \Inn K \longrightarrow 1.
\tag{$\star$}
\end{equation}
Under a scope hypothesis stated below (the gerbe's band is trivialised), the gluing data is a
$K$-gerbe over $N(\C)$ presented by a normalised $2$-cochain $\omega\in C^2(N(\C);\Zc)$, and its
class $[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$ is the tier-two obstruction. Our main results are:

\begin{theorem}[Rectification $\Leftrightarrow$ vanishing; Theorem~A]\label{thm:A-intro}
A strict global inventory --- a strict functor $\C\to BK$ realising the up-to-iso gluing ---
exists if and only if $[\gerbe]=0$ in $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Degree-genuineness; Theorem~B]\label{thm:B-intro}
If $N(\C)$ is homotopy $1$-truncated --- in particular if $\C$ is the free category on a
directed acyclic quiver (a tree-like or DAG chain) --- then $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)=0$, so $[\gerbe]=0$
for every fibre. There is a minimal one-node witness, $\C=B(\Z/2)$ with $K=\Z/2$, for which
$[\gerbe]\neq0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Independence from the weld; Theorem~C]\label{thm:C-intro}
$[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ and the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\Dfr)$ are
logically independent: neither vanishing implies the other. They are classes of the \emph{same
degree} over \emph{different coefficient systems} --- the fibre centre $Z(\Aut F)$ versus the
weld vertex-group presheaf $\Dfr$ --- and we exhibit chains realising each of
$([\gerbe]\neq0,[\omega]=0)$ and $([\gerbe]=0,[\omega]\neq0)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp nonvacuousness; Proposition~D]\label{prop:D-intro}
$[\gerbe]$ can be nonzero only if $Z(\Aut F)\neq1$. For $F$ a bare $n$-element set of
indistinguishable lots, $\Aut(F)=S_n$ has $Z(S_n)=1$ for $n\geq3$; hence generic $\geq3$-fold
fungibility carries no tier-two obstruction, and the gerbe is a phenomenon of two-fold or
gauge/torsor-structured ambiguity.
\end{proposition}

The main result is thus visible by page two, and the picture is a \emph{two-tier} obstruction.
Tier one compares stock by equality; tier two compares it up to relabelling; the two live in
consecutive degrees over different coefficients, and the weld is a third, structural
precondition orthogonal to both:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
tier & charts compared by & obstruction & coefficients, degree\\
\midrule
1 & equality of counts & $[\theta_R]$ & $R$ (resource group), $H^1$\\
2 & iso of stock-states (relabelling) & $[\gerbe]$ & $Z(\Aut F)$ (fibre centre), $H^2$\\
\midrule
(precond.) & does $\C$ factor at all & $[\omega]$ & $\Dfr$ (weld vertex groups), $H^2$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{The method.}
Two standard facts, specialised to $N(\C)$, do the work. First, the \emph{band} of a
band-trivialised $K$-gerbe is the centre $Z(K)$ with trivial $\C$-action (Lemma~\ref{lem:band}):
inner automorphisms can be undone by re-choosing local trivialisations, so only central
ambiguity survives global comparison. Second, when $K$ is nonabelian the unambiguous datum is a
functor $\rho:\C\to B(\Inn K)$, and the gerbe is its \emph{gerbe of lifts} through~\eqref{eq:star};
choosing set-section lifts $T_f=s(\rho_f)$ exhibits a $Z$-valued $2$-cocycle $\omega$ whose class
is $[\gerbe]$. Rectification then reads as trivialising $\omega$ by a central $1$-cochain --- an
elementary coboundary computation (Theorem~A) that is uniform across the abelian and nonabelian
faces. The remaining theorems are a simplicial computation on the nerve (Theorem~B), a
coefficient comparison with explicit witnesses (Theorem~C), and the classical centre table
(Proposition~D).

\paragraph{Consequences and placement.}
Three points are worth stating up front. (i) The refinement is \emph{exactly} one categorical
level: equality-gluing is $H^1$, iso-gluing is $H^2$, and nothing in between. (ii) Because
$[\gerbe]$ and $[\omega]$ share a degree, the temptation to identify ``inventory is inconsistent
up to relabelling'' with ``the chain fails to weld'' is now a \emph{degree-matched} trap, and the
only discriminator is the coefficient system; Theorem~C checks it. (iii) The nonvacuousness
criterion sharpens the applications story: the economically relevant tier-two case is not plain
fungibility of many interchangeable units (centreless $S_n$) but \emph{gauge-valued} stock ---
allocation defined up to a group action --- whose automorphism group has a centre. Within the
Kodamai program, $[\gerbe]$ is the \emph{fourth} distinct $H^2$ home on $\mathrm{DCont}\simeq
\Cat$, after the Zappa--Sz\'ep holonomy, Ferri prunability, and the Baues--Wirsching class
$H^2_{\Cat}$; unlike the first three it is distinguished by \emph{coefficients}, not by site.

\paragraph{Outline.}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} recalls supply chains as directed containers, the central
extension~\eqref{eq:star}, and the gerbe vocabulary we use. Section~\ref{sec:gerbe} sets up the
tier-two datum, proves the band is the centre (Lemma~\ref{lem:band}), and exhibits the cocycle
in both the abelian and nonabelian faces. Section~\ref{sec:A} proves Theorem~A.
Section~\ref{sec:B} proves degree-genuineness (Theorem~B). Section~\ref{sec:C} proves
independence from the weld (Theorem~C). Section~\ref{sec:D} proves the nonvacuousness criterion
(Proposition~D). Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} states the scope, the honest open problem (a
nontrivial outer band), and the two-tier bottom line.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

The section records only what the proofs use. A reader fluent in directed containers and
nonabelian $H^2$ can skip to Section~\ref{sec:gerbe}.

\subsection{Supply chains as directed containers and their nerve}

A supply chain is modelled as a small category $\C$: objects are nodes (warehouses, plants,
stock locations), morphisms are admissible logistics operations, composition is sequencing. By
Ahman--Uustalu~\cite{au-dccat} a small category is the same thing as a directed container, so the
model lives natively in the container program.

The \emph{nerve} $N(\C)$ is the simplicial set with $N(\C)_0=\Ob\C$, $N(\C)_1=\Hom\C$, and
$N(\C)_n$ the set of composable strings $c_0\xrightarrow{f_1}c_1\to\cdots\xrightarrow{f_n}c_n$.
For an abelian coefficient group $A$ with (here) trivial $\C$-action, $H^\bullet(N(\C);A)$ is the
cohomology of the normalised cochain complex $C^n(N(\C);A)=\{\,u:N(\C)_n\to A\mid u \text{
vanishes on degeneracies}\,\}$ with the usual alternating-face differential. Two facts are used
below:
\begin{itemize}
\item For a one-object category $\C=BG$ (a group $G$ viewed as a category), $N(BG)$ is the
standard classifying space and $H^\bullet(N(BG);A)=H^\bullet(G;A)$ is group cohomology.
\item For the free category $F(Q)$ on a quiver $Q$, the classifying space $|N(F(Q))|$ is homotopy
equivalent to the geometric realisation $|Q|$, a $1$-complex; hence $H^{\geq2}(N(F(Q));A)=0$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inventory fibres and the central extension}\label{sec:prelim-ext}

An \emph{inventory fibre} $F$ is the local type of a stock-state at a node: a configuration of
lots. Fungibility means the labels carry no information, so the relevant symmetry is the
\emph{relabelling group}
\[
K:=\Aut(F),\qquad \Zc:=Z(K)\ \text{(centre)},\qquad \Inn K:=K/\Zc\ \text{(inner group)},
\]
fitting into the central extension~\eqref{eq:star}. We write $\pi:K\to\Inn K$ for the quotient
and reserve $s:\Inn K\to K$ for a chosen \emph{set-section} with $s(e)=e$ (not assumed to be a
homomorphism).

\subsection{Gerbes, bands, and rectification}\label{sec:prelim-gerbe}

We use the gerbe vocabulary only to \emph{name} the object and import two classical facts; the
cocycles and all computations are derived in place.

A \emph{gerbe} over $N(\C)$ (for us: a $K$-gerbe) is a stack of groupoids that is locally
nonempty and locally connected --- locally equivalent to the trivial gerbe $BK$. Its local
objects are \emph{inventory trivialisations}; its \emph{band} (lien) is the sheaf of
automorphisms of those local objects. A gerbe has a \emph{global section} (a global object)
precisely when it is equivalent to the trivial gerbe. For an abelian band $A$, isomorphism
classes of $A$-gerbes over $N(\C)$ are classified by $H^2(N(\C);A)$, with the trivial class
corresponding to the gerbe that admits a global section (Giraud~\cite{giraud}, III; see also
Breen~\cite{breen} for the bitorsor/cocycle presentation). When the band is nonabelian the
classification is Giraud's full nonabelian $H^2$; we will not need it, because our scope
hypothesis trivialises the band to its centre (Lemma~\ref{lem:band}).

A \emph{strict global inventory} is a global section realised \emph{on the nose}: a strict
functor $\C\to BK$ whose transition data satisfy the gluing equations strictly rather than up to
coherent isomorphism. The passage from a pseudofunctor (coherent, up-to-iso gluing) to a strict
functor is \emph{rectification}; that a pseudofunctor into a bicategory can be replaced by an
equivalent strict one, and the obstruction to doing so compatibly with prescribed data, is the
Bénabou--Street rectification circle of ideas~\cite{benabou,street}. We cite these only to place
the object; the obstruction class and the rectification algebra are computed in
Sections~\ref{sec:gerbe}--\ref{sec:A}.

\section{The inventory gerbe: setup, band, and cocycle}\label{sec:gerbe}

\subsection{The tier-two datum}

\begin{definition}[Tier-two inventory datum]\label{def:tier2}
Fix an inventory fibre $F$ with $K=\Aut(F)$. Suppose local inventory charts at the nodes of $\C$
are comparable only up to relabelling, i.e.\ the transition along an operation $f$ is a
$K$-torsor of chart-identifications rather than a single one. The gluing data is then a
\emph{$K$-gerbe over $N(\C)$}: a stack whose local objects are inventory trivialisations and
whose automorphism band is (a subsheaf of) $K$. A \emph{strict global inventory} is a strict
global section of this gerbe.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scope hypothesis: trivial band]\label{def:scope}
We fix the \emph{same} fibre $F$ and relabelling group $K$ at every node and \emph{trivialise the
outer action}: the band/lien of the gerbe is the trivial (constant) one. A nontrivial outer band
twists the coefficients to a local system $\Zc_\rho$ and introduces a prior Giraud band
obstruction; that genuinely nonabelian case is outside this note and is marked open in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
\end{definition}

Under Definition~\ref{def:scope} the gerbe is \emph{band-trivialised}, and the next lemma
identifies the coefficients.

\subsection{The band is the centre}

\begin{lemma}[Band $=$ centre]\label{lem:band}
The band of a band-trivialised $K$-gerbe is the constant abelian sheaf $Z(K)$, with trivial
$\C$-action.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The band is the sheaf of automorphisms of the gerbe's local objects. Two local trivialisations
of the same fibre differ by an element of $K$, and an automorphism of a local object is realised
by an element $z\in K$ acting on every chart; for the action to be independent of the choice of
trivialisation --- i.e.\ to descend to the band of the band-trivialised gerbe --- $z$ must commute
with all of $K$, so $z\in Z(K)$. Equivalently, $Z(K)$ is the group of $2$-automorphisms of the
identity autoequivalence of the fibre (the Eckmann--Hilton centre of the automorphism $2$-group
of $F$~\cite{bl,js}). Inner automorphisms, which \emph{can} be undone by re-choosing local
trivialisations, fix $Z(K)$ pointwise, so the $\C$-action on the band is trivial. This is the
standard gerbe-band formalism (Giraud~\cite{giraud}, III; Breen~\cite{breen}); we import it
rather than re-derive it.
\end{proof}

So the coefficients are $Z(K)=Z(\Aut F)$ with trivial action, and the gerbe is classified by
$H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$.

\subsection{The cocycle in each face}

There are two faces, according to whether $K=\Aut(F)$ is abelian. Both have band $Z(K)$; both are
needed, because the minimal witness is abelian while the smallest nonabelian witnesses are
$D_4$ and $Q_8$.

\paragraph{Face 1 --- abelian relabelling ($K$ abelian, $Z(K)=K$).}
Here the fibre is a $K$-torsor-stack: stock is defined up to the gauge group $K$, and the torsor
itself only locally. The gerbe is a genuine abelian $K$-gerbe, presented by a normalised
$2$-cocycle $\omega\in Z^2(N(\C);K)$ (its transition cocycle), and $[\gerbe]:=[\omega]\in
H^2(N(\C);K)$. That a global section exists iff $[\omega]=0$ is the defining property of the
abelian-gerbe classification $H^2(N(\C);K)\cong\{K\text{-gerbes}\}/{\simeq}$, with the trivial
gerbe the unique one admitting a section (Giraud~\cite{giraud}). The minimal $B(\Z/2)$ witness of
Section~\ref{sec:B} lives in this face.

\paragraph{Face 2 --- nonabelian relabelling, trivial band (the lifting gerbe).}
When $K$ is nonabelian the inner part of a relabelling composes strictly: re-trivialising a fibre
conjugates the transport, so comparison of charts is canonical only up to inner automorphism, and
the unambiguous datum is a genuine functor
\[
\rho:\C\longrightarrow B(\Inn K).
\]
The gerbe is the \emph{gerbe of lifts of $\rho$ through $\pi$} in~\eqref{eq:star}. Choose
set-section lifts $T_f:=s(\rho_f)\in K$ for each operation $f$. Because both $T_gT_f$ and $T_{gf}$
map to $\rho_g\rho_f=\rho_{gf}$ under $\pi$, their ratio lands in $\ker\pi=\Zc$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defect}
T_gT_f=\omega(g,f)\cdot T_{gf},\qquad \omega(g,f)\in\Zc.
\tag{$\dagger$}
\end{equation}
Thus $\omega=\rho^*(\text{extension $2$-cocycle of }\eqref{eq:star})$ is a normalised
$2$-cochain. Associativity of multiplication in $K$, together with the centrality of $\Zc$ (so
every conjugation acts trivially on the values of $\omega$), gives the normalised $2$-cocycle
identity
\[
\omega(h,g)\,\omega(hg,f)=\omega(g,f)\,\omega(h,gf),\qquad\text{i.e. } \delta\omega=0,
\]
whence $[\gerbe]:=\rho^*[\star]=[\omega]\in H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$. Changing the section $s$ replaces
$\omega$ by a cohomologous cochain, so $[\gerbe]$ is well-defined.

In both faces $[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ and the rectification freedom is a $1$-cochain
valued in $Z(\Aut F)$; the next section treats both uniformly.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Rectification is the vanishing of $[\gerbe]$}{Rectification is the vanishing of the gerbe class}}\label{sec:A}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~A]\label{thm:A}
A strict global inventory --- a strict functor $\C\to BK$ realising the gluing of
Definition~\ref{def:tier2} --- exists if and only if $[\gerbe]=0$ in $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$, where
$\Zc=Z(\Aut F)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Re-choosing the local trivialisation at each operation $f$ multiplies the transition datum $T_f$
by an element $c_f$ of the band $\Zc=Z(\Aut F)$. (In Face~2, $c_f\in\Zc$ moves the lift $T_f$
within its $\Zc$-torsor of lifts of $\rho_f$; in Face~1, with $K$ abelian, $c_f\in K=\Zc$ rescales
the abelian-gerbe transition directly.) A candidate strict inventory is therefore a $1$-cochain
$c:\mathrm{Mor}\,\C\to\Zc$ producing new transitions $T'_f=c_f\cdot T_f$, and strictness is the
demand that $T'_{gf}=T'_gT'_f$. Using~\eqref{eq:defect} and the centrality of each $c_f$,
\[
c_{gf}\,T_{gf}=T'_{gf}\overset{!}{=}T'_gT'_f=c_g c_f\,T_gT_f=c_g c_f\,\omega(g,f)\,T_{gf},
\]
which holds iff
\[
\omega(g,f)=c_{gf}\,c_g^{-1}\,c_f^{-1}=(\delta c^{-1})(g,f).
\]
Hence a strict global inventory exists iff $\omega$ is the coboundary of a $\Zc$-valued
$1$-cochain on $N(\C)$, i.e.\ iff $[\gerbe]=[\omega]=0$ in $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$. The computation uses
only that each $c_f$ is central, so it is uniform across the two faces.
\end{proof}

This is the central-extension lifting criterion (Face~2) and the abelian-gerbe triviality
criterion (Face~1), both specialised to $N(\C)$; Giraud and Breen are cited only to name the
object, while the cocycle, its class, and the rectification algebra are derived above.

\begin{remark}[Concrete check: $D_4$ and $Q_8$]\label{rem:d4q8}
Take $\C=B((\Z/2)^2)$ and $K\in\{D_4,Q_8\}$, each with $Z(K)=\Z/2$, $\Inn K=(\Z/2)^2$, and
$\rho=\id$ (the inner transport is the full inner group). A strict lift would be a splitting
homomorphism $(\Z/2)^2\to K$ of~\eqref{eq:star}; none exists, since $K$ is nonabelian while
$(\Z/2)^2$ is abelian. Correspondingly the section-cocycle $\omega$ of~\eqref{eq:defect} is not a
coboundary, so $[\gerbe]\neq0$ and rectification fails --- exactly when the class is nonzero. Both
the non-splitting and $[\gerbe]\neq0$ are confirmed by brute-force enumeration
(\texttt{inventory\_gerbe\_h2.py}).
\end{remark}

\section{\texorpdfstring{$[\gerbe]$ is genuinely a degree-two datum}{The gerbe class is genuinely a degree-two datum}}\label{sec:B}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~B]\label{thm:B}
\emph{(i)} If $N(\C)$ is homotopy $1$-truncated --- in particular if $\C$ is the free category on
a directed acyclic quiver (a tree-like or DAG supply chain) --- then $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)=0$, so
$[\gerbe]=0$ for every fibre $F$. \emph{(ii)} There is a minimal one-node witness, $\C=B(\Z/2)$
with $K=\Z/2$, for which $[\gerbe]\neq0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (i)]
The classifying space of the free category $F(Q)$ on a quiver $Q$ is homotopy equivalent to
$|Q|$, a $1$-complex (no relations means no higher cells up to homotopy), so
$H^{\geq2}(N(F(Q));\Zc)=0$. It is worth seeing this directly on the reconvergent \emph{diamond}
$a\rightrightarrows\{b,c\}\rightrightarrows d$ --- the paradigmatic two-route chain --- because
its nerve is \emph{not} literally $1$-dimensional: it carries two nondegenerate $2$-simplices,
the composites $p;r$ and $q;s$ of the two routes. A direct simplicial computation over
$\mathbb{F}_2$ (\texttt{diamond\_nerve\_H2.py}) gives chain dimensions $(C^0,C^1,C^2)=(4,6,2)$,
boundary ranks $\mathrm{rk}\,d^0=3$ and $\mathrm{rk}\,d^1=2$, and hence
\[
H^0=1,\qquad H^1=1,\qquad H^2=0,\qquad \chi=4-6+2=0.
\]
The lone $H^1$ is precisely the tier-one reconvergence loop $[\theta_R]$; $H^2=0$ says there is
\emph{no gerbe on a DAG}, even one with genuine $2$-simplices.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of (ii)]
For $G=\Z/2$ and coefficients $\Z/2$ one has $H^n(\Z/2;\Z/2)=\Z/2$ for all $n\geq0$
(\texttt{inventory\_gerbe\_h2.py}); in particular $H^2(\Z/2;\Z/2)=\Z/2\neq0$. The nonzero class
is the extension $1\to\Z/2\to\Z/4\to\Z/2\to1$. Concretely, a reversible two-lot swap $g$ (with
$g^2=\id$ in $\C$) has a relabelling transport $T_g$ that cannot be chosen to satisfy
$T_g^2=\id$ strictly: rescaling $T_g$ by $a\in\Z/2$ changes $T_g^2$ by $2a=0$, so the
``square-root ambiguity'' cannot be absorbed --- one would need $\Z/4$ to lift. Hence
$[\gerbe]\neq0$.
\end{proof}

Thus $[\gerbe]$ lives at cohomological degree $2$ (nerve level $N_3$), vanishing on every
acyclic chain yet already nonzero on a single reversible two-lot loop. For the record, the
richer witness $H^2((\Z/2)^2;\Z/2)=\mathbb{F}_2^3$ shows that $(\Z/2)^2$ carries three
independent $\Z/2$-gerbes --- the $D_4$, $Q_8$, and $\Z/4\times\Z/2$ classes --- of which the
first two were used in Remark~\ref{rem:d4q8}.

\section{Independence from the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld}\label{sec:C}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem~C]\label{thm:C}
$[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ and the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\Dfr)$ are
logically independent: neither vanishing implies the other. They are classes of the same degree
over different coefficient systems --- the inventory-fibre centre $Z(\Aut F)$ versus the weld
vertex-group presheaf $\Dfr$ --- so, unlike the tier-one $H^1$ versus $H^2$ separation, the
distinction is not by degree but by coefficients, and must be checked by witnesses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The weld $[\omega]$ is determined by $\C$ alone: it obstructs the closing of a Zappa--Sz\'ep
transversal, i.e.\ whether $\C$ factors as procurement $\bowtiemap$ logistics~\cite{gobstr,orch}.
The gerbe $[\gerbe]$ depends on the inventory fibre $F$, which is data \emph{external} to $\C$.
For any fixed $\C$: choosing $F=\mathrm{pt}$ (so $\Aut F=1$, strict inventory) forces $[\gerbe]=0$
whatever $[\omega]$ is; choosing $\C=B(\Z/2)$ with $\Aut F=\Z/2$ forces $[\gerbe]\neq0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:B}) regardless of the weld. Since the two classes obstruct different data, no
implication holds. We make both directions explicit.

\emph{Witness $W_1'$ ($[\gerbe]\neq0,\ [\omega]=0$).} Let $\C=B(\Z/2)$, one reversible operation,
with the trivial Zappa--Sz\'ep factorisation $\Dfr=0$, forcing $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;0)=0$. Take
inventory relabelling $K=\Z/2$; then $[\gerbe]$ is the generator of $H^2(\Z/2;\Z/2)=\Z/2\neq0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:B}). The chain carries a non-rectifiable relabelling gerbe but no weld structure
at all.

\emph{Witness $W_2'$ ($[\gerbe]=0,\ [\omega]\neq0$).} Let $\Sk_\C=(a\to x\rightrightarrows y)$ be
the rigid-twist skeleton with $\Dfr=\Z/2$ and $[\omega]$ the generator of
$H^2(\Sk_\C;\Z/2)=\Z/2\neq0$~\cite{gobstr}, equipped with strict inventory ($F=\mathrm{pt}$,
$\Aut F=1$), so $[\gerbe]=0$. The chain fails to factor, yet its (trivial-relabelling) inventory
rectifies vacuously.
\end{proof}

The coefficient systems genuinely differ: in $W_1'$ the weld coefficient $\Dfr=0$ while the gerbe
coefficient $Z(\Aut F)=\Z/2\neq0$. There is no natural map comparing $H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ with
$H^2(\Sk_\C;\Dfr)$, let alone an identification --- the same ``rhyme, not identity'' trap as for
the two distinct $[\omega]$-sites studied earlier~\cite{twosites}, but now with \emph{matching}
degree, so the coefficient system is the only discriminator and must be examined.

\begin{remark}[Relation to tier one]\label{rem:tier1}
The tier-one class $[\theta_R]\in H^1$ and the tier-two class $[\gerbe]\in H^2$ differ in degree,
hence are immediately independent. The full inventory obstruction is the two-tier filtration with
associated graded $H^1(N(\C);R)$ (counts) and $H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$ (relabellings), with
$[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\Dfr)$ a separate structural precondition. Whether the two tiers combine
as a split sum or a nontrivial extension (a $d_3$-type coupling $H^1\to H^3$) is not determined
here; see Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
\end{remark}

\section{When the gerbe is nonvacuous}\label{sec:D}

A gerbe needs nonzero centre. This is a genuine --- and slightly surprising --- constraint on
\emph{which} fibres carry a tier-two obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Proposition~D]\label{prop:D}
$[\gerbe]$ can be nonzero only if $Z(\Aut F)\neq1$. In particular, for $F$ a bare $n$-element set
of indistinguishable lots, $\Aut(F)=S_n$ with
\[
Z(S_n)=1\ (n\geq3),\qquad Z(S_2)=\Z/2,\qquad Z(S_n)=1\ (n\leq1),
\]
so the tier-two gerbe is vacuous (always $[\gerbe]=0$) for $n\geq3$ fungible lots and nonvacuous
only for $n=2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:band} the coefficients are $Z(\Aut F)$, and $H^2(N(\C);1)=0$, so $[\gerbe]=0$
whenever $Z(\Aut F)=1$. The centre table for $S_n$ ($n\leq5$) is confirmed by direct computation
(\texttt{inventory\_gerbe\_h2.py}); $Z(S_n)=1$ for $n\geq3$ is classical.
\end{proof}

So a nontrivial relabelling gerbe requires either \emph{exactly two} fungible lots, or a
\emph{structured} fibre whose automorphism group has a centre: e.g.\ $F$ a $K$-\emph{torsor}
(stock defined up to a gauge group $K$, automorphisms acting by translation, centre $Z(K)$ ---
this is exactly the torsor-valued inventory the companion note anticipated), or graded/typed lots
with central symmetries (e.g.\ $\Aut F=\mathrm{GL}_k$, centre the scalars). The interpretation is
economic: generic fungibility of $\geq3$ interchangeable units creates \emph{no} degree-two
obstruction --- any relabelling ambiguity is absorbed by inner reparametrisation, because $S_n$
is centreless --- while two-fold or gauge/torsor-structured ambiguity does. The tier-two case
that matters for applications is therefore gauge-valued stock (allocation up to a group action),
not plain fungibility.

\section{Scope, open problem, and the two-tier bottom line}\label{sec:conclusion}

Refining inventory comparison from \emph{equality of counts} to \emph{isomorphism of
stock-states} raises the consistency obstruction by exactly one categorical level: from the
degree-one count-discrepancy class $[\theta_R]\in H^1(N(\C);R)$ to a degree-two gerbe class
$[\gerbe]\in H^2(N(\C);Z(\Aut F))$. A strict global inventory exists iff $[\gerbe]=0$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:A}); $[\gerbe]$ is genuinely degree-two, vanishing on every DAG or tree chain
but nonzero already on a single reversible two-lot loop $B(\Z/2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}); it is
independent of the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld $[\omega]$, with which it shares a degree but not a
coefficient system (Theorem~\ref{thm:C}); and it is nonvacuous exactly when $Z(\Aut F)\neq1$ ---
two fungible lots or gauge/torsor-structured stock, not generic $\geq3$-fold fungibility
(Proposition~\ref{prop:D}). Inventory consistency is thus a two-tier theory: \textbf{$H^1$ counts,
$H^2$ relabellings}, over a structural precondition $[\omega]$. Within the container program this
is the fourth distinct $H^2$ home on $\mathrm{DCont}\simeq\Cat$, after Zappa--Sz\'ep holonomy,
Ferri prunability, and the Baues--Wirsching class --- the first to be distinguished by its
coefficient system rather than its site.

\paragraph{Scope hypotheses (stated honestly).}
Two hypotheses bound the result.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Trivial band} (Definition~\ref{def:scope}): the same fibre $F$ and relabelling group
$K$ at every node, with the outer action trivialised. The computations above all live under this
hypothesis.
\item $[\gerbe]$ is the \emph{rectification} obstruction --- the existence of a strict global
section. Classifying all tier-two inventories up to equivalence (not merely their existence) is
Giraud's full nonabelian $H^2$, a torsor over $H^2(N(\C);\Zc)$ once nonempty; that is not treated
here.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The open problem (not claimed).}
A \emph{nontrivial outer band} --- different fibres or a nontrivial $\C$-action on $K$ --- twists
the coefficients to a local system $Z_\rho$ and introduces a \emph{prior} Giraud band obstruction,
a genuinely nonabelian $H^2$ class whose vanishing is necessary before the abelian rectification
class $[\gerbe]$ is even defined. We do not have this band obstruction; determining it for
directed-container supply chains with heterogeneous fibres is left open. Relatedly (see
Remark~\ref{rem:tier1}), the coupling between tiers one and two --- whether they split or form a
Postnikov-type extension via a $d_3$ map $H^1\to H^3$ --- is stated as a filtration but not
computed; a bar-complex oracle for the composite nerve $B(\Z/2)$ glued to the diamond is the
natural next verification.

\paragraph{For the grant.}
The worked economic example to develop is the torsor-fibre case $\Aut F=K$ acting on itself, with
$Z(\Aut F)=Z(K)$ --- gauge-valued allocation, where stock is defined only up to a group action
and the tier-two obstruction has real content. This is the applications payoff of
Proposition~\ref{prop:D}: the economically relevant non-rectifiable inventory is not the
many-interchangeable-units case but the gauge-structured one.
