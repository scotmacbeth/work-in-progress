\title[Directed containers are the object-level model of a composable agent]{Directed
containers are the object-level model\\ of a composable agent\\[4pt]
\large A convergence of four programmes on one equivalence}
\author{MacBeth}
\address{Kodamai}
\date{11 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
A directed container is exactly a small category. We argue that this single equivalence ---
Ahman--Uustalu's $\DCont\iso\Cat$ --- is the correct object-level model of a \emph{composable
agent}, and we support the argument by exhibiting four independent lines of work that all reach
for it. The equivalence is a \emph{classification target}: we have proved that the polynomial
monad liftings of the Reader monad are, one level down, small categories, and that the sound
liftings of the State monad embed one. It is
\emph{cited infrastructure}: an external programme in compositional active inference (Smithe,
Topos Institute\,/\,VERSES) invokes it as settled background --- verbatim, ``Famously,
$\seq$-comonoids correspond to categories'' --- to build a substrate of open dynamical systems.
It is a \emph{generalization frontier}: pullback-preserving comonads on $\Set$ are known to
correspond to Grothendieck toposes with enough points, a topos-theoretic refinement approached
from the opposite end. And it is a \emph{charted boundary}: two explicit witnesses (a bag
functor and a multigraph comonad) sit just outside the polynomial world and pin down where the
equivalence stops. We emphasise one honest distinction. The external programme and ours agree on
the \emph{object} --- a composable agent is a directed container --- but compose agents by
structurally different mechanisms: Smithe wires Bayesian lenses in a monoidal bicategory, whereas
we weld two agent categories by a Zappa--Sz\'ep product whose obstruction is a degree-two
cohomology class. Convergence on the object; divergence on the composition law. This is the
object-level foundation on which the Kodamai grant's compositional-agent narrative rests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection*{The object-level question}
Before one can ask how two agents \emph{compose}, one must answer a prior question: what
\emph{is} an agent, as a mathematical object? The applied-category-theory literature on
multi-agent systems has several answers --- an agent is a sheaf section, a coalgebra, a lens, a
node in a wiring diagram --- and the profusion is a symptom. Composition theorems are only as
good as the objects they compose. If the object is wrong, the composition law inherits the
wrongness.

This note makes and defends one answer. \emph{At the object level, a composable agent is a small
category --- equivalently, a directed container.} An agent exposes an interface: a set $S$ of
things it can be asked (requests, prompts, states) and, for each, a set $P(s)$ of things it can
reply. That is a container $(S \seq P)$. The agent is moreover \emph{directed}: replies can be
composed and there is a null reply, so the container carries the extra structure that makes its
$\Set$-endofunctor a comonad. Ahman and Uustalu proved that a directed container is exactly a
small category \cite{AhmanUustalu}. The agent's roles are objects, its admissible reply-chains
are morphisms, and its handoff structure is composition. \emph{The object is a category, and the
category is a directed container.}

\subsection*{The claim, and why it is not obvious}
That a single interface \emph{is} a small category is a clean statement, but on its own it is
just a re-description. What elevates $\DCont\iso\Cat$ from a re-description to a foundation is
that it is \emph{load-bearing in four independent ways at once}. During a single week of the
author's programme, the same theorem surfaced as the target of a classification, the cited
background of an external application, the special case of a topos-theoretic generalization, and
the interior of a boundary result. Four activities that look unrelated are one theorem seen from
four sides. That is the empirical content of ``this is the right object'': the best objects are
the ones many independent lines of work cannot avoid.

We state the main result of the note --- which is a thesis supported by evidence, not a new
theorem --- on this page.

\begin{quote}\itshape
The equivalence $\DCont\iso\Cat$ is the object-level model of a composable agent. It is
simultaneously (1) a classification target for the monad liftings that model effectful agents,
(2) the cited infrastructure of an independent programme in compositional active inference, (3)
a special case of a topos-theoretic generalization, and (4) a theorem whose boundary is charted
by explicit non-examples. Independent programmes converge on this object; they diverge only on
the law by which such objects compose.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{The surprise, and the discipline it demands}
The sharpest of the four fronts is the second. Toby Smithe's \emph{Compositional Active
Inference II} \cite{SmitheCAI2}, written from the Topos Institute (later VERSES) and rooted in
the free-energy / predictive-coding tradition --- a world with no obvious contact with directed
containers --- reaches for \emph{exactly} our equivalence, and treats it as textbook background.
At Proposition~2.7 the paper reads, verbatim, ``Famously, $\seq$-comonoids correspond to
categories and their comonoid homomorphisms are cofunctors'' \cite[Prop.~2.7]{SmitheCAI2},
citing Ahman--Uustalu. Two programmes that began at opposite ends --- ours from container
combinatorics, Smithe's from Bayesian inference --- arrive at the same object.

This convergence is genuine, and it is also easy to overclaim, so we impose a discipline on
ourselves throughout. Smithe uses $\DCont\iso\Cat$ as the substrate for open \emph{dynamical
systems} (the codiscrete comonoid $S y^S$ on a state space), not as the mechanism by which
agents compose; in his framework agents compose as \emph{Bayesian lenses} in a monoidal
bicategory. Our own composition law is different: a Zappa--Sz\'ep weld $\C\bowtie\D$ of two agent
categories --- the directed-container weld of Ahman--Uustalu \cite{AhmanUustaluDL}, which we
read into agents and equip with an $H^2$ obstruction. So the honest claim is that independent programmes
converge on \emph{the object}, $\DCont\iso\Cat$; it is \emph{not} that anyone but us composes
agents by the weld. Section~\ref{sec:contrast} states this contrast precisely, because getting
it wrong would turn a real convergence into a false scoop.

\subsection*{Outline}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} recalls the container/category dictionary and fixes the
agent-as-container reading. Section~\ref{sec:fronts} presents the four fronts: the classification
into the equivalence (\ref{sub:into}), its use as external infrastructure (\ref{sub:cited}), its
generalization upward (\ref{sub:up}), and its boundary from outside (\ref{sub:boundary}).
Section~\ref{sec:contrast} isolates the object/composition-law distinction between Smithe's
programme and ours. Section~\ref{sec:grant} states what the convergence buys a grant that
proposes to found a theory of compositional agents.

\section{Preliminaries: the object and its dictionary}\label{sec:prelim}

We recall only what the four fronts use. Proofs of the cited facts live in their sources and in
the author's expository notes; none are reproduced here.

\subsection*{Containers and directed containers}
A \emph{container} is a pair $(S \seq P)$ of a set $S$ of \emph{shapes} and a family
$P\colon S\to\Set$ of \emph{positions}; it presents the polynomial endofunctor
$X\mapsto\sum_{s\in S}X^{P(s)}$ on $\Set$. A \emph{directed container} equips $(S\seq P)$ with
operations $(o,\downarrow,\oplus)$ --- a root position, a re-indexing, and a position addition
--- satisfying five laws (D1--D5) which are exactly the conditions making the induced functor a
comonad \cite{AhmanUustalu}. Write $\DCont$ for the category of directed containers; the
positions of a directed container form a $\seq$-comonoid in the category $\Poly$ of polynomial
functors under substitution \cite{NiuSpivak}.

\begin{theorem}[Ahman--Uustalu \cite{AhmanUustalu}]\label{thm:AU}
\gExternal\ There is an equivalence $\DCont\iso\Cat$ between directed containers and small
categories. A directed container's shapes are the objects, its positions over a shape are the
morphisms out of that object, the root is the identity, and $\oplus$ is composition.
Under this equivalence, directed-container morphisms are the \emph{cofunctors} (equivalently
retrofunctors), so $\DCont\iso\Cof$ as well.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:AU} is the object of this entire note. Three of its instances recur below and
deserve names in the polynomial language.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Delooping.} A monoid $T$ is a one-object category $BT$; as a comonoid this is the
representable $y^T$ \cite[Prop.~2.7]{SmitheCAI2}.
\item \textbf{Codiscrete comonoid.} For a set $S$, the monomial $S y^{S}$ carries a canonical
comonoid structure --- the codiscrete groupoid on $S$, equivalently the store comonad. We have
elsewhere written this $\Delta S$ and shown $\Delta S \otimes \Delta T \iso \Delta(S\times T)$
\gLean.
\item \textbf{The fibrewise op.} The operation that reverses a container's positions fibrewise is
what makes a container \emph{directed}, and it is the same operation that reads a monad lifting on
$\Cont$ as a comonad --- hence a category --- on $\Set$. It is the hinge of front~\ref{sub:into}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Agents are directed containers}
The reading that turns Theorem~\ref{thm:AU} into an applied statement is due to the
Poly/interaction school and, in the agent setting, to the framing used at Kodamai: an agent is a
container whose shapes are the questions it can be asked and whose positions are the answers it
can give \cite{NiuSpivak}. A morphism to the representable $y$ is a single oracle (model) call;
a container morphism is a delegation; a comonad structure is the harness that lets an agent run.
The directedness is not decoration --- it is precisely the composability of an agent's own
replies, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:AU} it is exactly a category structure on the agent's roles. We
take this reading as given and study its consequences; it is the shared premise of every front
below.

\section{Four fronts on one theorem}\label{sec:fronts}

\subsection{Classified into it: effectful agents are categories one level down}\label{sub:into}

An agent that carries an effect is modelled not by a bare container but by a \emph{lifting} of a
monad along the container fibration --- a proof-relevant refinement of a predicate transformer.
The natural question is which liftings exist, and the answer turns out to be another instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:AU}.

\begin{theorem}[Reader and State liftings are categories; author \gProved\ \gLean]\label{thm:liftings}
The proof-relevant polynomial monad liftings of the Reader monad $y^E$ are, one level down,
exactly $E$-indexed small categories; the sound liftings of the State monad embed the product
category $\mathbb{S}\times\C$, and the machine-checked instances count the small monoids on their
fibres. In each case a lifting on $\Cont$ is, via the fibrewise op, a comonad on $\Set$ --- that
is, a category.
\end{theorem}

The point for this note is not the classification's internal detail but its shape: the objects
that classify effectful agents \emph{are the equivalence itself}, relocated one categorical level
down. The equivalence $\DCont\iso\Cat$ is not merely the model of a bare agent; it recurs as the
answer to ``which effectful agents are there?'' The hinge is the fibrewise op of
Section~\ref{sec:prelim}, which is simultaneously what makes a container directed and what reads a
lifting as a category. \emph{The same operation that gives us the object gives us its refinement.}

\begin{remark}[Scope, honestly]
The Reader classification and the sound half of the State classification are proved and, for the
witnessing instances, Lean-verified. The \emph{completeness} of the State classification (that
every sound lifting is of the exhibited form) rests on a single holonomy-triviality lemma that is
argued but not yet fully formalised; it is \gOpen\ at the level of a machine-checked converse.
Nothing in this note depends on that converse: front~\ref{sub:into} needs only that the
equivalence is the classification \emph{target}, which the proved direction already establishes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Cited as infrastructure: an external programme reaches for the same object}\label{sub:cited}

The second front is the one that turns a self-assessment into external evidence. Smithe's
\emph{Compositional Active Inference II} \cite{SmitheCAI2} builds a categorical semantics for
predictive-coding agents. Its substrate is Spivak-style polynomial dynamics: an open dynamical
system is a map out of the codiscrete comonoid $S y^S$ on a state space (Proposition~3.2), and
the condition for a coalgebra to be such a system is stated as its being a
$\seq$-comonoid homomorphism. To license this substrate the paper invokes Theorem~\ref{thm:AU}
directly, at Proposition~2.7, in words worth quoting in full:
\begin{quote}
``Famously, $\seq$-comonoids correspond to categories and their comonoid homomorphisms are
cofunctors [8]. If $T$ is a monoid, then the comonoid structure on $y^{T}$ \dots\ witnesses it as
the category $BT$. Monomials of the form $S y^{S}$ can be equipped with a canonical comonoid
structure witnessing the codiscrete groupoid on $S$.'' \cite[Prop.~2.7]{SmitheCAI2}
\end{quote}
Reference [8] there is Ahman--Uustalu. The word ``Famously'' does the sociological work: the
equivalence is invoked as settled background, neither proved nor re-derived. Every named instance
in that passage --- $\seq$-comonoids as categories, comonoid homs as cofunctors, $y^T$ as $BT$,
$S y^S$ as the codiscrete groupoid --- is an instance we also use.

\begin{proposition}[Independent recognition; \gExternal, deep-read verified]\label{prop:cai}
An external programme (Smithe, Topos Institute\,/\,VERSES; active-inference community) rooted in a
tradition disjoint from container theory uses $\DCont\iso\Cat$ as settled infrastructure for the
dynamical-systems substrate of compositional agents. The convergence is on the \emph{object}: two
independent programmes model a composable agent by the same equivalence.
\end{proposition}

Two caveats keep the proposition honest, and both are load-bearing for the contrast of
Section~\ref{sec:contrast}. First, in \cite{SmitheCAI2} the equivalence underwrites the
\emph{dynamical-systems substrate} (the state comonoids $S y^S$), not the mechanism by which
agents are composed --- there, agents compose as Bayesian lenses, discussed next. Second, the
citation is verified against a deep read of the source: reference [8] at Proposition~2.7,
verbatim as quoted. We therefore lean on it; we do not paraphrase a summary of it.

\subsection{Generalized upward: the topos refinement}\label{sub:up}

The third front approaches Theorem~\ref{thm:AU} from above. The equivalence identifies a
directed container with a category via a \emph{comonad} on $\Set$; asking which comonads on
$\Set$ arise, and pushing the answer past the polynomial case, leads into topos theory.
Pullback-preserving comonads on $\Set$ correspond to Grothendieck toposes with enough points,
via skyscraper/stalk constructions --- a topos-theoretic generalization of the situation
$\DCont\iso\Cat$ describes. The community reaches the theorem from the toposes-with-enough-points
end; the container programme reaches it from the directed-container end; the target is shared.
The most developed written treatment of comonads-as-spaces adjacent to this circle of ideas is
Fairbanks--Carlson--Spivak, \emph{Comonads as Spaces} \cite{ComonadsAsSpaces}, which situates
comonads (directed containers among them) in an explicitly spatial setting.

\begin{remark}[Signpost, not a load-bearing citation]
The precise topos correspondence (pullback-preserving comonads $\iff$ Grothendieck toposes with
enough points) is community knowledge whose cleanest reference --- Garner's ``Ionads'' and the
MathOverflow thread that assembles it --- the author has not yet deep-read. We therefore present
front~\ref{sub:up} as a \gSignpost: the generalization exists and is worth stating as evidence of
the theorem's reach, but it is named, not leaned on. The citable anchor here is
\cite{ComonadsAsSpaces}, which is deep-read; the sharper topos statement awaits a promotion pass.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Bounded from outside: two witnesses at the edge}\label{sub:boundary}

The fourth front charts where the equivalence \emph{stops}, which is as much a part of knowing an
object as knowing where it holds. A directed container is a \emph{polynomial} comonad; some
functors that look categorical are not polynomial, and fail to be categories for a common,
diagnosable reason. Two witnesses, one on each side of the monad/comonad duality, make the
boundary two-sided.

\begin{proposition}[Two boundary witnesses; author \gProved\ / \gComputed]\label{prop:boundary}
On the monad side, the bag (finite-multiset) functor is not a container: a direct count gives
$|\mathrm{Bag}(2\times2)| = 10 \neq 9$, breaking the product-preservation a polynomial functor
would satisfy. On the comonad side, the multigraph comonad
$F(X) = (X^{3}+X^{2})/2 + X$ --- the coequalizer of the identity and the swap on the quiver
comonad $X^{3}+X$ --- is not polynomial. Both fail the same test: neither is the kernel pair of
its terminal map $X\to 1$, the condition characterising the polynomial (hence categorical) case.
\end{proposition}

The bag count is the author's computation; the multigraph comonad is verified verbatim against
its source and belongs to the same spatial circle as front~\ref{sub:up}
\cite{ComonadsAsSpaces}. The moral is that ``a composable agent is a category'' is a
\emph{substantive} claim with a charted exterior: the quotients that identify distinct
reply-histories (the swap on $X^3$, the multiset forgetting of order) leave the
polynomial world, and with it the guarantee that composition is associative and unit-respecting
on the nose. An agent whose interface secretly quotients its histories is not, in this precise
sense, a category --- and front~\ref{sub:boundary} says exactly which quotients do the damage.

\section{Object-level convergence, composition-level divergence}\label{sec:contrast}

Fronts \ref{sub:into}--\ref{sub:boundary} establish that $\DCont\iso\Cat$ is the object.
It remains to say clearly how the two programmes that share this object nonetheless
\emph{differ}, because the difference is exactly where each programme's own contribution lives,
and conflating them would misrepresent both.

\subsection*{Smithe: composition as lens-wiring in a bicategory}
In \cite{SmitheCAI2} agents are open dynamical systems whose interfaces are Bayesian lenses, and
they compose in monoidal bicategories of ``hierarchical inference systems'' (Definition~3.15;
its differential version, Definition~3.21). The systems that live over an external-hom polynomial
and ``control optics'' are named \emph{cilia} (Definition~3.8, Remarks~3.9--3.10). Wiring uses
Bayesian-lens composition --- a forward map paired with a state-dependent backward inversion ---
together with a distributive law of the internal hom over the tensor (Definition~3.13) used to
assemble the bicategory. Composition, in short, is lens-wiring, and its coherence is bicategorical.

\subsection*{Ours: composition as a Zappa--Sz\'ep weld obstructed in $H^2$}
Our composition law is different in kind. Two agents that share a resource each present their own
handoff category, $\C$ and $\D$, and running them together is not a functor along one topology but
an \emph{interleaving} of two. The joint system is a Zappa--Sz\'ep product $\C\bowtie\D$ ---
equivalently a matched-pair distributive law $\lambda\colon\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ of one agent
category over the other. That a distributive law of directed containers assembles this weld,
generalizing the monoid Zappa--Sz\'ep product, is Ahman--Uustalu's \cite{AhmanUustaluDL}; what
is the author's is the applied reading and the obstruction. Unlike a functor, such a weld can
fail to exist, and the author has
proved that the failure is measured in a single cohomological degree: under the standard
hypothesis, the weld exists iff a class $[\omega]\in H^2(\Sk_\C;\dir)$ vanishes, with re-entrancy
the canonical nonzero class ($[\omega]=\varepsilon$, Lean-verified). This is a \emph{monad}
distributive law welding two categories; the obstruction is cohomological.

\subsection*{The precise relationship}
We state the relationship as carefully as the evidence allows.

\begin{proposition}[Convergent peers; contrast, do not conflate]\label{prop:contrast}
Smithe's programme and the author's converge on the object --- a composable agent is a directed
container, i.e.\ a small category (Theorem~\ref{thm:AU}, Proposition~\ref{prop:cai}) --- and
diverge on the composition law:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \cite{SmitheCAI2} composes agents as Bayesian lenses in a monoidal bicategory of cilia;
\item[(ii)] the author composes agent \emph{categories} by the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld $\C\bowtie\D$
of Ahman--Uustalu \cite{AhmanUustaluDL}, adding an obstruction in $H^2$.
\end{itemize}
These are structurally different mechanisms.
\end{proposition}

Two points of precision guard against the two ways this could be overstated. First,
\cite{SmitheCAI2} is \emph{not} distributive-law-free: it contains a distributive law
(Definition~3.13). But that is a distributive law of an internal hom over a tensor, used to build
a bicategory --- \emph{not} a monad distributive law welding two agent categories in the
Beck / Zappa--Sz\'ep sense. When we say our composition is a ``distributive-law weld'' we mean the
latter; the two gadgets share a name and nothing else. Second, and correspondingly, we do
\emph{not} claim that Smithe uses the weld, nor that anyone but the author composes agents by
$\C\bowtie\D$. The claim of this note is the convergence on the \emph{object}. The divergence on
the composition law is not a defect in the convergence; it is the map of where each programme's
own frontier lies.

\section{What the convergence buys}\label{sec:grant}

A programme that proposes to found a theory of compositional agents must first pin down what an
agent \emph{is}, and then earn confidence that the choice is not idiosyncratic. This note reports
that the choice --- $\DCont\iso\Cat$ --- is load-bearing four times over, and that one of the four
loads is external.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Foundation, not fiat.} The object is not chosen for convenience: effectful agents
\emph{classify into} it (\ref{sub:into}), its exterior is \emph{charted} (\ref{sub:boundary}), and
it \emph{generalizes upward} into topos theory (\ref{sub:up}). An object with a proved
classification, a charted boundary, and a generalization frontier is a foundation.
\item \textbf{External validation.} An independent group in a disjoint tradition reaches for the
same equivalence and calls it ``famous'' (\ref{sub:cited}). Convergence from the active-inference
end is the strongest available evidence that this is the field's object, not merely ours.
\item \textbf{A clean division of labour with the frontier.} The object-level question is settled
and shared; the live research is the \emph{composition law}. There the programmes genuinely part:
lens-wiring for predictive coding, a cohomologically obstructed weld for shared-resource
orchestration (\ref{sec:contrast}). The grant's own contribution --- the weld $\C\bowtie\D$ and
its class $[\omega]\in H^2$ --- is precisely located as the composition law over an object the
whole field already agrees on.
\end{itemize}

The hinge across all of this is the fibrewise op: the operation that makes a container directed is
the operation that reads an effectful lifting as a category and, dually, reads a monad as a
comonad. It is why the same equivalence can be a bare-agent model, an effectful-agent
classification, and a topos special case at once. \emph{Fair is foul, and foul is fair}: the
op that looks like a bookkeeping reversal is the structure doing the work. That one theorem should
be a target, an import, a frontier, and a boundary --- and that an outside programme should reach
for it independently --- is the operational meaning of having chosen the right object.
