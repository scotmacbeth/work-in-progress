Notes~1--3 built up the vocabulary: an agent is a \emph{container morphism}, and a \emph{stateful}
agent --- a \emph{worker} --- is a morphism out of a domain tensored with one fixed container, the
\emph{state object} $\Delta S$ (Note~3). Robin then asked the sharp question this note exists to
answer. Modelling \texttt{stalin}'s state as a store-comonad worker is a \emph{new} thing to do ---
\texttt{stalin} carries no such object today (\S\ref{sec:setup} explains why) --- so the honest
question is not ``is this what we already have?'' but ``\emph{is it worth adding?}''. Does the
comonadic reformulation earn its keep, or is it the same data in fancier dress?

The answer has two halves, and stating both up front is the whole point:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{For a single worker, it buys nothing} --- $\Store_S X = S\times(S\to X)$ is literally the
curried form of threading a state $s:S$ through the signatures, the same data spelled differently
(\S\ref{sec:single}). \textbf{For a \emph{composite} of workers, it buys compositional correctness}
--- interleaving two stateful workers that share state is a \emph{comonad distributive law}, which
does \emph{not} exist for free; whether a consistent interleaving exists is a cohomological
obstruction $[\omega]\in H^2$, and a wrong interleaving is precisely the re-entrancy /
state-divergence defect. Explicit threading does not remove that decision; it \emph{hides} it at
every call site. The comonadic view makes the obstruction \emph{visible and checkable}
(\S\S\ref{sec:compose}--\ref{sec:stalin}).
\end{quote}

So the reformulation is not single-worker convenience; it is a way to \emph{name}, in one place, a
correctness condition that hand-threading leaves implicit and scattered. That is the claim, and the
rest of the note earns it.

\section{Setup: what a worker is, and what \texttt{stalin} has now}\label{sec:setup}

We recall only what the argument uses. A reader of Note~3 can skim to \S\ref{sec:single}; a reader
who knows \texttt{stalin} should not skip the correction at the end of this section.

\begin{defbox}[Container, morphism, state object (Note~3)]
A \textbf{container} $S\ctr P$ is a shape set $S$ with a position family $P:S\to\Set$; its extension
is $\ext{S\ctr P}(X)=\sum_{s:S}(P\,s\to X)$. A \textbf{morphism} $(S\ctr P)\to(T\ctr Q)$ is a forward
map $f:S\to T$ on shapes and a backward, contravariant family $\bk f:\prod_{s:S}(Q(f\,s)\to P\,s)$ on
positions. The \textbf{state object} on a set $S$ is the container
\[
  \Delta S \;:=\; S\ctr(\lambda\_.\,S),
  \qquad\text{extension}\quad
  \ext{\Delta S}(X)=\textstyle\sum_{s:S}X^{S}=S\times X^{S}=\Store_S X,
\]
the \textbf{store comonad}: a current state paired with a reader $X^{S}=(S\to X)$. A
\textbf{worker of state $S$ from $X$ to $Y$} is a morphism $w:\Delta S\otimes X\to Y$, where
$\otimes$ is the Dirichlet tensor (multiply shapes, multiply positions).
\end{defbox}

\noindent Note~3's unfolding theorem is the fact we lean on: a worker $w:\Delta S\otimes X\to Y$ is
exactly a pair
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fwdbwd}
  \fwd : S\times X_0 \to Y_0,
  \qquad
  \bwd : \textstyle\prod_{(s,x_0)}\big(Y_1(\fwd(s,x_0)) \to S\times X_1\,x_0\big),
\end{equation}
with $\bwd$ splitting, by the universal property of $\times$, into a \emph{state-writeback}
$\bwd_S:\!\prod Y_1(\dots)\to S$ and an ordinary back-map $\bwd_X:\!\prod Y_1(\dots)\to X_1\,x_0$.
These are the forward/backward signatures Robin asked to see spelled out; \eqref{eq:fwdbwd} is the
whole of ``a stateful worker'', typed. The forward leg \emph{reads} $(\text{state},\text{input})$ and
answers; the backward leg \emph{writes back} a new state and answers the input.

\paragraph{What \texttt{stalin} carries today --- and the correction that matters.} It is tempting
(I made this mistake, and Robin corrected it) to look at the Church-encoded existential in
\texttt{morphisms.ts} and read it as a hidden state object $\Delta S$. It is not. That existential is
\[
  \exists\, j:\mathsf{Shape}\langle D\rangle.\ \big(\mathsf{PosAt}\langle D,j\rangle \to
  \mathsf{PosAt}\langle C,s\rangle\big)
  \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{j:Q}\,(Q'\,j \to P\,s),
\]
Ghani's container-morphism form $\Sigma(j{:}Q).(T\,j\to P\,s)$: the \emph{codomain shape} chosen
per call by the implementation, bundled with the amalgamate leg for that $j$. It is Church-encoded
only because TypeScript lacks existentials but has rank-2 polymorphism --- a generic
\texttt{delegate(s,k)} hands $(j,\mathit{back})$ to a continuation generic in $j$, so the two cannot
come apart; as the source comments, ``$j$ never becomes a type, it becomes a scope''. This is the
$\Sigma$ in the \emph{hom}, not the $\Delta S$ in the \emph{domain}. Consequently:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{stalin} has \textbf{no worker state} in the $\Delta S$ sense. Its \texttt{state.ts} is plain,
fully-visible domain data (\texttt{Stocks}, \texttt{Workforce}, grades) --- nothing existential, and
nothing that survives across a call the way $S$ survives in $\Delta S\otimes X\to Y$.
\end{quote}
This is exactly why the question is honest. Introducing a store-comonad worker is a genuine
\emph{addition} of structure, not a re-description of the existential that is already there. The rest
of the note asks whether the addition pays.

\section{The single-worker verdict: it buys nothing}\label{sec:single}

Start with the case where the sceptic is right, and say so plainly.

\begin{proposition}[One worker is threaded state, re-spelled]\label{prop:single}
Fix a state set $S$ and an output type $X$. The elements of the store $\Store_S X = S\times X^{S}$
are in natural bijection with the state-threading functions --- a current state together with a
map from states to outputs. On the lens fragment $X=\y$, $Y=A\ctr(\lambda\_.B)$, a worker
$\Delta S\to A\ctr(\lambda\_.B)$ is exactly a pair
\[
  \getop : S\to A,\qquad \putop : S\times B\to S,
\]
i.e.\ the ordinary ``read the state to answer; write the state back'' of hand-threaded code, verbatim.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bijection $\Store_S X = S\times X^S$ is the definition of $\ext{\Delta S}$. Specialising
\eqref{eq:fwdbwd} to $X=\y$ ($X_0=X_1=1$) and $Y=A\ctr(\lambda\_.B)$ gives $\fwd:S\to A$ and
$\bwd_S:\prod_{s}(B\to S)$, i.e.\ $\putop:S\times B\to S$, with $\bwd_X$ trivial (Note~3,
\S``lens view''). \qed
\end{proof}

\noindent There is no daylight here. A store is a state and a reader; a lens is a getter and a
setter; threading a state through a function signature gives a getter and a setter. If Kodamai's
interest stopped at one worker, the store comonad would be a rename --- more category theory, no more
safety --- and honesty would require us to drop it. It does not stop at one worker.

\begin{deepbox}
The comonad's counit is ``read the current state'' ($\getop$) and its comultiplication is
``duplicate the register''. For a \emph{single} worker these are exactly the operations
hand-threaded code already performs. The reformulation cannot pay for itself one worker at a time,
and we do not pretend it can.
\end{deepbox}

\section{The composition verdict: it buys a visible obstruction}\label{sec:compose}

Everything the reformulation is worth lives in how two workers combine. Here the store comonad stops
being a rename, because comonads --- unlike the data they carry --- do \emph{not} compose for free.

\paragraph{Two workers can be stacked in two ways, and only one is free.} Note~3 established the
arithmetic. Tensoring two state objects multiplies their states on the nose,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensor}
  \Delta S\otimes\Delta T \;=\; \Delta(S\times T),
\end{equation}
whereas composing them nests a store inside a store,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nest}
  \Delta S\comp\Delta T \;=\; (S\times T^{S})\ctr(\dots)\;\neq\;\Delta(S\times T),
\end{equation}
recording not just a pair of states but a whole branch map $g:S\to T$; at $|S|=|T|=2$ the two are
$4\cdot\y^4$ versus $8\cdot\y^4$, an explicit retract, not an equality (Note~3,
Corollary~``$\otimes\neq\comp$ witnessed''). The tensor \eqref{eq:tensor} is the composition of two
workers that do \emph{not} interact: independent registers, no shared reads or writes, and the
composite is forced --- ``ask both, answer both''. This is the free case, and it is free precisely
\emph{because} it assumes non-interference.

\paragraph{The interesting composite is neither of these.} The workers Kodamai actually wants to
compose \emph{do} interact: a supervisor over a worker that can call back into the supervisor
(re-entrancy), or two workers that read and write a shared ledger, each acting on data the other
produced. There the composite is not $\Delta S\otimes\Delta T$ --- the registers are not independent
--- and it is not a bare nesting either; it is a \textbf{Zappa--Sz\'ep weld} $C\zs D$, in which each
side acts on the other. A Zappa--Sz\'ep weld of two directed containers (and $\Delta S$ is a directed
container --- the codiscrete category on $S$) is exactly a \emph{distributive law} $\lambda$ saying
how the two stores interleave: which side reads first, whose write the other sees.

\begin{deepbox}
A distributive law is the interleaving discipline made explicit. Composing the store comonads of two
interacting workers requires choosing $\lambda:\Delta T\comp\Delta S\Rightarrow\Delta S\comp\Delta T$
(or its comonad-distributive dual) and checking it satisfies the coherence axioms. \emph{Comonads do
not compose for free}: without a valid $\lambda$ there is no composite worker at all, only two workers
and an unanswered question about the order of their effects.
\end{deepbox}

\paragraph{Whether a consistent interleaving exists is a cohomology class.} This is the fact the
grant is built on, and it is proved elsewhere; we only invoke it. A Zappa--Sz\'ep weld of the two
interacting workers exists iff two conditions hold --- a freeness condition (L) and a global-closure
condition (G) --- and the obstruction to (G) is a single cohomology class
\[
  [\omega]\in H^2,
  \qquad (G)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ [\omega]=0,
\]
so, once (L) holds, the weld exists exactly when $[\omega]=0$ (the (G)-obstruction-is-$H^2$ result;
distributive-law $\Leftrightarrow$ Zappa--Sz\'ep/bicrossed product is standard, Kassel/Brin at the
group level). In the smallest re-entrant supervisor--worker
model the class lives in $H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ and equals a single bit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega}
  [\omega]=\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}/2,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon = \text{``does the worker mutate shared state the supervisor depends on?''}
\end{equation}
so the weld exists iff $\varepsilon=0$. A \emph{wrong} interleaving --- a $\lambda$ that violates the
coherence axioms --- is not an abstract nuisance; it is the state-divergence / re-entrancy defect
(the Path-5 family), the concrete failure mode where one worker reads state another has already
invalidated.

\begin{remark}[Provenance, and where the machine check stops]\label{rem:prov}
The class computation \eqref{eq:omega} is \textbf{machine-checked in Lean~4}, sorry-free and
Mathlib-free (\texttt{Reentrancy.lean}): the file certifies $\varphi(\omega\,\varepsilon)=\varepsilon$
and $\mathsf{InB2}(\omega\,\varepsilon)\Leftrightarrow\varepsilon=\mathsf{false}$, i.e.\ that the
cocycle $\omega(\varepsilon)$ is a coboundary exactly when $\varepsilon=0$. What Lean certifies is the
finite $\mathbb{F}_2$ class computation; the \emph{reduction} of the categorical obstruction to that
cochain complex is proved pen-and-paper (analytic note, Prop.~3.1) and is \emph{not} formalised. The
arithmetic \eqref{eq:tensor}--\eqref{eq:nest} is proved and Lean-checked (\texttt{WorkersRetract.lean}:
$r\circ\sigma=\mathrm{id}$). We flag the boundary rather than blur it. The general reduction of
store-comonad interleaving to a distributive law of directed containers is invoked here at the
directed-container level ($\Delta S$ is one); a bespoke store-comonad restatement is not needed for
this note, and if one is later wanted it is a \texttt{[PROVE TODO]}, not a claim made here.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{What threading does, and does not, do about this.} Hand-threading two shared states does
\emph{not} escape the distributive law. Every call site that reads one register and writes another is
silently committing to an order of effects --- an implicit $\lambda$ --- and nothing checks that the
per-site choices are \emph{globally} consistent, which is exactly what the coherence axioms demand. A
program can hand-thread its way into a globally inconsistent interleaving one locally reasonable line
at a time; that inconsistency is $[\omega]\neq0$, discovered at run time as a re-entrancy bug. The
store-comonad worker does not make the composite exist automatically --- no formalism can, because
sometimes it genuinely does not exist. What it does is \emph{concentrate} the decision: the
interleaving is one named object $\lambda$, its validity is one class $[\omega]$, and both can be
inspected before the system runs, in one place, instead of being reconstructed from scattered call
sites after it breaks.

\section{The instance: where the interleaving lives in \texttt{stalin}}\label{sec:stalin}

To make \S\ref{sec:compose} concrete, here is the shape of the addition in \texttt{stalin}'s own
vocabulary. This is deliberately schematic: \texttt{stalin} has no state store today (\S\ref{sec:setup}),
so what follows is the structure a store-comonad reformulation \emph{would} introduce, and where the
correctness condition would sit --- not a description of present code.

Take two workers over the shared ledger.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Production} $w_P:\Delta S_P\otimes X_P\to Y_P$, with state $S_P=\texttt{Stocks}$: it
        \emph{reads} \texttt{Workforce} (as input $X_P$) to decide output, and \emph{writes back}
        an increased \texttt{Stocks} via $\bwd_{S_P}$.
  \item \textbf{Hiring} $w_H:\Delta S_H\otimes X_H\to Y_H$, with state $S_H=\texttt{Workforce}$: it
        \emph{reads} \texttt{Stocks} (as input $X_H$, the budget) to decide output, and
        \emph{writes back} an adjusted \texttt{Workforce} via $\bwd_{S_H}$.
\end{itemize}
Each is, on its own, exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:single}: a getter reading its state and a setter
writing it back --- threaded state, nothing gained. The content is that they \emph{share the ledger}:
$w_P$ writes the \texttt{Stocks} that $w_H$ reads, and $w_H$ writes the \texttt{Workforce} that $w_P$
reads. Their composite is therefore \emph{not} the free tensor $\Delta(S_P\times S_H)$ of
\eqref{eq:tensor} --- that would model two firms with no shared books --- but a Zappa--Sz\'ep weld,
and the weld needs an interleaving $\lambda$:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Within one tick, does hiring see this tick's production output, or last tick's? Does production
see this tick's new hires, or the pre-hire workforce?}
\end{quote}
That question is the distributive law, and in the fwd/bwd signatures it lives at exactly one place:
the order in which the two writebacks $\bwd_{S_P}$ and $\bwd_{S_H}$ are applied to the shared ledger
relative to the two forward reads. A consistent choice --- e.g.\ ``both read the pre-tick ledger,
both writebacks commit atomically at tick end'' --- is a valid $\lambda$, $[\omega]=0$. An
inconsistent choice --- ``hiring reads \texttt{Stocks} \emph{after} production's writeback but
production reads \texttt{Workforce} \emph{before} hiring's'' --- is a $\lambda$ that fails coherence:
hiring budgets against goods that production's own inputs have not yet accounted for, the ledger
double-counts, and that is $\varepsilon=1$ in \eqref{eq:omega} --- the worker mutating shared state
the other depends on.

The point of the reformulation is that this choice is written \emph{once}, as $\lambda$, and its
soundness is one check, $[\omega]=0$. In the hand-threaded version the same choice is distributed
across every function that touches \texttt{Stocks} or \texttt{Workforce}, and its soundness is
whatever those functions happen jointly to do --- verified, if at all, by a re-entrancy bug in
production.

\section{The honest ledger}\label{sec:ledger}

\begin{honestbox}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.28\textwidth}p{0.31\textwidth}p{0.31\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
 & \textbf{single worker} & \textbf{interacting composite} \\
\midrule
store-comonad worker & $\Store_S X=S\times X^S$ & Zappa--Sz\'ep weld; needs $\lambda$ \\
hand-threaded state & getter/setter & implicit $\lambda$ per call site \\
\textbf{what the comonad adds} & \emph{nothing} (a rename) & the interleaving as \emph{one} named $\lambda$; its validity as \emph{one} class $[\omega]$, checkable before run \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{honestbox}

\noindent The verdict, stated as plainly as the question deserves. \textbf{It does not buy}
single-worker convenience (\S\ref{sec:single}), and it does \textbf{not} make composition automatic:
the composite of two interacting workers can genuinely fail to exist, and no reformulation conjures
it into being. \textbf{It does buy} a change in \emph{where} and \emph{when} the composition's one
real decision --- the interleaving of shared effects --- is made and checked: once, up front, as a
named distributive law with a single cohomological soundness class, rather than implicitly, per call
site, and discovered on failure. For a system of one worker that is not worth the category theory. For
a system of many interacting workers --- which is what an agentic business is --- it is exactly the
compositional-correctness guarantee the grant is about, and it is a genuine addition to \texttt{stalin},
because \texttt{stalin} has no such state store today. That, honestly, is what it buys.
