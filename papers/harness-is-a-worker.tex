An agent reads a request and returns a response: it is a \emph{container morphism}, and the first three
notes told that story. But there is a thing that sits \emph{above} the agent --- the \emph{harness} that
offers it tools, dispatches its calls, feeds back the results, and loops. It is tempting to think of the
harness as a privileged meta-object, a special piece of runtime that stands outside the theory looking
in. It is not. Neil's remark, which this note makes precise, is that \textbf{the harness is a worker}:
it is the very same kind of object as the tools it wields and the agents it runs --- a container $S\ctr
P$ --- and it therefore composes with them by the two products we already have, $\comp$ and $\otimes$,
with no new machinery. The payoff is immediate and practical: an agent is a \emph{tool} for a
meta-agent, orchestration is \emph{composition}, and a trust boundary is a \emph{smart constructor} on a
morphism. This note spells out the signatures.

We are careful about one distinction, because it is exactly what Neil's remark disambiguates. The
``harness'' that is a worker is the \emph{interface} --- the container $(S,P)$ of admissible calls and
their responses. It is \emph{not} the agent's persistent memory, its skills, or its accumulated context.
Those, insofar as we model them at all, are the \emph{separate} store-comonad worker of Note~3. ``The
harness is a worker'' is the interface half; ``state via the tensor'' is the state half; \S\ref{sec:split}
makes their orthogonality explicit.

\section{Recap: containers, morphisms, the two products}\label{sec:recap}

We recall only what we use; a reader who has met the earlier notes can skim to \S\ref{sec:tool}.

\begin{defbox}[A container]
A \textbf{container} is a pair $S \ctr P$ of a set $S$ of \emph{shapes} and a family $P : S \to \Set$ of
\emph{positions} --- for each shape $s$, a set $P\,s$. Its \textbf{extension} is the polynomial functor
\[
  \ext{S \ctr P}(X) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{s : S} X^{P\,s}
  \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{s:S}\, \big(P\,s \to X\big).
\]
We write $\y = (1\ctr\lambda\_.\,1)$ for the unit container (a single shape with a single position).
\end{defbox}

\noindent As in Note~3, $\ctr$ is the \emph{constructor} pairing a shape set with a position family --- a
piece of syntax, not an operator. It is not either composition product.

\begin{defbox}[A container morphism]
A morphism $(S \ctr P) \to (T \ctr Q)$ is a pair
\[
  f : S \to T,
  \qquad
  \bk f : \textstyle\prod_{s:S}\, \big(Q(f\,s) \to P\,s\big),
\]
a map \emph{forward} on shapes and, for each shape $s$, a map \emph{backward} on positions. Control flows
down (delegate a shape), data flows back up (amalgamate a position). The backward leg is contravariant.
\end{defbox}

\begin{defbox}[The two products]
\textbf{Sequential} composition $p\comp q$ (substitution) has extension $\ext{p\comp q}=\ext p\circ\ext
q$: a shape of $p\comp q$ is a shape of $p$ \emph{together with}, at each of its positions, a shape of
$q$. It models \emph{dependent} pipelines --- the response to one call determines what may be called next.
\smallskip

\textbf{Parallel} composition $p\otimes q$ (the Dirichlet tensor) multiplies shapes and \emph{multiplies}
positions:
\[
  (S_0 \ctr P_0)\otimes(S_1 \ctr P_1)
  = (S_0\times S_1)\ctr\big(\lambda(s_0,s_1).\; P_0\,s_0 \times P_1\,s_1\big),
  \qquad \text{unit } \y .
\]
It models \emph{independent}, non-interfering interaction: ask both, answer both.
\end{defbox}

\section{A tool is a container}\label{sec:tool}

Here is the first move, and it is a definition rather than a theorem: a \emph{tool} is a container. The
identification is forced once you write down what a tool is.

\begin{defbox}[A tool, as a container]
A \textbf{tool} is a container $p = S\ctr P = \sum_{s:S}\y^{P\,s}$, where
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
  \item the \textbf{shapes} $S$ are the \emph{admissible calls} --- the request types the tool accepts,
        together with their arguments; and
  \item the \textbf{positions} $P\,s$ are the \emph{possible responses} to the call $s$ --- the results
        the tool may return when invoked with $s$.
\end{itemize}
\end{defbox}

\noindent To \emph{invoke} the tool is to name a shape $s\in S$ (issue a request) and receive a position
$i\in P\,s$ (a response). Nothing else is part of the tool. A weather API whose calls are ``forecast for
city $c$'' has shapes $S=\{\mathsf{forecast}\,c\mid c:\mathsf{City}\}$ and positions $P(\mathsf{forecast}\,c)
= \mathsf{Weather}$. A calculator tool has shapes $\{\mathsf{add}(m,n),\mathsf{mul}(m,n),\dots\}$ and
positions $\mathbb{N}$. A file reader has shapes $\{\mathsf{read}\,\mathit{path}\}$ and positions
``file contents or error''.

A \emph{monster} in the \texttt{dungeon} game --- to take the running example the implementation will
use --- has exactly this shape. Fix a monster. Its calls are the moves a player may make against it, and
its responses are the outcomes of those moves:
\[
  S \;=\; \{\,\mathsf{attack},\ \mathsf{defend},\ \mathsf{flee},\ \mathsf{cast}\,\sigma\ (\sigma:\mathsf{Spell})\,\},
\]
\[
  P(\mathsf{attack}) = \{\mathsf{hit},\ \mathsf{miss},\ \mathsf{slain}\},\quad
  P(\mathsf{flee}) = \{\mathsf{escaped},\ \mathsf{blocked}\},\quad
  P(\mathsf{cast}\,\sigma)=\{\mathsf{resisted},\ \mathsf{struck}\},\ \dots
\]
The monster \emph{is} the container $M=S\ctr P$. It is not ``like'' a container; it has no data beyond the
calls it admits and the outcomes it may return, which is precisely a container.

\paragraph{Using a tool is a morphism.} An agent that \emph{uses} the monster to answer its own caller is
a container morphism $\alpha : A \to M$, where $A = S_A\ctr P_A$ is the agent's own interface (its prompts
and replies). Unfolding the definition:
\[
  \fwd = \alpha : S_A \to S,
  \qquad
  \bwd = \bk\alpha : \textstyle\prod_{a:S_A}\big(P(\alpha\,a) \to P_A\,a\big).
\]
Read this operationally. The agent receives a prompt $a\in S_A$ from its caller; it \emph{delegates} by
choosing a monster-move $\alpha(a)\in S$ (control flows down); the monster returns an outcome
$i\in P(\alpha\,a)$; and the agent \emph{amalgamates} that outcome back into a reply $\bk\alpha_a(i)\in
P_A\,a$ to its caller (data flows back up). Delegation is the forward leg; interpreting the tool's
response is the backward leg. This is the whole of ``the agent used a tool'', typed.

\begin{deepbox}
A tool exposes exactly a container's worth of information: which calls it accepts (shapes) and which
responses each call may produce (positions). ``What can I ask, and what might come back?'' is the
question a container answers. Consequently \emph{a call to an LLM, a call to an API, a move against a
monster, and a prompt handed to a sub-agent are the same kind of object}, and using any of them is one
container morphism: delegate a shape, interpret the position.
\end{deepbox}

\section{The harness runs the coalgebra}\label{sec:harness}

The agent is not called once; it \emph{loops}. It issues a call, reads the response, decides the next
call in light of it, and continues until it is done. The object that carries out this loop is the
harness. The second move is that the harness is \emph{not} a new kind of thing: it is the \emph{matter}
that unfolds the tool's \emph{pattern}, in Spivak's sense, and the pattern it unfolds is a
\emph{coalgebra} of the same container.

\begin{defbox}[Running an agent is a coalgebra]
Let $p=S\ctr P$ be the tool interface. An agent \emph{driver} with internal states $W$ is a
$\ext p$-coalgebra
\[
  \run : W \longrightarrow \ext p(W) \;=\; \textstyle\sum_{s:S}\,\big(P\,s \to W\big).
\]
At state $w$, the driver emits a shape $\run(w)=(s,\,k)$: it makes the call $s\in S$, and $k:P\,s\to W$
says which state to transition to for each possible response $i\in P\,s$. The harness supplies the
matter: it takes the emitted call $s$ to the world, obtains an actual response $i\in P\,s$, and advances
the driver to $k(i)$. Then it repeats.
\end{defbox}

\noindent This is the tool-call loop verbatim: \emph{emit a shape} (make a tool-call), \emph{consume a
position} (receive the response), \emph{transition} (update and decide the next call). The harness is the
half that resolves each emitted shape against the real world and feeds the resulting position back; the
driver is the half that chooses. Neither stands outside the container theory --- both are expressed
entirely in $\ext p$.

\paragraph{The whole trace is the cofree comonad.} What does an entire \emph{run} look like? Starting
from a state and unfolding $\run$ as far as it will go produces a tree: at the root the first call
$s_0$, one child for each possible response $i_0\in P\,s_0$, at that child the next call, and so on. The
universal such object --- the behaviour that records \emph{all} possible runs of \emph{any} driver on
$p$ --- is the \textbf{cofree comonad} on $p$:
\[
  \ext{\cofree p}(X) \;\cong\; \nu Z.\,\big(X\times \ext p(Z)\big),
\]
whose elements are trees of tool-calls-and-responses, each node decorated by a value in $X$. A driver
$\run:W\to\ext p(W)$ is exactly a coalgebra, and it factors uniquely through $\cofree p$ (the trace it
generates); this is the couniversal property of $\cofree p$. It is the container-level form of Spivak's
slogan that a \emph{pattern runs on matter}: $p$ is the pattern (what may be called), and the harness is
the matter that unfolds it into an actual, branching history of interaction.

\begin{deepbox}
The harness does not add structure on top of the theory; it \emph{instantiates} structure already in it.
Running an agent is unfolding a $\ext p$-coalgebra; the accumulated interaction history is a branch of
the cofree comonad $\cofree p$ on the tool container. ``Harness = comonad'' (Neil's dictionary entry) is
this: the harness is where the cofree comonad of the tool gets \emph{run}.
\end{deepbox}

\begin{remark}[Provenance --- what is proved, and where]\label{rem:prov}
The cofree comonad on a container, its couniversal property, and the tree/$\nu$-description are proved in
container coordinates in the internal note \texttt{cofree-comonad-universal-property} (2026-07-25),
building on Niu--Spivak (\emph{Polynomial Functors}, Prop.~8.18/8.33, Thm.~8.45) and Spivak
(arXiv:2202.00534). The exact dual --- the \emph{free} monad on a container as a $\comp$-monoid --- is
additionally \textbf{machine-checked in Lean 4}, with all three monoid laws and zero \texttt{sorry}
(\texttt{Free.lean}). The cofree side is proved on paper but not yet formalised: its shape layer needs

\end{remark}

\section{Same object, same composition}\label{sec:compose}

If tools, agents, and the harness are all containers, then they compose by the two products of
\S\ref{sec:recap}, and \emph{that is the orchestration language}. This is the third move, and it is where
the identification pays off: nesting agents needs no new machinery.

\begin{defbox}[The two orchestration operators]
Let tools/agents be containers.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Sequential / dependent} pipelines are $\comp$. In $p\comp q$ the response to a $p$-call
        chooses the next $q$-call: a shape is a $p$-shape $s$ together with, for each $i\in P\,s$, a
        $q$-shape. Use $\comp$ when one tool's output determines which tool (or which call) comes next.
  \item \textbf{Independent / parallel} tools are $\otimes$. In $p\otimes q$ a shape is a pair of calls
        $(s,s')$ and a position is a pair of responses; the two run without interference. Use $\otimes$
        when calls may be issued together and answered together.
\end{itemize}
\end{defbox}

\noindent These are not new definitions --- they are the substitution product and the Dirichlet tensor,
already the sequential and parallel composition of containers. The content of the third move is only that
they are also the composition of \emph{agents and tools}, because agents and tools are containers.

\begin{proposition}[An agent is a tool for a meta-agent]\label{prop:nest}
Let a sub-agent be a container morphism $\alpha:A\to p$ (it answers prompts $A$ using tool $p$, as in
\S\ref{sec:tool}). Then $A$ is itself a container, so a \emph{meta-agent} may use $\alpha$ exactly as it
uses any tool: a meta-agent that orchestrates sub-agents $A_1,\dots,A_n$ is a morphism whose target is
built from the $A_i$ by $\comp$ and $\otimes$ --- $\comp$ where one sub-agent's reply selects the next,
$\otimes$ where they run independently. No construction beyond the two products is required.
\end{proposition}

\noindent There is no ``meta-level'' in the type theory: the sub-agent's \emph{interface} $A$ is a
container of the same standing as a weather API or a monster, and the meta-agent delegates to it with the
same morphism discipline. Nesting is $\comp$/$\otimes$, iterated.

\begin{deepbox}
Orchestration is composition. A supervisor calling workers, a pipeline of tools, a re-entrant
tool-call --- all are terms built from $\comp$ (dependent) and $\otimes$ (independent) over containers.
This is why ``the harness is a worker'' is more than a slogan: because the harness is an ordinary
container, an agent running under it can \emph{itself} be wrapped as a tool and run under a further
harness, ad infinitum, with the same two operators throughout.
\end{deepbox}

\paragraph{Where the trust boundaries live.} Composition is not always free, and the place it can fail is
worth naming, because it is where a trust boundary belongs. When two directed interactions are welded ---
a supervisor over a worker that can call \emph{back} into the supervisor (re-entrancy), or two agents each
able to act on the other's turn --- the composite is a \emph{Zappa--Sz\'ep} product $C\bowtie D$, not a
mere functor: a functor composes along one topology, a Zappa--Sz\'ep product interleaves two. Such a weld
exists exactly when a cohomological obstruction vanishes, $[\omega]=0\in H^2$; in the smallest re-entrant
supervisor--worker model the class equals the single bit ``does the worker mutate shared supervisor
state'', $[\omega]=\varepsilon\in H^2\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$ (proved in \texttt{orchestration-reentrancy};
the $[\omega]=\varepsilon$ computation is separately Lean-checked). Operationally: a \emph{trust boundary
is a smart constructor} on the orchestration morphism --- it refuses the weld precisely when
$\varepsilon=1$, i.e.\ when an untrusted sub-agent would write state the supervisor depends on. This note
does not develop that thread; it flags where in the composition it sits.

\section{The clean split: interface, state, and what the harness is not}\label{sec:split}

The fourth move is a subtraction. Neil's remark identifies the harness with a worker in a specific sense,
and the value of the remark is as much in what it \emph{excludes} as in what it includes.

\begin{defbox}[Three things, kept apart]
For an agent we distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item the \textbf{interface} --- the container $(S,P)$ of admissible calls and their responses. This
        is the ``harness is a worker'' object of \S\S\ref{sec:tool}--\ref{sec:compose}.
  \item the \textbf{carried state} --- a value threaded across calls, if the agent has one. This is
        \emph{not} part of $(S,P)$; it is the separate state object $\Delta S = S\ctr(\lambda\_.\,S)$ of
        Note~3, tensored onto the interface.
  \item \textbf{memory, skills, context} --- persistent stores, tool-libraries, accumulated prompt
        context. These are \emph{neither} the interface \emph{nor} the state object; they are
        implementation matter that the container theory does not model as part of $(S,P)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{defbox}

\noindent The first is this note; the second is Note~3; the third is deliberately outside the interface
theory. Keeping them apart is what disambiguates Neil's remark: ``the harness is a worker'' is a claim
about \emph{item 1}, the interface, and specifically \emph{not} about item 3.

\paragraph{State is orthogonal, and it is a tensor factor.} When an agent does carry state $S$, Note~3
showed that state needs no new machinery either: a stateful worker is a morphism $\Delta S\otimes X\to Y$
out of the interface tensored with the state object, and unfolding the tensor \emph{forces} exactly a
read-and-answer forward map and a state-writeback backward map. The state object's extension is the
store comonad $\ext{\Delta S}(X)=S\times X^{S}$, and states \emph{multiply} under the tensor,
$\Delta S\otimes\Delta T=\Delta(S\times T)$, making workers a $(\Set,\times)$-graded category (proved in
\texttt{delta-state-object-and-workers}; the retract and the collapse identity $r\circ\delta=\Delta(d)$
are Lean-checked in \texttt{WorkersRetract.lean}). The two halves compose cleanly because they occupy
different tensor factors:
\[
  \underbrace{\Delta S}_{\text{state (Note 3)}}\ \otimes\ \underbrace{p}_{\text{interface (this note)}}
  \;\longrightarrow\; q .
\]
``The harness is a worker'' is the $p$ factor; ``state via the tensor'' is the $\Delta S$ factor. They
are orthogonal by construction --- the tensor is the direct sum of the two disciplines, not their
entanglement --- which is precisely why Neil's two remarks are about two different factors of one
morphism and do not compete.

\begin{deepbox}
The harness-as-worker claim is about the \emph{interface} container $(S,P)$ and nothing else. Carried
state is a separate, orthogonal tensor factor $\Delta S$ (Note~3). Memory, skills, and context are not
modelled as part of $(S,P)$ at all. This is the disambiguation: the interface half and the state half
are the two tensor factors of a single worker morphism $\Delta S\otimes p\to q$, and the rest is matter
the harness runs on, not structure the container exposes.
\end{deepbox}

\section*{Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Summary}

A tool is a container $p=S\ctr P$: shapes are the admissible calls, positions the possible responses.
Using a tool is a container morphism --- delegate a shape, interpret the position. The harness that runs
an agent is not a privileged meta-object; it is the matter that unfolds a $\ext p$-coalgebra, and the
whole interaction trace is the cofree comonad $\cofree p$ on the tool. Because tools, agents, and the
harness are all the same kind of object, they compose by the same two products: $\comp$ for dependent
pipelines, $\otimes$ for independent ones --- so an agent is a tool for a meta-agent and orchestration is
composition, with trust boundaries appearing as smart constructors exactly where the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld's
obstruction $[\omega]$ could fail to vanish. Finally, the claim is precisely scoped: ``the harness is a
worker'' is about the \emph{interface} $(S,P)$; carried state is the orthogonal tensor factor $\Delta S$
of Note~3; and memory, skills, and context are matter the harness runs on, not part of the interface.
The harness, once again, is not new machinery: it is one more container, and the two products supply the
rest.
