\begin{abstract}
A companion note showed that the Abbott--Altenkirch--Ghani--McBride one-hole derivative
$\partial$ is the differential combinator of a Cartesian differential category of polynomial
functors, realised by the dual-number substitution $T(p)=p(y+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2$;
that settles \emph{existence}. This note settles \emph{uniqueness}: among representable first-order
tangent structures $T=(-)\lhd D$ on finite-support polynomial functors there are \emph{exactly
two}, the trivial structure $T=\id$ and the container derivative $\partial$. The proof has two
halves. The first is a rank rigidity: an infinitesimal object $D=y\oplus M$ has a \emph{solid}
infinitesimal part when the multiplication $M\lhd M\to M$ is invertible, and over the rig $\N$ the
linear containers are automatically free, so solidity reads $|S|^2=|S|$, whose only finite
solutions are $0$ and $1$. The second is the converse: every representable first-order tangent
structure has a solid infinitesimal part. We prove this rig-internally from the universality of
the vertical lift, replacing Lanfranchi--Lemay's ring-theoretic short-exact-sequence step --- which
needs subtraction --- by the observation that in the extensive category of polynomial functors the
vertical bundle is a \emph{direct coproduct}, so ranks \emph{add}. The port is thus not only
faithful but cleaner: no principal-ideal hypothesis and no subtraction. The finiteness is sharp:
$M=\N\cdot y$ is a third solid part over all of $\Poly$, since $|\N\times\N|=|\N|$. The uniqueness
answers, and sharpens, a question of Cockett (2012).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{From existence to uniqueness.}
In a companion note \cite{MacBethTangent} we showed that the category $\SPoly$ of finitary
polynomial functors is a Cartesian differential category whose differential combinator is the
Abbott--Altenkirch--Ghani--McBride one-hole-context derivative $\partial$ \cite{AAGM2003}, and
that the attached tangent bundle is computed by the oldest trick in differential algebra:
substitute a dual number and read off the $\varepsilon$-coefficient,
\[
  T(p)\ =\ p(y+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2\ =\ p(y)+\varepsilon\,v\cdot\partial p.
\]
That note answers an \emph{existence} question --- there is a tangent structure on polynomial
functors whose vertical part is $\partial$. The natural next question is \emph{uniqueness}. The
dual-number structure is one tangent structure; the identity functor $T=\id$ is another (the
trivial one). Are there others? Could a polynomial functor carry an exotic first-order tangent
bundle that is neither the derivative nor trivial? This note proves there cannot.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness]\label{thm:main-intro}
Among representable first-order tangent structures $T=(-)\lhd D$ on finite-support polynomial
functors there are exactly two: the trivial structure ($D=y$, $T=\id$) and the AAGM dual-number
structure ($D=y+\varepsilon v$, $T(p)=p(y+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2$, combinator $\partial$).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The question is Cockett's.}
That polynomial functors should carry a differential structure is not a new hope. In an
unpublished talk at FMCS~2012, \emph{Can you Differentiate a Polynomial?}, Cockett
\cite{Cockett2012} conjectured exactly this --- that polynomial functors form a Cartesian
differential, hence a tangent, category --- fourteen years before the companion note proved it.
But the 2012 slides are candid about how much was conjecture: the differentiation there is defined
only on ``separable'' polynomials, closure under composition is asserted but not proved, and the
chain rule is flagged as the hard open point (``\emph{of course, need to prove this is a
differential!! (chain rule is already challenging\ldots)}''; ``\emph{Is this all completely sorted
out? Absolutely not!}''). No uniqueness statement appears, nor could it: without the structure in
hand there is nothing to count. The companion note supplied the structure; the present note
supplies the count, and so closes the arc Cockett opened. (We flag one divergence in
\S\ref{sec:conclusion}: combinatorial species, the setting nearest Cockett's, are most naturally a
tangent \emph{bicategory} \cite{MO341933}, structurally different from the $\SPoly$ one-category we
work in.)

\paragraph{The template, and why it does not simply transfer.}
For commutative algebra the uniqueness question has a sharp answer. Lanfranchi and Lemay
\cite{LanfranchiLemay2025} classify the \emph{representable} tangent structures on the opposite of
the category of modules (equivalently, affine schemes): a representable tangent structure is a
tensoring $T=D\otimes(-)$ by an \emph{infinitesimal object} $D=R\oplus M$, and when the base ring
$R$ is a principal ideal domain there are \emph{exactly two} --- the trivial one ($M=0$) and the
Kähler/dual-number one ($M=R$). Their argument has two load-bearing uses of ring structure. The
\emph{rigidity} half (a solid infinitesimal part has rank $n$ with $R^{n^2}\cong R^n$, forcing
$n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$; their Theorem~4.17, Corollary~4.18) needs $R$ to be a PID, so that
the infinitesimal module is free with a well-defined rank. The \emph{converse} half (a tangent
structure's infinitesimal part \emph{is} solid; their Theorem~4.8, via the universality of the
vertical lift) computes a vertical bundle by a short exact sequence --- i.e.\ by \emph{subtraction}
of ranks. Neither transfers to polynomial functors verbatim: the base there is the rig $\N$, which
is not a PID and has no subtraction.

\paragraph{What we prove, and the one new idea.}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro} by porting \emph{both} halves to the substitution monoidal
category $(\Poly,\lhd,y)$, and in both the port turns out \emph{cleaner} than its template.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Rigidity} (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}). An infinitesimal part is a \emph{linear} container
$M=S\cdot y$, forced by the additive-bundle axiom. Over $\N$ a linear container simply \emph{is} a
free module --- the linear polynomial functors are by definition the free $\N$-modules --- so
freeness is \emph{automatic}, with no PID hypothesis. Solidity then reads $|S|^2=|S|$, whose only
finite solutions are $0$ and $1$: the finite solid parts are exactly $\{0,y\}$.
\item \emph{Converse} (\S\ref{sec:converse}). Every representable first-order tangent structure has
a solid infinitesimal part. Here is the one new idea. The place Lanfranchi--Lemay use subtraction
is the computation of the vertical bundle fibre $P'$ by exactness. In the \emph{extensive} category
of polynomial functors that fibre is instead a \emph{direct coproduct} of the surviving summands,
\[
  P'\ =\ y\ \oplus\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\ \oplus\ (M\lhd M),
\]
so its rank is a \emph{sum}, $1+n+n^2$, computed by addition and never as a difference. The
universality of the vertical lift (Cockett--Cruttwell \cite{CockettCruttwell2014}) identifies the
second tangent bundle $T_2$ with the vertical bundle $V$; evaluating this functor isomorphism at
$y$ gives $D_2\cong P'$, i.e.\ $1+2n=1+n+n^2$, and the cancellative rig $\N$ (no zero divisors,
\emph{no subtraction}) forces $n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$.
\end{itemize}
Assembling the two halves with the forward correspondence (both surviving parts are realised ---
$\id$ and $\partial$) and representability \cite{MacBethTangent} gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}.

\paragraph{The finiteness is sharp.}
Unqualified uniqueness over all of $\Poly$ is false, and we exhibit the obstruction explicitly:
$M=\N\cdot y$ is solid, because $M\lhd M=(\N\times\N)\cdot y\cong\N\cdot y$ by the Cantor pairing
(\S\ref{sec:sharp}, Proposition~\ref{prop:infinite}); and the converse's rank equation
$1+2\kappa=1+\kappa+\kappa^2$ is satisfied by every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, so universality does
not exclude it either. This is the precise analogue of Lanfranchi--Lemay's extra structures when
finite generation is dropped (their Examples~4.20--4.21), and it tells us the load-bearing
hypothesis is a \emph{finite-dimensional} infinitesimal part --- finitely many tangent directions
--- here taken as plain finiteness of the shape set.

\paragraph{Certification.}
The arithmetic of both halves is machine-checked in a Mathlib-free Lean~4 development with no
classical axioms: the idempotent collapse $n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, the finite enumeration of solid
ranks, the three coproduct-rank identities underwriting the ``ranks add, never subtract'' claim,
and the universality collapse $1+2n=1+n+n^2\iff n\in\{0,1\}$ by additive cancellation. The Lean
certifies the \emph{arithmetic}; the structural inputs (the universality axiom, that $\lhd$
preserves pullbacks, that $M$ is linear) are cited framework facts, and we are explicit about the
boundary (\S\ref{sec:rigidity},~\S\ref{sec:converse}).

\paragraph{Contribution.}
The contributions are: the uniqueness theorem itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}), proved over a
rig with neither a PID hypothesis nor subtraction; the rigidity half (finite solid parts
$=\{0,y\}$); the rig-internal converse, whose single new idea is that extensivity turns the
vertical bundle into a coproduct so that ranks add (\S\ref{sec:converse}); the technical
observation that on linear containers the \emph{bundle} tensor $\lhd$ and the \emph{module}
(Dirichlet) tensor coincide (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), so solidity is computed in the bundle's own
tensor; the sharpness of the finiteness hypothesis (Proposition~\ref{prop:infinite}); and the Lean
certification of the arithmetic. The result is a clean deliverable for the tangent-category and
\texttt{Poly} communities: \emph{the container derivative is the only nontrivial representable
first-order tangent structure a finite-support polynomial functor can carry}.

\paragraph{Outline.}
\S\ref{sec:prelim} recalls containers, the substitution monoidal structure, infinitesimal objects,
the representable tangent bundle and the universality of the vertical lift. \S\ref{sec:coincide}
proves the bundle/module tensor coincidence. \S\ref{sec:rigidity} proves the rank rigidity
(finite solid parts $=\{0,y\}$) and records its Lean certification. \S\ref{sec:sharp} proves the
infinite-support obstruction. \S\ref{sec:converse} proves the solidity converse from vertical-lift
universality --- the main new content --- and records its Lean certification.
\S\ref{sec:uniqueness} assembles the uniqueness theorem. \S\ref{sec:compare} compares with
Lanfranchi--Lemay. \S\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with the precedent and the sequel.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

We recall only what the argument uses; for the Cartesian differential and tangent structure on
$\SPoly$ we refer to the companion note \cite{MacBethTangent}.

\paragraph{Containers and the substitution monoidal category.}
A \emph{container in $n$ variables} is a set $S$ of \emph{shapes} together with, for each $s\in S$
and each $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$, a position set $P^i_s$; its extension is the polynomial functor
$\sem{S\lhd P}(\vec X)=\sum_{s\in S}\prod_{i=1}^n X_i^{P^i_s}$. We work with finitary containers, so
only the cardinalities $|P^i_s|$ matter up to isomorphism \cite{AAGM2003}. Composition of containers
is substitution of extensions, written $G\lhd F$, with unit the identity container $y$; this makes
$(\Poly,\lhd,y)$ a (strict, once we pass to isomorphism classes) monoidal category, and the
comonoids in it are exactly the small categories \cite{AhmanUustalu2016,GambinoKock2013}. A container
is \emph{linear} if it is of the form $S\cdot y$ for a set $S$, i.e.\ its extension is
$\vec X\mapsto S\times X$ in one variable; \emph{finite-support} if its shape set is finite. We
write $\SPoly$ for the finite-support polynomial functors.

\paragraph{Infinitesimal objects and representable tangent bundles.}
We use the representable presentation of tangent structure of \cite{LanfranchiLemay2025},
transported along the dictionary $(\Mod_R,\otimes_R,R)\rightsquigarrow(\Poly,\lhd,y)$.

\begin{definition}[Infinitesimal object]\label{def:inf}
An \emph{infinitesimal object} in $(\Poly,\lhd,y)$ is a polynomial $D$ equipped with an
augmentation: morphisms $z\colon y\to D$ (zero) and $p\colon D\to y$ (projection) with
$p\circ z=\id_y$. The splitting $D=y\oplus M$ defines the \emph{infinitesimal part}
$M=\ker(p)$. Right-$\lhd$-tensoring by $D$ is the endofunctor $T:=(-)\lhd D$, with tangent
projection $A\lhd p\colon A\lhd D\to A$ and zero section $A\lhd z\colon A\to A\lhd D$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Representable first-order tangent structure]\label{def:tangent}
A \emph{representable first-order tangent structure} is a tangent structure (in the sense of
Cockett--Cruttwell \cite{CockettCruttwell2014}, first order) whose bundle functor is $T=(-)\lhd D$
for an infinitesimal object $D=y\oplus M$. Its infinitesimal part $M$ is called \emph{solid} if
the tangentoid multiplication $\mu\colon M\lhd M\to M$ is an isomorphism.
\end{definition}

The dual-number point $D=y+\varepsilon v$ (read as the infinitesimal object of the base change
$\N\to\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$) is such an object: the augmentation is $\varepsilon\mapsto0$,
the zero is $y\hookrightarrow D$, and $(-)\lhd D$ is exactly the dual-number substitution $T$ of
\cite{MacBethTangent}. Its infinitesimal part is the linear container $M=\langle\varepsilon
v\rangle=1\cdot y$, of rank $1$; the trivial structure is $D=y$, $M=0$, $T=\id$. That $T=(-)\lhd D$
is a genuine tangent structure --- not merely an endofunctor --- is the content of
\cite{MacBethTangent}; we take representability of $\partial$ as established there and ask only
whether other infinitesimal objects give rise to further structures.

\paragraph{The universality of the vertical lift.}
Among the tangent-structure axioms of \cite{CockettCruttwell2014} we use one by name. The
\emph{second tangent bundle} is $T_2:=T\times_{\id}T$ (the pullback of $T\xrightarrow{p}\id$ along
itself), and the \emph{vertical bundle} $V$ is the pullback of the tangent map
$Tp\colon T^2\to T$ along the zero $0\colon\id\to T$. The \emph{vertical lift} is a canonical
comparison $T_2\to T^2$, and the axiom of \emph{universality of the vertical lift}
(\cite{CockettCruttwell2014}, part of the definition of a tangent structure) asserts that the
square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
T_2 & \longrightarrow & T^2\\[-2pt]
\downarrow & & \ \downarrow{\scriptstyle Tp}\\[-2pt]
\id & \xrightarrow{\ 0\ } & T
\end{array}
\]
is a pullback; equivalently, that the induced map $T_2\to V$ is an isomorphism. This is the single
structural axiom the converse of \S\ref{sec:converse} invokes.

\paragraph{Why the infinitesimal part is linear.}
For a tangent structure the projection $p\colon T\Rightarrow\id$ is an \emph{additive bundle}: each
fibre carries a commutative-monoid structure and the bundle addition is linear. For $T=(-)\lhd D$
this forces the infinitesimal part $M$ to be a \emph{linear} container $M=S\cdot y$. A non-linear
$M$ --- for instance $\varepsilon y^2$, whose $\varepsilon$-coefficient is $y^2 p'$ rather than a
directional $v\cdot p'$ --- has no vector-space fibre and fails the additive-bundle axiom. So
linearity of $M$ is \emph{forced}, not assumed, and it is this linearity (not any projective-module
theory) that supplies freeness in \S\ref{sec:rigidity}.

\section{The bundle and module tensors coincide on the linear layer}\label{sec:coincide}

The port of the Lanfranchi--Lemay argument has one notorious hazard: the tangent \emph{bundle} is
built from substitution $\lhd$, whereas the \emph{solid} condition $M\otimes M\cong M$ lives in the
``module layer'' where rank multiplies, and these look like two different monoidal products. One
might fear a mismatch between the tensor the bundle uses and the tensor in which solidity is
measured. The following observation dissolves the fear.

\begin{lemma}[Tensor coincidence on the linear layer]\label{lem:coincide}
For linear containers, substitution and the Dirichlet product agree:
\[
  (S\cdot y)\lhd(T\cdot y)\ =\ (S\times T)\cdot y\ =\ (S\cdot y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(T\cdot y).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The extension of $S\cdot y$ is $X\mapsto S\times X$. Composing,
$(S\cdot y)\lhd(T\cdot y)\colon X\mapsto S\times(T\times X)=(S\times T)\times X=(S\times T)\cdot y$.
The Dirichlet product of $\sum_{s}y^1$ and $\sum_t y^1$ is $\sum_{(s,t)}y^{1\times1}=(S\times
T)\cdot y$. Both equal $(S\times T)\cdot y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The two tensors agree on monomials ($y^a\lhd y^b=y^{ab}=y^a\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}y^b$), and in
particular on the linear layer, which is all the rigidity needs: the infinitesimal part is linear
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}), and $M\lhd M$ is computed there. On polynomials with several monomials the
two diverge --- already $(y+1)\lhd(y+1)=y+2$ while $(y+1)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(y+1)=y+3$ --- so
Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide} is genuinely a statement about the linear layer, not a global identity.
Neither tensor is the ``doubling'' Dirichlet action $(2y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(-)$, which sends
$p\mapsto2p$ and has nothing to do with the derivative.
\end{remark}

\emph{Consequence.} On the linear layer the \emph{bundle} tensor $\lhd$ already multiplies rank, so
the solid condition $M\lhd M\cong M$ reads $|S|^2=|S|$ in the very tensor the bundle uses. There is
no mismatch; this single coincidence is what makes the port faithful.

\section{Rank rigidity: the solid parts are $\{0,y\}$}\label{sec:rigidity}

We classify the finite solid infinitesimal parts. Two structural facts about linear containers,
then the rank computation.

\begin{lemma}[Freeness is automatic over $\N$]\label{lem:free}
Every linear container $S\cdot y$ is the free $\N$-module on the set $S$; two linear containers are
isomorphic iff their shape sets are equinumerous. The cardinal $|S|=:\rank(M)$ is a complete
isomorphism invariant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under coproduct of containers and the $\N$-action $n\cdot(S\cdot y)=(n\times S)\cdot y$, the linear
containers form the free-$\N$-module functor applied to sets: $S\cdot y$ is the $|S|$-fold copower of
$y$. A container isomorphism $S\cdot y\cong T\cdot y$ is, since morphisms between linear containers
are just functions on shapes (positions being singletons), a bijection $S\cong T$. Hence $\rank$ is
a complete invariant.
\end{proof}

This is the analogue of Lanfranchi--Lemay's ``projective $\Rightarrow$ free $\Rightarrow$ rank is a
complete invariant'' (their Lemma~4.16, via Rotman's structure theorem, which \emph{requires} the
base to be a PID). Here it is \emph{automatic}: the linear polynomial functors \emph{are} the free
$\N$-modules by definition, so no PID hypothesis is needed and the rigidity below holds over the
rig $\N$ --- strictly outside the Lanfranchi--Lemay hypotheses.

\begin{lemma}[Solid $\Rightarrow$ idempotent rank]\label{lem:solid}
If $M=S\cdot y$ is solid, i.e.\ $\mu\colon M\lhd M\to M$ is an isomorphism, then $|S|^2=|S|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}, $M\lhd M=(S\times S)\cdot y$, of rank $|S|^2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}.
An isomorphism $\mu\colon(S\times S)\cdot y\cong S\cdot y$ forces, by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}, a
bijection $S\times S\cong S$, i.e.\ $|S|^2=|S|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite rigidity]\label{thm:rigidity}
A finite-support solid infinitesimal part has rank in $\{0,1\}$. Hence there are exactly two:
$M=0$ (rank $0$) and $M=y$ (rank $1$). By \S\ref{sec:prelim} these are the trivial structure and
the AAGM dual-number structure, with combinator $\partial$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a natural number $n=|S|$ we have $n^2=n\iff n(n-1)=0\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, since $\N$ has no zero
divisors. By Lemma~\ref{lem:solid} a finite solid part has $|S|^2=|S|$, so $|S|\in\{0,1\}$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:free} the rank determines $M$, giving $M\in\{0,y\}$.
\end{proof}

This is the Lanfranchi--Lemay rank computation $R^{n^2}\cong R^n\Rightarrow n^2=n\Rightarrow
n\in\{0,1\}$, ported verbatim with the cardinality rank, and --- per Lemma~\ref{lem:free} --- without
their PID hypothesis.

\paragraph{Machine certification of the arithmetic.}
The finite arithmetic of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is certified in a Mathlib-free Lean~4
development with no classical axioms. The oracle proves: $n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$ on $\N$ (in
general, \texttt{omega}-free); that the solid ranks in $0\le n\le12$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$ (by
\texttt{decide}); that these two ranks map through the structure assignment to exactly $\{\id,
\partial\}$, with the dual-number object of arity $2$; that every rank $r\ge2$ (the Whitney sums
of \S\ref{sec:converse}) is non-solid; and that the Cantor pairing is injective on a
$12\times12$ window, witnessing $|\N\times\N|=|\N|$ for \S\ref{sec:sharp}. \texttt{\#print axioms}
on the packaged oracle reports only \texttt{propext} (a structural, non-classical axiom); the
finite \texttt{decide} claims are individually axiom-free, with no \texttt{sorryAx},
\texttt{Quot.sound} or \texttt{Classical.choice}.

\section{The finiteness hypothesis is sharp}\label{sec:sharp}

Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is stated for finite support, and the hypothesis cannot be dropped.

\begin{proposition}[Infinite-support obstruction]\label{prop:infinite}
The countable linear container $M=\N\cdot y$ is solid: $M\lhd M=(\N\times\N)\cdot y\cong\N\cdot
y=M$, because $|\N\times\N|=|\N|$. It is a solid infinitesimal part distinct from $0$ and $y$.
Consequently unqualified uniqueness over all of $\Poly$ is false.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide} gives $M\lhd M=(\N\times\N)\cdot y$, and the Cantor pairing supplies a
bijection $\N\times\N\cong\N$ (injectivity checked on a $40\times40$ window; see the Lean oracle of
\S\ref{sec:rigidity} for a certified $12\times12$ window). By Lemma~\ref{lem:free} this is an
isomorphism $\mu\colon M\lhd M\cong M$, and $\rank M=|\N|\notin\{0,1\}$.
\end{proof}

More generally every infinite cardinal $\kappa$ satisfies $\kappa^2=\kappa$ (under choice), so each
yields a solid infinitesimal part. This is the exact analogue of Lanfranchi--Lemay's extra
structures when the finite-generation hypothesis is relaxed --- their non-PID example $R=S\times S$
(Example~4.21) and non-finitely-generated $\mathbb{Z}\ltimes\mathbb{Q}$ (Example~4.20). The moral is
that the honest hypothesis is a \emph{finite-dimensional} infinitesimal part: finitely many tangent
directions, matching Lanfranchi--Lemay's finite-generation (Niefield coexponentiability) condition,
here realised as plain finiteness of the shape set. We will see in \S\ref{sec:converse} that the
converse does not exclude $\N\cdot y$ either, so finiteness is load-bearing for \emph{both} halves
of the uniqueness theorem.

\section{The solidity converse: universality of the vertical lift}\label{sec:converse}

Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} classifies the solid infinitesimal \emph{parts}. To pass from parts to
tangent \emph{structures} we need the converse direction of the correspondence
\begin{quote}
$(\star)$\quad representable first-order tangent structure $T=(-)\lhd D$ $\iff$ solid infinitesimal
part $M=\ker(D\to y)$.
\end{quote}
The forward direction is immediate once the parts are known (\S\ref{sec:uniqueness}). The converse
--- that every representable first-order tangent structure has a \emph{solid} infinitesimal part ---
is Lanfranchi--Lemay's Theorem~4.8, proved over a commutative ring, and is the step that did not
transfer verbatim: their derivation computes a vertical bundle by a short exact sequence, hence by
subtraction, which the rig $\N$ lacks. We now give a rig-internal proof. The engine is the
universality of the vertical lift (\S\ref{sec:prelim}); the one new idea is that, in the extensive
category of polynomial functors, the vertical bundle is a \emph{direct coproduct}, so its rank is
computed by addition and never by a difference.

Throughout, $T=(-)\lhd D$ with $D=y\oplus M$, $M=S\cdot y$ linear of finite rank $n=|S|$ (linearity
forced, \S\ref{sec:prelim}).

\subsection{Substitution preserves pullbacks on both sides}

\begin{lemma}[$\lhd$ preserves pullbacks]\label{lem:pullback}
For every $D\in\Poly$ the functor $(-)\lhd D$ preserves pullbacks; and for every polynomial $A$ the
functor $A\lhd(-)$ preserves pullbacks.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pullbacks in $\Poly$ are computed pointwise. For $(-)\lhd D$: if $F=G\times_H K$ pointwise then for
all $X$, $(F\lhd D)(X)=F(D(X))=G(D(X))\times_{H(D(X))}K(D(X))=(G\lhd D\times_{H\lhd D}K\lhd D)(X)$,
so $F\lhd D=G\lhd D\times_{H\lhd D}K\lhd D$. For $A\lhd(-)$: a polynomial functor $A$ preserves
connected limits (\cite{GambinoKock2013}, Prop.~1.9), and a pullback is a connected limit, so $A$
preserves the pointwise pullback $E_1(X)\times_{E_0(X)}E_2(X)$; hence $A\lhd(-)$ preserves
pullbacks.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence: the tangent data are all right-$\lhd$-tensorings.} The projection, zero, tangent
map and the two bundles of $T=(-)\lhd D$ are $(-)\lhd(\text{structure map on }D)$:
$p=(-)\lhd\hat p$ with $\hat p\colon D\to y$ the augmentation and $z=(-)\lhd\hat z$ with $\hat
z\colon y\to D$ the zero; and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback} applied so that $A\lhd(-)$ carries the
relevant pointwise pullback,
\[
  T_2=(-)\lhd D_2,\quad D_2:=D\times_y D;\qquad
  V=(-)\lhd P',\quad P':=\hat g^{-1}(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z),\ \ \hat g:=\hat p\lhd D\colon D\lhd D\to D.
\]
All of $D$, $D_2$, $D\lhd D$, $P'$ are objects of $\Poly$, and $y$ is one of them.

\subsection{The three coproduct counts}

We work in the based setting: $\varepsilon$-grading modulo $\varepsilon^2$ within each copy of $D$,
with the mixed term surviving across copies --- the Weil-algebra model
$\N[\varepsilon_1,\dots]/(\text{within-copy squares})$ that the base-change presentation of $T$
\cite{MacBethTangent} delivers. In the extensive category $\Poly$ each object below is a direct
coproduct of summands, so its rank is the \emph{sum} of the summand ranks.

\begin{lemma}[Coproduct ranks]\label{lem:counts}
With $n=\rank M$ and $\rank(M\lhd M)=n^2$ (Lemmas~\ref{lem:coincide},~\ref{lem:free}):
\[
\begin{aligned}
D\lhd D &= y\oplus M\oplus M\oplus(M\lhd M), &\rank &= 1+n+n+n^2=(1+n)^2;\\
D_2=D\times_y D &= y\oplus M\oplus M, &\rank &= 1+2n;\\
P' &= y\oplus \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\oplus(M\lhd M), &\rank &= 1+n+n^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{($D\lhd D$.)} Since $M$ is linear, $M(X)=S\times X$, so
$(D\lhd D)(X)=D(D(X))=X\oplus M(X)\oplus M(X)\oplus M(M(X))$: the base $y$, the inner copy's $M$
($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$), the outer copy's $M$ ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}$), and the mixed
$M\lhd M=(S\times S)\cdot y$. Ranks $1,n,n,n^2$.

\emph{($D_2$.)} A fibre product over the base shares $y$ and adjoins the two infinitesimal
directions but creates \emph{no} product of the two infinitesimals: $D_2=y\oplus M\oplus M$, rank
$1+2n$.

\emph{($P'$.)} The map $\hat g=\hat p\lhd D$ applies the augmentation $\hat p$ ($\varepsilon\mapsto0$)
to the \emph{outer} copy: it kills $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}$ and the mixed term $M\lhd M$ (both
carry an outer-$M$ factor) and fixes $y$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$. The zero $\hat z$ has
image the $y$-summand. Hence $P'=\hat g^{-1}(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z)$ is the sub-coproduct on which the
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$-component vanishes:
$P'=y\oplus\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\oplus(M\lhd M)$, rank $1+n+n^2$. In the extensive category
$\Poly$ this pullback \emph{is} the sub-coproduct of surviving summands, so its rank is the sum
$1+n+n^2$ --- not $\rank(D\lhd D)-\rank(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z)$. This is the step where
Lanfranchi--Lemay form a difference of ranks (a short exact sequence over a ring), and where we do
not.
\end{proof}

Both $D_2=(1+2n)\cdot y$ and $P'=(1+n+n^2)\cdot y$ are linear (a coproduct $y\oplus(\text{free})$ is
again linear), so Lemma~\ref{lem:free} applies to them.

\subsection{Universality forces $n^2=n$}

\begin{theorem}[Solidity converse]\label{thm:converse}
Let $T=(-)\lhd D$ be a representable first-order tangent structure on finite-support polynomial
functors, with $D=y\oplus M$ and $n=\rank M$ finite. Then $n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$; i.e.\ $M$
is solid and $M\in\{0,y\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the universality of the vertical lift (\S\ref{sec:prelim}), the comparison $T_2\to V$ is an
isomorphism of endofunctors, naturally in the argument. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback} and its
consequence, $T_2=(-)\lhd D_2$ and $V=(-)\lhd P'$. Evaluate the functor isomorphism at the object
$y\in\Poly$ and use $y\lhd E=E$:
\[
  D_2\ \cong\ P'\qquad\text{in }\Poly.
\]
Both are linear (Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:free} their shape-counts agree:
\[
  1+2n\ =\ 1+n+n^2.
\]
Now use only that $\N$ is a cancellative rig with no zero divisors --- \emph{no subtraction, no
additive inverses}. Additive left-cancellation of the common summand $1+n$ gives $n=n^2$. For
$n\ge1$, multiplicative cancellation of $n$ in $n\cdot n=n\cdot1$ gives $n=1$; and $n=0$ satisfies
$0^2=0$. Hence $n\in\{0,1\}$, so $M\in\{0,y\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}, and $\mu\colon M\lhd M\to M$
is an isomorphism in each case.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is rig-internal]
The subtraction that blocked a literal port lived in computing the vertical-bundle fibre $P'$ by
exactness. In $\Poly$ that fibre is a direct coproduct of the surviving summands
(Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}), so its rank is a \emph{sum}. Everything downstream is
additive/multiplicative cancellation in $\N$. The derivation never forms a difference of ranks,
never inverts an element, and never constructs the solid map $\mu$ explicitly --- solidity is
\emph{output}, read off from $n\in\{0,1\}$. Lanfranchi--Lemay's Theorem~4.8 instead builds $\mu$ and
argues over a ring; the content we have replaced is precisely their one subtraction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The non-solid finite candidates are auxiliary bundles]\label{rem:whitney}
The finite candidate objects excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} are the higher square-zero
objects $D_r=y+\varepsilon_1 v_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_r v_r$ ($\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j=0$, $r\ge2$),
with $M=\N^r$, $M\lhd M=\N^{r^2}$, rank $r^2\ne r$: not solid. These $D_r$ are precisely the Whitney
sums $T_r=T\times_X\cdots\times_X T$, which occur in the tangent axioms as \emph{auxiliary} bundles,
not as standalone tangent structures. So the objects the converse rejects are exactly the ones that
are not primitive structures --- solidity coincides with indecomposability, as it should. (The
non-solidity of every $r\ge2$ is part of the certified Lean oracle below.)
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Consistency with sharpness]
The rank equation $1+2n=1+n+n^2$ is satisfied by every infinite cardinal $\kappa$
($1+2\kappa=\kappa=1+\kappa+\kappa^2$), so universality does \emph{not} exclude $M=\N\cdot y$. The
converse is therefore as sharp as the rigidity: both hold only under finiteness, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:infinite}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Machine certification of the arithmetic.}
The rig-internal arithmetic of Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} is certified in the same Mathlib-free,
axiom-light Lean~4 style. The oracle proves the three coproduct-rank identities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:counts} --- $\rank(D\lhd D)=(1+n)^2$, $\rank(D\lhd D)=\rank P'+n$,
$\rank(D\lhd D)=\rank D_2+n^2$ --- which are the honesty certificate for ``ranks add, never
subtract''; the universality collapse $\rank D_2=\rank P'\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, proved by
\texttt{Nat.add\_left\_cancel} (literal additive cancellation, \emph{no} \texttt{omega}) reducing to
$n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$; the finite enumeration (survivors in $0\le n\le12$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$);
and the two witnesses $n=1$ (universality holds, $\rank D_2=\rank P'=3$, the Weil rank of
$\N[\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2]/(\varepsilon_1^2,\varepsilon_2^2,\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)$) and
$n=2$ (excluded, $5\ne7$). \texttt{\#print axioms} on the packaged oracle reports only
\texttt{propext}. The Lean certifies the \emph{arithmetic collapse} and the additivity of the
counts; it does \emph{not} formalise the structural inputs --- the universality axiom
(\cite{CockettCruttwell2014}), that $\lhd$ preserves pullbacks (Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback}, via
\cite{GambinoKock2013}), or that $M$ is linear (the additive-bundle axiom) --- which are cited
framework facts, and we are explicit that they are the mathematical, not the decidable, content.

\section{The uniqueness theorem}\label{sec:uniqueness}

With both halves in hand the correspondence $(\star)$ is an equivalence, and the uniqueness theorem
assembles.

\paragraph{Forward: solid $\Rightarrow$ structure.}
A finite solid part yields a tangent structure in the two surviving cases. $M=0$ gives $T=\id$;
$M=y$ gives the AAGM dual-number structure, which \emph{is} a tangent structure --- the full
Cartesian differential / tangent verification is the content of \cite{MacBethTangent}. Since
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} leaves no third finite solid part, the forward direction never has to
manufacture a third structure.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness]\label{thm:uniqueness}
Among representable first-order tangent structures $T=(-)\lhd D$ on finite-support polynomial
functors there are exactly two: the trivial structure ($D=y$, $T=\id$) and the AAGM dual-number
structure ($D=y+\varepsilon v$, $T(p)=p(y+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2$, combinator $\partial$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=(-)\lhd D$ be such a structure, $D=y\oplus M$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} its
infinitesimal part is solid with $\rank M\in\{0,1\}$; by Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} (equivalently
Lemma~\ref{lem:free}) this forces $M\in\{0,y\}$, hence $D\in\{y,\ y+\varepsilon v\}$ up to
isomorphism. By the forward direction both are realised, by $\id$ and $\partial$ respectively.
Hence exactly two.
\end{proof}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}, now unconditional: the conditional hypothesis of the earlier
draft --- that every representable first-order tangent structure has a solid infinitesimal part ---
is discharged by Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}.

The theorem is a clean deliverable for two audiences. For the tangent-category community
\cite{CockettCruttwell2014,LanfranchiLemay2025} it exhibits a rig-based model in which the

reasons than over a PID --- no principal ideals, no subtraction --- isolating exactly which uses of

infinitesimal parts) and which were incidental (the PID and the short exact sequence, both

the \emph{only} nontrivial one,'' a uniqueness theorem for a construction that is otherwise defined

\section{Conclusion: precedent and sequel}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proved that the container derivative $\partial$ is the unique nontrivial representable
first-order tangent structure a finite-support polynomial functor can carry
(Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}). The proof ports Lanfranchi--Lemay's affine-scheme classification in
both halves, and in both the rig $\N$ makes the port \emph{cleaner}: freeness is automatic, so the
rigidity needs no PID (Lemma~\ref{lem:free}); and the vertical bundle is a direct coproduct, so the
converse needs no subtraction (Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}, Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}). Two technical
observations carry the port: on the linear layer the bundle tensor $\lhd$ and the module tensor
coincide (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), and $\Poly$ is extensive so that ranks add. The arithmetic of
both halves is Lean-certified with no classical axioms. The finiteness is sharp: $\N\cdot y$ is a
third solid part over all of $\Poly$, and neither half of the theorem excludes it, so the honest
hypothesis is a finite-dimensional infinitesimal part.

\paragraph{Precedent.}
The hope that polynomial functors carry a differential structure is Cockett's. His unpublished
FMCS~2012 talk \emph{Can you Differentiate a Polynomial?} \cite{Cockett2012} conjectured that they
form a Cartesian differential, hence a tangent, category --- the thesis the companion note
\cite{MacBethTangent} later proved. The 2012 slides, though, are explicit that this was conjecture:
the differentiation there is defined only on ``separable'' polynomials, closure under composition is
asserted rather than proved, and the chain rule is named as the hard open point. (The slides even
record, as an aside, that this is ``a differential category in which negation is unnatural'' --- the
same absence of additive inverses that we have just turned to advantage, since it is exactly what
makes the vertical-bundle coproduct, rather than a short exact sequence, the right tool.) No
uniqueness statement appears in 2012. The present note supplies it, and so answers Cockett's
question in its strongest form: not only \emph{can} you differentiate a polynomial, but
differentiation is the \emph{only} nontrivial way to do so. We flag one caveat, due to Cruttwell via
\cite{MO341933}: combinatorial species --- the setting closest to Cockett's --- are most naturally a
tangent \emph{bicategory}, with species as $1$-morphisms, which is structurally different from the
$\SPoly$ one-category treated here; our uniqueness is a statement about the latter.

\paragraph{Sequel.}
A rigid tangent structure is the entry condition for the Cartan-calculus / Lie-algebroid program of
Aintablian and Blohmann \cite{AintablianBlohmann2026}, which asks when a tangent category supports a
scalar ``ring object'' $R$ making each $TX$ an $R$-module compatibly with the tangent axioms, and
thereby upgrades a tangent structure to a Lie-algebroid structure with a full Cartan calculus. That
$\SPoly$ now has a \emph{unique} nontrivial first-order tangent structure makes the question
well-posed: is there such an $R$ for $(\SPoly,\partial)$? The negation remark above is a warning
sign --- a scalar ring object requires additive inverses, which $\N$ does not have --- and
determining exactly how much of the Cartan calculus survives over a rig is the natural next problem.
We leave it, together with higher Weil algebras $\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^k$ ($k\ge3$) and
iterated tangents (first-order out of scope here, as for \cite{AAGM2003,LanfranchiLemay2025,CCGZ2024}),
to future work.
