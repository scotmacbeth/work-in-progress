\begin{abstract}
A companion note showed that the Abbott--Altenkirch--Ghani--McBride one-hole derivative
$\partial$ is the differential combinator of a Cartesian differential category $\SPoly$ of
polynomial functors, with the dual-number substitution $p\mapsto p(y+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2$
computing its combinator; that settles \emph{existence}. This note settles \emph{uniqueness}. The
right setting is the subcategory $\Lin(\SPoly)$ of \emph{linear} maps --- morphisms given by
$\N$-matrices, composition the matrix product --- equivalent to the finite-rank part of the category
$\FreeMod_\N$ of free $\N$-modules. On this subcategory the tangent structure is \emph{base change}
$T=(-)\otimes_{\N}D$ by an augmented square-zero $\N$-algebra (a Weil algebra over $\N$), the
dual numbers $W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$ being the first instance; this is the module layer of
Lanfranchi--Lemay's representable tangent structures, now over the rig $\N$. We prove that among
representable first-order tangent structures of this form there are \emph{exactly two}, the trivial
structure $T=\id$ and the container derivative $\partial$. The proof has two halves. The first is a
rank rigidity: an infinitesimal object $D=y\oplus M$ has a \emph{solid} infinitesimal part when the
vertical-lift component $\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ is invertible, and over $\N$ a linear module
is automatically free of a well-defined rank, so solidity forces $|S|^2=|S|$, whose only finite
solutions are $0$ and $1$. The second is the converse: every such tangent structure has a solid
infinitesimal part. We prove this rig-internally from the universality of the vertical lift,
replacing Lanfranchi--Lemay's ring-theoretic short-exact-sequence step --- which needs subtraction
--- by the observation that the vertical bundle is a \emph{direct sum} of its surviving summands, so
its rank is computed by addition: free $\N$-modules are closed under direct sum and rank is additive
over it ($\rank(A\oplus B)=\rank A+\rank B$), with no subtraction available to undo it. The port is thus not only faithful but cleaner: no
principal-ideal hypothesis and no subtraction. Crucially, on the linear layer base change $\otimes D$
coincides with container substitution $\lhd$ (and with the Dirichlet tensor), so the classification
is at once a statement about Weil base change and about the container derivative --- but it is
\emph{not} a statement about $\lhd$-substitution on all of $\Poly$, where there is no
$\varepsilon^2=0$ truncation ($y^2\lhd 2y=4y^2$) and only $T=\id$ survives. The uniqueness holds for
a free infinitesimal part of \emph{any} rank. Finiteness is \emph{not} load-bearing: although
$M=\N\cdot y$ is \emph{abstractly} solid, because $|\N\times\N|=|\N|$ supplies an abstract
isomorphism $M\otimes M\cong M$, abstract solidity is strictly weaker than underlying a tangent
structure at infinite rank --- the flip and
lift-coassociativity axioms force the vertical lift to be the diagonal, which is surjective only in
rank $\le 1$, so $\N\cdot y$ is \emph{not} a tangent structure (an earlier draft's ``finiteness is
sharp'' claim, which measured solidity by an abstract isomorphism, is withdrawn). The uniqueness
answers, and sharpens, a question of Cockett (2012).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{From existence to uniqueness.}
In a companion note \cite{MacBethTangent} we showed that the category $\SPoly$ of finite-support
polynomial functors is a Cartesian differential category whose differential combinator is the
Abbott--Altenkirch--Ghani--McBride one-hole-context derivative $\partial$ \cite{AAGM2003}, and that
its combinator is computed by the oldest trick in differential algebra: substitute a dual number
into a \emph{map} and read off the $\varepsilon$-coefficient,
\[
  \partial[f](x,v)\ =\ \varepsilon\text{-coefficient of } f(x+\varepsilon v)\bmod\varepsilon^2
  \ =\ v\cdot\partial f.
\]
That note answers an \emph{existence} question --- there is a tangent structure whose vertical part
is $\partial$. The natural next question is \emph{uniqueness}. This $\partial$-structure is one
tangent structure; the identity functor $T=\id$ is another (the trivial one). Are there others?
Could there be an exotic first-order tangent bundle that is neither the derivative nor trivial? This
note proves there cannot.

First we must say precisely in which category the question is asked --- and here the right setting
is not $\Poly$ at large but its \emph{linear} subcategory. Let $\Lin(\SPoly)$ be the subcategory of
$\SPoly$ whose morphisms are the \emph{linear} maps $n\to m$ --- those given by an $\N$-matrix, with
composition the matrix product --- which is equivalent to the category $\FreeMod_\N$ of finite free
$\N$-modules. On $\Lin(\SPoly)$ the tangent structure is \emph{base change} $T=(-)\otimes_\N D$ by an
augmented square-zero $\N$-algebra $D$ (a Weil algebra over $\N$, the dual numbers
$W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$ being the first instance); this is precisely the module layer on
which Lanfranchi--Lemay's representable tangent structures live, now over the rig $\N$ rather than a
ring. Two cautions fix the setting. First, base change is \emph{not} the substitution formula: the
ring map $\N[y]\to W[y]$ sends $y^2\mapsto y^2$, whereas the dual-number \emph{substitution}
$y\mapsto y+\varepsilon v$ reads off $y^2+2\varepsilon vy$; the two agree only on \emph{linear}
arguments, and a non-linear polynomial is in any case neither an object nor a morphism of
$\FreeMod_\N$. The substitution formula is how the companion note computes the combinator $\partial$
at the level of $\SPoly$ \emph{maps} \cite{MacBethTangent}; the tangent \emph{functor} on modules is
base change. Second, it is \emph{tempting}, but wrong, to read $T$ as right-substitution $(-)\lhd W$
in the full substitution-monoidal category $(\Poly,\lhd,y)$: full $\lhd$ has no $\varepsilon^2=0$
truncation ($y^2\lhd 2y=(2y)^2=4y^2$, not $3y^2$; in general $y^n\lhd W=W^n$ is multiplicative), so on
all of $\Poly$ the functor $(-)\lhd W$ forces the trivial structure alone. The derivative survives
precisely because the truncation --- the Weil algebra --- is present. What rescues the container
reading is a coincidence (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}): on the linear layer $S\cdot y$, base change
$\otimes D$, substitution $\lhd$, and the Dirichlet tensor all agree, and it is on this layer ---
the finite-rank part of $\FreeMod_\N$, namely $\Lin(\SPoly)$ --- that the container-derivative reading
of the classification lives.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness]\label{thm:main-intro}
Among representable first-order tangent structures $T=(-)\otimes D$ on $\FreeMod_\N$ --- base change
by an augmented square-zero $\N$-algebra $D=y\oplus M$ with $M$ a free $\N$-module of \emph{any} rank
--- there are exactly two: the trivial structure ($D=y$, $M=0$, $T=\id$) and the AAGM dual-number
structure ($D=W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$, $M=\langle\varepsilon v\rangle$, combinator
$\partial$). Restricted to the finite-rank part $\Lin(\SPoly)$ --- the linear layer of $\SPoly$,
where $\otimes D$ and $\lhd$ coincide --- this says the container derivative is the only nontrivial
representable first-order tangent structure.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{The question is Cockett's.}
That polynomial functors should carry a differential structure is not a new hope. In an
unpublished talk at FMCS~2012, \emph{Can you Differentiate a Polynomial?}, Cockett
\cite{Cockett2012} conjectured exactly this --- that polynomial functors form a Cartesian
differential, hence a tangent, category --- fourteen years before the companion note proved it.
But the 2012 slides are candid about how much was conjecture: the differentiation there is defined
only on ``separable'' polynomials, closure under composition is asserted but not proved, and the
chain rule is flagged as the hard open point (``\emph{of course, need to prove this is a
differential!! (chain rule is already challenging\ldots)}''; ``\emph{Is this all completely sorted
out? Absolutely not!}''). No uniqueness statement appears, nor could it: without the structure in
hand there is nothing to count. The companion note supplied the structure; the present note
supplies the count, and so closes the arc Cockett opened. (We flag one divergence in
\S\ref{sec:conclusion}: combinatorial species, the setting nearest Cockett's, are most naturally a
tangent \emph{bicategory} \cite{MO341933}, structurally different from the $\SPoly$ one-category we
work in.)

\paragraph{The template, and why it does not simply transfer.}
For commutative algebra the uniqueness question has a sharp answer. Lanfranchi and Lemay
\cite{LanfranchiLemay2025} classify the \emph{representable} tangent structures on the opposite of
the category of modules (equivalently, affine schemes): a representable tangent structure is a
tensoring $T=D\otimes(-)$ by an \emph{infinitesimal object} $D=R\oplus M$, and when the base ring
$R$ is a principal ideal domain there are \emph{exactly two} --- the trivial one ($M=0$) and the
Kähler/dual-number one ($M=R$). Their argument has two load-bearing uses of ring structure. The
\emph{rigidity} half (a solid infinitesimal part has rank $n$ with $R^{n^2}\cong R^n$, forcing
$n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$; their Theorem~4.17, Corollary~4.18) needs $R$ to be a PID, so that
the infinitesimal module is free with a well-defined rank. The \emph{converse} half (a tangent
structure's infinitesimal part \emph{is} solid; their Theorem~4.8, via the universality of the
vertical lift) computes a vertical bundle by a short exact sequence --- i.e.\ by \emph{subtraction}
of ranks. Neither transfers to polynomial functors verbatim: the base there is the rig $\N$, which
is not a PID and has no subtraction.

\paragraph{What we prove, and the one new idea.}
We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro} by porting \emph{both} halves of Lanfranchi--Lemay to the
Weil base-change setting over $\N$ --- the tangent functor $T=(-)\otimes W$ on $\FreeMod_\N$, whose
finite-rank part is $\Lin(\SPoly)$ --- and in both the port turns out \emph{cleaner} than its template.
Throughout, the objects that carry the classification are \emph{linear} ($S\cdot y$), the layer on
which base change $\otimes W$, substitution $\lhd$ and the Dirichlet tensor coincide
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}); this is what licenses the container-derivative reading of the result.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Rigidity} (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}). Inside $\FreeMod_\N$ the infinitesimal part $M$ is a
free module with a well-defined rank, so freeness is \emph{automatic}, with no PID hypothesis.
Solidity (invertibility of the vertical lift $\ell_M$) gives an abstract isomorphism $M\otimes
M\cong M$, which reads $|S|^2=|S|$, whose only finite solutions are $0$ and $1$: the finite solid
parts are exactly $\{0,y\}$.
\item \emph{Converse} (\S\ref{sec:converse}). Every such tangent structure has
a solid infinitesimal part. Here is the one new idea. The place Lanfranchi--Lemay use subtraction
is the computation of the vertical bundle fibre $P'$ by exactness. Over $\N$ that fibre is instead a
\emph{direct sum} of the surviving summands,
\[
  P'\ =\ y\ \oplus\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\ \oplus\ (M\lhd M),
\]
so its rank is a \emph{sum}, $1+n+n^2$, computed by addition and never as a difference. The
universality of the vertical lift (Cockett--Cruttwell \cite{CockettCruttwell2014}) identifies the
second tangent bundle $T_2$ with the vertical bundle $V$; evaluating this functor isomorphism at
$y$ gives $D_2\cong P'$, i.e.\ $1+2n=1+n+n^2$, and the cancellative rig $\N$ (no zero divisors,
\emph{no subtraction}) forces $n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$.
\item \emph{Rank-free exclusion} (\S\ref{sec:sharp}). The rank count above needs finiteness, since
$\kappa^2=\kappa$ for infinite $\kappa$. But the vertical lift of a tangent structure is not merely
an abstract isomorphism: the flip and lift-coassociativity axioms force it to be the diagonal, which
exists only in rank $\le1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}). So the infinite
solid part $\N\cdot y$ is a \emph{phantom} --- solid but not a structure --- and uniqueness holds at
every rank.
\end{itemize}
Assembling the three with the forward correspondence (both surviving parts are realised ---
$\id$ and $\partial$) gives Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}.

\paragraph{Finiteness is not load-bearing: solidity versus a tangent structure.}
The rank count above --- the step $1+2n=1+n+n^2\Rightarrow n\in\{0,1\}$ --- genuinely uses
finiteness: the equation $1+2\kappa=1+\kappa+\kappa^2$ holds for every infinite cardinal $\kappa$, so
it does not by itself exclude $M=\N\cdot y$. One might therefore fear, as an earlier draft did, that
finiteness is sharp and $\N\cdot y$ a third solid structure. It is not, and the fear rested on
measuring solidity by an abstract isomorphism. It is true that $\N\cdot y$ is \emph{solid}: the
Cantor pairing $\N\times\N\cong\N$ supplies an isomorphism $M\otimes M\cong M$, hence an isomorphism
$\ell_M$. But a tangent structure demands more of $\ell_M$ than invertibility. Its vertical lift must
be \emph{flip-compatible} ($c\ell=\ell$) and \emph{coassociative}, and these two axioms force
$\ell_M$ to be the diagonal $M\to M\otimes M$ --- which is surjective only when $\rank M\le1$. Hence
$\rank M\le1$ \emph{at every cardinality}: $\N\cdot y$ carries no tangent structure, and uniqueness
holds for a free infinitesimal part of any rank (\S\ref{sec:sharp}, where this is proved in full).
Solidity is thus strictly weaker than
underlying a tangent structure once the rank is infinite, and the two coincide (landing in
$\{0,1\}$) at finite rank. The honest statement is the rank-free one; finiteness was guarding a
phantom.

\paragraph{Certification.}
The arithmetic of both halves is machine-checked in a Mathlib-free Lean~4 development with no
classical axioms: the idempotent collapse $n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, the finite enumeration of solid
ranks, the three coproduct-rank identities underwriting the ``ranks add, never subtract'' claim,
and the universality collapse $1+2n=1+n+n^2\iff n\in\{0,1\}$ by additive cancellation. The Lean
certifies the \emph{arithmetic}; the structural inputs (the universality axiom, that $\lhd$
preserves pullbacks, that $M$ is linear) are cited framework facts, and we are explicit about the
boundary (\S\ref{sec:rigidity},~\S\ref{sec:converse}).

\paragraph{Contribution.}
The contributions are: the uniqueness theorem itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}), proved over a
rig with neither a PID hypothesis nor subtraction; the rigidity half (finite solid parts
$=\{0,y\}$); the rig-internal converse, whose single new idea is that the vertical bundle is a direct
sum of free summands so that ranks add, never subtract (\S\ref{sec:converse}); the technical
observation that on the linear layer the base-change tensor $\otimes W$, the substitution tensor
$\lhd$ and the Dirichlet tensor all coincide (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), which is simultaneously
what makes solidity computable in the bundle's own tensor and what connects the Weil base-change
classification to the container derivative; the observation that solidity is strictly weaker than
underlying a tangent structure at infinite rank, so that uniqueness is rank-free
(\S\ref{sec:sharp}); and the Lean certification of the finite arithmetic. The result is a clean
deliverable for the tangent-category and \texttt{Poly} communities: \emph{on the linear layer of
finite-support polynomial functors --- the finite free $\N$-modules --- the container derivative is
the only nontrivial representable first-order tangent structure}.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

We recall only what the argument uses; for the Cartesian differential and tangent structure on
$\SPoly$ we refer to the companion note \cite{MacBethTangent}.

\paragraph{Containers and the substitution monoidal category.}
A \emph{container in $n$ variables} is a set $S$ of \emph{shapes} together with, for each $s\in S$
and each $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$, a position set $P^i_s$; its extension is the polynomial functor
$\sem{S\lhd P}(\vec X)=\sum_{s\in S}\prod_{i=1}^n X_i^{P^i_s}$. We work with finitary containers, so
only the cardinalities $|P^i_s|$ matter up to isomorphism \cite{AAGM2003}. Composition of containers
is substitution of extensions, written $G\lhd F$, with unit the identity container $y$; this makes
$(\Poly,\lhd,y)$ a (strict, once we pass to isomorphism classes) monoidal category, and the
comonoids in it are exactly the small categories \cite{AhmanUustalu2016,GambinoKock2013}. A container
is \emph{linear} if it is of the form $S\cdot y$ for a set $S$, i.e.\ its extension is
$\vec X\mapsto S\times X$ in one variable; \emph{finite-support} if its shape set is finite. We
write $\SPoly$ for the finite-support polynomial functors.

\paragraph{The linear layer $\Lin(\SPoly)$ and the free $\N$-modules $\FreeMod_\N$.}
The classification lives not in $\SPoly$ at large but in its subcategory of \emph{linear} maps. Let
$\Lin(\SPoly)$ be the (wide) subcategory with the same objects $n\in\N$ but only the \emph{linear}
morphisms $n\to m$: those given by an $m\times n$ matrix of natural numbers (equivalently, tuples of
finite-support linear containers $S\cdot y$), with composition the matrix product. Write
$\FreeMod_\N$ for the category of \emph{all} free $\N$-modules and $\N$-linear maps; the functor
$n\mapsto\N^n$ is an equivalence of $\Lin(\SPoly)$ onto its full subcategory of \emph{finite-rank}
modules. Finite-support $\SPoly$ sees only finite ranks; the uniqueness theorem is proved in the
larger $\FreeMod_\N$, so that ``no exotic structure at infinite rank'' is a theorem and not a blind
spot of the finiteness restriction (\S\ref{sec:sharp}). What matters for the morphisms is this: the
augmentation $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ of a Weil algebra and the other structure maps below are
$\N$-matrices --- legitimate morphisms of $\FreeMod_\N$ --- even though, read naively as container
maps in $(\Poly,\lhd,y)$, $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ is not a container morphism. On $\Lin(\SPoly)$ the
Cartesian differential / tangent structure of $\SPoly$ \cite{MacBethTangent} restricts, and there it
is computed by base change (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), so a linear container $S\cdot y$ appears as a
\emph{morphism} ($1\times1$ matrix $[\,|S|\,]$) and $\lhd$ appears as \emph{composition}, not as a
tensor of objects.

\paragraph{Infinitesimal objects and representable tangent bundles.}
We use the representable presentation of tangent structure of \cite{LanfranchiLemay2025},
transported along the dictionary $(\Mod_R,\otimes_R,R)\rightsquigarrow(\Mod_\N,\otimes_\N,\N)$: the
\emph{same} module/algebra layer as Lanfranchi--Lemay, but over the rig $\N$ in place of a
commutative ring. The tangent functor is \emph{base change by a Weil algebra}, not substitution; the
link to $\Poly$ is that the finite-rank free $\N$-modules are exactly the objects of
$\Lin(\SPoly)$, on which base change coincides with $\lhd$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}).

\begin{definition}[Infinitesimal object / Weil algebra]\label{def:inf}
An \emph{infinitesimal object} is an augmented square-zero $\N$-algebra $D=y\oplus M$: a pointed
object with unit/zero $z\colon y\to D$ and augmentation $p\colon D\to y$, $p\circ z=\id_y$,
\emph{infinitesimal part} $M=\ker(p)$ a free $\N$-module with $M\cdot M=0$ inside $D$ (the
$\varepsilon^2=0$ condition). The associated tangent functor is \emph{base change}
$T:=(-)\otimes_\N D$, with tangent projection $A\otimes p\colon A\otimes D\to A$ and zero section
$A\otimes z\colon A\to A\otimes D$. The dual numbers $W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$ are the first
such object. We do \emph{not} rank $M$ finite here: the classification below holds at every rank.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Base change is not the substitution formula]\label{rem:basechange}
It is worth stating once, cleanly, what base change $T=(-)\otimes W$ is and is not. On the free module
$\N^n$ it is $\N^n\mapsto\N^n\otimes_\N W=\N^n\oplus\varepsilon\N^n$, doubling the rank; the ring map
$\N[y]\to W[y]$ underlying it sends $y\mapsto y$ and $y^2\mapsto y^2$. This is distinct from the
dual-number \emph{substitution} $y\mapsto y+\varepsilon v$, which sends $y^2\mapsto y^2+2\varepsilon
vy$: the substitution is the unit of the adjunction between $(-)\otimes W$ and the K\"ahler functor,
and it is how the companion note \cite{MacBethTangent} computes the combinator $\partial$ on $\SPoly$
\emph{maps} (where a polynomial is a morphism). The two agree on \emph{linear} arguments --- which is
all the tangent \emph{functor} on $\FreeMod_\N$ ever sees --- and a non-linear polynomial is neither
an object nor a morphism of $\FreeMod_\N$, so no confusion arises inside the module category.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Representable first-order tangent structure]\label{def:tangent}
A \emph{representable first-order tangent structure} is a tangent structure (in the sense of
Cockett--Cruttwell \cite{CockettCruttwell2014}, first order) whose bundle functor is base change
$T=(-)\otimes D$ by an infinitesimal object $D=y\oplus M$. Its infinitesimal part $M$ is called
\emph{solid} if the \emph{vertical-lift component} $\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ is an isomorphism.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why solidity is $\ell_M$, not $\mu$]\label{rem:solid}
The square-zero condition $M\cdot M=0$ makes the multiplication $\mu\colon M\otimes M\to M$ the
\emph{zero} map (e.g.\ $\mu(\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^2=0$), so $\mu$ is never an
isomorphism except in the degenerate case $M=0$; an earlier draft's ``$\mu$ an isomorphism'' was
therefore the wrong map. The map that is forced to be an isomorphism is the vertical-lift component
$\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ (for $W$, $\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$); universality
of the vertical lift forces exactly this (\S\ref{sec:converse}). Only the abstract isomorphism
$M\otimes M\cong M$ it provides is used below, so the rigidity computation is unchanged.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Convention: $\otimes$ versus $\lhd$, and why it matters]\label{rem:convention}
The tangent functor is base change $(-)\otimes D$, never substitution $(-)\lhd D$ on all of $\Poly$.
The distinction is real: full $\lhd$ has no $\varepsilon^2=0$ truncation (for $D=W$,
$y^2\lhd W=y^2\lhd 2y=(2y)^2=4y^2\neq 3y^2$, and $y^n\lhd W=W^n$ is multiplicative), so $(-)\lhd W$ is
\emph{not} the dual-number substitution and, as one checks, forces the trivial structure alone. What
saves the container reading is Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}: on the \emph{linear layer} $S\cdot y$ --- the
only layer the rank arithmetic of \S\ref{sec:rigidity}--\S\ref{sec:converse} ever touches --- base
change $\otimes$, substitution $\lhd$ and the Dirichlet tensor all agree. Every infinitesimal object
below ($D=y\oplus M$, the second-order object $D_2$, the vertical object $P'$) is a free module, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide} lets us compute it by whichever of the three is convenient. We do these
computations inside $\FreeMod_\N$, where $\lhd$ is matrix composition and the structure maps
(augmentation, zero, $\hat g$) are $\N$-matrices --- which is why the ``quick substitution formula''
is legitimate: the maps in sight are genuine morphisms of $\FreeMod_\N$, not container maps of
$(\Poly,\lhd,y)$. The tangent \emph{functor} itself remains base change $(-)\otimes D$.
\end{remark}

The dual numbers $W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2=y\oplus\langle\varepsilon v\rangle$ are such an
object: the augmentation is $\varepsilon\mapsto0$ ($p\colon W\to y$) and the zero is
$y\hookrightarrow W$ ($z$). Base change $(-)\otimes W$ on $\FreeMod_\N$ is the module-level tangent
functor; its restriction is the $\partial$-structure of \cite{MacBethTangent} on the linear layer
(Remark~\ref{rem:basechange}). Its infinitesimal part is the rank-$1$ free module
$M=\langle\varepsilon v\rangle$; the trivial structure is $D=y$, $M=0$, $T=\id$. That base change by
a Weil algebra is a genuine tangent structure --- not merely an endofunctor --- is standard
(Cockett--Cruttwell \cite{CockettCruttwell2014}; the general representable case,
\cite{LanfranchiLemay2025}); that its combinator on $\SPoly$ is the container derivative $\partial$
is \cite{MacBethTangent}. We take both as established and ask only whether other infinitesimal
objects give rise to further structures.

\paragraph{The universality of the vertical lift.}
Among the tangent-structure axioms of \cite{CockettCruttwell2014} we use one by name. The
\emph{second tangent bundle} is $T_2:=T\times_{\id}T$ (the pullback of $T\xrightarrow{p}\id$ along
itself), and the \emph{vertical bundle} $V$ is the pullback of the tangent map
$Tp\colon T^2\to T$ along the zero $0\colon\id\to T$. The \emph{vertical lift} is a canonical
comparison $T_2\to T^2$, and the axiom of \emph{universality of the vertical lift}
(\cite{CockettCruttwell2014}, part of the definition of a tangent structure) asserts that the
square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
T_2 & \longrightarrow & T^2\\[-2pt]
\downarrow & & \ \downarrow{\scriptstyle Tp}\\[-2pt]
\id & \xrightarrow{\ 0\ } & T
\end{array}
\]
is a pullback; equivalently, that the induced map $T_2\to V$ is an isomorphism. This is the single
structural axiom the converse of \S\ref{sec:converse} invokes.

\paragraph{The infinitesimal part is a free module.}
Inside $\FreeMod_\N$ there is nothing to force: the objects \emph{are} the finite free $\N$-modules,
so the infinitesimal part $M=\ker(p)$ of any infinitesimal object is a free $\N$-module, with a
well-defined rank (Lemma~\ref{lem:free}). This is what supplies freeness in \S\ref{sec:rigidity}, and
it replaces --- indeed trivialises --- Lanfranchi--Lemay's projective-to-free step, which over a ring
needs a PID.

\section{Three tensors coincide on the linear layer}\label{sec:coincide}

This is the linchpin of the whole note, and it carries two burdens at once. First, it reconciles the
two readings of the tangent functor: the \emph{bundle} is base change $\otimes W$, whereas the
container derivative is phrased with substitution $\lhd$, and these are genuinely different operations
on $\Poly$ (Remark~\ref{rem:convention}). Second, the \emph{solid} condition $M\otimes M\cong M$
lives in the ``module layer'' where rank multiplies, which looks like yet a third product. The
following observation collapses all three onto the linear layer.

\begin{lemma}[Three tensors coincide on the linear layer]\label{lem:coincide}
For linear modules, base change by a linear object, substitution, and the Dirichlet product all
agree:
\[
  (S\cdot y)\otimes(T\cdot y)\ =\ (S\cdot y)\lhd(T\cdot y)\ =\ (S\times T)\cdot y\ =\
  (S\cdot y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(T\cdot y).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The extension of $S\cdot y$ is $X\mapsto S\times X$. Composing,
$(S\cdot y)\lhd(T\cdot y)\colon X\mapsto S\times(T\times X)=(S\times T)\times X=(S\times T)\cdot y$.
The Dirichlet product of $\sum_{s}y^1$ and $\sum_t y^1$ is $\sum_{(s,t)}y^{1\times1}=(S\times
T)\cdot y$. Base change of the free module $S\cdot y$ by the free module $T\cdot y$ tensors the
generating sets, $(S\times T)\cdot y$. All three equal $(S\times T)\cdot y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The identity is special to the linear layer; off it the three products diverge. Substitution and
Dirichlet already part on two-term polynomials ($(y+1)\lhd(y+1)=y+2$ while
$(y+1)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(y+1)=y+3$), and base change parts from substitution precisely at the
$\varepsilon^2=0$ truncation: $y^2\lhd(2y)=4y^2$ whereas $y^2\otimes W$ reads off
$y^2+2\varepsilon vy$. So Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide} is genuinely a statement about the linear layer,
not a global identity --- which is exactly why the uniqueness theorem is a statement about that layer.
None of the three is the ``doubling'' Dirichlet action $(2y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Dir}}(-)$, which sends
$p\mapsto2p$ and has nothing to do with the derivative.
\end{remark}

\emph{Consequence.} On the linear layer the bundle's own base-change tensor $\otimes$ already
multiplies rank, and agrees with $\lhd$, so the solid condition $M\otimes M\cong M$ reads $|S|^2=|S|$
in the very tensor the bundle uses --- and the entire rank arithmetic of
\S\ref{sec:rigidity}--\S\ref{sec:converse}, taking place on this layer, may be read interchangeably
in any of the three. This single coincidence is what makes the port faithful and what licenses the
container-derivative reading of a result about Weil base change.

\section{Rank rigidity: the solid parts are $\{0,y\}$}\label{sec:rigidity}

We classify the finite solid infinitesimal parts. Two structural facts about linear containers,
then the rank computation.

\begin{lemma}[Freeness is automatic over $\N$]\label{lem:free}
Every linear container $S\cdot y$ is the free $\N$-module on the set $S$; two linear containers are
isomorphic iff their shape sets are equinumerous. The cardinal $|S|=:\rank(M)$ is a complete
isomorphism invariant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under coproduct of containers and the $\N$-action $n\cdot(S\cdot y)=(n\times S)\cdot y$, the linear
containers form the free-$\N$-module functor applied to sets: $S\cdot y$ is the $|S|$-fold copower of
$y$. A container isomorphism $S\cdot y\cong T\cdot y$ is, since morphisms between linear containers
are just functions on shapes (positions being singletons), a bijection $S\cong T$. Hence $\rank$ is
a complete invariant.
\end{proof}

This is the analogue of Lanfranchi--Lemay's ``projective $\Rightarrow$ free $\Rightarrow$ rank is a
complete invariant'' (their Lemma~4.16, via Rotman's structure theorem, which \emph{requires} the
base to be a PID). Here it is \emph{automatic}: the linear polynomial functors \emph{are} the free
$\N$-modules by definition, so no PID hypothesis is needed and the rigidity below holds over the
rig $\N$ --- strictly outside the Lanfranchi--Lemay hypotheses.

\begin{lemma}[Solid $\Rightarrow$ idempotent rank]\label{lem:solid}
If $M=S\cdot y$ is solid, i.e.\ the vertical-lift component $\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ is an
isomorphism, then $|S|^2=|S|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}, $M\otimes M=M\lhd M=(S\times S)\cdot y$, of rank $|S|^2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}.
An isomorphism $\ell_M\colon S\cdot y\cong(S\times S)\cdot y$ forces, by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}, a
bijection $S\cong S\times S$, i.e.\ $|S|=|S|^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite rigidity]\label{thm:rigidity}
A finite-support solid infinitesimal part has rank in $\{0,1\}$. Hence there are exactly two:
$M=0$ (rank $0$) and $M=y$ (rank $1$). By \S\ref{sec:prelim} these are the trivial structure and
the AAGM dual-number structure, with combinator $\partial$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a natural number $n=|S|$ we have $n^2=n\iff n(n-1)=0\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, since $\N$ has no zero
divisors. By Lemma~\ref{lem:solid} a finite solid part has $|S|^2=|S|$, so $|S|\in\{0,1\}$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:free} the rank determines $M$, giving $M\in\{0,y\}$.
\end{proof}

This is the Lanfranchi--Lemay rank computation $R^{n^2}\cong R^n\Rightarrow n^2=n\Rightarrow
n\in\{0,1\}$, ported verbatim with the cardinality rank, and --- per Lemma~\ref{lem:free} --- without
their PID hypothesis.

\paragraph{Machine certification of the arithmetic.}
The finite arithmetic of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is certified in a Mathlib-free Lean~4
development with no classical axioms. The oracle proves: $n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$ on $\N$ (in
general, \texttt{omega}-free); that the solid ranks in $0\le n\le12$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$ (by
\texttt{decide}); that these two ranks map through the structure assignment to exactly $\{\id,
\partial\}$, with the dual-number object of arity $2$; that every rank $r\ge2$ (the Whitney sums
of \S\ref{sec:converse}) is non-solid; and that the Cantor pairing is injective on a
$12\times12$ window, witnessing $|\N\times\N|=|\N|$ for \S\ref{sec:sharp}. \texttt{\#print axioms}
on the packaged oracle reports only \texttt{propext} (a structural, non-classical axiom); the
finite \texttt{decide} claims are individually axiom-free, with no \texttt{sorryAx},
\texttt{Quot.sound} or \texttt{Classical.choice}.

\section{Infinite rank: solidity without a tangent structure}\label{sec:sharp}

The rank count of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} --- and of the converse's step $1+2n=1+n+n^2\Rightarrow
n\in\{0,1\}$ (\S\ref{sec:converse}) --- used finiteness, because $\kappa^2=\kappa$ for every infinite
cardinal $\kappa$ (under choice). One might therefore expect the finite-rank hypothesis to be sharp,
with $\N\cdot y$ a third solid structure. It is not. The expectation conflates two distinct notions of
solidity, which we name once and keep apart. Call $M$ \emph{abstractly solid} if there merely
\emph{exists} an $\N$-module isomorphism $M\cong M\otimes M$ (equivalently the cardinality condition
$|S|^2=|S|$), and \emph{vertical-lift solid} if the structural vertical-lift component
$\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ of a tangent structure --- the map of Lemma~\ref{lem:solid} --- is an
isomorphism. At finite rank the two coincide, both landing in $\{0,1\}$. At infinite rank they part:
an infinite free module \emph{is} abstractly solid but, as we now show, is never vertical-lift solid,
because the tangent axioms force $\ell_M$ to be the diagonal. So $\N\cdot y$ passes the abstract test
and fails the one that matters. Uniqueness is rank-free.

\begin{proposition}[$\N\cdot y$ is abstractly solid]\label{prop:infinite}
The countable free module $M=\N\cdot y$ is \emph{abstractly} solid: the Cantor pairing supplies a
bijection $\N\times\N\cong\N$, hence an $\N$-module isomorphism $M\cong(\N\times\N)\cdot y=M\otimes M$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), so $M\otimes M\cong M$ with $\rank M=|\N|\notin\{0,1\}$. This isomorphism
is \emph{abstract}: it is some module iso, not the vertical-lift component of a tangent structure.
Abstract solidity therefore does not by itself bound the rank --- and, as Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}
shows, it does not supply a vertical lift either.
\end{proposition}

So the rank rigidity (Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}) is about \emph{finite} solid parts, and its
hypothesis is genuinely used. The point of this section is that this does \emph{not} leave a gap in
the uniqueness theorem, because the extra axioms of a tangent structure exclude $\N\cdot y$ outright.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-free exclusion of infinite parts]\label{thm:rankfree}
Let $T=(-)\otimes D$ be a representable first-order tangent structure with $D=y\oplus M$, $M$ a free
$\N$-module of \emph{any} rank. Then $\rank M\le1$. In particular $\N\cdot y$ carries no first-order
tangent structure, and the only infinitesimal objects underlying one are $y$ and $W$.
\end{theorem}

The one-sentence reason is that the isomorphism $\ell_M$ of a tangent structure is not merely abstract
but \emph{is} the vertical lift, and the flip axiom $c\ell=\ell$ together with lift-coassociativity
force $\ell_M$ to be the diagonal $M\to M\otimes M$ --- which, being an isomorphism onto $M\otimes M$,
exists only when $\rank M\le1$. We give the argument in full.

\begin{proof}
Because a natural transformation of base-change functors $(-)\otimes V\Rightarrow(-)\otimes V'$ is,
by evaluation at the unit $y$, the same datum as an $\N$-module map $V\to V'$, the vertical lift
$\ell\colon T\Rightarrow T^2$ and the canonical flip $c\colon T^2\Rightarrow T^2$ of the tangent
structure are
\[
  \ell_A=\id_A\otimes\delta,\qquad c_A=\id_A\otimes\gamma
\]
for fixed module maps $\delta\colon D\to D\otimes D$ and $\gamma\colon D\otimes D\to D\otimes D$.
Decompose $D\otimes D=y\oplus M_1\oplus M_2\oplus(M\otimes M)$ with $M_1=M\otimes y$, $M_2=y\otimes M$,
and $M\otimes M$ the mixed part of rank $(\rank M)^2$. Fix a basis $\{e_k\}_{k\in K}$ of $M$, so
$\rank M=|K|$. We argue in five steps; none uses finiteness.

\emph{(0) The lift lands in the mixed part: $\delta(M)\subseteq M\otimes M$.} Two of the
tangent-bundle axioms, $p_T\circ\ell=0\circ p$ and $T(p)\circ\ell=0\circ p$ (with $p\colon D\to y$ the
augmentation and $0$ the zero section), restrict on $M$ to $(p\otimes\id)\circ\delta=0$ and
$(\id\otimes p)\circ\delta=0$. Since $\ker(p\otimes\id)\cap\ker(\id\otimes p)=M\otimes M$ inside
$D\otimes D$, this gives $\delta(M)\subseteq M\otimes M$. Write $\ell_M:=\delta|_M\colon M\to M\otimes
M$ for the resulting \emph{vertical-lift component}.

\emph{(1) Universality makes $\ell_M$ an isomorphism.} By the universality of the vertical lift
(\S\ref{sec:prelim}) the comparison $v\colon T_2\to V$ is an isomorphism of functors. Evaluated at $y$
and written in coordinates, $v(a;u,w)=a+\sum_{k}u_k e_k+\sum_{k}w_k\,\ell_M(e_k)$; as a map of fibres
$y\oplus M\oplus M\to y\oplus M\oplus(M\otimes M)$ it is the identity on the summand $y\oplus M$ and
$\ell_M$ on the last summand. Hence $v$ is an isomorphism iff
$\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ is: universality \emph{produces} the solidity of
Definition~\ref{def:tangent}, rather than assuming it, and it refines the rank shadow $T_2\cong V$
(i.e.\ $1+2n=1+n+n^2$) of \S\ref{sec:converse} to an honest isomorphism of the lift map itself.

\emph{(2) Over $\N$ an isomorphism of free modules permutes bases.} A free $\N$-module is the free
commutative monoid on its basis, whose irreducible elements (those with $x=a+b\Rightarrow a=0$ or
$b=0$) are exactly the basis vectors --- no proper multiple $2e_k=e_k+e_k$ is irreducible. A monoid
isomorphism carries irreducibles to irreducibles bijectively, so $\ell_M$ sends each basis vector to a
basis vector of $M\otimes M$:
\[
  \ell_M(e_k)=e_{\beta_1(k)}\otimes e_{\beta_2(k)},\qquad
  \beta:=(\beta_1,\beta_2)\colon K\to K\times K\ \text{a bijection.}
\]
This holds at every cardinality --- which is why the abstract escape ``$\kappa=\kappa^2$ lets $\ell_M$
be an isomorphism at $\aleph_0$'' yields nothing structured.

\emph{(3) Flip-compatibility forces $\gamma=\id$ on the mixed part.} The axiom $c\circ\ell=\ell$ gives
$\gamma\circ\ell_M=\ell_M$; since $\ell_M$ is surjective onto $M\otimes M$ (step 1), this forces
$\gamma=\id$ on $M\otimes M$ --- \emph{whatever} the flip is. So for $\rank M\ge2$ the flip is
\emph{not} the swap but is forced to act as the identity on the mixed part; this is the only
configuration compatible with an invertible $\ell_M$, and there is no exotic non-representable flip to
escape through.

\emph{(4) Lift-coassociativity makes $\ell_M$ strictly coassociative.} The vertical-lift
coassociativity coherence of \cite{CockettCruttwell2014} reads $T(\ell)\circ\ell=(c\text{-twist})\circ
\ell_T\circ\ell$. Both composites land in the fully mixed part $M\otimes M\otimes M$ (each application
of $\delta$ maps $M$ into $M\otimes M$, step 0). The $c$-twist is a composite of flips on adjacent
tensor factors; on $M\otimes M\otimes M$ each acts through $\gamma|_{M\otimes M}=\id$ (step 3), so the
$c$-twist term is idle --- it acts as the identity there --- and the coherence collapses to
\[
  (\ell_M\otimes\id)\circ\ell_M=(\id\otimes\ell_M)\circ\ell_M,
\]
strict coassociativity of $\ell_M$. The conclusion is robust to the exact form of the twist: whatever
composite of flips the axiom places there, each is the identity on the mixed part by step 3.

\emph{(5) A coassociative basis-bijection is the diagonal.} Substituting
$\ell_M(e_k)=e_{\beta_1(k)}\otimes e_{\beta_2(k)}$ into strict coassociativity and comparing basis
triples gives
\[
  \beta_1\circ\beta_1=\beta_1,\qquad \beta_2\circ\beta_2=\beta_2,\qquad
  \beta_2\circ\beta_1=\beta_1\circ\beta_2.
\]
Since $\beta$ is a bijection onto $K\times K$ its first coordinates exhaust $K$, so $\beta_1$ is
surjective; a surjective idempotent on a set is the identity, whence $\beta_1=\id$, and likewise
$\beta_2=\id$. Then $\beta(k)=(k,k)$ is the diagonal, surjective onto $K\times K$ only when $|K|\le1$.
Hence $\rank M=|K|\le1$. The surviving cases are $\rank M=0$ ($M=0$, $T=\id$) and $\rank M=1$ ($M=y$,
$T=\partial$), where the diagonal is all of $K\times K$.\footnote{At finite rank $\ge2$ there is in
any case no bijection $K\cong K\times K$ at all --- $|K|<|K|^2$ --- so the diagonal cannot be
surjective and the obstruction is already immediate from cardinality, with nothing to search over. The
work of steps (4)--(5) is to make the bound hold \emph{uniformly}, in particular at infinite rank,
where $\kappa=\kappa^2$ and cardinality alone decides nothing. The finite-rank arithmetic
$1+2n=1+n+n^2\Rightarrow n\in\{0,1\}$ is Lean-certified (\S\ref{sec:rigidity}).} No step used
finiteness, so the bound holds at every cardinality.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[An independent route in the representable case]\label{rem:repflip}
When the flip is $\lhd$-monoidal --- $\gamma$ an algebra automorphism of $D\otimes D$ --- fixing $y$
and exchanging $M_1\leftrightarrow M_2$ forces
$\gamma(e_i\otimes e_j)=e_{\psi(j)}\otimes e_{\varphi(i)}$, a swap-type map that equals the identity
on $M\otimes M$ only when $\rank M\le1$. Combined with step (3) this already gives $\rank M\le1$
\emph{without} invoking lift-coassociativity. So in the representable setting the exclusion closes by
two independent routes; lift-coassociativity (steps 4--5) is what additionally rules out the exotic
non-monoidal flip.
\end{remark}

The exclusion is thus a theorem about the vertical-lift \emph{data}, not about the abstract
isomorphism type of $M$.

\begin{remark}[Solidity versus structure, and the contrast with Lanfranchi--Lemay]\label{rem:part-not-structure}
The correspondence $(\star)$ of \S\ref{sec:converse} --- tangent structure $\iff$ solid part --- is
therefore a bijection \emph{only} at finite rank, where both sides land in $\{0,1\}$. At infinite
rank the forward direction fails: $\N\cdot y$ is abstractly solid but is not a structure
(Proposition~\ref{prop:infinite}, Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}). This is where our setting diverges
from Lanfranchi--Lemay. Over a ring their extra solid parts at non-PID / non-finitely-generated bases
(their Examples~4.20--4.21) are genuine, so they must impose finite generation (Niefield
coexponentiability) to recover uniqueness. Over $\N$ the extra solid part $\N\cdot y$ is a
\emph{phantom}: it is abstractly solid, but the flip and coassociativity axioms refuse it a tangent
structure, so uniqueness needs no finiteness hypothesis at all. An earlier draft of this note read
$\N\cdot y$ as a genuine third solid structure and declared ``finiteness is sharp''; that reading
mistook abstract solidity for vertical-lift solidity, and is withdrawn.
\end{remark}

\section{The solidity converse: universality of the vertical lift}\label{sec:converse}

Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} classifies the solid infinitesimal \emph{parts}. To pass from parts to
tangent \emph{structures} we need the converse direction of the correspondence
\begin{quote}
$(\star)$\quad representable first-order tangent structure $T=(-)\otimes D$ $\iff$ solid infinitesimal
part $M=\ker(D\to y)$.
\end{quote}
The forward direction is immediate once the parts are known (\S\ref{sec:uniqueness}). The converse
--- that every representable first-order tangent structure has a \emph{solid} infinitesimal part ---
is Lanfranchi--Lemay's Theorem~4.8, proved over a commutative ring, and is the step that did not
transfer verbatim: their derivation computes a vertical bundle by a short exact sequence, hence by
subtraction, which the rig $\N$ lacks. We now give a rig-internal proof. The engine is the
universality of the vertical lift (\S\ref{sec:prelim}); the one new idea is that the vertical bundle
is a \emph{direct sum} of its surviving summands, so its rank is computed by addition and never by a
difference --- rank is additive over direct sums of free $\N$-modules, and $\N$ has no subtraction.

The entire argument lives within the finite-rank free modules $\Lin(\SPoly)$, where by Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}
the tangent functor $T=(-)\otimes D$ \emph{coincides} with substitution $(-)\lhd D$ --- base change
of a free module by a free module is substitution. We therefore compute the objects below with the
quick substitution formula $\lhd$, which preserves pullbacks pointwise (Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback}).
This is legitimate because all the maps in sight are morphisms of $\FreeMod_\N$ --- the augmentation
$\hat p$ and zero $\hat z$ are $\N$-matrices, and $\lhd$ between free modules is their matrix
composition --- so the pullbacks and the rank counts are taken in $\FreeMod_\N$, not in
$(\Poly,\lhd,y)$ (where $\hat p$, sending $\varepsilon\mapsto0$, is not a container morphism at all).
Here $D=y\oplus M$, $M=S\cdot y$ a free module of finite rank $n=|S|$ (the finiteness used in this
section; the infinite case is handled by Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}).

\subsection{Substitution preserves pullbacks on both sides}

\begin{lemma}[$\lhd$ preserves pullbacks]\label{lem:pullback}
For every $D\in\Poly$ the functor $(-)\lhd D$ preserves pullbacks; and for every polynomial $A$ the
functor $A\lhd(-)$ preserves pullbacks.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Pullbacks in $\Poly$ are computed pointwise. For $(-)\lhd D$: if $F=G\times_H K$ pointwise then for
all $X$, $(F\lhd D)(X)=F(D(X))=G(D(X))\times_{H(D(X))}K(D(X))=(G\lhd D\times_{H\lhd D}K\lhd D)(X)$,
so $F\lhd D=G\lhd D\times_{H\lhd D}K\lhd D$. For $A\lhd(-)$: a polynomial functor $A$ preserves
connected limits (\cite{GambinoKock2013}, Prop.~1.9), and a pullback is a connected limit, so $A$
preserves the pointwise pullback $E_1(X)\times_{E_0(X)}E_2(X)$; hence $A\lhd(-)$ preserves
pullbacks.
\end{proof}

\emph{Consequence: the tangent data are all right-tensorings.} The projection, zero, tangent
map and the two bundles of $T=(-)\otimes D$ are $(-)\otimes(\text{structure map on }D)$ --- which on
the linear layer is $(-)\lhd(\text{structure map})$:
$p=(-)\lhd\hat p$ with $\hat p\colon D\to y$ the augmentation and $z=(-)\lhd\hat z$ with $\hat
z\colon y\to D$ the zero; and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback} applied so that $A\lhd(-)$ carries the
relevant pointwise pullback,
\[
  T_2=(-)\otimes D_2,\quad D_2:=D\times_y D;\qquad
  V=(-)\otimes P',\quad P':=\hat g^{-1}(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z),\ \ \hat g:=\hat p\lhd D\colon D\lhd D\to D.
\]
All of $D$, $D_2$, $D\lhd D$, $P'$ are linear objects, and $y$ is one of them.

\subsection{The three coproduct counts}

We work in the based setting: $\varepsilon$-grading modulo $\varepsilon^2$ within each copy of $D$,
with the mixed term surviving across copies --- the Weil-algebra model
$\N[\varepsilon_1,\dots]/(\text{within-copy squares})$ that the base-change presentation of $T$
\cite{MacBethTangent} delivers. This is the content of ``$T=(-)\otimes D$ is base change,'' and it is
what makes $D_2$ (fibre product, no mixed term) differ from the composite $D\lhd D$ (mixed term
present): the $\varepsilon^2=0$ truncation lives in $D$, not in the composition. Each object below is
a direct sum of free summands, so its rank is the \emph{sum} of the summand ranks (rank is additive
over $\oplus$, and $\N$ has no subtraction to undo the sum).

\begin{lemma}[Coproduct ranks]\label{lem:counts}
With $n=\rank M$ and $\rank(M\lhd M)=n^2$ (Lemmas~\ref{lem:coincide},~\ref{lem:free}):
\[
\begin{aligned}
D\lhd D &= y\oplus M\oplus M\oplus(M\lhd M), &\rank &= 1+n+n+n^2=(1+n)^2;\\
D_2=D\times_y D &= y\oplus M\oplus M, &\rank &= 1+2n;\\
P' &= y\oplus \varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\oplus(M\lhd M), &\rank &= 1+n+n^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{($D\lhd D$.)} Since $M$ is linear, $M(X)=S\times X$, so
$(D\lhd D)(X)=D(D(X))=X\oplus M(X)\oplus M(X)\oplus M(M(X))$: the base $y$, the inner copy's $M$
($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$), the outer copy's $M$ ($\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}$), and the mixed
$M\lhd M=(S\times S)\cdot y$. Ranks $1,n,n,n^2$.

\emph{($D_2$.)} A fibre product over the base shares $y$ and adjoins the two infinitesimal
directions but creates \emph{no} product of the two infinitesimals: $D_2=y\oplus M\oplus M$, rank
$1+2n$.

\emph{($P'$.)} The map $\hat g=\hat p\lhd D$ applies the augmentation $\hat p$ ($\varepsilon\mapsto0$)
to the \emph{outer} copy: it kills $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}$ and the mixed term $M\lhd M$ (both
carry an outer-$M$ factor) and fixes $y$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$. The zero $\hat z$ has
image the $y$-summand. Hence $P'=\hat g^{-1}(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z)$ is the sub-coproduct on which the
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$-component vanishes:
$P'=y\oplus\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}\oplus(M\lhd M)$, rank $1+n+n^2$. Because $\hat g$ and $\hat z$
respect the direct-sum decomposition --- $\hat g$ acts summand-wise and $\mathrm{im}\,\hat z$ is the
$y$-summand --- this pullback \emph{is} the direct sum of the surviving summands, so its rank is the
sum $1+n+n^2$ --- not $\rank(D\lhd D)-\rank(\mathrm{im}\,\hat z)$. This is the step where
Lanfranchi--Lemay form a difference of ranks (a short exact sequence over a ring), and where we do
not.
\end{proof}

Both $D_2=(1+2n)\cdot y$ and $P'=(1+n+n^2)\cdot y$ are linear (a coproduct $y\oplus(\text{free})$ is
again linear), so Lemma~\ref{lem:free} applies to them.

\subsection{Universality forces $n^2=n$}

\begin{theorem}[Solidity converse]\label{thm:converse}
Let $T=(-)\otimes D$ be a representable first-order tangent structure on finite-rank $\N$-modules,
with $D=y\oplus M$ and $n=\rank M$ finite. Then $n^2=n$, hence $n\in\{0,1\}$; i.e.\ $M$
is solid and $M\in\{0,y\}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the universality of the vertical lift (\S\ref{sec:prelim}) --- the same input as step~(1) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree} --- the comparison $T_2\to V$ is an isomorphism of endofunctors, naturally
in the argument. By Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback} and its
consequence, $T_2=(-)\otimes D_2$ and $V=(-)\otimes P'$. Evaluate the functor isomorphism at the
unit object $y$ and use $y\otimes E=E$:
\[
  D_2\ \cong\ P'.
\]
Both are linear (Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:free} their shape-counts agree:
\[
  1+2n\ =\ 1+n+n^2.
\]
Now use only that $\N$ is a cancellative rig with no zero divisors --- \emph{no subtraction, no
additive inverses}. Additive left-cancellation of the common summand $1+n$ gives $n=n^2$. For
$n\ge1$, multiplicative cancellation of $n$ in $n\cdot n=n\cdot1$ gives $n=1$; and $n=0$ satisfies
$0^2=0$. Hence $n\in\{0,1\}$, so $M\in\{0,y\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:free}. In each surviving case the
vertical-lift component $\ell_M\colon M\to M\otimes M$ is an isomorphism, as one exhibits directly.
For $M=0$ the mixed part $M\otimes M=0$ and $\ell_M=0\colon 0\to 0$ is the identity. For $M=y$, take
the single basis vector $\varepsilon$ of $M=\langle\varepsilon v\rangle$; then $M\otimes M$ is free of
rank $1$ on $\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon$ (Lemmas~\ref{lem:coincide},~\ref{lem:free}), and the
vertical lift is $\ell_M\colon\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon$ (the
$\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$ of $W\otimes W$), a bijection of the two one-element
bases, hence an isomorphism. So both surviving parts are genuinely solid, as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why this is rig-internal]
The subtraction that blocked a literal port lived in computing the vertical-bundle fibre $P'$ by
exactness. In $\Poly$ that fibre is a direct sum of the surviving summands
(Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}), so its rank is a \emph{sum}. Everything downstream is
additive/multiplicative cancellation in $\N$. The derivation never forms a difference of ranks,
never inverts an element, and never constructs the solidity isomorphism explicitly --- solidity is
\emph{output}, read off from $n\in\{0,1\}$. Lanfranchi--Lemay's Theorem~4.8 instead builds it and
argues over a ring; the content we have replaced is precisely their one subtraction.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The non-solid finite candidates are auxiliary bundles]\label{rem:whitney}
The finite candidate objects excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} are the higher square-zero
objects $D_r=y+\varepsilon_1 v_1+\cdots+\varepsilon_r v_r$ ($\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j=0$, $r\ge2$),
with $M=\N^r$, $M\lhd M=\N^{r^2}$, rank $r^2\ne r$: not solid. These $D_r$ are precisely the Whitney
sums $T_r=T\times_X\cdots\times_X T$, which occur in the tangent axioms as \emph{auxiliary} bundles,
not as standalone tangent structures. So the objects the converse rejects are exactly the ones that
are not primitive structures --- solidity coincides with indecomposability, as it should. (The
non-solidity of every $r\ge2$ is part of the certified Lean oracle below.)
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The universality shadow alone needs finiteness]\label{rem:shadow}
The rank equation $1+2n=1+n+n^2$ is satisfied by every infinite cardinal $\kappa$
($1+2\kappa=\kappa=1+\kappa+\kappa^2$), so this \emph{rank shadow} of universality does not by itself
exclude $M=\N\cdot y$ --- which is exactly why Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} is stated for finite rank.
The full vertical-lift data impose more than the rank shadow (the flip and coassociativity axioms),
and \emph{that} excludes $\N\cdot y$ at every cardinality (Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}). So the
classification is rank-free even though this cleaner universality argument, which only sees ranks, is
not; the two agree on the finite range, where both give $\{0,1\}$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Machine certification of the arithmetic.}
The rig-internal arithmetic of Theorem~\ref{thm:converse} is certified in the same Mathlib-free,
axiom-light Lean~4 style. The oracle proves the three coproduct-rank identities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:counts} --- $\rank(D\lhd D)=(1+n)^2$, $\rank(D\lhd D)=\rank P'+n$,
$\rank(D\lhd D)=\rank D_2+n^2$ --- which are the honesty certificate for ``ranks add, never
subtract''; the universality collapse $\rank D_2=\rank P'\iff n\in\{0,1\}$, proved by
\texttt{Nat.add\_left\_cancel} (literal additive cancellation, \emph{no} \texttt{omega}) reducing to
$n^2=n\iff n\in\{0,1\}$; the finite enumeration (survivors in $0\le n\le12$ are exactly $\{0,1\}$);
and the two witnesses $n=1$ (universality holds, $\rank D_2=\rank P'=3$, the Weil rank of
$\N[\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2]/(\varepsilon_1^2,\varepsilon_2^2,\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2)$) and
$n=2$ (excluded, $5\ne7$). \texttt{\#print axioms} on the packaged oracle reports only
\texttt{propext}. The Lean certifies the \emph{arithmetic collapse} and the additivity of the
counts; it does \emph{not} formalise the structural inputs --- the universality axiom
(\cite{CockettCruttwell2014}), that $\lhd$ preserves pullbacks (Lemma~\ref{lem:pullback}, via
\cite{GambinoKock2013}), or that the infinitesimal part is a free module (automatic in
$\FreeMod_\N$) --- which are cited framework facts, and we are explicit that they are the
mathematical, not the decidable, content.

\section{The uniqueness theorem}\label{sec:uniqueness}

With both halves in hand the correspondence $(\star)$ is an equivalence, and the uniqueness theorem
assembles.

\paragraph{Forward: solid $\Rightarrow$ structure.}
A finite solid part yields a tangent structure in the two surviving cases. $M=0$ gives $T=\id$;
$M=y$ gives the AAGM dual-number structure, which \emph{is} a tangent structure --- the full
Cartesian differential / tangent verification is the content of \cite{MacBethTangent}. Since
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} leaves no third finite solid part, the forward direction never has to
manufacture a third structure.

\begin{theorem}[Uniqueness]\label{thm:uniqueness}
Among representable first-order tangent structures $T=(-)\otimes D$ on $\FreeMod_\N$ (base change by
an augmented square-zero $\N$-algebra $D=y\oplus M$, with $M$ a free $\N$-module of \emph{any} rank)
there are exactly two: the trivial structure ($D=y$, $T=\id$) and the AAGM dual-number structure
($D=W=\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^2$, combinator $\partial$). Restricted to the finite-rank part
$\Lin(\SPoly)$ --- the linear layer of $\SPoly$, where $\otimes D$ and $\lhd D$ coincide
(Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}) --- this says the container derivative is the only nontrivial such
structure.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=(-)\otimes D$ be such a structure, $D=y\oplus M$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}, $\rank M\le1$;
by Lemma~\ref{lem:free} the rank determines $M$, so $M\in\{0,y\}$ and $D\in\{y,\ W\}$ up to
isomorphism. (When $M$ is finite to begin with, the same conclusion follows from the note's own
rig-internal converse, Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}, without appeal to the flip argument of
\S\ref{sec:sharp}.) By the
forward direction both are realised, by $\id$ and $\partial$ respectively. Hence exactly two.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Uniqueness of the full structure, not just the object]\label{rem:fulldata}
Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness} counts infinitesimal \emph{objects}, but the classification is in fact
of the full first-order tangent structure $(T,p,0,+,\ell,c)$. Over $\N$ the remaining data are forced
once the object is fixed. For $D=y$ only $T=\id$ is possible. For $D=W$ the additive bundle $+$ is the
unique commutative-monoid structure on the rank-$1$ fibre, the projection and zero are the
augmentation and unit of $W$, and the vertical lift and flip are pinned by
$\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon_1\varepsilon_2$ and $c\ell=\ell$ (the same forcing, run at rank $1$,
that excludes higher ranks in Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}). So each of the two
infinitesimal objects carries a \emph{unique} first-order tangent structure, and ``exactly two'' is a
statement about structures, not merely objects.
\end{remark}

This is Theorem~\ref{thm:main-intro}, now unconditional and rank-free: the conditional hypothesis of
an earlier draft --- that every representable first-order tangent structure has a solid infinitesimal
part --- is discharged by Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}, and the finite-rank hypothesis is removed by
Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}.

The theorem is a clean deliverable for two audiences. For the tangent-category community
\cite{CockettCruttwell2014,LanfranchiLemay2025} it exhibits a rig-based model in which the
representable-tangent rigidity \emph{and} its vertical-lift converse hold for strictly weaker
reasons than over a PID --- no principal ideals, no subtraction --- isolating exactly which uses of
ring structure in \cite{LanfranchiLemay2025} were essential (none, for finite-dimensional
infinitesimal parts) and which were incidental (the PID and the short exact sequence, both
replaced). For the \texttt{Poly} and containers community \cite{AAGM2003,AhmanUustalu2016} it
upgrades ``the container derivative is \emph{a} tangent structure'' \cite{MacBethTangent} to ``it is
the \emph{only} nontrivial one among representable Weil-base-change tangent structures,'' a uniqueness
theorem for a construction that is otherwise defined by a formula.

\section{Conclusion: precedent and sequel}\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proved that among the representable first-order tangent structures given by Weil base change
$T=(-)\otimes D$ on $\FreeMod_\N$ --- for a free infinitesimal part of any rank ---
there are exactly two, the trivial one and the container derivative $\partial$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:uniqueness}); and because base change coincides with container substitution on the
linear layer (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), this is at the same time the statement that $\partial$ is
the unique nontrivial representable first-order tangent structure on that layer of finite-support
polynomial functors. The proof ports Lanfranchi--Lemay's affine-scheme classification in both halves,
and in both the rig $\N$ makes the port \emph{cleaner}: freeness is automatic, so the rigidity needs
no PID (Lemma~\ref{lem:free}); and the vertical bundle is a direct sum of free summands, so the
converse needs no subtraction (Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}, Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}). Two technical observations carry
the port: on the linear layer the base-change tensor $\otimes D$, the substitution tensor $\lhd$ and
the Dirichlet tensor all coincide (Lemma~\ref{lem:coincide}), and free $\N$-modules are closed under
direct sum with rank additive over it, so ranks add and never subtract. The arithmetic of the finite case is Lean-certified with no classical axioms. Finiteness
turns out \emph{not} to be load-bearing: although the countable part $\N\cdot y$ is abstractly solid
(an abstract isomorphism $M\otimes M\cong M$ exists, by the Cantor pairing), abstract solidity is strictly weaker
than underlying a tangent structure at infinite rank --- the flip and lift-coassociativity axioms
force the vertical lift to be the diagonal, excluding every rank $\ge2$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:rankfree}). So $\N\cdot y$ is a phantom, not a third structure,
and the uniqueness holds at every rank; the earlier draft's ``finiteness is sharp'' is withdrawn
(Remark~\ref{rem:part-not-structure}).

\paragraph{Precedent.}
The hope that polynomial functors carry a differential structure is Cockett's. His unpublished
FMCS~2012 talk \emph{Can you Differentiate a Polynomial?} \cite{Cockett2012} conjectured that they
form a Cartesian differential, hence a tangent, category --- the thesis the companion note
\cite{MacBethTangent} later proved. The 2012 slides, though, are explicit that this was conjecture:
the differentiation there is defined only on ``separable'' polynomials, closure under composition is
asserted rather than proved, and the chain rule is named as the hard open point. (The slides even
record, as an aside, that this is ``a differential category in which negation is unnatural'' --- the
same absence of additive inverses that we have just turned to advantage, since it is exactly what
makes the vertical-bundle coproduct, rather than a short exact sequence, the right tool.) No
uniqueness statement appears in 2012. The present note supplies it, and so answers Cockett's
question in its strongest form: not only \emph{can} you differentiate a polynomial, but --- among
representable Weil-base-change tangent structures --- differentiation is the \emph{only} nontrivial
way to do so. We flag one caveat, due to Cruttwell via
\cite{MO341933}: combinatorial species --- the setting closest to Cockett's --- are most naturally a
tangent \emph{bicategory}, with species as $1$-morphisms, which is structurally different from the
$\SPoly$ one-category treated here; our uniqueness is a statement about the latter.

\paragraph{Sequel.}
A rigid tangent structure is the entry condition for the Cartan-calculus / Lie-algebroid program of
Aintablian and Blohmann \cite{AintablianBlohmann2026}, which asks when a tangent category supports a
scalar ``ring object'' $R$ making each $TX$ an $R$-module compatibly with the tangent axioms, and
thereby upgrades a tangent structure to a Lie-algebroid structure with a full Cartan calculus. That
the linear layer of $\SPoly$ now has a \emph{unique} nontrivial representable first-order tangent
structure makes the question well-posed: is there such an $R$ for $(\SPoly,\partial)$? The negation remark above is a warning
sign --- a scalar ring object requires additive inverses, which $\N$ does not have --- and
determining exactly how much of the Cartan calculus survives over a rig is the natural next problem.
We leave it, together with higher Weil algebras $\N[\varepsilon]/\varepsilon^k$ ($k\ge3$) and
iterated tangents (first-order out of scope here, as for \cite{AAGM2003,LanfranchiLemay2025,CCGZ2024}),
to future work.
