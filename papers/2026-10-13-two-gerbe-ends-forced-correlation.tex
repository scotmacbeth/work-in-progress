\begin{abstract}
For a structure group $K$ of nilpotency class $\le 2$, the nonabelian inventory gerbe on the
torus $T^2=\B\Z^2$ carries two obstruction classes sharing the hinge $\Inn K$: the
\emph{strictification} class $\delta(\rho)=[A,B]\in H^2(T^2;\Inn K)$ and the \emph{central}
class $[\gerbe]\in H^2(T^2;Z(K))$. We prove they are \emph{not} independent. They satisfy a
one-way implication
\[
\delta\text{ couples on }T^2\ \Longrightarrow\ [\gerbe]\text{ couples on }T^2.
\]
For nilpotent $K$ the central class couples exactly when $K$ is nonabelian, so the implication
amounts to `$\delta$ couples $\Rightarrow K$ nonabelian'; the converse fails ($K=D_4$). The
substantive question is which nonabelian $K$ $\delta$-couple. So the corner
$(\delta\neq0,\ [\gerbe]=0)$ is \emph{forced empty}. The cause is a structural
asymmetry between two abelian-kernel extensions: the central extension
$1\to Z(K)\to K\to\Inn K\to1$ is non-split for every nonabelian class-$2$ $K$ and carries the
\emph{trivial} $\Inn K$-action, so $[\gerbe]$ universally couples, while the strictification
extension $\Aut K\to\Out K$ \emph{can} split, so $\delta$ is selective. The implication is
\emph{strict}: the converse fails at $K=D_4$ ($[\gerbe]\neq0$ while $\delta\equiv0$), the
dihedral $4\mid n$ witness at $n=4$. This is the $T^2$-analogue of the $S^1$-floor
$D1\Rightarrow D2$ dichotomy. A battery cross-tabulation confirms the empty corner.
\end{abstract}

\section{Verdict}\label{sec:verdict}

Over the base $T^2=\B\Z^2$ we decide, for the two Giraud obstruction stages of the inventory
gerbe, between
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(A)} \emph{independence} --- both off-diagonal corners $(\delta\neq0,[\gerbe]=0)$
and $(\delta=0,[\gerbe]\neq0)$ realized; and
\item \textbf{(B)} \emph{forced correlation} --- some implication always holds.
\end{itemize}
The answer is \textbf{(B)}. For every finite group $K$ of nilpotency class $\le2$ (the domain on
which both obstructions take the clean abelian-$H^2$ form; the implication extends to all finite
nilpotent $K$, Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}),
\[
\boxed{\ \delta\text{ couples on }T^2\ \Longrightarrow\ [\gerbe]\text{ couples on }T^2\ }
\]
i.e. the corner $(\delta\neq0,\ [\gerbe]=0)$ is \emph{forced empty}. The converse corner
$(\delta=0,\ [\gerbe]\neq0)$ \emph{is} occupied (e.g.\ $K=D_4$, the dihedral $4\mid n$ witness
at $n=4$), so the correlation is \emph{strict and one-directional}. There is no logical
independence.

The group-theoretic cause is a structural asymmetry between the two extensions that the
symmetric picture --- ``both are commutator obstructions of an abelian-kernel extension through
the hinge $\Inn K$'' --- hides:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{central} extension $1\to Z(K)\to K\to\Inn K\to1$ is \emph{non-split for every
nonabelian} class-$2$ $K$, and its coefficient $Z(K)$ carries the \emph{trivial}
$\Inn K$-action, so $[\gerbe]$ is \emph{universally obstructed} --- every nonabelian class-$2$
group couples at the central end;
\item the \emph{strictification} extension
$1\to\Inn K\to\Aut K\to\Out K\to1$ \emph{can split} (it does for
$D_4,Q_8,\Heis(\F_3),M_{16},C_4\rtimes C_4$), killing $\delta$ \emph{selectively}.
\end{itemize}
Strictification coupling is therefore a strictly stronger non-commutativity condition than
central coupling, and it cannot occur without the weaker one.

\paragraph{What is really being said (a temperance note).} For nilpotent $K$ the central end is
elementary: $[\gerbe]$ \emph{couples} $\iff K$ \emph{nonabelian} (Lemma~\ref{lem:central} and
Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}). So the headline implication reduces to
``$\delta$ couples $\Rightarrow K$ nonabelian'' (Lemma~\ref{lem:strict}, elementary). In other
words the central end merely \emph{detects nonabelianness}; it carries no information beyond
that. The genuine mathematical content --- and the hard, unresolved part --- is not the
correlation itself but \emph{which} nonabelian groups $\delta$-couple at all. That is an
$\Aut$-structure question (does $(\Sigma)$ admit a homomorphic section?), and the cross-tab shows
it is delicate: most nonabelian groups do \emph{not} $\delta$-couple. We therefore present (B)
not as a deep coupling law but as the observation that one end is a coarse nonabelianness
detector dominated by the other.

\section{Setup: the two ends as one lemma, applied twice}\label{sec:setup}

Let $K$ be finite of nilpotency class $\le 2$, so $[K,K]\subseteq Z(K)$ and
$M:=\Inn K=K/Z(K)$ is \emph{abelian}. Two abelian-kernel group extensions share the hinge
$\Inn K$:
\[
(\Sigma)\quad 1\to\Inn K\xrightarrow{\ }\Aut K\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\Out K\to1,
\qquad
(Z)\quad 1\to Z(K)\xrightarrow{\ }K\xrightarrow{\ \Ad\ }\Inn K\to1 .
\]
Over the base $T^2=\B\Z^2$ (nerve of $\C=\B\Z^2$), the proved $\delta$-commutator lemma
(\texttt{coupled-T2-general-delta-lemma}) computes the degree-$2$ connecting/lifting
obstruction of \emph{any} such extension $1\to A\to E\to Q\to1$ ($A$ abelian) for a band
$b:\Z^2\to Q$: with lifts $x,y\in E$ of $b(s),b(t)$,
\[
\mathrm{obs}(b)=\big[\,[x,y]\,\big]\in H^2(T^2;A)=A\big/\big((\sigma-1)A+(\tau-1)A\big),\qquad
[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\in\ker(E\to Q)=A,
\]
independent of lifts, where $\sigma,\tau$ are the $b$-twisted actions of $s,t$ on $A$. We
instantiate it on both extensions.

\paragraph{Strictification end $(\Sigma)$.} Band $\rho:\Z^2\to\Out K$, lifts
$A,B\in\Aut K$:
\[
\delta(\rho)=\big[[A,B]\big]\in H^2(T^2;\Inn K).
\]

\paragraph{Central end $(Z)$.} Here the coefficient is $A=Z(K)$ and the action is
\emph{trivial}: for $z\in Z(K)$ and any lift $k\in K$ of $\bar k\in\Inn K$,
$\;\bar k\cdot z=kzk^{-1}=z$ (central). Hence $(\sigma-1)Z(K)+(\tau-1)Z(K)=0$ and
\[
H^2(T^2;Z(K))=Z(K)\quad(\text{trivial coefficients, no quotient}).
\]
An \emph{inner band} $\beta:\Z^2\to\Inn K$ (automatically valid: $\Inn K$ abelian $\Rightarrow$
any pair of images commutes) with lifts $k,k'\in K$ gives
\[
[\gerbe]=\gamma(\beta)=\big[[k,k']\big]=[k,k']\in Z(K).
\]
This is exactly the registry's Face~2 central-extension lifting class
(\texttt{lift-cocycle-derivation}, proved), now read through the same lemma.

\begin{remark}[What ``independence'' means here]
$\delta$ and $[\gerbe]$ live in \emph{different} coefficient groups ($\Inn K$ vs $Z(K)$) and
arise from \emph{different} band targets ($\Out K$ vs $\Inn K$); for a single physical gerbe
the central stage is only reached \emph{after} strictification succeeds, so within one gerbe the
pattern $(\delta\neq0,\,[\gerbe]\text{ defined})$ cannot co-occur \emph{by construction} and the
question would be empty. The theorem is therefore a statement about the \emph{group $K$} --- does
$K$ admit a coupling band at each end, independently? --- and \emph{not} about any single gerbe.
This is the four-corner cross-tabulation, and it is what we decide.
\end{remark}

\section{Two lemmas}\label{sec:lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[central end is universally obstructed]\label{lem:central}
For $K$ finite of class $\le2$: $\ [\gerbe]\neq0$ for some inner band $\iff K$ is nonabelian.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) $K$ nonabelian $\Rightarrow$ $\exists\,k,k'\in K$ with $[k,k']\neq1$. By class
$\le2$, $[k,k']\in[K,K]\subseteq Z(K)$, so $[k,k']\in Z(K)\setminus\{1\}$. Their images
$\bar k,\bar k'\in\Inn K$ commute (abelian), so $\beta:s\mapsto\bar k,\ t\mapsto\bar k'$ is a
band, and by \S\ref{sec:setup} $\gamma(\beta)=[k,k']\neq0$ in $H^2(T^2;Z(K))=Z(K)$.
($\Rightarrow$) If $K$ abelian then $[k,k']=1$ for all $k,k'$, so $\gamma(\beta)=0$ for every
band.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[strictification end needs a nonabelian group]\label{lem:strict}
For any finite $K$: $\ \delta(\rho)\neq0$ for some band $\rho\ \Rightarrow\ K$ is nonabelian.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contrapositive. If $K$ is abelian then $\Inn K=1$, so $(\Sigma)$ reads
$1\to1\to\Aut K\xrightarrow{=}\Out K\to1$: the kernel is trivial, every band lifts uniquely, and
$\delta\equiv0$ (the lemma gives $[A,B]\in\Inn K=1$).
\end{proof}

\section{Theorem: forced correlation}\label{sec:theorem}

\begin{theorem}[forced correlation]\label{thm:main}
Let $K$ be finite of nilpotency class $\le2$. Then
\[
\big[\ \exists\,\rho:\delta(\rho)\neq0\ \big]\ \Longrightarrow\
\big[\ \exists\,\beta:[\gerbe]=\gamma(\beta)\neq0\ \big].
\]
Equivalently, the corner $(\delta\text{ couples},\ [\gerbe]=0\ \text{for all bands})$ is
\emph{empty}. The implication is \emph{strict}: its converse fails --- $K=D_4$ has
$[\gerbe]\neq0$ but $\delta\equiv0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $\delta(\rho)\neq0$ for some $\rho$, Lemma~\ref{lem:strict} gives $K$ nonabelian, and then
Lemma~\ref{lem:central} gives a band $\beta$ with $[\gerbe]=\gamma(\beta)\neq0$. Strictness:
for $K=D_4$ (dihedral of order $8$, class $2$), $\Inn D_4=(\Z/2)^2$, and the extension
$(\Sigma)$, $1\to(\Z/2)^2\to\Aut D_4\to\Out D_4\to1$ with $\Aut D_4\cong D_4$,
$\Out D_4\cong\Z/2$, \emph{splits}: the automorphism $\phi:r\mapsto r^{-1},\ s\mapsto rs$ is an
order-$2$ element of $\Aut D_4$ that is \emph{not} inner (it lies outside the inner Klein
four-subgroup $\{1,r^2,s,r^2s\}$), so $\langle\phi\rangle\to\Out D_4$ is a homomorphic section
--- hence $\delta\equiv0$. Yet $D_4$ is nonabelian, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:central}
$[\gerbe]=[r,s]=r^2\neq0\in Z(D_4)$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The structural cause (the asymmetry).} The two ``split?'' questions underlying the
four corners are \emph{not} logically free:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$(Z)$ is non-split for every nonabelian class-$2$ $K$.} A splitting
$\Inn K\to K$ would be a homomorphic section with image a complement $S$ to $Z(K)$; because
$(Z)$ is central, $K=Z(K)\times S$ with $Z(K)$ and $S\cong\Inn K$ both abelian, forcing $K$
abelian --- a contradiction. (Coupling is then supplied directly by Lemma~\ref{lem:central},
using the trivial center-action.)
\item \textbf{$(\Sigma)$ can split} ($D_4,Q_8,\Heis(\F_3),M_{16},C_4\rtimes C_4$ all do), and
split $(\Sigma)\Rightarrow\delta\equiv0$ (a homomorphic section
$\theta:\Out K\to\Aut K$ turns any band $\rho$ into a homomorphic lift $\theta\rho$).
\end{itemize}
So among class-$\le2$ groups the column ``$(Z)$ splits'' of the cross-tabulation contains
\emph{only abelian groups}; the central end is forced on for all nonabelian such $K$, while the
strictification end is free. That collapse of one column \emph{is} the forced correlation. (The
``$(Z)$ split $\Rightarrow$ abelian'' step is a \emph{nilpotency} phenomenon and fails outside
it; see \S\ref{sec:boundary}.)

\begin{remark}[extension to all finite nilpotent $K$; attributed to R.]\label{rem:nilpotent}
The class-$\le2$ hypothesis above is a convenience, not the true domain. The following argument,
which we owe to R.\ (referee), shows the Theorem holds \emph{verbatim} for every finite nilpotent
$K$. Let $c$ be the nilpotency class and $\gamma_c=[\gamma_{c-1},K]\neq1$ the last nontrivial
lower-central term. Since $\gamma_c\subseteq Z(K)$ and $\gamma_c$ is generated by commutators,
there exist $x,y\in K$ with $[x,y]\in\gamma_c\setminus\{1\}\subseteq Z(K)\setminus\{1\}$. Their
images $\bar x,\bar y\in\Inn K$ commute (as $[x,y]$ is central, $\bar x\bar y=\overline{xy}$ and
$\overline{xy}=\overline{yx}$ up to the central factor $[x,y]$, which acts trivially by
conjugation), so $\beta:s\mapsto\bar x,\ t\mapsto\bar y$ is an inner band and the central-end
class is $\gamma(\beta)=[x,y]\neq0$. Thus $K$ nonabelian $\Rightarrow[\gerbe]$ couples holds for
all nilpotent $K$ (not only class $\le2$), $(Z)$ remains a central extension with trivial
coefficients, and the $\delta$-end is Giraud's nonabelian obstruction on $T^2$ (no commuting lift
pair), still computable. Composing with Lemma~\ref{lem:strict} (which assumed nothing about the
class) gives the implication $\delta\Rightarrow[\gerbe]$ for all finite nilpotent $K$. We keep
the class-$\le2$ proof above as the case originally established here; the nilpotent extension is
R.'s.
\end{remark}

\section{Verification (hostile-referee pass)}\label{sec:verification}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\S\ref{sec:setup} central-end identification.} Trivial center-action is immediate
($z$ central). The lemma applies verbatim to any abelian-kernel extension; $(Z)$ qualifies with
$A=Z(K)$. $H^2(T^2;Z(K))=Z(K)$ for trivial coefficients is standard ($T^2=K(\Z^2,1)$,
$H^2(\Z^2;M)=M$). The class is the bare commutator --- matches
\texttt{lift-cocycle-derivation} and the $D_4$ arithmetic $[r,s]=r^2$.
\item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:central}.} The class-$\le2$ hypothesis enters at one point: to land
$[k,k']$ in $Z(K)$ directly, so that the \emph{abelian} hinge $\Inn K=K/Z(K)$ makes any pair of
images commute and $[k,k']$ is read as a central-end class. This is a convenient sufficient
condition, \emph{not} a necessary one: see the attributed extension to all finite nilpotent $K$
in Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent} above, where centrality of a nontrivial commutator is recovered
from the lower-central series rather than assumed.
\item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:strict}.} Elementary; $\Inn K=1\Rightarrow\delta\equiv0$.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.} Pure composition of the two lemmas; the empty corner is
their conjunction. The structural-cause paragraph (central extension of a nonabelian class-$2$
group never splits) is an independent elementary check, and matches $(Z)$-split$=$False on
every nonabelian computed row.
\item \textbf{Strictness witness $D_4$.} $\Aut D_4\cong D_4$ splits over the inner Klein four
via the non-inner order-$2$ automorphism $\phi:r\mapsto r^{-1},\ s\mapsto rs$; verified by the
engine ($\Sigma$ split$=$True, $\delta$-couple$=$False) and by hand. $D_4$ is the dihedral
$4\mid n$ instance at $n=4$.
\item \textbf{$(\Sigma)$-split check for $\Heis(\F_3)$.} $|\Out|=48$, too large to read off by
hand; the split was verified through $\Aut\Heis(\F_3)\cong C_3^2\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ (order
$432$, with $\Inn\cong C_3^2$), in which a complement to $\Inn$ is exhibited by the
$\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ factor, giving a homomorphic section of $\pi$.
\item \textbf{Boundary/degenerate cases.} Abelian $K$: both ends trivial (corner
$(\text{no},\text{no})$). Rank-$1$ base $S^1$: $H^2(S^1;-)=0$, both ends vanish, no content.
Trivial band: commutator $=0$, both obstructions $0$.
\item \textbf{Scope honesty.} $SD_{16},D_{16}$ (both of order $16$) have $\Inn$ nonabelian
(class $3$), so the \emph{class-$\le2$} proof does not cover them directly; but they are
\emph{nilpotent} --- the $2$-power dihedral group $D_{2^k}$ has class $k-1$ --- so the attributed
extension (Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}) does cover them, and both ends are computed ($\delta$ no,
$[\gerbe]$ yes) with no violation. The wider dihedral family $D_n$ with $4\mid n$, $n>4$, is
\emph{not} uniformly nilpotent: $D_{12}$ and $D_{20}$ (orders $12,20$) are non-nilpotent, hence
outside Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}, yet they too compute as ($\delta$ no, $[\gerbe]$ yes) and so
\emph{happen} to satisfy the implication. The genuine failures lie strictly beyond nilpotency
(\S\ref{sec:boundary}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and (non-)gaps}\label{sec:scope}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Proved domain $=$ class $\le2$; the true boundary is nilpotency.} The proof above
is written for class $\le2$ because then $\Inn K$ is abelian, both obstructions are ordinary
abelian $H^2$ ($\delta\in H^2(T^2;\Inn K)$, $(Z)$ a genuine central extension with
$[k,k']\in Z(K)$), and Lemma~\ref{lem:central} is immediate. This is \emph{not} the maximal
domain: the implication in fact holds verbatim for \emph{every} finite nilpotent $K$ (see
Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}, an argument we attribute to R.). The genuine dividing line is
\textbf{nilpotency}, not class $\le2$; it is identified, and shown sharp, in
\S\ref{sec:boundary}.
\item \textbf{Grade.} The Theorem (forced correlation) is \emph{proved} --- it rests only on
the proved $\delta$-commutator lemma and two elementary lemmas. The battery cross-tabulation is
\emph{computed} over the enumerated family (not a census of all groups of each order; minimality
is not claimed).
\item \textbf{Not inflated.} The claim is a one-directional implication with a strict
counterexample to its converse, \emph{not} a biconditional and \emph{not} a census theorem. The
decisive content is the forced-empty corner plus its structural cause (the $(Z)$-split column is
abelian-only).
\end{itemize}

\section{The true boundary: nilpotency}\label{sec:boundary}

The implication is \emph{not} unconditional: it fails as soon as $K$ is non-nilpotent. The
cleanest witness is $K=\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)=C_3^2\rtimes C_2$ of order $18$, which is
\emph{centreless} ($Z(K)=1$). Here $\Aut K\cong C_3^2\rtimes\mathrm{GL}(2,3)$ (order $432$),
$\Inn K\cong K$, and $\Out K\cong\mathrm{PGL}(2,3)\cong S_4$. The normal $V_4\subset
\mathrm{PGL}(2,3)$ lifts to $Q_8\subset\mathrm{SL}(2,3)$, so lifts of a commuting $V_4$-pair
\emph{anticommute}: the $\delta$-end couples. (The cross-tabulation script finds $12$ unordered
$\Out$-pairs that $\delta$-couple.) But $Z(K)=1$ forces $[\gerbe]\equiv0$. Hence the corner
$(\delta\neq0,\ [\gerbe]=0)$ --- which the Theorem forces empty in the nilpotent world --- is
\emph{occupied} for this non-nilpotent $K$. Controls: $S_3$ and $A_4$ (also centreless, with
cyclic $\Out$) do \emph{not} $\delta$-couple, so centrelessness alone does not produce a
violation --- the $\delta$-end still needs the right $\Aut$-structure. (The
$\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)$ witness, the count of $12$ unordered $\delta$-coupling $\Out$-pairs, and
the $S_3,A_4$ controls are due to R.\ (Rick), 2026-10-14.)

\paragraph{Correcting the structural cause.} This pins down what the ``$(Z)$-split column is
abelian-only'' statement of \S\ref{sec:theorem} really depended on. ``$(Z)$ splits
$\Rightarrow K$ abelian'' is a \emph{nilpotency} fact, \emph{not} a general one: for a centreless
$K$ the sequence $(Z)$ reads $1\to1\to K\xrightarrow{\sim}\Inn K\to1$ and splits
\emph{trivially}, yet $K$ is nonabelian. So the central end is switched off exactly when no
nontrivial commutator is central, and in that regime the strictification end can still couple
($\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)$, though not $S_3$ or $A_4$). This is
exactly what nilpotency prevents, via the $\gamma_c$-central mechanism of
Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}. (Centre size is not the parameter: R.'s $K=\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)\times
C_2$ has $Z(K)=C_2\neq1$ yet still occupies the forbidden corner, because its commutators lie in
the centreless $\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)$ factor.) Nilpotency is therefore the honest
boundary of the forced correlation.

\begin{remark}[open lead: no class-$\ge3$ $\delta$-coupling witness yet]\label{rem:openlead}
The nilpotent extension (Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent}) is true but, at class $\ge3$, so far
\emph{vacuous as to $\delta$-coupling}: in the small-order battery
($D_{16},SD_{16},Q_{16},D_{32},SD_{32},Q_{32},D_{16}\times C_2,Q_{16}\times C_2,
SD_{16}\times C_2,C_8\rtimes C_4,C_3\wr C_3,\dots$) \emph{no} class-$\ge3$ nilpotent group
$\delta$-couples. Thus every known $\delta$-coupling nilpotent witness is class $2$
($D_4\times C_2$, $Q_8\times C_2$). Finding a class-$\ge3$ nilpotent $K$ that $\delta$-couples
(or proving none exists) is open; the natural place to look is the $D_4\times C_2$ mechanism ---
central automorphisms arising from $\mathrm{Hom}(K,Z(K))$.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{One-line summary.} On $T^2$, the strictification obstruction
$\delta\in H^2(T^2;\Inn K)$ and the central obstruction $[\gerbe]\in H^2(T^2;Z(K))$ of a
class-$\le2$ inventory gerbe are \emph{not independent}: $\delta$-coupling forces $K$
nonabelian, and every nonabelian class-$2$ group has $[\gerbe]\neq0$ (central extension
non-split, trivial center-action), so $\delta\neq0\Rightarrow[\gerbe]\neq0$ --- a strict
one-way forced correlation \textbf{(B)}; the converse fails at $K=D_4$ ($[\gerbe]\neq0$,
$\delta\equiv0$, the dihedral $4\mid n$ witness), and the corner $(\delta\neq0,[\gerbe]=0)$ is
forced empty. The same holds for every finite \emph{nilpotent} $K$ (Remark~\ref{rem:nilpotent},
attributed to R.), and nilpotency is sharp: the centreless $K=\mathrm{Dih}(C_3^2)$ occupies the
forbidden corner. The content is modest --- the central end only detects nonabelianness --- so
the live question is which groups $\delta$-couple.
