{The Dirichlet tensor of two polynomial comonoids is the\\
product internal category --- in every finitely complete category}

\author{MacBeth}

\begin{abstract}
In $\Set$, comonoids for the substitution product $\sub$ of polynomial functors are exactly small
categories, and the Dirichlet tensor $\otimes$ of two such comonoids carries a comonoid whose
represented category is the product $C\times D$ on the nose --- the $\Set$ case is Niu--Spivak
\cite[Prop.~8.79]{NiuSpivak2023}. We show this is not an artefact of $\Set$, nor of any size,
coproduct, or well-pointedness hypothesis. Let $\Ee$ be any finitely complete category and $C,D$
internal categories in $\Ee$ whose domain maps are exponentiable, so that they are
$\sub$-comonoids $c,d$ in the category $\Poly(\Ee)$ of internal polynomials in the sense of
Shapiro--Spivak \cite{ShapiroSpivak2023}. Then $c\otimes d$ carries a canonical $\sub$-comonoid
structure whose represented internal category is the product $C\times D$ in $\Cat(\Ee)$ ---
componentwise objects, morphisms, identities and composition --- \emph{on the nose}. Beyond the
ambient Shapiro--Spivak polynomial framework the proof uses only finite limits: its single
load-bearing coherence is the commutation of products with pullbacks, a finite-limit fact that needs
no local cartesian closure, no coproducts, no extensivity and no well-pointedness. The statement
therefore holds uniformly across every elementary and Grothendieck topos and every locally cartesian
closed category, with $\Set$ as the degenerate case. We separate carefully this (true) identification
of the represented category from the (false) assertion that $\otimes$ is the categorical product of
comonoids: among comonoids --- equivalently cofunctors --- $\otimes$ is a monoidal, non-cartesian
product, and the genuine categorical product lives on the functor side $\Cat(\Ee)$. A worked witness
in the presheaf topos $\Graph$ illustrates the identification and is machine-checked in Lean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%============================================================

\subsection*{The equivalence chain, and whether it is a feature of \texorpdfstring{$\Set$}{Set}}

A recurring discovery of the last fifteen years is that three superficially different notions of
``directed shape'' coincide:
\[
   \text{directed containers} \;\simeq\; \text{polynomial comonoids} \;\cong\;
   \text{small categories and cofunctors }(\Cat^{\sharp}).
\]
(Bare \emph{containers} --- polynomials carrying no comonoid structure --- are \emph{not} a fourth
item on this list: they are strictly more general, and are \emph{not} equivalent to directed
containers.) The last of these identifications is the one this note is about. Write $\Poly$ for the
category of polynomial endofunctors on $\Set$, equipped with the substitution (composition) monoidal
product $\sub$ with unit $y=\mathrm{Id}$. A theorem of Ahman and Uustalu, given its modern comonoid
phrasing by Spivak and collaborators \cite{SGF2021,NiuSpivak2023}, identifies the comonoids in
$(\Poly,\sub,y)$ with small categories on objects, and with \emph{cofunctors} on morphisms:
\[
   \Comon(\Poly,\sub,y) \;\cong\; \Cat^{\sharp},
\]
a bijection on objects with $\Cat$ whose morphisms are cofunctors --- forward on objects, backward on
arrows --- \emph{not} functors \cite[Cor.~5.12]{ShapiroSpivak2023}; and $\Cat^{\sharp}\neq\Cat$, as
\S\ref{sec:notproduct} will show.
A polynomial carries a \emph{second} monoidal product, the Dirichlet tensor $\otimes$, which in
$\Set$ is the Day convolution induced on $\Poly$ by the cartesian structure $(1,\times)$ ---
Niu--Spivak \cite[Prop.~3.79]{NiuSpivak2023}. The two products interlock into a normal duoidal
structure, so $\sub$-comonoids are closed under $\otimes$; and a short computation shows that the
resulting comonoid on $c\otimes d$ is precisely the product category $C\times D$. This last fact, in
$\Set$, is Niu--Spivak \cite[Prop.~8.79]{NiuSpivak2023}: carrier $c\otimes d$, counit
$\varepsilon_c\otimes\varepsilon_d$, comultiplication through the duoidal interchanger, with
codomains and composition coordinatewise.

This is a clean fact about $\Set$. It is natural to ask whether it is \emph{only} a fact about
$\Set$. The question is not idle. The entire value of the equivalence chain, as a foundation for
compositional systems, is that it should be a theorem about \emph{structure}, not about a privileged
ambient universe; and the concrete compositional models one wants --- presheaves on a site of
locations, sheaves on a space of states, graphs of a network --- live in categories other than
$\Set$. If the Dirichlet-tensor-is-product fact ceased to hold the moment one left $\Set$, the chain
would be a $\Set$-level coincidence rather than a transportable principle.

\subsection*{The main result}

It is not a coincidence. The identification transports, verbatim, to \emph{any finitely complete
category} in which the ambient polynomial framework is available --- which, following
Shapiro--Spivak \cite{ShapiroSpivak2023}, asks only that the relevant maps be exponentiable.

\begin{lemma}[On-the-nose identification]\label{lem:main}
Let $\Ee$ be a finitely complete category, and let $C,D$ be internal categories in $\Ee$ whose
domain maps $\dom_C,\dom_D$ are exponentiable, with associated $\sub$-comonoids $c,d$ in
$\Poly(\Ee)$. Then $c\otimes d$ carries a canonical $(\sub,y)$-comonoid structure --- counit
$\varepsilon_c\otimes\varepsilon_d$, comultiplication $\iota\circ(\delta_c\otimes\delta_d)$ via the
normal-duoidal interchanger $\iota$ of \cite[Thm.~4.5]{ShapiroSpivak2023} --- and the internal
category it represents is the product internal category $C\times D$, \emph{on the nose}:
object-of-objects $C_0\times D_0$, object-of-morphisms $C_1\times D_1$, with domain, codomain,
identities and composition all componentwise. In symbols, $c\otimes d=\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ in
$\Comon(\Poly(\Ee),\sub,y)$.
\end{lemma}

Over $\Set$ this is \cite[Prop.~8.79]{NiuSpivak2023} word for word. The internal statement does not
appear in \cite{ShapiroSpivak2023} or \cite{NiuSpivak2023}; it is a short corollary of their results
--- normal duoidality \cite[Thm.~4.5]{ShapiroSpivak2023} plus the comonoid--category identification
\cite[Thm.~5.6, Rem.~5.7]{ShapiroSpivak2023} --- and that is how we present it. We claim the
internal corollary and the honest accounting around it; we do not claim either input.

Accordingly we state the identification as a \emph{lemma}, not as a standalone theorem. Its proof is
half a page, and its natural home is a larger unit that uses internal polynomial comonoids in
presheaf or sheaf models --- the $\Graph$ models of \S\ref{sec:graph} being the first such host. As a
self-contained note it would want a second result of its own: for instance an explicit description of
the genuine categorical product in $\Cat^{\sharp}(\Ee)$ (or a proof that it fails to exist over a
general base), or a Day-convolution identification over Grothendieck $\Ee$ in a stated sense. Both are
left to future work, and neither is claimed here. What this write-up contributes is the lemma, its
honest scope, and the machine-checked $\Graph$ witness.

\subsection*{What is, and what is not, a product}

One warning belongs on the first page, because an earlier version of this note got it wrong. The
identification $c\otimes d=\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ says that the \emph{represented internal category}
of $c\otimes d$ is $C\times D$. It does \emph{not} say that $c\otimes d$ is the categorical product
of $c$ and $d$ in the category of comonoids. That stronger statement is \emph{false}, already in
$\Set$. Comonoid morphisms in $\Poly$ are \emph{cofunctors}, not functors
\cite[Cor.~5.12, Def.~5.11]{ShapiroSpivak2023}; and among cofunctors $C\times D$ is not the
categorical product --- as Niu--Spivak note, the usual product of categories ``is not the categorical
product in $\Cat^{\sharp}$; it is, however, a monoidal product on $\Cat^{\sharp}$, coinciding with the
parallel product'' \cite[\S8.2.4]{NiuSpivak2023}. We give the two-line counterexample in
\S\ref{sec:notproduct}. The genuine categorical product of $C$ and $D$ lives on the \emph{functor}
side: in $\Cat(\Ee)$, where morphisms are internal functors, $C\times D$ is the product, by the
elementary fact that $\Cat(\Ee)$ has all limits $\Ee$ has. That is a statement about $\Cat(\Ee)$, not
about $\otimes$ on comonoids, and we keep the two apart.

\subsection*{Why ``on the nose'', and what the proof does not use}

The phrase ``on the nose'' is doing real work, and it means two slightly different things for the two
kinds of data. \emph{Polynomials are objects} of $\Poly(\Ee)$, so an equality of polynomials --- such
as the carrier equality of $c\otimes d$ and $\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ at the level of bundles --- is a
\emph{literal} equality of objects, once a choice of finite products in $\Ee$ is fixed (we fix one in
\S\ref{sec:setI}). The \emph{structure maps} $\varepsilon,\delta$, by contrast, are \emph{morphisms}
of $\Poly(\Ee)$, and morphisms are iso-classes of diagrams \cite[Def.~3.7, 3.13]{ShapiroSpivak2023};
so the claim that the structure maps of $c\otimes d$ --- read off through the comonoid-to-category
dictionary --- agree with those of $C\times D$ is an equality of such iso-classes, the only
isomorphism in sight being the canonical comparison $\kappa$ witnessing that products commute with
pullbacks \eqref{eq:star}. The reason is a clean division of labour. The non-trivial, non-pointwise
ingredient of the substitution product $\sub$ (it uses the dependent product $\Pi$ along domain maps,
hence their exponentiability --- it is \emph{not} computed objectwise in a presheaf topos) is absorbed
entirely into the ambient framework and into the comonoid-to-category translation, and never
resurfaces in the identification. What remains, once one is speaking the language of internal-category
data $(C_0,C_1,\dom,\cod,\id,\comp)$, is componentwise finite limits.

Three ingredients the reader might expect to see are conspicuously absent:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No well-pointedness.} The counit $\varepsilon_c:c\to y$, which in the $\Set$ picture
``picks the identity $\id_i$ in each hom-set'', is here literally the single identity-assigning
section $\id:C_0\to C_1$ of $\Ee$ --- not a choice of global elements. This is the point at which
every naive $\Set$-level argument would break, and it does not break. The $\Graph$ witness of
\S\ref{sec:graph} is built with a \emph{loopless} object-of-objects, which has no global element at
all, to make the point concrete.
\item \emph{No local cartesian closure for the identification itself.} Exponentiability of the
domain maps is what makes $\Poly(\Ee)$, $\sub$ and $\otimes$ exist at all
\cite[Thm.~3.26, Def.~4.1, Lem.~4.3, Thm.~4.5]{ShapiroSpivak2023}; but the \emph{identification}
$c\otimes d=\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ consumes, beyond that ambient framework, nothing more than finite
limits. Its one coherence is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:star}
  (C_1\times_{C_0}C_1)\times(D_1\times_{D_0}D_1)\;\cong\;(C_1\times D_1)\times_{C_0\times D_0}(C_1\times D_1),
  \tag{$\star$}
\end{equation}
the commutation of products with pullbacks --- a finite-limit fact, true in any finitely complete
category, needing no dependent products, no coproducts, and no extensivity.
\item \emph{No extensivity, no disjointness of coproducts.} None is used; \eqref{eq:star} is a
limit--limit interchange, not an extensivity axiom.
\end{itemize}

So the honest reach of the method is the Shapiro--Spivak setting itself: any finitely complete
category with the exponentiable maps their framework requires. Every locally cartesian closed
category and every elementary topos --- Grothendieck or not, with or without a natural-numbers
object --- is an instance, and so is $\Set$.

\subsection*{A question that inverted into its own answer}

This note is the positive residue of a negative-looking investigation. For some months the program
pursued a ``which base $V$?'' question: over which monoidal bases does the product-is-Dirichlet-tensor
phenomenon survive? The characteristic failure case was $V=\mathbf{Vec}$. Read under ``for \emph{all}
$V$?'', the failure looks like a dead end. Read under ``for \emph{which} $V$?'', it is informative:
the phenomenon asks for a base with genuine dependent-type structure --- enough that the maps carrying
the directions of a polynomial are exponentiable. A finitely complete category with that much
structure supplies it, and the present lemma states the positive side: the fact does not merely
survive the passage away from $\Set$, it is \emph{uniform} across every such base. We can even locate
the $\mathbf{Vec}$ obstruction precisely, and it is not where one might guess. $\mathbf{Vec}$ is
finitely complete, so the Shapiro--Spivak framework applies to it verbatim: there is no missing
polynomial construction. What is scarce is exponentiable maps. In $\mathbf{Vec}$ the exponentiable
morphisms are exactly the isomorphisms --- a pullback functor $s^\ast$ must preserve the fold
$A\oplus A\to A$, and the resulting comparison forces $s$ to be both injective and surjective --- so
the only internal categories in $\mathbf{Vec}$ whose domain maps are exponentiable are the discrete
ones. The lemma therefore holds over $\mathbf{Vec}$, but degenerately. The $\mathbf{Vec}$ obstruction
is a \emph{shortage of exponentiable maps}, not the absence of a construction; cartesianness of the
tensor was never the issue.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
%============================================================

We fix a finitely complete category $\Ee$. Following Shapiro--Spivak \cite{ShapiroSpivak2023}, the
polynomial framework requires no more: a \emph{polynomial} is an \emph{exponentiable} morphism, and
all of $\Poly(\Ee)$, the substitution product $\sub$, the Dirichlet tensor $\otimes$ and the normal
duoidal structure are available over any such $\Ee$. Recall that a morphism $s:B\to A$ is
exponentiable when the pullback functor $s^\ast:\Ee/A\to\Ee/B$ has a right adjoint $\Pi_s$ (dependent
product along $s$); in a locally cartesian closed category \emph{every} morphism is exponentiable,
and in an elementary topos likewise, but we never assume this globally.

\subsection{Internal polynomial functors}

\begin{definition}[{\cite{GambinoKock2013,ShapiroSpivak2023}}]
A \emph{polynomial} $p$ in $\Ee$ is an exponentiable morphism (a \emph{bundle}) $s_p:B_p\to A_p$ of
$\Ee$. Its \emph{positions} object is $A_p$ and its \emph{directions} bundle is $s_p$; the
\emph{fibre} over a generalized element $I$ of $A_p$ is $p\dir{I}:=s_p^{-1}(I)$. The associated
endofunctor $\Ee\to\Ee$ is
\[
  p(X)\;=\;\Sigma_{A_p}\,\Pi_{s_p}\,B_p^\ast X,
\]
where $B_p^\ast X=X\times B_p$ (pullback of $X\to1$ along $B_p\to1$) and $\Sigma_{A_p},\Pi_{s_p}$ are
dependent sum and product. Over a locally cartesian closed base Gambino--Kock \cite{GambinoKock2013}
first constructed the category $\Poly(\Ee)$ with its \emph{substitution} (composition) product $\sub$
and unit $y:=(\id:1\to1)$; Shapiro--Spivak \cite[Thm.~3.26]{ShapiroSpivak2023} give the same product
over any finitely complete $\Ee$ for polynomials whose bundles are exponentiable. Morphisms run
\emph{forward} on positions and \emph{backward} on directions.
\end{definition}

The construction of $\sub$ is where exponentiability is spent: composing the functors $p\circ q$ and
re-presenting the composite as a polynomial requires the dependent product $\Pi$ along a direction
bundle. The identification of \S\ref{sec:setI} does \emph{not} revisit this; it works at the level of
internal-category data, where only finite limits occur.

\begin{definition}[Dirichlet tensor {\cite[Def.~4.1]{ShapiroSpivak2023}}]
The \emph{Dirichlet tensor} of bundles $p=(B_p\to A_p)$ and $q=(B_q\to A_q)$ is the product bundle
\[
   s_p\times s_q\;:\;B_p\times B_q\;\longrightarrow\;A_p\times A_q,
\]
formed with the finite products of $\Ee$ (exponentiability is preserved by products,
\cite[Lem.~4.3]{ShapiroSpivak2023}). Its positions object is $A_p\times A_q$ and its fibre over
$(I,J)$ is $p\dir{I}\times q\dir{J}$. On representables $y^A\otimes y^B=y^{A\times B}$; its unit is
again $y$. Over $\Set$ this $\otimes$ is the Day convolution on $\Poly$ induced by $(1,\times)$,
\cite[Prop.~3.79]{NiuSpivak2023}; over a general $\Ee$ we take \eqref{eq:star}'s ambient definition
--- $\otimes$ is \emph{the product of bundles} --- and make no Day-convolution claim (see
\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{The comonoid--category dictionary}\label{sub:dictionary}

A $\sub$-comonoid is a triple $(c,\varepsilon,\delta)$ with $c=(s_c:B_c\to A_c)$, counit
$\varepsilon:c\to y$ and comultiplication $\delta:c\to c\sub c$, satisfying counit and
coassociativity. Unwinding over $\Ee$ (every ``element of a fibre'' read as a generalized element /
section / $\Ee$-morphism):

\begin{itemize}
\item $\varepsilon:c\to y$ is, forward on positions, the unique map $A_c\to1$, and backward on
directions a \emph{section} $e:A_c\to B_c$ of $s_c$. Dictionary: $A_c=C_0$ (object-of-objects),
$B_c=C_1$ (object-of-morphisms), $s_c=\dom$, and $e=\id:C_0\to C_1$ the identity-assigning section.
\item $\delta:c\to c\sub c$ is, forward on positions, the $\Pi_{\dom}$-transpose of $\cod$ ---
a map $C_0\to A_{c\sub c}$ whose global content is $\cod:C_1\to C_0$ --- and backward, over the
pullback of composable pairs $C_1\times_{\cod,C_0,\dom}C_1$, the composition $\comp:C_1\times_{C_0}C_1\to C_1$.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The comonoid laws are exactly the internal-category axioms (left/right identity,
associativity). This is the internal form of the Ahman--Uustalu equivalence; its \emph{full} form
over a finitely complete $\Ee$, both directions and a one-by-one match of structure equations on
unconstrained data, is Shapiro--Spivak \cite[Thm.~5.6, Rem.~5.7]{ShapiroSpivak2023}. We use only the
\emph{forward} direction --- internal category $\leadsto$ comonoid, a direct construction --- for $C$,
$D$ and $C\times D$.

\begin{remark}[No well-pointedness]\label{rem:wp}
The counit's backward datum is the single section $\id:C_0\to C_1$ of $\Ee$. No global elements of
$C_0$ are chosen, needed, or available in general. The $\Set$-level gloss ``$\varepsilon$ picks
$\id_i\in c\dir{i}$'' is, internally, this one morphism. \S\ref{sec:graph} illustrates the point
against a $C_0$ with no global element whatsoever.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The normal-duoidal interchanger}\label{sub:interchanger}

$(\Poly(\Ee),\otimes,\sub,y)$ is a \emph{normal} duoidal category \cite[Thm.~4.5]{ShapiroSpivak2023}:
the units of $\otimes$ and $\sub$ coincide (both $y$), so the unit-comparison maps are identities and
the only non-trivial structure map is the lax interchanger
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iota}
  \iota:\;(p\sub p')\otimes(q\sub q')\;\longrightarrow\;(p\otimes q)\sub(p'\otimes q'),
  \qquad\text{natural in }p,p',q,q',
\end{equation}
given by \cite[Thm.~4.5, eq.~(16)]{ShapiroSpivak2023} (the $\Set$ formula is \cite[Ex.~4.6]{ShapiroSpivak2023}).
Its coherence is cited, not load-bearing: the comonoid laws of $c\otimes d$ follow in
\S\ref{sec:setI} from the on-the-nose identification, independently of the abstract coherence of
$\iota$. On positions, $\iota$ is the canonical comparison
$\Pi_X A\times\Pi_Y B\to\Pi_{X\times Y}(A\times B)$ (fibrewise $A^X\times B^Y\to(A\times B)^{X\times Y}$);
we will only use that it sends the pair of transposes of $\cod_C,\cod_D$ to the transpose of
$\cod_C\times\cod_D$, by naturality of transposition.

%============================================================
\section{The identification \texorpdfstring{$c\otimes d=\sharpc{(C\times D)}$}{c tensor d = (CxD)sharp}}\label{sec:setI}
%============================================================

We prove Lemma~\ref{lem:main}. Fix, once and for all, a choice of finite products and pullbacks in
$\Ee$; with this choice fixed, every ``$=$'' below is a literal equality of the chosen limits in
$\Ee$. Fix internal categories $C,D$ with comonoids $c=(\dom_C:C_1\to C_0)$ and
$d=(\dom_D:D_1\to D_0)$, and set
\[
  \varepsilon_{c\otimes d}:=\lambda\circ(\varepsilon_c\otimes\varepsilon_d),
  \qquad
  \delta_{c\otimes d}:=\iota\circ(\delta_c\otimes\delta_d),
\]
where $\lambda:y\otimes y\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }y$ is the unitor of $\otimes$ --- on positions the
chosen iso $1\times1\cong1$, an identity if one takes $1\times1=1$. The point to record for the
``on the nose'' reading is that $\lambda$ is part of the \emph{definition} of
$\varepsilon_{c\otimes d}$, not a coherence isomorphism suppressed after the fact; likewise the
comparison $\kappa$ of \eqref{eq:star} will be part of the definition of $\delta_{c\otimes d}$, not a
smuggled identification.

\subsection*{Step 1 --- the underlying object, from finite limits alone}

By the definition of $\otimes$ as the product of bundles, the underlying bundle of $c\otimes d$ is
the product map
\[
  \dom_C\times\dom_D\;:\;C_1\times D_1\;\longrightarrow\;C_0\times D_0 .
\]
This is, on the nose, the underlying bundle of $\sharpc{(C\times D)}$, whose domain map is by
definition $\dom_{C\times D}=\dom_C\times\dom_D$. The fibre identity
$(B_p\times B_q)_{(I,J)}=p\dir{I}\times q\dir{J}$ is the universal property of a product of maps.
Hence $c\otimes d$ and $\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ have equal underlying objects of $\Ee$.

\subsection*{Step 2 --- the interchanger}

Instantiate \eqref{eq:iota} at $p=p'=c$, $q=q'=d$. On positions, $\iota$ sends
$\big((I,\varphi),(K,\psi)\big)\mapsto\big((I,K),\varphi\times\psi\big)$, a product of $\Ee$-morphisms;
this is the position-level comparison $\Pi_X A\times\Pi_Y B\to\Pi_{X\times Y}(A\times B)$ recalled in
\S\ref{sub:interchanger}. On directions (backward), $\iota$ reassociates the composable-pair objects,
and the content of the reassociation is the comparison map $\kappa$ of \eqref{eq:star}. The interchanger
itself is cited from \cite[Thm.~4.5]{ShapiroSpivak2023}; the only fact we verify by hand is that
\eqref{eq:star} is a genuine isomorphism, which it is in any finitely complete category because
products commute with pullbacks. No local cartesian closure, no extensivity, no disjointness of
coproducts, and no cartesianness of $\otimes$ is invoked here.

\subsection*{Step 3 --- the structure trace}

Tracing $\varepsilon_{c\otimes d}$ and $\delta_{c\otimes d}$ through Steps 1--2, with every
``element'' an $\Ee$-morphism:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Codomain.} $(\delta_c\otimes\delta_d)$ carries, on positions, the pair of
$\Pi$-transposes of $\cod_C$ and $\cod_D$; by naturality of transposition $\iota$ sends this to the
transpose of $\cod_C\times\cod_D$. As an $\Ee$-morphism,
$\cod_{c\otimes d}=\cod_C\times\cod_D:C_1\times D_1\to C_0\times D_0$.
\item \emph{Composition.} On directions, the backward leg of $\iota$ is the comparison map $\kappa$
of \eqref{eq:star}, and $(\delta_c\otimes\delta_d)$ applies $\comp_C,\comp_D$ componentwise. By
definition, the composition of the product internal category is
$\comp_{C\times D}=(\comp_C\times\comp_D)\circ\kappa$ --- that is, $\kappa$ is built into
$\comp_{C\times D}$. The backward leg of $\delta_{c\otimes d}$ is exactly
$(\comp_C\times\comp_D)\circ\kappa$. Hence the two composition maps are \emph{equal}, not merely equal
up to \eqref{eq:star}: the only isomorphism involved, $\kappa$, is a constituent of both sides.
\item \emph{Identities.} $\varepsilon_c\otimes\varepsilon_d$ yields the section
$\id_C\times\id_D:C_0\times D_0\to C_1\times D_1$.
\end{itemize}

These are, morphism for morphism, the structure maps of the internal product category $C\times D$.
Hence $(c\otimes d,\varepsilon_{c\otimes d},\delta_{c\otimes d})=\sharpc{(C\times D)}$ as comonoids.
Its comonoid laws are those of the genuine internal category $C\times D$ (a category because $C,D$
are, and because products of internal categories are internal categories --- \S\ref{sec:notproduct});
and that these are matched by the comonoid laws under the dictionary is the one-by-one correspondence
of structure equations established in the proof of \cite[Thm.~5.6]{ShapiroSpivak2023}, not merely a
bijection of lawful structures. This proves Lemma~\ref{lem:main}. \qed

\begin{remark}[Reduction to a presheaf category]
Shapiro--Spivak's Lemma~5.5 \cite{ShapiroSpivak2023} transports both the comonoid structure and the
internal-category structure along the fully faithful singular functor
$\Ee\hookrightarrow\widehat{\Ee}$ into presheaves, so it suffices to prove the identification in
$\widehat{\Ee}$. This is a genuine reduction, but \emph{not} a trivialisation: the substitution
product $\sub$ is not computed objectwise in $\widehat{\Ee}$ --- its dependent product $\Pi$ along
domain maps is not evaluation at each object (\cite[Ex.~3.21]{ShapiroSpivak2023} and the diagram there
compute $\mathrm{Hom}$ over the whole base, not fibrewise) --- so Step~3 must still be carried out
internally to $\widehat{\Ee}$. In particular it does \emph{not} collapse to the $\Set$ case
\cite[Prop.~8.79]{NiuSpivak2023} applied separately at each object: that would concern a different
comonoid at each stage. We have given the direct trace precisely because it exhibits which finite
limit \eqref{eq:star} is at stake.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No smuggled assumptions]\label{rem:honest}
The proof uses no choice (only chosen pullbacks enter, and the dependent-product transposes are
canonical), no excluded middle (there is no case split or decidability appeal), and no pointwise
coend (none is computed; $\otimes$ is the product of bundles). The element notation throughout is
legitimate as generalized elements.
\end{remark}

\section{The tensor is not the categorical product of comonoids}\label{sec:notproduct}
%============================================================

Lemma~\ref{lem:main} identifies the \emph{represented internal category} of $c\otimes d$ with
$C\times D$. It is tempting to upgrade this to ``$c\otimes d$ is the categorical product of $c$ and
$d$''. That upgrade is false, and it is worth seeing why, because the failure is already visible in
$\Set$.

The category of $\sub$-comonoids is, by \cite[Cor.~5.12, Def.~5.11]{ShapiroSpivak2023}, isomorphic to
the category of internal categories and internal \emph{cofunctors} --- written $\Cat^{\sharp}$ over
$\Set$. A cofunctor $F:C\to D$ goes forward on objects and \emph{backward} on morphisms, lifting
each $D$-arrow out of $F(x)$ to a $C$-arrow out of $x$. In $\Cat^{\sharp}$ the product category
$C\times D$ is \emph{not} the categorical product; it is a monoidal, non-cartesian product,
coinciding with the parallel product \cite[\S8.2.4]{NiuSpivak2023}. Lemma~\ref{lem:main} is the
internal, general-$\Ee$ form of this monoidal product, not of a categorical product.

\begin{example}[The cone exists; universality fails]\label{ex:walking}
Work in $\Set$. Let $C=D=\mathbf 2=\{0\to1\}$, the walking arrow, and let $T$ be the category with
objects $e,x,z$ and non-identity arrows $u:e\to x$, $v:e\to z$. Define cofunctors $F,G:T\to\mathbf2$
by $e\mapsto0$, $x,z\mapsto1$, with $F$ lifting the arrow $a:0\to1$ at $e$ to $u$ and $G$ lifting it
to $v$. (The cofunctor laws hold trivially: there are no composable non-identity pairs.) The
projections out of the monoidal product, $\pi_C=\lambda\circ(\id_c\otimes\varepsilon_d)$ and
$\pi_D=\lambda\circ(\varepsilon_c\otimes\id_d)$, are \emph{cofunctors}, so they act backward on
morphisms: $\pi_C$ is $(i,j)\mapsto i$ on objects, and it lifts an arrow $f:i\to i'$ of $C$ at a pair
$(i,j)$ to $(f,\id_j)$, because the backward leg of $\varepsilon_d$ is the identity. Thus $(F,G)$ is a
cone over $\pi_C,\pi_D$. But any cofunctor $H:T\to\mathbf2\times\mathbf2$ with $\pi_C H=F$ and
$\pi_D H=G$ must have $H(e)=(0,0)$ and $H(x)=H(z)=(1,1)$, and must lift $(a,\id):(0,0)\to(1,0)$ ---
the very arrow $\pi_C$ produces --- to an arrow out of $e$ whose codomain maps to $(1,0)$. No such
arrow of $T$ exists. So $(c\otimes d,\pi_C,\pi_D)$ is not a categorical product in $\Cat^{\sharp}$.
\end{example}

The genuine categorical product of $C$ and $D$ lives on the \emph{functor} side. Internal categories
in $\Ee$ are models of the finite-limit sketch $T_{\Cat}$, so for finitely complete $\Ee$ the category
$\Cat(\Ee)=\Mod(T_{\Cat},\Ee)$ has all limits $\Ee$ has, computed objectwise; in particular the
product $C\times D$ exists, with $(C\times D)_0=C_0\times D_0$, $(C\times D)_1=C_1\times D_1$,
structure componentwise, and projections internal \emph{functors}. (For a presheaf topos
$\Ee=[\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}},\Set]$ this is $\Cat(\Ee)\simeq[\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}},\Cat]$ with the
pointwise product.) This is the universal property one wants --- but it is a fact about $\Cat(\Ee)$,
proved by the sketch argument, and it is \emph{not} a statement about the tensor $\otimes$ on
comonoids. Lemma~\ref{lem:main} supplies the bridge: the \emph{object} $c\otimes d$ carries exactly
the product internal category, even though the \emph{morphism} theory of comonoids sees $\otimes$ as a
non-cartesian monoidal product.

\section{A witness in \texorpdfstring{$\Graph$}{Graph}, as an illustration}\label{sec:graph}
%============================================================

As a finite sanity check we exhibit Lemma~\ref{lem:main} in the presheaf topos
$\Ee=\Graph=[\,\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet,\Set\,]$ of directed graphs. Two honest caveats,
following the referee: in a presheaf topos the internal-category side is computed \emph{pointwise}, so
this check cannot probe the genuinely non-pointwise content of $\sub$ (that is discharged by the
general proof, not here); and the ``no global element'' point needs no test at all, since the
counit's datum is the morphism $\id$ and global elements never enter. The witness is therefore an
illustration of the finished lemma in a concrete topos, not evidence for it.

By $\Cat(\Graph)\simeq[\,\bullet\rightrightarrows\bullet,\Cat\,]$, an internal category $C$ in

\section{Discussion: honest bounds}\label{sec:discussion}
%============================================================

\subsection*{The maximal reach is the Shapiro--Spivak setting}
The identification uses, beyond finite limits, only that the polynomial framework of
\cite{ShapiroSpivak2023} is available --- i.e. that the direction bundles are exponentiable. That is
strictly weaker than local cartesian closure, and needs no coproducts: the dependent sums $\Sigma_f$
are postcomposition, which exists in any category. So there is no ``open upper bound'' to flag: the
honest maximal reach is the Shapiro--Spivak setting itself --- any finitely complete category with
exponentiable direction bundles --- which includes every locally cartesian closed category and every
elementary topos, Grothendieck or not, with or without a natural-numbers object.

\subsection*{``Day convolution'' is $\Set$-level motivation only}
Over $\Set$, the tensor $\otimes$ is genuinely a Day convolution: \cite[Prop.~3.79]{NiuSpivak2023} is
a co-Yoneda coend computation in $\Set$. Over a general $\Ee$ we make \emph{no} Day-convolution claim.
Stating one would require saying Day convolution \emph{of what} (internal functors on $\Ee$?
$\Ee$-enriched?), and the construction would need coends over a large category with copowers by
internal homs and pullback-stability --- genuinely base-sensitive data, and exactly where an
elementary topos (no infinite coproducts) might differ from a Grothendieck one. Our proof never
touches any of this: $\otimes$ is used only as the product of bundles \cite[Def.~4.1]{ShapiroSpivak2023}.
This is why the title says ``Dirichlet tensor''.

\subsection*{No converse packaged in}
We do \emph{not} assert any converse of the form ``these hypotheses force $\Ee$ to be a topos''. The
lemma is stated directly over finitely complete categories with exponentiable direction bundles,
and \S\ref{sec:transport} records which strictly weaker properties each line consumes.

\subsection*{What this buys the chain}
The identification $\text{directed containers}\simeq\cdots\cong\Cat^{\sharp}$ now carries its
object-level monoidal content --- the Dirichlet tensor represents the product internal category ---
uniformly across every finitely complete base the framework admits. A compositional model built in presheaves on a site, or sheaves on
a space, inherits the product structure of its directed shapes with no recomputation and no appeal to
global elements. The one thing it does \emph{not} inherit for free is a universal property \emph{among
comonoids}: there the tensor is a monoidal, non-cartesian product (\S\ref{sec:notproduct}), and the
categorical product lives on the functor side $\Cat(\Ee)$. Keeping those two apart is the main honest
correction this note makes to the folklore.
