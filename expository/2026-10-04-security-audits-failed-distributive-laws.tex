\begin{abstract}
Real-world smart-contract exploits repeatedly take the same shape: two or more components,
each provably correct in isolation, produce a global failure when composed. This is precisely
the picture that distributive-law theory names: composition of effect structures is coherent
only when a distributive law exists, and its absence is an obstruction. We frame three recurring
audit failure classes --- state divergence, temporal/cross-callback re-entrancy, and
self-composition via iteration --- as three \emph{different} categorical laws breaking, work a
clean toy example (the Provenance Blockchain state-divergence bug) as a failed distributive law
between two component monads, and connect the temporal case to an existing machine-checked
obstruction result. The state-divergence class alone is now populated by five public witnesses
across four sub-families, ranging from a re-entrancy accounting slip to a \$290M cross-chain
bridge exploit --- the \emph{same} missing distributive law each time, differing only in where the
two non-commuting channels sit. The point is not a new theorem but a unifying frame with
predictive and economic bite: one categorical defect, its blast radius spanning roughly two orders
of magnitude of loss across the witnesses on record.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: local correctness is not compositional correctness}

The dominant failure mode in audited smart-contract systems is not a broken component. It is
correct components that break \emph{each other}. Trail of Bits, describing the Provenance
Blockchain bug discussed below, put it in exactly these words: ``two independently-correct
components \ldots\ created a security failure through incorrect interaction.''

This is the laxator-as-obstruction picture. When we compose two effectful structures $S$ and
$T$ --- two state monads, two ledgers, two order-of-effect disciplines --- the composite $S\circ
T$ is a well-behaved structure again \emph{only if} a distributive law
\[
  \delta : S\,T \;\Longrightarrow\; T\,S
\]
exists and satisfies its coherence conditions. The distributive law is exactly the datum that
lets the two structures interleave without contradiction. A missing or non-coherent $\delta$ is
not a cosmetic gap: it is the obstruction, and downstream it is the bug. Local correctness of
$S$ and of $T$ says nothing about whether $\delta$ exists.

We claim the recurring audit patterns are indexed by \emph{which} law fails, and this indexing
is the contribution --- the audit literature carries all three as separate ``patterns'' with no
unifying frame:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*),leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item \textbf{State divergence} --- the distributive law between two component monads fails;
        two representations of one logical value drift apart under composition.
  \item \textbf{Temporal / cross-callback misalignment} --- classical re-entrancy; the
        order-of-effects obstruction living in a second cohomology group.
  \item \textbf{Self-composition via iteration} --- one locally-correct operation composed with
        itself in a loop; an interchange/associativity failure of the iterate.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The worked toy example: state divergence as a failed distributive law}

\paragraph{The bug (Provenance Blockchain, Trail of Bits, 2026-08-25).}
Two modules each hold a notion of token supply. The \emph{Marker} module carries a cached
\texttt{supply} field; the \emph{Bank} module carries the live, authoritative supply. Each module
is correct on its own terms. An authorization check compares the two supplies to decide whether a
privileged action is permitted. When the cached and the live values both read as $0$ --- which the
composed system allows --- the check degenerates to \texttt{0 == 0} $\ra$ \texttt{true},
granting unauthorized admin/mint/withdraw. Two correct components, one trivial bypass under
composition.

\paragraph{The categorical model.}
Let $L$ be the logical value ``token supply.'' Two components each present a monadic view of an
$L$-valued state:
\[
  S = (-\,)\times L_{\mathrm{cache}} \quad(\text{Marker's stale view}),
  \qquad
  T = (-\,)\times L_{\mathrm{live}} \quad(\text{Bank's authoritative view}),
\]
each a writer/state monad over its own copy of $L$. Composing the two modules means forming
$S\circ T$ and reading a single logical supply out of it. Coherence --- ``the two views agree on
the one value they both denote'' --- is exactly a distributive law
\[
  \delta : S\,T \Longrightarrow T\,S
\]
whose unit-square commutes: the value read through the cache equals the value read through the
authoritative store. The intended invariant $L_{\mathrm{cache}} = L_{\mathrm{live}}$ is the
statement that $\delta$ is well-defined and coherent.

The bug is that no such coherent $\delta$ exists. The cache and the store are updated on
different code paths, so the square
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
  L_{\mathrm{cache}} & \xrightarrow{\ \text{compose}\ } & L_{\mathrm{live}}\\
\end{array}
\]
fails to commute after certain interleavings, and the two representations diverge. The
authorization predicate $\varphi(L_{\mathrm{cache}}, L_{\mathrm{live}})$ was written assuming
coherence; under divergence it is evaluated off its intended domain, where
$L_{\mathrm{cache}} = L_{\mathrm{live}} = 0$ makes it fire spuriously. The divergence
\emph{is} the failed distributive law; the \texttt{0 == 0} bypass is what the missing $\delta$
looks like at the call site.

\begin{remark}
The model is deliberately light. What a category theorist should see is a non-existent
distributive law between two monads over a shared object $L$; what a security engineer should
see is their own bug --- ``two sources of truth for one value, kept in sync by convention rather
than by construction.'' The two readings are the same statement.
\end{remark}

\section{The taxonomy: three failure shapes, three laws}

\paragraph{(1) State divergence $=$ failed distributive law between two monads.}
The shape of Section~2, and the best-populated: five public witnesses across four sub-families,
each a pair of locally-correct components whose composite admits no coherent distributive law
between two representations of one logical value. The sub-families are distinguished by
\emph{what} the two representations are --- two modules, two entry points, two channels onto one
pot, or two cross-chain channels (one that verifies, one that settles) --- but the missing
$\delta$ is the same in each. The last sub-family is where the frame earns its keep: the same
defect that yields an eight-figure accounting slip yields, at the bridge, the largest single loss
on the list.

\emph{(1a) Module/module.} Two distinct modules each carry a view of the shared value. The
Provenance toy of Section~2 is the clean case; the same shape drives the Uniswap~v4 hook
accounting bug in which the \texttt{PoolManager}'s delta-zero settlement check and a hook's
internal ledger are each locally sound but jointly unsound --- the \textbf{Bunni exploit},
\$8.4M. Two ledgers, no distributive law between them.

\emph{(1b) Entry-point/entry-point.} The two representations need not live in two modules: they
can be two entry points into \emph{one} component. In the \textbf{Solv Protocol BRO Vault}
exploit (2026-03, $\approx$\$2.7M), an ERC-3525 semi-fungible vault runs an external deposit-mint
(\texttt{onERC721Received}) and an internal value-rebalance
(\texttt{ERC3525TransferHelper.doTransfer}) as two operations on the same underlying state
(\texttt{mintedSupply} versus \texttt{collateralValue}). Both funnel through the same callback
with no processed-flag guard, so rebalance-then-mint interleaves with deposit-then-mint and the
composite is ill-defined; the attacker looped transfer$\ra$mint 22 times, inflating 135 BRO into
$\approx$567M BRO. Here the second ``operation'' is an internal helper re-entry, not an external
attacker-contract callback --- so the missing $\delta$ lives between two entry points into one
component, widening the shape from ``two modules'' to ``two entry points.''

\emph{(1c) Channel/channel.} Sharper still: the two representations are two \emph{channels} onto
one pot. In the \textbf{Venus Protocol donation attack} (2026-03, $\approx$\$14.9M), a
Compound-fork lending market reads its exchange rate from \texttt{getCashPrior()} --- the raw
ERC20 \texttt{balanceOf} of the vToken contract --- rather than from an internally-tracked mint
ledger. The attacker bypassed \texttt{mint()} entirely and \texttt{transfer}-ed tokens straight
into the contract: the cash balance rose but the minted-share \texttt{totalSupply} did not,
inflating the exchange rate $3.81\times$ and slipping past a supply cap that is checked only
against mint-pathway volume. Looping borrow$\ra$swap$\ra$donate$\ra$borrow extracted \$14.9M and
left \$2.18M of bad debt. The two non-commuting channels are a public, permissionless ERC20
transfer and the protocol's own accounting channel onto the same pot; the distributive law is
assumed to hold only because everyone is expected to route through \texttt{mint()}, and nothing
enforces it. This is the general shape of ERC-4626-style ``donation''/share-inflation attacks.
Tellingly, the canonical fix --- read a state variable mutated only at protocol entry points
instead of the live balance --- forces the two channels to coincide \emph{by construction}: it
literally makes the distributive law hold. The same channel/channel shape in its oracle flavour is
the \textbf{YieldBlox/Blend} incident on Stellar ($\approx$\$10M), where a thin-liquidity VWAP
price feed (a single \$1$\ra$\$106 trade) is one channel onto a collateral value and the
protocol's own accounting is the other; the price-read and the accounting update fail to commute,
and the inflated read is again the value taken off its intended domain.

\emph{(1d) Bridge: verification-channel vs settlement-channel.} The sharpest sub-family, and the
costliest: the two channels sit on \emph{different chains}. A cross-chain transfer has a
\emph{settlement} channel --- the source-chain burn events, sequenced by a nonce $n$, recording
what actually happened --- and a \emph{verification} channel --- an off-chain attestation telling
the destination chain what to mint. Model them as two monads $\Sigma$ (settlement, the burn
sequence) and $V$ (verification, the attestation) over one logical transfer; the destination mint
reads a value out of the composite $V\circ\Sigma$, an \emph{attested settlement}. Coherence ---
``every attestation corresponds to a real burn at the matching nonce'' --- is exactly a
distributive law
\[
  \delta : V\,\Sigma \;\Longrightarrow\; \Sigma\,V
\]
whose square commutes at the nonce index. In the \textbf{KelpDAO/LayerZero} exploit (2026-04-18,
$\approx$\$290M, per public Rekt/Chainalysis postmortems), a single non-redundant, zero-threshold
Decentralised Verifier Network attested a forged burn message at nonce $308$ while the true
source-chain burn sequence stood at nonce $307$; the destination endpoint performed only hash
validation against the (compromised) attestation. The verification channel thus reported a
settlement the settlement channel never produced --- $\delta$ fails at exactly that nonce --- and
value was minted against a burn that never happened. Structurally the single zero-threshold DVN
\emph{is} the absence of any construction forcing $\delta$: nothing makes $V$ track $\Sigma$. The
standing remedy --- redundant verifiers with a nonzero attestation threshold --- forces $V$ to
track $\Sigma$ \emph{by construction}, exactly as the Venus fix forced its two channels to
coincide. The two remedies are the same categorical move (make $\delta$ hold by construction, not
by assumption) at two scales: a \$14.9M lending market and a \$290M bridge.

\paragraph{(2) Temporal / cross-callback misalignment $=$ re-entrancy with an $H^2$ obstruction.}
Classical re-entrancy is an order-of-effects failure of a single lax structure composed across a
callback boundary: an external call re-enters before the caller's effects have settled, so the
intended interleaving order is violated. This is the case the author has already pinned down
formally. The obstruction is a cocycle $[\omega]\in H^2$, and the composition is safe exactly
when $[\omega]=\varepsilon$, the coboundary/identity class; this equivalence is machine-checked
(\textsf{Reentrancy.lean}, $[\omega]=\varepsilon$, with $H^2\cong\FF$). The Uniswap~v4 hook
patterns of temporal state misalignment across \texttt{beforeSwap}/\texttt{afterSwap}, and of
cross-callback shared-state mutation across pools, are instances of this same failed distributive
law of order.

\paragraph{(3) Self-composition via iteration $=$ interchange/associativity failure.}
The subtlest shape: a \emph{single} locally-correct operation composed with \emph{itself} through
a loop. In the Slither issue \#940 (Hashmasks) pattern, \texttt{mintNFT} loops over
\texttt{\_safeMint}; each iteration individually respects checks-effects-interactions, but a
reentrant call mid-loop bypasses the \texttt{MAX\_NFT\_SUPPLY} check that only fires once at
function entry. Structurally the obstruction lives in the iterate $T\circ T\circ\cdots$ of one
operation, not between two distinct operations. This is the security-domain shadow of a phenomenon
already visible in the author's wreath/plethysm work: self-composition $M\circ M$ can carry a
symmetry (a stabilizer / block-swap) that no single application exhibits, and composition survives
self-plethysm only when flatness was present to begin with. A check that holds per step but not
under the fold is a locally-flat operation whose iterate is non-flat.

\begin{remark}[What is and is not claimed]
This note is a framing, graded as expository. No new theorem is proved here. The one formally
verified result invoked is the pre-existing re-entrancy obstruction ($[\omega]=\varepsilon$,
machine-checked). The categorical models of shapes (1) and (3) are structural descriptions, not
proofs. Related dynamical-systems ``under-generation-under-composition'' analogies exist in the
literature but use no category theory and are cited elsewhere only as analogy.
\end{remark}

\section{Why this matters for the grant}

Compositional correctness has direct economic consequences, and the consequences scale with the
same defect. The Bunni exploit is \$8.4M attached to a single missing distributive law between two
ledgers; the Venus donation attack is \$14.9M attached to the same failure between two channels
onto one pot; the KelpDAO/LayerZero bridge exploit is $\approx$\$290M attached to that \emph{same}
failure between a verification channel and a settlement channel across two chains. The categorical
defect does not change from the smallest witness to the largest --- it is $\delta$ absent in every
case --- but the blast radius grows by more than an order of magnitude as the two non-commuting
channels move from inside one module to across a bridge. The audit industry already
\emph{discovers} these bugs one at a time; what it lacks is a frame that says why they recur, where
to look next, and why the same slip is worth thousands in one place and hundreds of millions in
another.

The distributive-law frame supplies three things the grant narrative wants:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{A unification.} Three ostensibly unrelated exploit classes are three instances of
        one phenomenon --- composition failing for lack of a coherence datum --- distinguished
        only by \emph{where} the datum should live (between two monads; in the order of one lax
        structure; in the iterate of one operation).
  \item \textbf{Prediction.} The frame tells an auditor what to hunt for: two sources of truth
        for one value --- two modules, two entry points, two channels onto one pot, or a
        verification channel and a settlement channel across a bridge (look for
        the distributive law); a callback boundary (look for the $H^2$
        class); a loop over an externally-observable operation (look for the non-flat iterate).
        The taxonomy turns ad hoc pattern-matching into a checklist indexed by categorical
        structure.
  \item \textbf{A checkable criterion.} For the temporal case the criterion is already sharp and
        machine-checked: the composition is safe if and only if $[\omega]=\varepsilon$. This is
        the template for the other two shapes --- a compositional-correctness certificate that a
        verification tool could in principle discharge, rather than a code smell a human hopes to
        notice.
\end{itemize}

In short: local correctness is cheap and compositional correctness is expensive precisely because
the latter is a statement about a distributive law, not about any component. The framework names
that law, predicts its absence, and --- in the case already formalized --- decides it.
