\title[What the weld's non-associativity is, and is not]
{What the weld's non-associativity is --- and is \emph{not}:\\
a triangulation across four obstruction theories,\\
and the idempotent normalizer that receives it}

\author{MacBeth}
\address{Kodamai}
\date{19--20 October 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
The worker/harness \emph{weld} --- the Zappa--Sz\'ep (orchestration) composition of directed
containers, the program's central tool for \emph{when} compositions exist --- is non-associative in
general. We ask which categorical invariant measures that non-associativity, and answer in two
movements. First, by \emph{negative triangulation}: four \emph{named} candidate maps are refuted,
each in a structurally distinct way. The renormalization idempotent $k=n\comp m$ of
Di~Lavore--Rom\'an--Sz\'ekely is not a $2$-cocycle (no receptacle: the cocycle condition \emph{is}
associativity), not a Karoubi/Cauchy splitting obstruction (vacuous receptacle: $k$ already splits),
and not a lax distributive law of Schoen/Tholen (wrong locus: laxness is a fixed-direction inequality
on a coherence cell, whereas the associator defect compares two \emph{order-incomparable} normalized
distributions). Second, and positively: the surviving invariant is received \emph{exactly} by an
idempotent \emph{normalizer} $K$ --- a hom-operator obeying a left- but not right-absorption law.
Writing $f\sh g:=K(f\cdot g)$, associativity of $\sh$ is equivalent to $K$ being two-sided absorbing,
so the handedness obstruction is genuine and non-vacuous; and it is provably \emph{operator-level},
never \emph{element-level}, which refutes the fourth named map --- the near-semiring's
one-sided-absorbing \emph{zero}. The normalized-kernel magmoid $\Norm$ is an instance, and a
machine-checked finite model certifies the construction axiom-free. The originality of the surviving
invariant is reported honestly, as the current state of a literature sweep, not as a theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: the weld and its non-associativity}

Throughout, $\Poly$ is the category of polynomial functors on $\Set$ with the composition monoidal
product $\lhd$; a comonoid for $\lhd$ is a \emph{polynomial comonad}, equivalently a small category.
For a set $S$ we write $\Store{S}=S\yon{}^{S}$ (notation $\yon{}$ for the representable) for the
\emph{store} comonad on $S$, the directed container of the full state space on $S$. All composites
are written in diagrammatic order: $f\comp g$ (equivalently $f\cdot g$) means ``$f$ then $g$'', and
vertical composition of natural transformations is likewise diagrammatic.

\begin{definition}[The weld]
Given two directed containers presented by store comonads $\Store{S}$ (the \emph{worker} state) and
$\Store{T}$ (the \emph{harness} state), their \emph{weld} is the orchestration composite
\[
  B \;=\; \Store{S}\lhd\Store{T},
\]
the Zappa--Sz\'ep product that interleaves worker and harness transitions. The weld is the program's
canonical answer to ``when does a composite of two stateful directed containers exist?'': the
Zappa--Sz\'ep datum is exactly the mutual-action data that makes the interleaving well defined.
\end{definition}

The weld is \emph{not} associative in general. This is not a defect of our encoding; it is
intrinsic. The sharpest extant witness is probabilistic. Di~Lavore, Rom\'an and Sz\'ekely
(``Normalized Probabilistic Semantics is Not Associative'', arXiv:2510.01131) show that
\emph{normalization} of substochastic kernels is a \emph{sesquilaw}, not a distributive law, and that
the induced Kleisli-style composition of normalized kernels is a genuine \emph{magmoid} --- a
category minus associativity.

\begin{definition}[Sesquilaw and the renormalization idempotent, after arXiv:2510.01131]
\label{def:sesquilaw}
A \emph{sesquilaw} $(S,T,m,n)$ consists of a distributive law $m\colon ST\Rightarrow TS$ together
with an \emph{almost-distributive} law $n\colon TS\Rightarrow ST$ satisfying all four
distributive-law axioms \emph{except} multiplicativity, which holds only up to the natural
transformation
\[
  k \;=\; n\comp m \;\colon\; TS \Rightarrow TS
  \qquad\text{(the ``renormalization'' of Lemma 3.5).}
\]
For normalization one takes $T=D$ (distribution) and $S=M$ (maybe), so $TS=DM$ and $ST=MD$; then
$n=(-)^{\circ}\colon DM\Rightarrow MD$ is normalization and $m=(-)^{\bullet}\colon MD\Rightarrow DM$
is the subdistribution inclusion. The induced magmoid $\Norm$ of normalized kernels $X\to MDY$ is a
category minus associativity; Proposition~4.9 of loc.\ cit.\ isolates the triples that do associate.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
We treat $k=n\comp m$ as a faithful, finite-dimensional stand-in for the weld's associativity defect.
The identification is justified computationally below (\S\ref{sec:instance}, reproducing
arXiv:2510.01131's Prop.~4.9 purity cases exactly) and at the level of principle by the fact that
both objects are instances of ``an idempotent measuring departure from a distributive law.'' The
question of this note is: \emph{which categorical invariant is $k$ (equivalently, the weld's
non-associativity)?}
\end{remark}

\section{The method: name the map, then decide}
\label{sec:method}

We adopt one discipline, learned the hard way across a month of cross-domain bridge-hopes that died
on inspection.

\begin{remark}[Naming the map buys decidability, not survival]
\label{rem:method}
A candidate obstruction home ranked highly because its \emph{vocabulary} matches (``cocycle'',
``splitting'', ``Yang--Baxter'') has earned nothing until a \emph{structure map} is exhibited between
the \emph{specific} objects at hand --- not the words. ``This sounds like it should connect'' is a
prior of roughly zero. The payoff of naming a candidate map is not that it survives; it is that the
question becomes \emph{decidable}. Our record on resonance-ranked leads is $0/n$; our record on
\emph{named}-map leads is: all decided, crisply, one way or the other.
\end{remark}

The note has two movements. In the first (\S\S\ref{sec:exists}--\ref{sec:laxdl}) we record the one
named map that lands (the associative corner) and refute three of the four candidate obstruction
homes. In the second (\S\S\ref{sec:home}--\ref{sec:missed}) we exhibit the structure that \emph{does}
receive the invariant, and show that building it refutes the fourth candidate as a by-product. Each verdict is one of three epistemic kinds, kept
strictly separate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{proved} --- a registered result of the program (the four verdicts and the positive
        construction; several carry Lean-verified leaves);
  \item \textbf{evidence} --- a literature-sweep conclusion, \emph{not} a theorem (the
        handedness-originality claim, \S\ref{sec:missed});
  \item \textbf{computed} --- a corroborating but non-formalized numerical fact (so flagged in place).
\end{itemize}

\section{The associative corner: the weld is a regular bicomodule (EXISTS)}
\label{sec:exists}

Before the refutations, we record the one named map that \emph{does} land --- so the reader sees the
method is not merely destructive, and so the associative regime is kept firmly separate from the
defect we chase.

\begin{theorem}[Spivak--Garner--Fairbanks, ``Functorial Aggregation'', arXiv:2111.10968,
Prop.~3.9(5) + Thm.~4.28]
\label{thm:sgf}
For polynomial comonoids (equivalently small categories) $c$ and $d$, there is an equivalence
\[
  \Comod(\Poly)(c,d) \;\simeq\; \PRA(c\text{-}\Set,\,d\text{-}\Set)
\]
between $(c,d)$-bicomodules in $\Poly$ and parametric right adjoint functors
$c\text{-}\Set\to d\text{-}\Set$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[weld is a regular bicomodule; registry \texttt{weld-is-regular-bicomodule}, \textbf{proved}]
\label{prop:weld-bicomodule}
The weld $B=\Store{S}\lhd\Store{T}$ is the \emph{regular} $(\Store{T},\Store{S})$-bicomodule. Under
Theorem~\ref{thm:sgf} it represents the prafunctor
\[
  \bar F_B(V)\;=\;(T\times V)^{S}\;=\;T^{S}\cdot V^{S},
\]
whose slice (aggregation) factorization keeps the power $(-)^{S}$ as connected-limit content and
aggregates over the branch-map set $T^{S}$ by a coproduct $\Sigma$.
\end{proposition}

This result is peer-reviewed within the program (Rick's referee pass, rounds~1 and~2) and carries a
Lean-verified leaf checking the five regular-bicomodule coaction axioms.

\begin{remark}[The bicomodule is associative; keep it separate from the defect]
\label{rem:separate}
Crucially, the bicomodule/PRA picture of Proposition~\ref{prop:weld-bicomodule} lives in the
\emph{associative} regime. The weld, \emph{once its Zappa--Sz\'ep data make it well defined}, is a
genuine comodule, and the cohomological class $[\omega]\in H^{2}$ attached to it (elsewhere in the
program) is a \emph{splitting} obstruction of an \emph{already-associative} weld, not a measure of
non-associativity. The non-associativity we chase here is the Kleisli-side sesquilaw defect --- the
\emph{departure} from that associative regime. This separation is the hinge of the whole note.
\end{remark}

\section{Refutation I: \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k} is not a 2-cocycle (no receptacle)}
\label{sec:cocycle}

\begin{claim}[registry: refuted; cf.\ \texttt{h2-weld-class-is-splitting-not-associativity}]
\label{clm:cocycle}
The renormalization idempotent $k=n\comp m$ is \emph{not} a $2$-cocycle. The refutation is not that
the putative class vanishes; it is that the \emph{receptacle does not exist}.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}[Why the receptacle is empty]
A $2$-cocycle condition, in every incarnation (group cohomology, the Sweedler/Hopf cocycle, the
obstruction cocycle of an extension), \emph{is} an associativity equation: the cocycle identity is
precisely the statement that two bracketings of a triple agree. Thus a $2$-cocycle presupposes an
associative multiplication whose coherence it then measures. A genuinely non-associative magmad has
no associative multiplication to begin with, hence no cocycle at all --- there is nothing for a class
to be a class \emph{of}. One cannot carry the non-associativity as a cohomology class because the
apparatus that would carry it is \emph{defined by} associativity. (Confirmed independently by
Rom\'an's magmads: associativity-failure is a \emph{magma} phenomenon, not a cocycle phenomenon.)
\end{proof}

This is a \emph{well-posed negative of the first kind}: the map was named, checked, and the home
turned out to be empty. Its locus is the \emph{cochain} (single-morphism) level.

\section{Refutation II: \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k} is not a Karoubi splitting obstruction (vacuous receptacle)}
\label{sec:karoubi}

Here the receptacle \emph{exists}, and that makes the negative subtler and, we think, more
instructive.

\begin{lemma}[$k$ lives in an associative hom-set and is idempotent]
\label{lem:k-idem}
$n\colon DM\Rightarrow MD$ and $m\colon MD\Rightarrow DM$ are morphisms in the functor category
$[\Set,\Set]$, whose composition (vertical composition of natural transformations) is strictly
associative. Hence $k=n\comp m\in\End_{[\Set,\Set]}(DM)$, and ``is $k$ idempotent?'' and ``does $k$
split?'' are both well posed. Moreover $k$ is idempotent: from the \emph{section identity}
$m\comp n=\id_{MD}$ (a normalized distribution, included as a subdistribution, already has unit mass,
so renormalizing is the identity),
\[
  k\comp k \;=\; (n\comp m)\comp(n\comp m)
           \;=\; n\comp(m\comp n)\comp m
           \;=\; n\comp \id_{MD}\comp m
           \;=\; n\comp m \;=\; k.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[sesquilaw $k$ is a split idempotent; registry \texttt{sesquilaw-magmoid-karoubi}, \textbf{proved}]
\label{thm:split}
$k=n\comp m$ is a \emph{split} idempotent, split by
\[
  DM \xrightarrow{\;n\;} MD \xrightarrow{\;m\;} DM,
  \qquad m\comp n=\id_{MD},\qquad n\comp m=k,
\]
with splitting object the magmad carrier $MD$ itself. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the Karoubi/Cauchy (idempotent) completion is a \emph{no-op} on $k$ --- $k$ already lies in
        the image of $[\Set,\Set]\hookrightarrow\Kar([\Set,\Set])$, so there is no ``missing
        splitting'';
  \item there is \emph{no splitting obstruction} to carry the non-associativity: the feared failure
        (that $k$ does not split) is empty.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A split idempotent is exactly a factorization $e=s\comp r$ with $r\comp s=\id$ through the image;
Lemma~\ref{lem:k-idem} exhibits $r=n$, $s=m$, with $m\comp n=\id_{MD}$ and $n\comp m=k$. The
splitting object $MD$ is already present in $[\Set,\Set]$, so idempotent completion adds nothing for
$k$. Both consequences follow.
\end{proof}

What makes this decisive is the meta-lemma it instantiates.

\begin{lemma}[idempotent completion preserves associativity]
\label{lem:meta}
For any category $\mathcal{D}$, the Karoubi/Cauchy completion $\Kar(\mathcal{D})$ is a
\emph{category}: its composition is associative (Borceux I, \S6.5; nLab, ``idempotent completion'').
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[splitting can never measure non-associativity]
\label{cor:orthogonal}
Splitting idempotents in an associative $\mathcal{D}$ yields only an \emph{associative} category.
Hence no ``split some idempotents in an associative core'' construction can \emph{produce} a
non-associative composition; dually, the failure of a composition to be associative can never be
\emph{identified with} the failure of an idempotent to split, because the latter is repaired by a
construction (Lemma~\ref{lem:meta}) that cannot repair the former.
\end{corollary}

Corollary~\ref{cor:orthogonal} is why this candidate was doomed \emph{before} any computation: even
had $k$ failed to split, completing to repair it would land in an associative category, so the
repaired object could not witness the magmoid's genuine non-associativity. This is a
\emph{well-posed negative of the second kind}: the home exists but carries no obstruction content.
Its locus is the \emph{single idempotent morphism}.

\subsection*{The Karoubi vacuity is generic, not weld-specific}
\label{sec:generic}

An independent source, from a problem with no connection to the weld, confirms that this vacuity is a
general feature of the weak-distributive-law family rather than an artifact of our particular $k$.
B\"ohm--Lack--Street (``Idempotent Splittings, Colimit Completion, and Weak Aspects of the Theory of
Monads'', arXiv:1102.4931) show that weak monad notions --- e.g.\ \emph{demimonads}, which drop the
unit law but keep associativity $\beta\cdot T\beta=\beta\cdot\mu$ --- reduce systematically to
\emph{strict} structure inside the Cauchy-completed $Q_{*}\mathcal{K}$: an independent, pre-weld
proof that Cauchy/Karoubi completion \emph{absorbs} one-sided weakenings of the monad laws rather
than \emph{detecting} them, exactly Corollary~\ref{cor:orthogonal} reached from a wholly different
problem. This upgrades the verdict from ``true for our $k$'' to ``structurally true for the family''.
(A second, probabilistic echo --- that idempotent Markov kernels generically split --- is recorded in
the program's reading log at summary grade, flagged for a deep read before it is cited.)

\section{Refutation III: \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k} is not a lax distributive law (wrong locus)}
\label{sec:laxdl}

The one technical (non-vocabulary) lead for a prior home of one-sidedness is \emph{lax distributive
laws by inequality} --- Schoen, ``Lax Liftings and Lax Distributive Laws'' (arXiv:2307.16552, ACT
2022), and the companion $(T,V)$-program of Tholen, ``Lax Distributive Laws for Topology I/II''
(arXiv:1603.06251, 1605.04489). We decide it, and it is the subtlest negative: the home exists, is
inhabited, and is genuinely one-sided --- but one-sided \emph{about the wrong thing}.

\begin{definition}[lax distributive law, after Schoen]
Let $P$ be a monad on $\Set$ and $F$ an \emph{endofunctor} (no algebraic structure). A \emph{lax
distributive law} is a transformation $\lambda_{X}\colon FP(X)\to PF(X)$ subject to three
\emph{one-directional inequalities} in the inclusion order $\subseteq$ of relations (here $\Set$ is
order-enriched to $\Rel$, with $\Rel(X,Y)=P(X\times Y)$ a poset under $\subseteq$): lax naturality,
and lax compatibility with the unit $\eta$ and multiplication $\mu$ of $P$ --- the last two pointing
in \emph{opposite} directions by design. The laxified cells are the coherences of the \emph{one}
monad $P$; $F$ is a bare functor and contributes none.
\end{definition}

The one-sidedness of a lax distributive law is a \emph{fixed-direction order inequality on a
coherence cell}. The weld's one-sidedness is of a different grammar, and the mismatch is a theorem,
not an impression.

\begin{claim}[registry: refuted; \texttt{handedness-not-lax-DL} \textbf{proved}]
\label{clm:laxdl}
The weld's handedness is not an instance of, and is not faithfully receivable by, a Schoen/Tholen lax
distributive law.
\end{claim}

We give two independent reasons. The first is bookkeeping; the second is the conceptual kill.

\emph{Variance.} Schoen's template needs a triple $(P\text{ monad},\,F\text{ bare functor},\,
\lambda\colon FP\to PF)$; the weld supplies $(D,M,m,n,k)$. Only two variance assignments type-check.
With $P=M,\ F=D,\ \lambda=n\colon DM\to MD$, the map $n$ does carry the defect --- but its failing
coherence is $D$-multiplicativity, a coherence with the multiplication of $T=D$, which in Schoen's
template (where $F=D$ is a \emph{bare} functor) is \emph{invisible}; the three laxified cells are all
about $P=M$. With $P=D,\ F=M$, the only map of the right variance is the \emph{strict} law $m$, which
carries no defect; the defect-carrying $n$ has the wrong variance. In both assignments the handedness
defect is not among the laxifiable cells. No structure map exists; the same analysis applies verbatim
to Tholen (same coherence-cell laxness, quantale-enriched).

\emph{The discriminator.} The conceptual kill is one sentence and is independent of the variance
bookkeeping.

\begin{lemma}[elementary]
\label{lem:incomparable}
If $\mu\le\nu$ pointwise for probability measures $\mu,\nu$ on a set $Y$ (total mass
$\sum\mu=\sum\nu=1$), then $\mu=\nu$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\nu-\mu\ge 0$ pointwise and $\sum(\nu-\mu)=1-1=0$; a nonnegative function with zero total is zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[handedness is not a laxness]
\label{prop:not-lax}
The associator defect of $\Norm$ is not expressible as a fixed-direction lax inequality in the
canonical (pointwise sub-probability) order-enrichment --- the order under which the Schoen/Tholen
framework applies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a triple $(f,g,h)$ whose composite succeeds, both bracketings $(f\sh g)\sh h$ and $f\sh(g\sh h)$
lie in $\Norm$'s hom-set, i.e.\ are \emph{normalized} distributions of total mass $1$ (the outer
normalization $(-)^{\circ}$; cf.\ Definition~\ref{def:sesquilaw}). A Schoen/Tholen laxness would
require a \emph{single fixed direction} --- $(f\sh g)\sh h\le f\sh(g\sh h)$ for all triples, or the
reverse for all triples. But whenever the two bracketings differ, Lemma~\ref{lem:incomparable}
forbids them from being $\le$-comparable; and non-associativity is precisely the regime where they
differ. So neither fixed direction can hold: the defect lives strictly between $\le$-incomparable
elements and is not a laxness.
\end{proof}

The honest near-miss, dispatched. There \emph{is} a genuine lax cell in the $DM$ order, namely
$\id_{DM}\le k$ pointwise (normalization raises mass: $k(\mu)(y)=\mu(y)/|\mu|\ge\mu(y)$ since
$|\mu|\le 1$). But that cell is a statement about $D$'s renormalization, not one of Schoen's
$P=M$-coherences, and it is not \emph{what the handedness is}. Handedness is the statement ``$k$
absorbs in $\sh$'s left slot but not the right'' --- a fact about $\sh$ as a binary operation ---
and Proposition~\ref{prop:not-lax} shows that fact cannot be any fixed-direction $\le$. A lax cell
existing \emph{somewhere} in the ambient order does not make the \emph{associator defect} a laxness.

This is a \emph{well-posed negative of the third kind}: the home exists and is one-sided, but its
one-sidedness is a fixed-direction $\le$ on a \emph{coherence cell} (one monad), whereas handedness
is an asymmetry of the \emph{argument slots} of the induced binary operation (two monads), with
$\le$-incomparable outputs. The locus is the \emph{coherence 2-cell}.

\begin{remark}[honest scope of the Tholen half]
Schoen arXiv:2307.16552 is deep-read; Tholen arXiv:1603.06251/1605.04489 is not directly read, and
the no-match for Tholen rests on the \emph{family-level} fact that its laxness is quantale-order
laxness of coherence cells --- the same locus Proposition~\ref{prop:not-lax} rules out --- not on any
fine Tholen detail. Were a future read to surface a Tholen construction laxifying an \emph{argument
slot} of a composition rather than a coherence cell, this verdict should be revisited; we judge it
unlikely but mark it as the one unread dependency.
\end{remark}

Three named maps are now decided, of three distinct kinds --- no receptacle (cocycle), vacuous
receptacle (Karoubi), wrong-locus receptacle (lax-DL) --- and each probes a level (cochain,
idempotent morphism, coherence cell) at which, we will see, the invariant is simply not visible.

\section{The positive home: an idempotent normalizer}
\label{sec:home}

If the defect is neither a cocycle, nor a splitting obstruction, nor a laxness, what \emph{is} it?
The honest answer is concrete, non-cohomological, and lives one level up from every map so far ---
at the binary operation itself. We isolate the minimal abstract structure that carries it. Two terms
below, ``normalizer'' and ``one-sided absorbing idempotent'', are this note's own coinages, not
standard vocabulary; we define them from scratch.

Fix a category $(\mathcal{A},\cdot,1)$ with strictly associative composition --- think of the
honest, associative ambient (substochastic kernels under matrix product; or the Kleisli category
$\Kl(DM)$). Diagrammatic order: for $f\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)$, $g\in\mathcal{A}(Y,Z)$, $f\cdot g\in
\mathcal{A}(X,Z)$ is ``$f$ then $g$''.

\begin{definition}[normalizer, normalized magmoid]
\label{def:normalizer}
A \emph{normalizer} on $\mathcal{A}$ is a family of functions
$K=K_{X,Y}\colon\mathcal{A}(X,Y)\to\mathcal{A}(X,Y)$ such that
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsf{(K1)} $K$ is idempotent: $K(K(a))=K(a)$;
  \item \textsf{(U)} $K$ fixes identities: $K(1_{X})=1_{X}$;
  \item \LA{} \emph{left-absorption}: $K\big(K(x)\cdot c\big)=K(x\cdot c)$ for all composable $x,c$.
\end{itemize}
$K$ is \emph{two-sided} if additionally, for every $X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z\xrightarrow{h}W$
with $f,g,h$ in the image of $K$,
\begin{itemize}
  \item \RA{} \emph{right-absorption for} $\sh$: $K\big(f\cdot K(g\cdot h)\big)=K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$.
\end{itemize}
The \emph{normalized magmoid} $N_{K}$ has $\mathrm{Ob}\,N_{K}=\mathrm{Ob}\,\mathcal{A}$,
$N_{K}(X,Y):=\Img K_{X,Y}=\{a:K(a)=a\}$, composition $f\sh g:=K(f\cdot g)$, and units
$1^{\sh}_{X}:=1_{X}$. A structure \emph{with a one-sided absorbing idempotent} is
$(\mathcal{A},\cdot,K)$ with $K$ a normalizer that is \emph{not} two-sided.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[$N_K$ is a magmoid, and associativity is not assumed]
\label{rem:welldef}
$f\sh g=K(f\cdot g)\in\Img K$ by \textsf{(K1)}, and \textsf{(U)} gives units: for $f\in N_{K}$ (so
$K(f)=f$), $f\sh 1_{X}=K(f\cdot 1)=K(f)=f$ and $1_{X}\sh g=K(g)=g$. Associativity is \emph{not}
assumed --- that is the whole point.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the laws are not vacuous]
\label{rem:nonvac}
In \LA{} the argument $x$ ranges over \emph{all} of $\mathcal{A}(X,Y)$, in particular over products
$x=f\cdot g$ of $N_{K}$-morphisms, which are generally \emph{not} in $\Img K$ (composing two
normalized kernels de-normalizes). Where $K(x)\ne x$, \LA{} is a genuine constraint. Likewise \RA{}
concerns the \emph{un-normalized} product $y=g\cdot h$; it is \emph{not} the triviality
$K(f\cdot K(y))=K(f\cdot y)$ for $y\in\Img K$.
\end{remark}

\section{The characterization: associativity is two-sided absorption}
\label{sec:char}

Two theorems pin down $N_{K}$ completely. Both use only $\cdot$ and $K$; neither invokes any
2-categorical machinery.

\begin{theorem}[the left bracketing is clean]
\label{thm:A}
Let $K$ be a normalizer. Then for all composable $f,g,h\in N_{K}$,
\[
  (f\sh g)\sh h \;=\; K(f\cdot g\cdot h).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Expand and apply \LA{} with $x:=f\cdot g$:
\[
  (f\sh g)\sh h = K\big((f\sh g)\cdot h\big) = K\big(K(f\cdot g)\cdot h\big)
     \overset{\LA}{=} K\big((f\cdot g)\cdot h\big) = K(f\cdot g\cdot h),
\]
the last step by associativity of $\cdot$ in $\mathcal{A}$. The left bracketing normalizes once, at
the very end.
\end{proof}

The proof uses only \LA{} and associativity of $\cdot$ --- not \RA{}, not even the unit axiom. The
right bracketing does not simplify this way, and the gap between them is the whole content.

\begin{theorem}[associativity $\Longleftrightarrow$ two-sided absorption]
\label{thm:B}
Let $K$ be a normalizer. Then $N_{K}$ is associative \emph{if and only if} $K$ is two-sided, i.e.\
\RA{} holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$(\Leftarrow)$ Suppose \RA{}. For $f,g,h\in N_{K}$,
\[
  f\sh(g\sh h) = K\big(f\cdot(g\sh h)\big) = K\big(f\cdot K(g\cdot h)\big)
     \overset{\RA}{=} K(f\cdot g\cdot h) = (f\sh g)\sh h,
\]
the last equality by Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.
$(\Rightarrow)$ Suppose $\sh$ is associative. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} gives $(f\sh g)\sh
h=K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$, while $f\sh(g\sh h)=K(f\cdot K(g\cdot h))$ by definition; associativity equates
the two, which is precisely \RA{}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[handedness is a genuine, non-vacuous obstruction]
\label{cor:genuine}
If $K$ satisfies \LA{} but not \RA{}, then $N_{K}$ is a \emph{non-associative} magmoid, and its
associator defect $(f\sh g)\sh h$ versus $f\sh(g\sh h)$ equals exactly the defect $K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$
versus $K(f\cdot K(g\cdot h))$ of right-absorption --- nothing more and nothing less. Handedness
$=\LA\wedge\neg\RA$.
\end{corollary}

This is the sharp contrast with Refutation~II. The Karoubi receptacle was \emph{vacuous}: $k$ already
splits, so idempotent completion measures nothing (\S\ref{sec:karoubi}). Here the receptacle is
\emph{inhabited}: Corollary~\ref{cor:genuine} identifies a concrete, nonempty set of triples --- those
with $K(f\cdot K(g\cdot h))\ne K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$ --- that \emph{is} the obstruction.

\section{The instance: the normalized-kernel magmoid \texorpdfstring{$\Norm$}{Norm}}
\label{sec:instance}

The abstract home is inhabited by the very object that forced the question.

\begin{proposition}[$\Norm$ is a normalized magmoid]
\label{prop:norm-instance}
Let $\mathcal{A}$ be the category of \emph{substochastic kernels}: objects finite sets; morphisms
nonnegative $X\times Y$ matrices with row sums $\le 1$ (sub-Markov kernels, $=X\to DMY$ for the
subdistribution monad $DM$); composition $=$ matrix product. Let $K$ be \emph{row-normalization},
$K(M)_{xy}=M_{xy}/r_{x}$ with $r_{x}=\sum_{y'}M_{xy'}$, acting as the identity on zero-mass
(failure/$\bot$) rows. Then $\Img K$ is the set of normalized kernels $X\to MDY$ (each input a
probability distribution of mass $1$, or total failure $\bot$ of mass $0$), and $N_{K}=\Norm$ with
$f\sh g=K(f\cdot g)$ the Di~Lavore--Rom\'an--Sz\'ekely composition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[\LA{} holds for row-normalization]
\label{lem:norm-LA}
$K(K(x)\cdot c)=K(x\cdot c)$ for all composable substochastic $x,c$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On positive-mass rows $K(M)=D_{M}\cdot M$ with $D_{M}=\mathrm{diag}(1/r_{i})$ a \emph{positive
diagonal}. Two observations. \emph{(1) Positive-diagonal invariance:} for any positive diagonal
$P=\mathrm{diag}(p_{i})$, the matrix $(P\cdot M)_{ij}=p_{i}M_{ij}$ has row sum $p_{i}r_{i}$, so
$K(P\cdot M)_{ij}=p_{i}M_{ij}/(p_{i}r_{i})=M_{ij}/r_{i}=K(M)_{ij}$; hence $K(P\cdot M)=K(M)$. \emph{(2)}
By associativity of matrix product, $K(x)\cdot c=(D_{x}\cdot x)\cdot c=D_{x}\cdot(x\cdot c)$.
Therefore $K(K(x)\cdot c)=K\big(D_{x}\cdot(x\cdot c)\big)\overset{(1)}{=}K(x\cdot c)$. On zero-mass
rows $K(x)=x$ and both sides agree trivially.
\end{proof}

Note $K$ is \emph{not} left-multiplication by any \emph{fixed} element: $D_{x}$ depends on $x$. So
\LA{} is a genuine property here, not an identity forced by $k^{2}=k$ --- a point that becomes sharp
in \S\ref{sec:minimal}.

\begin{lemma}[\RA{} fails; exact witness]
\label{lem:norm-RA}
There exist $f,g,h\in\Norm$ with $K(f\cdot K(g\cdot h))\ne K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$, so by
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} $\Norm$ is non-associative.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Over state sets of sizes $1\to 2\to 3\to 2$, take the normalized kernels (every row of mass $1$ or
$0$)
\[
  f=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac23 & \tfrac13\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  g=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac23 & \tfrac13 & 0\\[2pt] 0 & \tfrac12 & \tfrac12\end{bmatrix},\qquad
  h=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\ \ (\text{middle state }1=\bot).
\]
Then $g\cdot h=\begin{bmatrix}\tfrac23&0\\0&\tfrac12\end{bmatrix}$ has rows of \emph{unequal} mass
$\tfrac23,\tfrac12$ (the failure $\bot$ at the middle state destroys different amounts of mass on
$g$'s two rows). Computing,
\[
  (f\sh g)\sh h = K(f\cdot g\cdot h) = \begin{bmatrix}\tfrac{8}{11}&\tfrac{3}{11}\end{bmatrix}
  \quad(\text{Theorem~\ref{thm:A}}),\qquad
  f\sh(g\sh h) = K\big(f\cdot K(g\cdot h)\big) = \begin{bmatrix}\tfrac23&\tfrac13\end{bmatrix}.
\]
These differ, so \RA{} fails.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is exactly Corollary~\ref{cor:genuine} made concrete. The inner normalization
$K(g\cdot h)=D_{g\cdot h}\cdot(g\cdot h)$ with $D_{g\cdot h}=\mathrm{diag}(\tfrac32,2)$ rescales the two
middle rows by \emph{different} factors $\tfrac32\ne 2$; sandwiched between $f$ and $g\cdot h$ it
reweights the contraction and is not removed by the outer $K$. Had the two middle rows lost equal
mass ($D_{g\cdot h}\propto I$) the defect would vanish --- which is why a $\Norm$-defect needs a middle
contraction of dimension $\ge 3$ (see \S\ref{sec:minimal}).

\begin{remark}[computational corroboration]
\label{rem:numerics}
A deterministic sweep (seed $20261019$; $20000$ trials) confirms the picture: \LA{} violations
$0/20000$; Theorem~\ref{thm:A} ($(f\sh g)\sh h=K(f\cdot g\cdot h)$) violations $0/20000$; generic
triples non-associative; and the two \emph{recovery} regimes predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:B} ---
$g,h$ stochastic so $D_{g\cdot h}=I$, and middle dimension $1$ --- associative $5000/5000$. This
reproduces arXiv:2510.01131's Proposition~4.9 purity cases exactly (each purity condition drives the
defect to $\sim 2\times10^{-16}$, its negation to $\sim 10^{-2}$). These are \emph{computed}
corroborations; the proved content is Theorems~\ref{thm:A}--\ref{thm:B} and
Lemmas~\ref{lem:norm-LA}--\ref{lem:norm-RA}.
\end{remark}

\section{Minimality: the home is operator-level, and the fourth map falls}
\label{sec:minimal}

The dream that launched this search named the classical template as a \emph{near-semiring with a
one-sided absorbing zero} $0$ ($0\cdot a=0$ imposed, $a\cdot 0=0$ not), with ``the strict zero replaced
by an idempotent''. The natural first guess for the home is then an \emph{element}: a distinguished
idempotent $\kappa$ with the normalizer $K(x)=\kappa\cdot x$. This is the fourth named map. We show it
\emph{provably cannot work} --- and so building the home refutes it.

Call a normalizer \emph{principal} if $K(x)=\kappa\cdot x$ for a fixed $\kappa\in\mathcal{A}$ with
$\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa$, and \emph{genuine} otherwise.

\begin{proposition}[principal normalizers are associativity-preserving]
\label{prop:principal}
If $K(x)=\kappa\cdot x$ with $\kappa\cdot\kappa=\kappa$, then \LA{} holds automatically \emph{and}
$N_{K}$ is \emph{associative} --- always. A distinguished idempotent \emph{element} cannot carry
handedness.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\LA{} is free: $K(K(x)\cdot c)=\kappa(\kappa x)c=(\kappa\kappa)xc=\kappa xc=K(x\cdot c)$. For
associativity, note $\kappa\cdot g=g$ for $g\in\Img K$ (if $g=\kappa g'$ then
$\kappa g=\kappa\kappa g'=\kappa g'=g$). Hence
\[
  f\sh(g\sh h)=\kappa\cdot f\cdot(\kappa\cdot g\cdot h)=(\kappa f)\cdot(\kappa g)\cdot h
     =(\kappa f)\cdot g\cdot h=\kappa\cdot f\cdot g\cdot h,
\]
while $(f\sh g)\sh h=\kappa\cdot(\kappa\cdot f\cdot g)\cdot h=(\kappa\kappa)\cdot f\cdot g\cdot h
=\kappa\cdot f\cdot g\cdot h$. The two agree.
\end{proof}

In Theorem~\ref{thm:B}'s terms a principal normalizer \emph{always} satisfies \RA{}: the inner
$\kappa$ in $f\cdot K(g\cdot h)=f\cdot\kappa\cdot(g\cdot h)$ meets $g$ on its left, and $\kappa g=g$,
so it is silently absorbed. The entire handedness phenomenon is \emph{impossible} for
$K=\kappa\cdot(-)$.

\begin{corollary}[handedness requires a genuine, non-principal normalizer]
\label{cor:nonprincipal}
If $N_{K}$ is non-associative then $K$ is \emph{not} $\kappa\cdot(-)$ for any fixed $\kappa$. The
row-normalization $K$ of $\Norm$ is genuine: it is \emph{non-linear} ($K(\lambda M)=K(M)\ne\lambda
K(M)$), whereas every principal $K$ is linear.
\end{corollary}

Proposition~\ref{prop:principal} is the fourth refutation, and it is the sharpest of the four because
it is not about a failed map into some theory but about the \emph{kind} of object handedness can
live on.

\begin{remark}[the triple-elimination, and why the near-semiring is only a shadow]
\label{rem:triple}
The four named maps probe four loci, and each is now seen to be \emph{inert} for handedness:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{cochain / single morphism} (cocycle, \S\ref{sec:cocycle}) --- the apparatus is defined
        by associativity;
  \item \emph{single idempotent morphism} (Karoubi, \S\ref{sec:karoubi}) --- $k$ is two-sided tame as
        a morphism ($k\comp n=n$ \emph{and} $m\comp k=m$), and splitting preserves associativity;
  \item \emph{coherence 2-cell} (lax-DL, \S\ref{sec:laxdl}) --- a fixed-direction $\le$ cannot
        separate $\le$-incomparable outputs;
  \item \emph{distinguished element} (near-semiring zero, this section) --- an idempotent
        \emph{element} is always two-sidedly absorbing (Prop.~\ref{prop:principal}), hence
        associativity-preserving.
\end{itemize}
Morphism, cell, and element loci are all inert; what remains is the \emph{argument slots of the
binary operation} $\sh$, and that is where Definition~\ref{def:normalizer} places $K$. The classical
near-semiring is a recognizable \emph{shadow} --- its one-sided distributivity has the right
\emph{shape} --- but it carries its one-sidedness in an \emph{element} (the zero) over extra data
(the $+$ that $\sh$ does not need), and Proposition~\ref{prop:principal} proves an element can never
be the source of non-associativity. Handedness is operator-level; its home is original ground.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[machine-checked, axiom-free]
\label{rem:lean}
The construction is certified by a finite model (registry leaf \texttt{lean-positive-home-oracle},
\textbf{Lean-verified}). Since row-normalization uses rational division --- not kernel-decidable ---
the $\Norm$ matrix instance stays computational (Python); to \texttt{decide} \LA{} as a
\emph{universal} law and witness $\neg\RA{}$, we formalise the \emph{smallest} abstract
monoid-with-normalizer realizing $\LA\wedge\neg\RA$, found by exhaustive search (none exists at order
$\le 3$): $M=\{e,z,a,b\}$ with two-sided zero $z$, $a\cdot a=z$, $a\cdot b=a$, $b\cdot b=b$, and
$K=\mathrm{id}$ on $\{e,a,b\}$, $K(z)=b$. Lean \texttt{decide} checks, over this model: $M$ is a
monoid; \textsf{(K1)}, \textsf{(U)}; \LA{} \emph{universally}; Theorem~\ref{thm:A}; that \RA{}
\emph{fails} and $\sh$ is non-associative (witness $a,a,a$: $(a\sh a)\sh a=b\ne a=a\sh(a\sh a)$);
Theorem~\ref{thm:B} pointwise; the magmoid axioms (Remark~\ref{rem:welldef});
Corollary~\ref{cor:nonprincipal} ($K$ non-principal); and the Proposition~\ref{prop:principal}
control (every idempotent-element principal normalizer is associative). An \texttt{\#print axioms}
audit of the bundled oracle shows \texttt{[propext]} only --- no \texttt{sorry}, no
\texttt{Classical.choice}, no \texttt{native\_decide}. This is the positive twin of the
Karoubi-vacuity leaf \texttt{lean-split-idempotent-oracle}: together they make ``genuine obstruction

\end{remark}

\section{What the four maps missed, and why}
\label{sec:missed}

The four refutations are not four coincidences; they are one structural fact read four ways. The
cocycle and Karoubi homes are the \emph{same fact read twice} --- two independent obstruction
theories, group-cohomological ($H^{2}$/cocycle) and Cauchy/Karoubi (idempotent-splitting), both
\emph{orthogonal} to associativity, and both say so because their apparatus is \emph{defined by} or
\emph{preserves} associativity. The lax-DL and near-semiring homes extend this: a fixed-direction
order cannot see a defect between incomparable outputs, and a distinguished element is always
two-sidedly absorbing. In all four, the candidate lives at a locus --- cochain, idempotent morphism,
coherence cell, element --- at which handedness is invisible. It lives one level up, at the argument
slots of $\sh$, exactly where Theorem~\ref{thm:B} places it. The mismatch is structural, not
accidental: this is \emph{why} the maps missed.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\hline
named map & locus it probes & verdict & kind of negative \\
\hline
$k\mapsto$ 2-cocycle & cochain/morphism & refuted & no receptacle \\
$k\mapsto$ Karoubi splitting & idempotent morphism & refuted & vacuous receptacle \\
$k\mapsto$ lax distributive law & coherence 2-cell & refuted & wrong locus ($\le$-incomparable) \\
near-semiring zero & distinguished element & refuted & associativity-preserving \\
\textbf{normalizer} $K$ & \textbf{argument slots of} $\sh$ & \textbf{receives it} & \textbf{the home} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{remark}[originality: evidence, not theorem]
\label{rem:originality}
A dedicated literature sweep for a prior categorical home of ``one-sided absorptivity of a single
distinguished map'' found none. The two genuine precedents are algebra/ideal-flavored, structurally
one level off: \emph{near-semirings} (a one-sided absorbing zero --- an element, refuted above by
Proposition~\ref{prop:principal}), and Grandis, ``Categories with Negation'' (arXiv:1709.03186),
whose left/right-absorbing morphism \emph{classes} form an \emph{ideal}, not a single operator. We
report this as the current state of evidence and would retract it the moment a genuine precedent
surfaced.
\end{remark}

\section{Grant relevance}

The program's obstruction-theory narrative gains a complete, honestly-bounded unit. For the central
compositional tool --- the Zappa--Sz\'ep weld of directed containers --- we now know precisely
\emph{what the non-associativity is not}: not a cocycle, not a Karoubi-splitting obstruction, not a
lax distributive law, and not a near-semiring zero; four named maps, decided, of four distinct kinds,
with an independent confirmation that the Karoubi vacuity is generic. And we know \emph{what it is}:
a one-sided absorptivity (handedness) datum received by an idempotent normalizer $K$, with
``associativity $=$ $K$ two-sided absorbing'' (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}) a theorem rather than a slogan,
with the normalized-kernel magmoid $\Norm$ as a faithful instance, with the home proved
\emph{operator-level} rather than element-level (Proposition~\ref{prop:principal}), and with the
abstract construction machine-checked axiom-free (Remark~\ref{rem:lean}). ``Handedness datum, not
characteristic class'' is a crisp, defensible statement about where compositional non-associativity
lives --- and the kind of negative-plus-positive result that keeps the Impact narrative (``obstruction
$=$ cohomology'') honest about which obstructions are actually ours, and which home is new ground.
