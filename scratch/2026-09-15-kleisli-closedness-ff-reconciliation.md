## 1. The criterion (restated and confirmed) — grade: proved (standard)

Let `C` be symmetric monoidal and `T` a strong monad on `C`.

**Monoidal.** The strength gives two maps `TA ⊗ TB → T(A⊗B)`; `T` is **commutative** when they
agree. *Kock's theorem* (Kock, *Monads on symmetric monoidal closed categories*, Arch. Math. 21
(1970) 1–10; *Closed categories generated by commutative monads*, J. Austral. Math. Soc. 12 (1971)
405–424): **commutative strong monads = symmetric monoidal monads**. Whenever `T` is commutative,
`C_T` inherits a symmetric monoidal structure with the same tensor on objects and `F: C → C_T`
strict monoidal. *This is proved and standard.*

**Cartesian closed (sharp criterion).** By Yoneda on `C_T(X, A×B) ≅ C(X, TA×TB)`, the inherited
product is a genuine categorical product iff the canonical `T(A×B) → TA×TB` is iso, i.e. **`T`
preserves finite products** (`T1 ≅ 1` included). By the same Yoneda on `C_T(C×A, B) ≅ C(C, (TB)^A)`,
`C_T` is closed iff every `(TB)^A` lies **in the image of `T` up to iso**. So `C_T` is **cartesian
closed ⟺ `T` preserves finite products AND each `(TB)^A ≅ T E`**. Structural obstruction: effectful
monads whose Kleisli category is additive have biproducts, hence a zero object; a CCC with a zero
object is trivial (`A ≅ 1×A ≅ 0×A ≅ 0`). So genuinely effectful monads are *permanently barred* from
cartesian closure — the best they reach is monoidal closure.

**Monoidal closed.** For `T` commutative on symmetric-monoidal-closed `C`, Kock makes `C^T`
monoidal closed with `Hom_T(FA,B) ≅ [A,UB]`. Since `C_T ↪ C^T` strong-monoidally onto the free
algebras, **`C_T` is monoidal closed ⟺ each `[A,TB]` is free (in the image of `T` up to iso) ⟺ the
free algebras form an exponential ideal**. All the positive cases below are ways of making `[A,TB]`
free: self-duality (`[A,B]=A*⊗B`), function-space-into-fixed-object (`[S,−]`), or idempotence
(`C_T ≃ C^T ≃` a monoidal-closed reflective subcategory).

## 3. The reconciliation (core contribution)

**The apparent paradox.** Neil's notes: `Kl(Reader) = coKl(−×S)` is **cartesian closed**. MacBeth's
THM2: the reader monad **fails fullness** of `⟦−⟧_M`. Contradiction?

**No** — the two claims are about **different functors into different targets, with different
notions of naturality.** There are *three* extensions in play; keeping them apart dissolves the
paradox.

**(A) The ordinary extension** `⟦−⟧_M : Fam(Kl(M)^op) → [Set,Set]`, `⟦S,P⟧_M(X)=Σ_s Set(P_s, MX)`
— `M` acts on **values**, the target is ordinary **Set-endofunctors**, and morphisms are
**pure-natural** transformations. THM2: `⟦−⟧_M` is always faithful, and **full ⟺ (i) each
`Set(A,M−)` connected AND (ii) `M` codense** (`M ⇒ Ran_M M` iso). Reader is affine, so (i) holds,
but (ii) fails (`Φ(A)=(RA)^R ≠ A^R = MA` for `|R|≥2`). **Reader fails fullness here.**

**(B) The Kleisli-enriched (presheaf) extension** `⟦−⟧^{Kl} : Fam(Kl(M)^op) → [Kl(M), Set]`,
`⟦S,P⟧^{Kl}=Σ_s Kl(M)(P_s, −)`. This is the free-coproduct-completion / co-Yoneda embedding of
`Kl(M)^op`. Its transformations are **Kleisli-natural** (natural w.r.t. *all* Kleisli maps).
By ordinary co-Yoneda (representables are connected in `[Kl(M),Set]`, coproducts pointwise) it is
**ALWAYS fully faithful** — no closedness, no condition on `M`. This is MacBeth's factorisation:
`⟦−⟧_M = R ∘ ⟦−⟧^{Kl}`, where `R = (−∘F)` restricts the domain along the free functor
`F: Set → Kl(M)`. On objects the two agree; `R` forgets the constraint of Kleisli-naturality, keeping
only pure-naturality. **All** loss of ordinary fullness lives in `R`, and its size is measured exactly
by the codensity monad `Ran_M M`: pure-natural transformations `⊋` Kleisli-natural ones, and the
excess is `Ran_M M` vs `M`. (Kleisli-natural transformations biject with container morphisms;
pure-natural ones are strictly more unless `M` is codense.)

**(C) Neil's self-enriched extension** `⟦S,P⟧ = Σ_s [P_s, −] : C → C`, using **`C`'s internal hom**,
landing in **`C`-enriched endofunctors** `C-[C,C]`. This is *only available when `C` is closed*
(you need the internal hom to make `[P_s,−]` a `C`-valued, `C`-enriched functor). When `C` is closed,
**enriched co-Yoneda** gives Neil's desideratum

>  `Nat(Σ_s [P_s, −], F) ≅ Π_s F(P_s)`

at the level of enriched hom-**objects**, *unconditionally in the closedness*. But **full
faithfulness of the embedding onto enriched endofunctors is a separate matter**: by the T1 criterion
(`containers-over-a-base`, Thm on unit-connectedness) it holds **iff the monoidal unit `I` of `C` is
connected** (`C(I,−)` preserves coproducts). The point is that matching the *set* of transformations
to the container hom-set `Π_s Σ_t C(Q_t,P_s)` requires `C(I,−)` to commute with the shape-coproduct
`Σ_t`.

**Where the paradox goes.** Neil's CCC claim is about the **base** `C = Kl(Reader)` having internal
homs — construction (C) is *available*. It does **not** assert fullness of any container extension.
And in fact fullness fails in *every* picture, consistently:

- **(A) ordinary:** reader is not codense ⇒ not full.
- **(C) self-enriched over `C = Kl(Reader)`:** the base is CCC, so (C) exists; but its unit is the
  terminal object `1` with `C(1,−) = coKl(−×S)(1,−) = Set(S,−) = (−)^S`, which does **not** preserve
  coproducts for `|S|≥2`. **Disconnected unit ⇒ (C) not full**, by T1.
- **(B) Kleisli-presheaf:** always full and faithful — but its transformations are the *correct*
  Kleisli-natural ones, not the pure-natural ones THM2 asks about.

So CCC-ness buys the **availability** of an internal-hom extension, **not** fullness. Reader's
fullness failure is *enrichment-invariant* (it shows up as non-codensity in (A) and as a
disconnected unit in (C)); the only place fullness is automatic is the always-ff Kleisli-presheaf
embedding (B), whose naturality notion is different.

**The clean dictionary.**

| | availability | fully faithful ⟺ |
|---|---|---|
| **(C) self-enriched** `Σ_s[P_s,−]: C→C` (Neil) | `C` closed | unit `I` of `C` connected (T1) |
| **(B) Kleisli-presheaf** `⟦−⟧^{Kl}` (MacBeth) | always | always (co-Yoneda) |
| **(A) ordinary** `⟦−⟧_M: →[Set,Set]` (THM2) | always | (i) `Set(A,M−)` connected **and** (ii) `M` codense |

and the bridge `⟦−⟧_M = R∘⟦−⟧^{Kl}`, with `R` = restriction along `F: Set→Kl(M)`, whose defect is
the **codensity monad `Ran_M M`** — the exact gap between Kleisli-natural (enriched, always ff) and
pure-natural (ordinary, codensity-gated).

*One-liner:* **closedness of the base makes the enriched extension exist; connectedness of the unit
(equivalently, codensity of the effect) makes it full — CCC-ness is the former, not the latter, and
the reader fails the latter in every enrichment.**

## 4. Verdict on Neil's uniform theorem

**The enriched-Yoneda full-and-faithfulness theorem is available exactly when the base `C` is
(symmetric monoidal) closed** — this is the enriched co-Yoneda formula `Nat(Σ_s[P_s,−],F) ≅ Π_s F(P_s)`
of construction (C). Concretely:

- **Available (base closed):** the *function-space-into-a-fixed-object* (reader-like `[S,−]`),
  *idempotent/reflective* (localization, sheafification, completion, abelianization), and
  *compact-closed / self-dual* bases (Rel, Par, `V`-Rel, Vec/FinVect, writer on a compact-closed
  base). Cartesian form (`B^A`): only Set, Cat, Kl(Reader), idempotent reflections.
- **Not available (base not even monoidal-closed):** ultrafilter `β`, continuation, list are **not
  monoidal** (non-commutative ⇒ premonoidal), so no enriched Yoneda at all; Stoch/Markov is monoidal
  but **not closed** (`(DB)^A` not free), so no internal-hom extension.

Two honest refinements Neil should have:

1. **Closed ≠ full.** Availability (closedness) is *not* fullness. Fullness of the enriched extension
   needs the **unit connected** (T1). This bites precisely on the reader-like bases whose whole point
   is a disconnected unit `(−)^S` — so *the flagship "good" closed base for CCC, `Kl(Reader)`, still
   fails enriched fullness.* Closedness is necessary for the theorem to be *stated*, unit-connectedness
   for it to be *true*.

2. **Enriched vs non-enriched — the separation Neil asked for.** For **non-closed** bases (Vec as a
   `⊗`-non-CCC, Cat aside, `β`, continuation, list, Stoch), construction (C) is simply unavailable;
   the **ordinary** extension `⟦−⟧_M : →[Set,Set]` is the only game, and there full-and-faithfulness
   is governed **not** by enriched Yoneda but by MacBeth's **codensity criterion** (THM2): faithful
   always, full ⟺ `Set(A,M−)` connected and `M` codense. This is why the survey needs *both* engines:
   enriched Yoneda + unit-connectedness on closed bases, codensity on the rest.

## 5. For the report

*Slots into the monad/comonad survey as the "when is the Kleisli category closed, and what does that
buy the container calculus" section.* Two independent facts, cleanly separated. **First** (Kock,
standard): the Kleisli category of `T` is monoidal iff `T` is commutative, cartesian closed iff `T`
preserves finite products and every `(TB)^A` is free, and monoidal closed iff the free algebras form
an exponential ideal — so genuinely effectful monads (biproducts ⇒ zero object) never reach CCC, the
"nice" cases being reader-like, idempotent, and compact-closed. **Second** (MacBeth, this program):
closedness of the base is exactly what makes the *enriched-Yoneda* container extension available, but
it does **not** deliver full faithfulness — that is a *separate* invariant, unit-connectedness in the
enriched picture and the codensity monad `Ran_M M` in the ordinary `[Set,Set]` picture. The reader
monad is the pivot: its Kleisli category *is* cartesian closed (so Neil's enriched extension exists),
yet it fails fullness in every enrichment, because closedness and fullness are orthogonal. The
dictionary `⟦−⟧_M = R ∘ ⟦−⟧^{Kl}` — always-ff Kleisli-presheaf embedding, followed by restriction to
pure maps whose defect is the codensity monad — is the one picture that carries both messages at once,
and is the natural bridge from the comonad/coKleisli half of the survey (Seely's `!`, product
comonads) to the effect-monad half.
