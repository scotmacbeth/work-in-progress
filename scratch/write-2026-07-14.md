> "Cont has four monoidal structures" is a census, not a theorem. The theorem is that
> the product is the *only* one whose extension is the pointwise product — and that the
> Dirichlet tensor is the sequential operator with its dependency universally switched off.

much more than WRITE.md anticipated:

- **Theorem A** — the Day construction is an *equivalence* onto the "convolutional" structures
  (D1: preserves coproducts in each variable; D2: representables closed). The literature has

  completed novelty audit.

So the chapter is no longer "four structures + a taxonomy as payoff". It is a classification
chapter with the four as its worked instances. That IS the Day spine WRITE.md hoped for, and now
it is carried by theorems rather than by a table.

## Revision pass — what changed while writing

**A real gap, found by hostile-referee reading and fixed (exposition, not mathematics).**
Theorem C applies the adjunction `Lan_J ⊣ res_J` to `Φ_p = p ◁ −`, which is *not*
coproduct-preserving — that is the entire point of the theorem. But the proof note justified the
formula `(Lan_J G)(q) = Σ_t G(q[t])` by the universal property of the free coproduct completion
(Lemma 1.3), which only compares functors that *already* preserve coproducts. So the citation was
to the wrong lemma.

The formula is nonetheless correct against arbitrary `F`, and I verified it: the comma category
`J/q` splits over `t ∈ S_q` (a morphism `y^A → y^{A'} → q` has a forced shape map, so it cannot
change `t`), and each fibre is `(q[t]/Set)^op`, which has `(q[t], id)` as a **terminal** object.
A colimit over a category with a terminal object is the value there, so the fibre colimit is
`G(q[t])`. This is now **Lemma 2.6** with proof, and Theorem C cites it. Full faithfulness of
`Lan_J` also falls straight out of the formula: `(Lan_J G)(y^A) = G(A)`, since `y^A` has one shape.

Other referee fixes:
- §3 wrongly attributed `+`'s coherence to the Day argument. `+` is not a Day tensor (Lemma 4.5!).
  Corrected: `+` and `×` get coherence free as categorical (co)products; `×` and `⊗` get a second,
  more informative derivation via Day.
- Theorem A: made explicit that (D2) applied *twice* is what makes the associator an isomorphism
  *between representables*; that `E = Cont` has small coproducts, so Lemma 2.5 applies; and that
  both associators restrict to `⋆`'s on representables (that is what n=3 needs).
