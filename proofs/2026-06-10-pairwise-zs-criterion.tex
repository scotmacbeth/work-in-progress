\title{The pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion:\\
strict factorization over a wide subcategory has\\
the two-level (local freeness $\wedge$ global closure) shape}
\author{MacBeth\\\small Kodamai}
\date{10 June 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We lift the single-category Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion to the genuinely
\emph{pairwise} setting. Let $\K$ be a small category and $\D\subseteq\K$ a wide
subcategory --- the prescribed \emph{right factor}, no longer required to be the
endomorphism subcategory. We prove that $\K$ admits a strict factorization
$\K=\C\bowtie\D$ (every morphism factors \emph{uniquely} as a $\C$-arrow after a
$\D$-arrow, for some wide subcategory $\C$) \emph{if and only if} a two-level
criterion of exactly the shape found in the single-category case holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textnormal{(L)}] \emph{fibrewise freeness} --- for every object $b$ the
hom-presheaf $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is a free right $\D$-set (a coproduct of
representables); and
\item[\textnormal{(G)}] \emph{global closure / holonomy} --- free bases can be
chosen, one per target, that close up into a wide subcategory $\C$.
\end{itemize}
The proof rests on a single clean lemma (a free presheaf is exactly a coproduct
of representables, so a basis is exactly a unique-factorization system) and the
observation that a free basis of $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is precisely the family of
$\C$-arrows into $b$. The whole structure $\K\cong\C\bowtie\D$ is governed by a
\emph{distributive law of categories} $\lambda:\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ whose
matched-pair axioms ZS1--ZS4 we re-type for two genuine categories and show
equivalent to associativity of $\K$. The single-category criterion is the case
$\D=\E$. We anchor the statement at the monoid$\times$monoid case, where it
recovers Brin's classical Zappa--Sz\'ep axioms, with the semidirect product
(one trivial action) as the degenerate instance; we state carefully and honestly
how Ahman--Uustalu's update monads relate (an analogy, not a literal instance).
Finally we exhibit a \emph{genuinely pairwise} obstruction: a four-object
category with a wide $\D$ carrying both a holonomy loop and a cross-object arrow,
in which (L) holds but (G) fails --- so the two-level phenomenon is not an
artifact of $\D$ being the endomorphisms. All claims are machine-checked.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and statement}

In the companion note \cite{single} we resolved SEED-Q2 for a single small
category: a category $\C$ admits a source-oriented Zappa--Sz\'ep decomposition
over its endomorphism subcategory $\E$ iff every $\Hom(a,b)$ is free as a right
$\End(a)$-set \emph{(local freeness, L)} \emph{and} the per-orbit
representatives can be chosen to form a wide subcategory \emph{(global closure,
G)}. The forward-pointing question --- the actual prize --- is the
\emph{pairwise} distributive law: when do \emph{two} categories assemble into a
single one that factors as one-after-the-other?

We make this precise as a recognition problem with a prescribed right factor.

\begin{definition}[Strict factorization system]\label{def:sfs}
Let $\K$ be a small category and $\C,\D\subseteq\K$ wide subcategories (i.e.\
$\ob\C=\ob\D=\ob\K$, each closed under composition and containing all
identities). The pair $(\C,\D)$ is a \emph{strict factorization system (SFS)} of
$\K$ if every morphism $f\in\K$ factors \emph{uniquely} as $f=c\circ d$ with
$c\in\C$ and $d\in\D$. We then write $\K=\C\bowtie\D$ and call $\K$ the
\emph{Zappa--Sz\'ep product} of $\C$ and $\D$.
\end{definition}

The orientation is the one of \cite{single}: the $\D$-part $d$ acts first
(source side), the $\C$-part $c$ second (target side). The single-category
problem is the instance $\D=\E$, the endomorphism subcategory, with $\C$ a chosen
transversal. The pairwise problem fixes a \emph{general} wide subcategory $\D$
(it may have cross-object arrows) and asks for a complementary $\C$.

\paragraph{Main theorem.} Our central result (Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}) is
that the SFS exists iff the two-level criterion (L)$\wedge$(G) holds, with (L)
now the freeness of the hom-presheaf $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ as a right $\D$-set and (G)
the closure of a chosen family of free bases. This is the same two-level shape as
the single-category case, and recovers it when $\D=\E$. The algebraic content ---
that $\K\cong\C\bowtie\D$ is governed by a distributive law of categories whose
axioms are exactly associativity --- is Theorems~\ref{thm:axioms} and the
extraction Lemma~\ref{lem:extract}, generalizing the matched-pair calculus of
\cite{single} from a family of vertex monoids to a genuine second category.

\section{The free-presheaf lemma}

Everything local rests on one standard fact, which we state in the exact form we
need. Recall a \emph{right $\D$-set} is a presheaf $M:\D^{op}\to\Set$: for
$d:a\to m$ in $\D$ a function $M(d):M(m)\to M(a)$, written $x\mapsto x\cdot d$,
with $x\cdot\id=x$ and $(x\cdot d')\cdot d=x\cdot(d'\circ d)$ (contravariance).
The \emph{representable} at $m$ is $\D(-,m)$. A presheaf is \emph{free} if it is
isomorphic to a coproduct of representables.

\begin{lemma}[Free presheaf $=$ unique factorization]\label{lem:free}
Let $\D$ be a small category and $M:\D^{op}\to\Set$. The following are
equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $M$ is free.
\item There is a family $B=(B_m\subseteq M(m))_{m\in\ob\D}$ such that for every
object $a$ and every $x\in M(a)$ there is a \emph{unique} pair $(c,d)$ with
$c\in B_m$ (some $m$) and $d\in\D(a,m)$ satisfying $x=c\cdot d$.
\end{enumerate}
When they hold, $M\cong\coprod_{m}\coprod_{c\in B_m}\D(-,m)$, and $B$ is called a
\emph{basis}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(2)\Rightarrow(1)$. Define $\varphi:\coprod_{m}\coprod_{c\in B_m}\D(-,m)\to M$ by
$\varphi_a(c,d)=c\cdot d$ for $d\in\D(a,m)$. It is a morphism of presheaves: for
$e:a'\to a$ in $\D$, $\varphi_{a'}\big((c,d)\cdot e\big)=\varphi_{a'}(c,d\circ
e)=c\cdot(d\circ e)=(c\cdot d)\cdot e=\varphi_a(c,d)\cdot e$, using contravariance
of $M$ and that the action on the representable $\D(-,m)$ is precomposition. By
hypothesis $\varphi_a$ is a bijection for every $a$ (existence and uniqueness of
$(c,d)$). A presheaf morphism that is objectwise bijective is an isomorphism, so
$M$ is free.

$(1)\Rightarrow(2)$. Suppose $\psi:\coprod_{i\in I}\D(-,m_i)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\
}M$. Let $c_i:=\psi_{m_i}(\id_{m_i})\in M(m_i)$ be the image of the $i$-th
identity, and set $B_m:=\{c_i:m_i=m\}$. By the Yoneda lemma the $i$-th component
$\D(-,m_i)\to M$ is "multiply $c_i$": its value at $d\in\D(a,m_i)$ is
$\psi_{m_i}(\id)\cdot d=c_i\cdot d$. Since $\psi$ is an isomorphism, every
$x\in M(a)$ equals $c_i\cdot d$ for a unique $i$ and (the representable being a
\emph{set} of morphisms) a unique $d\in\D(a,m_i)$. This is exactly (2) with basis
$B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Specialization to a family of monoids]\label{rem:monoids}
If $\D=\E$ is a disjoint union of vertex monoids ($\E(a,a)=\End(a)$,
$\E(a,b)=\varnothing$ for $a\neq b$), then $\E^{op}$ is the disjoint union of the
monoids $\End(a)^{op}$, and a presheaf on $\E$ is exactly a family of right
$\End(a)$-sets $a\mapsto M(a)$. Freeness of $M$ is freeness of each component over
its monoid, and a basis $B_a\subseteq M(a)$ is one representative per
$\End(a)$-orbit. Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:free} reduces to the freeness criterion of
\cite{single} when $\D=\E$.
\end{remark}

\section{The pairwise criterion}

We can now prove the main theorem directly. The key observation is that, for an
SFS, a free basis of $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is forced to be \emph{the family of
$\C$-arrows into $b$}.

\begin{theorem}[Pairwise Zappa--Sz\'ep criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $\K$ be a small category and $\D\subseteq\K$ a wide subcategory. The following
are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item There is a wide subcategory $\C\subseteq\K$ with $(\C,\D)$ a strict
factorization system of $\K$ (Definition~\ref{def:sfs}); i.e.\ $\K=\C\bowtie\D$.
\item Both:
\begin{itemize}
\item[\textnormal{(L)}] for every object $b$, the right $\D$-set $\Hom_\K(-,b)$
is free; and
\item[\textnormal{(G)}] free bases $B_b$ of $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ can be chosen so that
$\C:=\bigcup_{b}B_b$ is a wide subcategory of $\K$.
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First note the right $\D$-set structure on $\Hom_\K(-,b)$: for $f:a\to b$ in $\K$
and $d:a'\to a$ in $\D$, $f\cdot d:=f\circ d:a'\to b$; this is contravariant and
well defined because $\D\subseteq\K$. A basis element $c\in B_m\subseteq
\Hom_\K(m,b)$ together with $d\in\D(a,m)$ gives $c\cdot d=c\circ d:a\to b$, a
composite \emph{in $\K$} of a basis morphism after a $\D$-morphism.

$(1)\Rightarrow(2)$. Let $(\C,\D)$ be an SFS. For each object $b$ put
$B_b:=\{c\in\C:\cod(c)=b\}$, the $\C$-arrows into $b$. We claim $B_b$ is a basis of
$\Hom_\K(-,b)$. Indeed, for $f:a\to b$ the SFS gives a unique $c\in\C$, $d\in\D$
with $f=c\circ d$; necessarily $\cod(c)=\cod(f)=b$, so $c\in B_b$, and
$d\in\D(a,\dom c)$. Conversely any expression $f=c\circ d$ with $c\in B_b$,
$d\in\D$ is a factorization with $c\in\C$, $d\in\D$, hence the unique one. So each
$f$ is uniquely $c\cdot d$ with $c\in B_b$, $d\in\D(a,\dom c)$; by
Lemma~\ref{lem:free} $B_b$ is a basis and $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is free. This is (L).
Moreover $\bigcup_b B_b$ is exactly the set of all $\C$-arrows (each arrow of $\C$
has a unique codomain), i.e.\ $\bigcup_b B_b=\C$, a wide subcategory by
hypothesis. This is (G).

$(2)\Rightarrow(1)$. Assume (L) and (G); let $\C=\bigcup_b B_b$ be the wide
subcategory furnished by (G), with $B_b$ a basis of $\Hom_\K(-,b)$. Since each
arrow of $\C$ has a single codomain, $B_b=\{c\in\C:\cod(c)=b\}$. For any
$f:a\to b$ in $\K$, freeness with basis $B_b$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:free}) gives a
\emph{unique} pair $(c,d)$ with $c\in B_b\subseteq\C$ and $d\in\D(a,\dom c)$ and
$f=c\circ d$. Any factorization $f=c'\circ d'$ with $c'\in\C$, $d'\in\D$ has
$\cod(c')=b$, so $c'\in B_b$; uniqueness over $B_b$ forces $(c',d')=(c,d)$. Hence
the factorization is unique over all of $\C$, and $(\C,\D)$ is a strict
factorization system.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recovery of the single-category criterion]\label{cor:single}
Taking $\D=\E$ the endomorphism subcategory, Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}
specializes to the single-category criterion of \cite{single}: $\C$ exists iff
(L$'$) each $\Hom_\K(a,b)$ is free as a right $\End(a)$-set, and (G$'$) per-orbit
representatives close into a wide subcategory.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Remark~\ref{rem:monoids}, freeness of the $\E$-set $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is freeness
of each $\Hom_\K(a,b)$ over $\End(a)$, and a basis is one representative per
$\End(a)$-orbit. (G) becomes closure of the representatives, i.e.\ (G$'$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why two levels]
Condition (L) is about the right $\D$-action alone: it asks that the action be
\emph{free} (orbits are torsors), so that bases --- unique-factorization
representatives --- \emph{exist}, one choice per target. It is the direct analog
of ``free module.'' Condition (G) is the genuinely global, cross-target
constraint: the independently-chosen bases must \emph{cohere} into a single
subcategory. (L) can hold while (G) fails (Proposition~\ref{prop:ce}); the gap is
a holonomy class. When $\D=\E$ this is exactly the local-freeness / global-closure
split of \cite{single}.
\end{remark}

\section{The distributive law: structure of $\C\bowtie\D$}

Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} decides \emph{existence}. We now record the
\emph{algebra}: when the SFS exists, $\K$ is reconstructed from $\C$, $\D$ and a
distributive law, generalizing the matched-pair calculus of \cite{single} from a
family of vertex monoids to a second genuine category. This is the
categorical form of the classical equivalence ``distributive laws $\leftrightarrow$
strict factorization systems'' (Rosebrugh--Wood \cite{rw}, Street \cite{street}).

Throughout, $\C,\D$ are wide subcategories on a shared object set $O$.

\begin{definition}[Distributive law / matched pair of categories]\label{def:law}
A \emph{distributive law} $\lambda:\D\C\Rightarrow\C\D$ assigns to each composable
pair $(d,c)$ with $d\in\D$, $c\in\C$, $\dom(d)=\cod(c)$ a pair
$\lambda(d,c)=(\lact{d}{c},\rest{d}{c})$ with $\lact{d}{c}\in\C$ and
$\rest{d}{c}\in\D$ such that the types match a rewriting of $d\circ c$:
\[
  \dom(\rest{d}{c})=\dom(c),\quad \cod(\lact{d}{c})=\cod(d),\quad
  \dom(\lact{d}{c})=\cod(\rest{d}{c}),
\]
i.e.\ $\lact{d}{c}\circ\rest{d}{c}$ has the same source and target as
$d\circ c$. It is a \emph{matched pair} if it satisfies, for all composable data,
\begin{description}
\item[(U)] $\lact{\id}{c}=c,\ \rest{\id}{c}=\id_{\dom c}$, and
$\lact{d}{\id}=\id_{\cod d},\ \rest{d}{\id}=d$;
\item[(ZS2)] $\lact{(d'\circ d)}{c}=\lact{d'}{(\lact{d}{c})}$\quad
($d:\cod c\to\cdot$, $d'$ composable after $d$ in $\D$);
\item[(ZS3)] $\rest{(d'\circ d)}{c}=\rest{(d')}{\,\lact{d}{c}}\circ\rest{d}{c}$\quad
(composite in $\D$);
\item[(ZS1)] $\lact{d}{(c_2\circ c_1)}
=\lact{d}{c_2}\circ\lact{(\rest{d}{c_2})}{c_1}$\quad
($c_1:a\to m,\ c_2:m\to\cdot$ in $\C$, $d\in\D$ at $\cod c_2$);
\item[(ZS4)] $\rest{d}{(c_2\circ c_1)}=\rest{(\rest{d}{c_2})}{c_1}$.
\end{description}
\end{definition}

The four axioms are the Brin/Zappa--Sz\'ep matched-pair axioms, now typed for two
many-object categories: ZS2 says $\D$ acts on $\C$ on the left; ZS3 is the cocycle
for the restriction over $\D$-composition; ZS1, ZS4 are the compatibilities with
$\C$-composition. They reduce verbatim to (ZS1)--(ZS4) of \cite{single} when
$\D=\E$ (then $\rest{d}{c}$ lands in a vertex monoid and $d'\circ d$ is a vertex
product).

\begin{definition}[The product]\label{def:prod}
Given a matched pair, $\C\bowtie\D$ has object set $O$, hom-sets
$(\C\bowtie\D)(a,b)=\coprod_{m}\C(m,b)\times\D(a,m)$, identities
$(\id_a,\id_a)$, and composition
\[
  (c_2,d_2)\cdot(c_1,d_1)
  =\bigl(c_2\circ\lact{d_2}{c_1},\ \rest{d_2}{c_1}\circ d_1\bigr),
\]
for $(c_1,d_1):a\to b$ (so $d_1:a\to m_1$, $c_1:m_1\to b$) and $(c_2,d_2):b\to e$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Axioms $\iff$ associativity]\label{thm:axioms}
$\C\bowtie\D$ of Definition~\ref{def:prod} is a category if and only if the
distributive law $\lambda$ is a matched pair (satisfies \textnormal{(U)} and
\textnormal{(ZS1)--(ZS4)}). The unit laws are equivalent to \textnormal{(U)}; a
single associativity instance splits into its $\C$-coordinate, governed by
\textnormal{(ZS1)+(ZS2)}, and its $\D$-coordinate, governed by
\textnormal{(ZS3)+(ZS4)}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The computation is identical to \cite[Thm.~3]{single} after replacing the vertex
monoid $M_a$ by the category $\D$ and the vertex product by $\D$-composition; the
only change is that the restriction $\rest{d}{c}$ may change object, which the
typing of Definition~\ref{def:law} accommodates. We verify both directions
exhaustively by machine for genuine two-object factors below
(\S\ref{sec:comp}); the symbolic proof is the bracketing computation of
\cite{single}, whose every step uses only associativity in $\C$, associativity in
$\D$, and the rewriting $d\circ c=\lact{d}{c}\circ\rest{d}{c}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Extraction from an SFS]\label{lem:extract}
Let $(\C,\D)$ be an SFS of $\K$. For $d\in\D$, $c\in\C$ with $\dom(d)=\cod(c)$,
let $\lact{d}{c}\in\C$, $\rest{d}{c}\in\D$ be the unique factorization of
$d\circ c\in\K$:
\[
  d\circ c=\lact{d}{c}\circ\rest{d}{c}.
\]
Then $\lambda$ is a matched pair, and $\Phi:\C\bowtie\D\to\K$,
$\Phi(c,d)=c\circ d$, is an isomorphism of categories.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each axiom is obtained by factoring one $\K$-morphism two ways and invoking
uniqueness, exactly as in \cite[Lem.~5]{single}, now reading the middle factor in
the genuine category $\D$. We spell out (ZS2)/(ZS3), where the cross-object
typing is least obvious, to confirm it goes through verbatim. Take $c\in\C$,
$d\in\D$ with $\dom(d)=\cod(c)$, and $d'\in\D$ with $\dom(d')=\cod(d)$. Then
\[
  (d'\circ d)\circ c
  = d'\circ(d\circ c)
  = d'\circ\bigl(\lact{d}{c}\circ\rest{d}{c}\bigr)
  = \bigl(d'\circ\lact{d}{c}\bigr)\circ\rest{d}{c}
  = \lact{d'}{(\lact{d}{c})}\circ\rest{(d')}{\,\lact{d}{c}}\circ\rest{d}{c},
\]
the last step factoring $d'\circ\lact{d}{c}$ (composable since
$\cod\lact{d}{c}=\cod d=\dom d'$). Here $\rest{(d')}{\,\lact{d}{c}}$ and
$\rest{d}{c}$ are composable \emph{in $\D$}, because
$\dom\rest{(d')}{\,\lact{d}{c}}=\dom\lact{d}{c}=\cod\rest{d}{c}$ --- the only
point where $\D$ being a category, not a family of monoids, matters, and it
matches. Comparing with the direct factorization $(d'\circ d)\circ
c=\lact{(d'\circ d)}{c}\circ\rest{(d'\circ d)}{c}$ and using uniqueness gives the
$\C$-part $\lact{(d'\circ d)}{c}=\lact{d'}{(\lact{d}{c})}$ (ZS2) and the $\D$-part
$\rest{(d'\circ d)}{c}=\rest{(d')}{\,\lact{d}{c}}\circ\rest{d}{c}$ (ZS3). The
symmetric computation factoring $d\circ(c_2\circ c_1)$ as $d\circ(c_2\circ c_1)$
versus $(d\circ c_2)\circ c_1$ yields (ZS1)/(ZS4); the units are immediate. For
the converse (matched pair $\Rightarrow$ category) the same bracketing identities
read backwards give associativity, as in \cite[Thm.~3]{single}. $\Phi$ is a
bijection on each hom-set ($=$ the SFS factorization), preserves identities, and
preserves composition by the rewriting, hence is an isomorphism.
\end{proof}

Thus when the criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} holds, $\K$ is genuinely
the Zappa--Sz\'ep product of the two categories $\C$ and $\D$, and the law
$\lambda$ is the precise carrier of the mutual action. The obstruction (G) is the
failure of the bases to close, i.e.\ the failure of the candidate $\lambda$ to be
single-valued/consistent --- the holonomy.

\section{The monoid$\times$monoid anchor}\label{sec:monoid}

We check the criterion against classical ground truth in the one-object case,
where $\C=M$ and $\D=N$ are monoids.

\begin{proposition}[Classical Zappa--Sz\'ep recovered]\label{prop:monoid}
Let $M,N$ be monoids, viewed as one-object categories. A matched pair
(Definition~\ref{def:law}) is exactly a pair consisting of a left action
$N\times M\to M$, $(n,m)\mapsto\lact{n}{m}$ and a right action
$N\times M\to N$, $(n,m)\mapsto\rest{n}{m}$ satisfying Brin's Zappa--Sz\'ep
axioms; the product $M\bowtie N$ of Definition~\ref{def:prod} is the classical
Zappa--Sz\'ep product of monoids, with multiplication
$(m_2,n_2)(m_1,n_1)=(m_2\,\lact{n_2}{m_1},\ \rest{n_2}{m_1}\,n_1)$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:axioms} it is a monoid iff the axioms hold. Such products are
exactly the monoids $K$ with two submonoids $M,N$ for which the multiplication
$M\times N\to K$ is a bijection (an internal exact factorization).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With one object all typing constraints in Definition~\ref{def:law} are vacuous,
$\D$-composition is the product in $N$ and $\C$-composition the product in $M$;
the axioms (U),(ZS1)--(ZS4) become precisely the unit laws and the four
Zappa--Sz\'ep identities of Brin \cite{brin}. The product formula is
Definition~\ref{def:prod} with $\coprod_m$ trivial. The last sentence is the
classical characterization of exact factorizations of monoids, and is also
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} read at one object: (L) is the freeness of $K$ as a
right $N$-set and (G) is the existence of a closing basis, which for the basis
$M$ is exactly that $M\times N\to K$, $(m,n)\mapsto mn$, is a bijection onto $K$
with $M$ a submonoid.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The degenerate instance, and update monads --- honestly]
\label{rem:au}
The genuine \emph{degenerate} case of the monoid Zappa--Sz\'ep product is the
\emph{semidirect} product $M\ltimes N$, where one of the two actions is trivial:
e.g.\ $\rest{n}{m}=n$ for all $m,n$, leaving only the left action of $N$ on $M$
and the multiplication $(m_2,n_2)(m_1,n_1)=(m_2\,\lact{n_2}{m_1},\,n_2 n_1)$. This
is the monoid analogue of a semidirect product of groups.

It is tempting --- and the SEED roadmap suggested --- to call Ahman--Uustalu's
update monad \cite{au-update} the degenerate instance. This is an
\emph{overclaim} and we avoid it. The update monad of an act
$(S,(P,\oplus,o),\downarrow)$ is $TX=S\to(P\times X)$, the compatible composition
of the writer monad $P\times(-)$ \emph{over} the reader monad $S\to(-)$ via the
distributive law $\theta(p,f)=\lambda s.(p,f(s\downarrow p))$
\cite[\S2.2--2.3]{au-update}; its directed container is the
\emph{action (transport) category} $S\rtimes P$ (objects $S$, morphisms
$s\to s'$ the set $\{p\in P:s\downarrow p=s'\}$, composition $\oplus$, identities
$o$) \cite{au-cats}. The honest relationship is by \emph{analogy and handedness}:
a Zappa--Sz\'ep product is the monad-over-monad twist of two \emph{monoids}
through two mutually-twisting actions, whereas the update monad is the
writer-over-reader twist of a \emph{monoid and a set} $S$ through a single
one-sided action. It is \emph{not} literally a Zappa--Sz\'ep product of two
monoids; in fact a bare action category need not admit any nontrivial strict
factorization (for $S=P=\mathbb{Z}/2$ with $s\downarrow p=s+p$ one gets the
codiscrete category on two objects, whose only SFSs are trivial). The legitimate
``degenerate / semidirect'' reading of the update monad is internal to the
directed-container hierarchy: it is the \emph{non-dependent} (state-independent)
directed container, equivalently a plain monoid action; a genuine Zappa--Sz\'ep
\emph{factorization} enters only if $P$ itself splits as $P=A\bowtie B$.
\end{remark}

\section{A genuinely pairwise obstruction}\label{sec:ce}

The single-category counterexample of \cite{single} (the ten-morphism rigid
twist) is the instance $\D=\E$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} where (L) holds and
(G) fails. We now show the two-level phenomenon is \emph{not} an artifact of
$\D$ being the endomorphisms: the same holonomy survives when $\D$ carries a
genuine cross-object arrow.

\begin{example}[Rigid twist over a cross-object factor]\label{ex:ce}
Let $\K$ have four objects $w,a,x,y$ and morphisms
\[
  \End(a)=\{\id_a,g\},\ g^2=\id_a;\quad \End(w)=\{\id_w\},\
  \End(x)=\{\id_x\},\ \End(y)=\{\id_y\},
\]
\[
  \Hom(a,x)=\{p,pg\},\ \Hom(x,y)=\{s,s_2\},\ \Hom(a,y)=\{q,qg\},
\]
\[
  \Hom(w,a)=\{d,gd\},\ \Hom(w,x)=\{pd,pgd\},\ \Hom(w,y)=\{qd,qgd\},
\]
with $pg=p\circ g$, $qg=q\circ g$, $gd=g\circ d$, and the rigid twist
$s\circ p=q$, $s_2\circ p=qg$ (so $s\circ pg=qg$, $s_2\circ pg=q$), together with
the forced composites $p\circ d=pd$, $p\circ gd=pgd$ ($=pg\circ d$),
$q\circ d=qd$, etc., and $s\circ pd=qd$, $s_2\circ pd=qgd$,
$s\circ pgd=qgd$, $s_2\circ pgd=qd$. Let
\[
  \D=\{\id_w,\id_a,\id_x,\id_y,\ g,\ d,\ gd\}.
\]
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ce}
In Example~\ref{ex:ce}, $\K$ is a small category and $\D$ is a wide subcategory
containing the cross-object arrows $d,gd:w\to a$. Every $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is free as
a right $\D$-set \emph{(L holds)}, yet no wide subcategory $\C$ makes $(\C,\D)$ an
SFS \emph{(G fails)}. Hence $\K$ has no strict factorization over this $\D$,
despite the cross-object factor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\D$ is closed: $g\circ g=\id_a$, $g\circ d=gd$, $g\circ gd=d$, and identities;
it contains the cross arrows $d,gd$. Freeness: the bases are
$B_w=\{\id_w\}$; $B_a=\{\id_a\}$ (then $g=\id_a\cdot g$, $d=\id_a\cdot d$,
$gd=\id_a\cdot gd$, uniquely --- the orbit $\{\id_a,g,d,gd\}$ is a single free
$\D$-orbit on the basis $\id_a$); $B_x=\{\id_x,p\}$ (with $pg=p\cdot g$,
$pd=p\cdot d$, $pgd=p\cdot gd$); $B_y=\{\id_y,q,s,s_2\}$ (with $qg,qd,qgd$ the
$\D$-multiples of $q$, while $s,s_2$ are forced into the basis since $\D$ has no
nonidentity arrow out of $x$). So $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is free for every $b$: (L) holds.

Now (G). Any closing $\C$ has $B_x\in\{\{\id_x,p\},\{\id_x,pg\}\}$ and $B_y$ a
free basis containing $s,s_2$ and exactly one of $q,qg$ (one representative of the
$\D$-orbit $\{q,qg,qd,qgd\}$, namely $q$ or $qg$). Closure of $\C$ requires
$s\circ t,\ s_2\circ t\in B_y$ for the chosen $t\in B_x\cap\Hom(a,x)$. If
$t=p$: $s\circ p=q$ and $s_2\circ p=qg$, so $B_y$ must contain \emph{both} $q$ and
$qg$ --- impossible, as it contains exactly one. If $t=pg$: $s\circ pg=qg$,
$s_2\circ pg=q$, the same contradiction. Hence no $\C$ closes: (G) fails. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} there is no SFS $(\C,\D)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The cross arrow $d$ is adjoined to the endomorphism factor as a \emph{free
$\langle g\rangle$-torsor} of arrows $\{d,gd\}$ into $a$: it enlarges $\D$ to a
genuine many-object category and keeps every hom-presheaf free (the new $w$-rows
factor uniquely through the existing $x,y$-bases), but it is inert against the
branch obstruction at $a\to x\rightrightarrows y$. The holonomy is the same
$\mathbb{Z}/2$ class as in \cite{single}; promoting $\D$ from a family of vertex
monoids to a category with cross-object arrows does not dissolve it. This is the
crown-jewel identification ((G) $=$ holonomy $=$ the laxator of the pairwise
distributive law) in its genuinely pairwise form.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational verification}\label{sec:comp}

All claims are machine-checked by scripts in \texttt{projects/scratch/}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{pairwise\_zs\_check.py}: finite-category framework; builder of
$\C\bowtie\D$ from a law $\lambda$; checker of (U),(ZS1)--(ZS4); extractor of
$\lambda$ from an SFS.
\item \texttt{pairwise\_zs\_tests.py}, \texttt{pairwise\_zs\_tests2.py}:
exhaustive over \emph{all} type-correct laws $\lambda$, confirm
Theorem~\ref{thm:axioms} (axioms $\iff$ associativity) with zero disagreements.
Monoid cases: $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ ($4$ laws, $1$ valid),
$\mathbb{Z}/3\times\mathbb{Z}/2$ and $\mathbb{Z}/2\times\mathbb{Z}/3$ ($36$ laws,
$2$ valid each). Genuine two-object case with loops \emph{and} cross arrows in
both factors: $64$ laws, exactly $4$ valid $=4$ associative, the other $60$
failing both --- the equivalence is non-vacuous.
\item \texttt{pairwise\_end\_to\_end.py}: implements the criterion (freeness of
each $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ via Lemma~\ref{lem:free}; closure search for (G)) and checks
it against brute-force search over \emph{all} wide subcategories $\C$. Confirms
Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} on: the walking retract (SFS exists, over a
cross-arrow $\D$); the walking retract over its idempotent (L fails); the
ten-morphism rigid twist over $\D=\E$ (L holds, G fails).
\item \texttt{pairwise\_counterexample\_FOUND.py}: builds Example~\ref{ex:ce} and
verifies it is a category, $\D$ is wide with cross arrows, every $\Hom_\K(-,b)$ is
free, and no SFS exists (over all $1152$ wide subcategories) --- confirming
Proposition~\ref{prop:ce}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Grant relevance}

Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} is the pairwise decision procedure promised by
SEED-Q2: for two directed containers presented as a category $\K$ with a
prescribed right factor $\D$, a distributive law $\K=\C\bowtie\D$ exists iff a
cheap fibrewise freeness test (L) and a global closure search (G) succeed, and the
obstruction to (G) is a computable holonomy --- the finite shadow of the laxator
in the ga-containers framing. The matched-pair calculus (\S4) exhibits the law
$\lambda$ explicitly as the carrier of the mutual action; the monoid anchor
(\S\ref{sec:monoid}) ties it to Brin's classical theory and clarifies, honestly,
the place of update monads; and the genuinely pairwise obstruction
(\S\ref{sec:ce}) shows the two-level structure is intrinsic, not an artifact of
the endomorphism special case. The separation of a local, divisibility-style
test from a global holonomy search is exactly what an implementable
compositional-correctness checker needs.
